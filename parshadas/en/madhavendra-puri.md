# Madhavendra Puri
*Мадхавендра Пури · @madhavendra-puri*

## The First Sprout of the Tree of Devotion
Madhavendra Puri came into the world as early as the fourteenth century, long before Sri Chaitanya, and yet in His lila he stands at
the very source. Before coming into the world, the Lord sends ahead those whom He will honour as parents, teachers and
elders, so that each of them may serve Him in his own way:

“Before descending to the Earth, Krishna sends here many of His devotees, who accept the roles of His venerable
forebears, teachers and elders.” (CC, Adi 3.92)

“Madhavendra Puri, Ishvara Puri, Srimati Shachimata and Jagannatha Mishra appeared together with Advaita Acharya.”
(CC, Adi 3.94)

Elsewhere the “Charitamrita” (Adi 13.52–55) names this circle of elders in greater detail: “Madhava Puri,” Shachi,
Jagannatha Mishra, Keshava Bharati, Ishvara Puri, Advaita, Srivasa, Acharyaratna, Pundarika Vidyanidhi and Haridasa.
Thus Gaurasundara, in whom Radha and Madhava are united, revealed on earth His elders in the chain of teachers even
before His own appearance in Sridham Mayapur-Navadvipa. Lochana Dasa adds in the “Chaitanya-mangala” that together
with the Lord, with Nityananda — Balarama — and Advaita — Shiva — and His other eternal companions, both Ishvara Puri
and Madhavendra Puri descended. <!-- F1 F2 F3 F4 Q4 -->

Vrindavana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 9.155–160) speaks of him in a way that leaves little to add: his
body is woven of divine love, and all who are near him are full of it; he knows no food but the taste of Krishna;
Krishna Himself plays within his body; and if one more proof of his love is needed, it is enough to say that his
disciple was Advaita Acharya. Gaurachandra would say again and again, “Madhavendra is the first architect
(adi-sutradhara) of the rasa of devotion,” and He called him the original fountainhead of jubilant love for Krishna.
<!-- F23 F24 Q1 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja, describing Sri Chaitanya's tree of devotion, begins with him:

“All glory to Sri Madhavendra Puri, the treasury of unalloyed loving service to Krishna! In him this wish-fulfilling
tree of bhakti first sprouted.” (CC, Adi 9.10)

“Then this seed of the wish-fulfilling tree of bhakti took the form of a new shoot — Sri Ishvara Puri. And soon the
gardener Himself, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, became the main trunk of this tree of bhakti.” (CC, Adi 9.11)

The gardener and the trunk of this tree is Mahaprabhu Himself; it was planted in Navadvipa and grew up in Puri and
Vrindavana. Madhavendra is its first sprout, the one in which the seed of devotion first bore fruit; Ishvara Puri is
that same sprout grown strong and tall; higher up the trunk divides into two — Advaita and Nityananda (CC, Adi 9.21) —
while below it is held by nine roots, the sannyasis of whom we shall speak further on (CC, Adi 9.13–15).
<!-- F25 F26 Q2 Q3 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.2272–2274) calls Madhavendra loving devotion embodied: the mere remembrance of his name
brings every perfection, and Ishvara Puri, Ranga Puri and his other disciples are intoxicated with the taste of
bhakti; and he had many disciples firm in such devotion throughout Bengal and Orissa. The “Charitamrita” extols him as
the spiritual teacher of the whole world, the giver of divine love (CC, Antya 8.31). The Gaudiya tradition sees in him
an intimate companion of Sri Vrishabhanu-nandini — Radha, the original abode of love — and of Sri Radha-Giridhari;
even eternally perfect devotees worship his feet. He is the parama-guru of Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita, playing the
part of a revered preceptor, and both Vallabhacharya and his son Vitthalanatha always worshipped him.
<!-- F27 F28 F30 Q10 -->

He bore several names. Most often he is called Madhavendra Puri or Madhava Puri; his disciples and intimates said
“Puri Gosani,” “Puri Gosvami,” “Shripada,” “Puripada”; in the writings of Vallabha's followers he is Madhavendra Yati
or Madhava Yatindra, and the “Vallabha-digvijaya” calls him simply “the brahmana Madhava.” <!-- F5 -->

## Heir to the Line of Madhva
In the age of Kali the world is purified by four Vaishnava lines of succession — the sampradayas of Brahma, Sri, Rudra
and the Sanakas. Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” that the Vaishnavas who teach pure
devotion on the basis of the Vedanta are divided into these four branches. Madhavendra belonged to the Brahma, or
Madhva, sampradaya: he accepted the line of Sri Madhvacharya and Vaishnava sannyasa and became a guru within it.
<!-- F6 F7 -->

The succession of Madhva is recorded in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” in the “Prameya-ratnavali” and by Gopala Guru
Gosvami; almost the same verses, with small variant readings, are given in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.2149–2162).
According to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (22), the chain runs thus: the Lord of the spiritual world — Brahma —
Narada — Vyasa — Shuka; from Vyasa Madhvacharya received the mantra of Krishna, and after him came Padmanabha,
Narahari, Madhava-dvija, Akshobhya, Jayatirtha, Jnanasindhu, Mahanidhi, Vidyanidhi, Rajendra and Jayadharma; from
Jayadharma's circle also came Vishnu Puri, the author of the “Bhakti-ratnavali.” Then the line passes through
Purushottama[^1] to Vyasatirtha, the author of the “Vishnu-samhita,” from him to Lakshmipati Tirtha, and from
Lakshmipati to Madhavendra Puri. <!-- F8 F9 F10 -->

Vyasatirtha was an ascetic of the Vyasaraya Matha and stood fourteenth in descent from Madhva in the Madhva-Gaudiya
chain. According to one opinion, his disciple was Lakshmipati Tirtha, and Madhavendra was the follower of the latter;
Madhavendra, then, was a disciple of Vyasatirtha's disciple. Vyasatirtha left the “Nyayamrita,” the
“Tatparya-chandrika,” the “Tarka-tandava,” the “Bheda-ujjivana,” the three “Khandana-traya-mandara-manjaris” and the
“Tattva-viveka-mandara-manjari”; even the foremost Mayavadis acknowledged the “Nyayamrita.” An old Bengali verse says
the same: Vyasatirtha was Madhva's servant, Lakshmipati was Vyasa's servant, from him came Madhavendra Puri, and among
the disciples of the great Madhavendra were Ishvara, Nityananda and Advaita; and a Sanskrit stanza honours his
disciples — Ishvara, Advaita and Nityananda, the gurus of the world — and Sri Chaitanya, the disciple of Ishvara.
<!-- F14 F15 -->

The “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” adds that it was Madhavendra who set this dharma in motion — and his path lives on to
this day. There he is also called an incarnation of the wish-fulfilling tree that grows in Vraja, bearing four fruits:
servitude (dasya), friendship (sakhya), parental love (vatsalya) and conjugal love (ujjvala). His disciple Ishvara
Puri accepted the fruit of shringara, Advaita two fruits, dasya and sakhya, and Sri Ranga Puri took shelter of
vatsalya (GGD 23–24).[^2] <!-- F11 F12 F13 Q5 -->

Of his homeland only Vallabha's followers give any account. Yadunatha's “Vallabha-digvijaya” (section 1, 1658 of the
Vikrama era) calls him “Madhavendra Yati, an Andhra, a follower of the Madhva sampradaya, an incarnation of the rishi
Shandilya”; the “Nij-varta” calls him a Telugu brahmana, an acharya of the Madhva sampradaya and the guru of Krishna
Chaitanya; the “252 Vartas” — a sannyasi of the Madhva sampradaya who lived at Arail. <!-- F22 -->

What was new in this ancient line was what Madhavendra himself brought into it. Bhaktivinoda Thakura writes that the
disciple of his disciple was Sri Chaitanya, and that before him there is no evidence of loving devotion (prema-bhakti)
in the line of Madhva; from Madhvacharya down to Lakshmipati, Madhavendra's guru, it knew no devotion filled with the
taste of conjugal love — what the spirit of Madhva's followers then was can be seen from Mahaprabhu's conversation
with the Tattvavadis in the south. Madhavendra, by composing the verse “ayi dīna-dayārdra nātha,” sowed the seed of
such devotion, and in that verse lies the germ of Mahaprabhu's entire teaching. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati calls
him the first sprout of the wish-fulfilling tree of divine love to rise from the line of Madhva: before him there was
not a trace of the conjugal mood in it. <!-- F16 F17 F18 -->

Before Madhavendra, down to Lakshmipati Tirtha, this line worshipped Krishna alone; from Madhavendra there descended
into it love for Krishna in separation (vipralambha), rooted in wholehearted service to Sri Radha. Only those in whom
a taste for it has awakened, and who are bound to him by dear ties, are eligible for it. The great souls say:
Madhavendra is the root of the tree of shringara-rasa, Ishvara Puri its shoot, Mahaprabhu the main trunk, and His
followers the branches and twigs. And in our own time Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha, in a speech at Udupi, recalled that the
root of the Gaudiya sampradaya is the succession of Madhva, and that Madhavendra, Mahaprabhu's parama-guru and the
first artist of loving devotion, belonged to it, while Mahaprabhu is the chief preacher of that devotion in the mood
of separation. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta visited Udupi more than once, collected Madhva literature and invited a swami
from there to teach Madhva philosophy at the head matha in Mayapur. <!-- F19 F20 F21 -->

## Intoxicated with Love
Even before the Lord's appearance Madhavendra lived in a state that the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Antya 4.402–412)
describes with wonder. Trembling, tears, hair standing on end, roaring and thunderous cries, loud laughter, stupor and
perspiration never left him. Absorbed in Govinda, he was unaware of the outer world: walking along a road he would
break into dance of his own accord, calling out to Hari; at times he lay unconscious for two or three praharas; in
separation he wept in torrents like the Ganga, and in a moment of bliss he would forget even his clothing. Day and
night, intoxicated with love for Krishna, he would laugh, weep and cry “Hai, hai!” like a madman (CB, Adi 9). At the
mere sight of a cloud — it reminded him of Krishna — he would fall senseless. <!-- F31 F32 F33 -->

He lived by the vow of ayachaka-vritti: he never asked for food and ate only what was given unasked; if nothing was
given, he fasted. He felt neither hunger nor thirst, for the nectar of love sustained him, and he remained detached
and indifferent to everything worldly (CC, Madhya 4.123–124, 4.180). <!-- F34 -->

## Grief for the World and the Meeting with Advaita
Happy in Krishna, Madhavendra suffered deeply to see people without devotion. Before Sri Chaitanya's appearance the
world was bereft of love for Vishnu and sunk in passion and ignorance: people revelled in songs about Yogipala,
Bhogipala and Mahipala, even sannyasis called themselves “Narayana,” and the “men of knowledge, yogis and ascetics”
only argued and did not accept the form of Krishna (CB, Antya 4.416–436). He had no one to talk to, and he stopped
speaking with sannyasis. “Better to go and live in the forest,” he decided; “there at least there is no talk with
non-Vaishnavas.” <!-- F32 F35 F36 -->

Day after day he thought of the good of the people, and his mind was turned to one thing: the descent of Krishna.
Vrindavana Dasa shows how Madhavendra, seeing the wretched state of the world, longed ardently for Krishna to come,
and on meeting Advaita passed on to Him the whole force of that longing. <!-- F37 I10 Q6 -->

So, grieving for the world, he came to Shantipur, to the house of Advaita Acharya. Advaita, who Himself grieved for
the people, was then teaching the “Gita” and the “Bhagavatam,” revealing their meaning as devotion. Seeing in His
guest the signs of a Vaishnava, He at once fell at his feet; Madhavendra embraced Him and bathed Him in tears.
Absorbed in talk of Krishna, both forgot their bodies: at the sight of a cloud Madhavendra fell senseless, at the name
of Krishna he roared. Advaita rejoiced to see such devotion to Vishnu and accepted instruction from him (CB, Antya
4.419–440). Madhavendra, delighted by Advaita's love for Krishna, gave Him initiation into the mantra of Krishna,
instruction and all that goes with them.[^3] Advaita is Himself a manifestation of the Supreme Lord; and yet,
convinced by His guest's ecstatic love that here was His guru, He underwent the rite of initiation to show conditioned
souls that no one can do without a guru: He played the part of Madhavendra's disciple.[^4]
<!-- F38 F39 F40 F41 F42 Q13 -->

The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Antya 4.398) says that Madhavendra and Advaita are not different from each other, and yet
Advaita is his disciple. That is why the Lord honoured Advaita as a guru, while Advaita Himself saw in Chaitanya the
Lord and regarded Himself as His servant — His principal limb (CC, Adi 6.36–41). <!-- F43 F44 Q7 Q22 -->

At about the same time Madhavendra, with his disciple Sri Ranga Puri, a resident of Pandharpur, also visited Sridham
Mayapur, the house of Jagannatha Mishra. Many years later Ranga Puri told the Lord in Pandharpur:

“They had taken their meal then in the house of Jagannatha Mishra, and he still remembered the indescribable taste of
the banana-flower curry.” (CC, Madhya 9.296)

“Jagannatha Mishra's wife had stayed in his memory as a deeply devoted and chaste woman. She showed them such care, as
though she were the mother of the whole universe.” (CC, Madhya 9.297)

Shachi fed the sannyasis as if they were her own sons. She and Jagannatha had a worthy son who took sannyasa under the
name Shankararanya and attained perfection in Pandharpur. <!-- F45 F46 -->

## The Meeting with Nityananda
Wandering as he pleased, together with disciples who relished the taste of love for Krishna, Madhavendra once met
Nityananda by chance as the latter was touring the holy places of western India.[^5] On seeing each other, both fell
senseless and grew stunned with love; the disciples, led by Ishvara Puri, wept. Coming to themselves, the two embraced
and rolled on the ground, roaring with love for Krishna; rivers of tears flowed from their eyes, and the earth thought
itself blessed; trembling, tears, hair on end — “Chaitanya was playing in both their bodies,” says the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 9.158–168). <!-- F47 F48 F49 -->

“However many holy places I have visited,” said Nityananda, “only today have I gained their true fruit, in seeing your
feet. Nowhere have I seen such love.” And Madhavendra embraced Him, took Him on his lap and could not utter a word for
tears — he only held Him to his breast. <!-- F50 F51 -->

Madhavendra's disciples — Ishvara Puri, Brahmananda Puri and others — at once came to love Nityananda. They had met
many “sadhus” before, but found love for Krishna in none of them: all were seeking something else — the fruits of
karma, knowledge, yoga, vows and austerities — and the disciples had wandered through the forests in sorrow. Now their
grief vanished. Ishvara Puri, seeing how dear Nityananda was to his guru, was himself, like the others, filled with
great love for Him. <!-- F52 F53 -->

For several days Nityananda wandered with Madhavendra, and through the forests they seemed to be enacting a search for
Krishna. Neither knew day from night, the disciples sang the name of Hari unceasingly, and what the two spoke of only
Krishna knows. “Nowhere have I seen such love,” Madhavendra would say. “Where it is, there are all my holy places.
Krishna is merciful to me, since He has given me such a friend. Where there is Nityananda's company, there are all the
tirthas and Vaikuntha itself. Whoever hears of such a devotee will surely attain Krishna; and whoever bears Nityananda
even the slightest ill will is not dear to Krishna, even if he is a devotee.” So day and night he spoke of Nityananda
and served Him with love (CB, Adi 9.169–185). Madhavendra treated Him as a friend and an equal, while Nityananda
always honoured him only as His guru — for He, the teacher of all the world's teachers, was teaching people thus (CB,
Adi 9.187–188; BR 5.2330–2334). <!-- F54 F55 F56 Q9 -->

At last they parted: Nityananda went to Setubandha, Madhavendra to see the Sarayu. Both were so absorbed in Krishna
that they did not remember their bodies — that is why they survived the separation; had they kept their outer
consciousness, they could not have borne it (CB, Adi 9.189–192). In this mystery — of a life that does not break off
even in unbearable separation — lies the foundation of Gaudiya Vaishnava dharma. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that
life is preserved because service gives an unbroken inner bliss; such love, in the words of the “Charitamrita,” is not
to be found in the world of men (CC, Madhya 2.43). And whoever hears with faith the story of Nityananda and
Madhavendra obtains the treasure of love for Krishna. <!-- F57 F58 I8 Q30 -->

Whose disciple Nityananda Himself was, the traditions tell differently: some consider Him a disciple of Madhavendra,
others of Lakshmipati Tirtha, and so Madhavendra's godbrother; the “Prema-vilasa” names Ishvara Puri as His guru.[^6]
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, listing Madhavendra's principal disciples, names Ishvara Puri, Nityananda and Advaita.

## Gopala on Govardhana
The main part of his story was preserved for us by Mahaprabhu Himself. Having accepted sannyasa at Katwa, He set out
from Shantipur, from Advaita's house, for Puri — along the Ganga, through Chhatrabhoga, Atisara, Panihati and
Baranagar, as far as Vriddha-mantreshvara on the border of Utkala. With Him were Nityananda, Mukunda Datta,
Jagadananda and Damodara. This was in the month of Phalguna in the year 1431 of the Shaka era (1510), immediately
after His sannyasa. At Remuna, in what is now Baleshvar district, before the Deity of Kshirachora-Gopinatha, the Lord
told His companions about Madhavendra as He had heard it from His guru Ishvara Puri — and explained in passing how
Gopinatha came by that name:

“In days of old this Deity stole sweet rice for Madhavendra Puri, and so became famous as ‘Kshira-chora Hari.’” (CC,
Madhya 4.20) <!-- F60 -->

Coming to Vraja, Madhavendra, intoxicated with love, circled Govardhana Hill, bathed in Govinda-kunda and towards
evening sat down under a tree for his evening sandhya. A little cowherd boy came up to him, smiling, with a pot of
milk. “What are you thinking about?” he asked. “Why don't you beg for food? Drink this milk.” The boy's beauty made
Madhavendra forget both hunger and thirst. “Who are You?” he asked. “Where do You live? And how did You know I was
fasting?” The boy answered that he was a cowherd from the village nearby, and that here no one goes hungry: some beg,
some drink milk, and to those who do not beg he himself brings food. The women who had come to fetch water had noticed
the stranger and sent the milk with him; now it was time for him to milk the cows, and he would come back for the pot
later. And he vanished. <!-- F61 F62 F63 -->

Madhavendra drank the milk, washed the pot and waited, chanting the holy names without closing his eyes. Only at the
end of the night did a natural samadhi resembling sleep come over him, and he had a dream. The same boy took him by
the hand, led him to a thicket and said: “I live here and endure cold, rain, wind and heat. Call the people of the
village: let them take Me out, carry Me up to the top of the hill, build Me a temple and bathe Me with plenty of cool
water. Long have I waited for you, thinking: when will Madhavendra come to serve Me?” <!-- F64 F65 -->

“Your love has conquered Me, and I wish to accept your service. Revealing Myself to the world, I shall grant everyone
liberation from samsara.” (CC, Madhya 4.40)

“My name is Sri Gopala. I am the One who lifted Govardhana Hill. Krishna's grandson Vajra installed Me here. I am the
true Lord of this holy abode.” (CC, Madhya 4.41)

Vajra, the son of Aniruddha and great-grandson of Krishna, had been brought from Dvaraka by the Pandavas and made king
of Mathura. Later, Gopala said, the pujari who served Him, fearing a Muslim raid, hid Him in this thicket and fled —
and here He had remained ever since.[^7] <!-- F66 F67 -->

On waking, Madhavendra wept: “Krishna Himself came to me as a cowherd boy, and I did not recognise Him!” — and he
rolled on the ground in love. Then, for the sake of the command, he composed himself, took his morning bath, gathered
the villagers and told them that Govardhana-dhari, the lord of their village, lay hidden in the thicket and must be
dug out. The people cleared the thicket and found a large Deity covered with earth and grass. They carried Him up to
the summit and set Him on a great stone throne.[^8] The brahmanas strained the water of Govinda-kunda and brought it
in a hundred new pitchers; music sounded, people sang and danced, and the hill was covered with the villagers'
offerings — ghee, milk, yoghurt and sweets. <!-- F68 F69 F70 F71 -->

Madhavendra performed the bathing ceremony (abhisheka) himself, according to every rule. He cleansed the body of the
Deity with barley and wheat flour, lodhra powder, pistachios, saffron and pulse flour, with a brush of ushira grass
and the hair of a cow's tail; he anointed Him with oil and bathed Him with panchagavya and panchamrita — first milk
from a conch, then yoghurt, ghee, honey and sugar, each separately, as the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa” (6.30) enjoins — and
finally with a hundred pitchers of water, in the great bath (maha-snana). For this, it is explained, ghee and water
are taken in equal parts, about a hundred sers of water in all (a ser being roughly a litre); the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa”
prescribes a hundred palas of water for a bath, twenty-five palas of oil, and two thousand palas for the maha-snana (a
pala being about forty-five grams). Then the Deity was again anointed and bathed with fragrant sandalwood water from a
conch, dried, dressed in fresh cloth and adorned with sandal paste, tulasi and garlands. Thus Madhavendra bathed
Krishna and in every way gave himself to Him. <!-- F72 F73 F74 Q12 -->

As Krishna at the end of the Dvapara-yuga told the cowherds to honour Govardhana with a mountain of food, so in the
age of Kali Madhavendra had such a mountain prepared for Giridhari Gopala — the annakuta — from the provisions all the
villagers had brought. Ten brahmanas cooked rice, five cooked vegetables, five or six baked flatbreads; around the
mountain of rice were set pots of soups and vegetables, of milk, yoghurt and whey, of shikharini, sweet rice, butter
and cream. Madhavendra offered all this together with pitchers of fragrant water, and Gopala, who had long gone
hungry, ate everything — yet at the touch of His hands everything became as full as before: <!-- F75 F76 -->

“Sri Gopala ate all the offerings, touching them with His hand, but outwardly it was as if nothing had changed.” (CC,
Madhya 4.77)

“Only Madhavendra Puri could see this, for the Lord has no secrets from His dear devotees.” (CC, Madhya 4.78)

The people marvelled at Puri's spiritual power: it was as if the ancient annakuta had come again. Then Gopala was
offered water to rinse His mouth and betel, arati was performed and a new bed was prepared; and when He lay down to
rest, Madhavendra distributed the prasada — first to the brahmanas, then to all the villagers. The news spread through
the district, and village after village began to hold such feasts for Gopala: <!-- F76 F77 F78 -->

“The residents of Vrindavana are always inclined to show their love for Sri Krishna, and Sri Krishna always returns
their love.” (CC, Madhya 4.95)

Madhavendra made all the brahmanas Vaishnavas and gave each of them his own service. A wealthy kshatriya built a
temple, others a kitchen, a storehouse and an enclosing wall;[^9] every family of Vraja gave a cow, so that Gopala
came to have ten thousand cows, and His worship became royal. In time two renunciant brahmanas came from Gauda;
Madhavendra received them with care, made them his disciples and entrusted them with the service of Gopala. By his
mercy, says the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.812–813), both were strong in renunciation and full of loving devotion. Thus
Madhavendra founded a service by renunciants — not a hereditary service of householders passing from son to grandson.
For two years he served Gopala himself. <!-- F79 F80 F81 F82 F195 Q11 -->

So the Lord looks for a pretext to engage in service the devotee He longs for: Gopala had been waiting for
Madhavendra. And in our own time, the biographers remark, the Deities of Jagadisha Pandita seemed of Their own accord
to “offer Themselves” to Bhakti Dayita Madhava Maharaja. <!-- I5 -->

## For Sandalwood: Remuna and Puri
Two years later Gopala came again in a dream. He felt hot in the summer heat, He said; let Madhavendra anoint Him with
Malaya sandalwood — the kind called “malayaja,” because its home is Malaya: the Malabar coast, the mountains of the
Western Ghats and the Nilgiris. “Go yourself, at once, to Nilachala and bring the sandalwood: it cannot be done
through anyone else.” Delighted by the direct command, Madhavendra entrusted the service to a worthy man and
immediately set out east, to Gauda, on the road to Puri. On the way he stopped at Shantipur, at Advaita's house, and
from there went south and came to Remuna. <!-- F88 F89 F90 -->

On seeing the beautiful Gopinatha, Madhavendra sang and danced with love. Delighted with the worship, he asked the
brahmana servitor what was offered to the Deity — so that he might offer the same to his Gopala. The man replied: in
the evening, twelve pots of amrita-keli, thick sweetened milk famed everywhere as “Gopinatha's kshira,” and there is
none better in the world. Madhavendra wished to taste a little of this kshira without asking, so as later to prepare
the same for Gopala. But at once he felt ashamed: this was a whim of the tongue, an offence — and he began to remember
Vishnu. After watching the arati he left and sat down in the deserted marketplace of the village, chanting the holy
names. <!-- F91 F92 F93 -->

Meanwhile the pujari put Gopinatha to rest and lay down himself. In a dream the Deity told him to rise and open the
temple doors: behind the folds of His garment was hidden a pot of kshira for a sannyasi named Madhava Puri, who was
sitting in the market; and how He had set it aside the pujari had not noticed, by the will of His yogamaya.[^10] The
pujari found the kshira where Gopinatha had said and went through the market calling out loudly: <!-- F94 -->

“With the pot of sweet rice in his hands the pujari cried: ‘Madhavendra Puri, answer! Sri Gopinatha Himself has stolen
this pot for you!’” (CC, Madhya 4.133)

“‘Take this pot and enjoy the sweet rice. Truly, there is no one more fortunate than you in the whole universe!’” (CC,
Madhya 4.134)

Madhavendra made himself known. The pujari gave him the kshira, fell at his feet and told him everything; he
understood that before him was the greatest of devotees, one who holds Krishna in his power. And love overwhelmed
Madhavendra. He reverently ate the prasada — to the joy of both Gopinatha and Gopala — washed the clay pot, broke it
into pieces, tied them in the corner of his upper cloth, and afterwards ate one piece every day, each time sinking
again into love. Ever since, Gopinatha has been called Kshirachora — “he who stole the kshira.”
<!-- F95 F96 F97 F98 -->

He knew that by morning the news would spread and crowds would come. Fearing fame, he bowed to Gopinatha and left for
Puri before dawn. But his fame outran him: on seeing Jagannatha he was filled with love, while people who had already
heard of the miracle came to him in a stream to pay their respects. <!-- F99 F100 -->

“Throughout the world the wayward nature of fame is well known. Even if someone does not desire it, by the grace of
providence it sometimes comes to him of itself.” (CC, Madhya 4.146)

“Dreading the honour of all, Sri Madhavendra Puri left Remuna. But fame followed him, born of his pure love for
Krishna.” (CC, Madhya 4.147)

Fame comes of itself to one who has love for God, even if he runs from it. <!-- I3 Q14 -->

He could not leave Puri: first he had to keep his promise. He told the servitors and managers of Jagannatha's temple
about the sandalwood, and those who had connections with the king obtained, through the king's men, sandalwood and
camphor — the very camphor used in Jagannatha's arati. They gave him a brahmana and a servant to help him and money
for the road, and the king gave him a letter exempting him from tolls at the river crossings. He carried one maund of
sandalwood and about twenty tolas of camphor, and the road ran through Muslim lands, past countless toll-posts, while
he had not a single coin. The toll-collectors of Orissa detained him but let him pass on the strength of the king's
letter. Covering a thousand kroshas on this errand, he gave no thought to the hardships — he only rejoiced that he
would anoint Gopala (CC, Madhya 4.176–186): <!-- F101 F102 -->

“Such is the natural conduct of one who possesses selfless devotion to God: he does not think of his own comfort, and
no difficulties or sufferings ever trouble him.” (CC, Madhya 4.186)

“And to show people the depth of Madhavendra Puri's divine love, Sri Gopala ordered him to bring sandalwood and
camphor from Jagannatha Puri.” (CC, Madhya 4.187)

Gopala gave this order to show the world his love and to test him. He knew how hard it was to carry sandalwood through
Muslim lands, and by accepting it He made His devotee's labour fruitful: having tested him, He showed His mercy. Deep
love takes no account of its own toil. <!-- F107 I4 -->

On the way back, at Remuna, Madhavendra again sang and danced before Gopinatha, took the prasada of kshira and spent
the night in the temple. Towards morning Gopala appeared to him in a dream: “Madhava, I have already received all the
sandalwood and camphor. Grind the sandalwood with the camphor and anoint Gopinatha: His body and Mine are one, and
this will cool Me too. Do not hesitate.” Madhavendra passed the command on to Gopinatha's servitors; they rejoiced,
for it was the height of the heat. Two of his companions and two of the temple's men ground sandalwood every day, and
Madhavendra stayed in Remuna the whole summer, until the sandalwood was used up. When the rains began he returned to
Puri and spent the chaturmasya there. <!-- F103 F104 F105 F106 Q15 -->

Having finished this story, Mahaprabhu asked Nityananda:

“‘O Nityananda,’ the Lord asked, ‘what do you think — could anyone in this world be more fortunate than Madhavendra
Puri?’” (CC, Madhya 4.171)

“Krishna Himself appeared before him, giving him a pot of milk. Three times, too, He came to him in dreams and gave
him direct instructions.” (CC, Madhya 4.172)

“Conquered by his pure love, Sri Krishna appeared as Sri Gopala and, accepting his service, delivered the whole
world.” (CC, Madhya 4.173)

“For his sake Sri Gopinatha stole sweet rice, becoming known after that as the Deity ‘Kshira-chora Gopinatha.’” (CC,
Madhya 4.174)

And the Lord stressed further that Madhavendra had gone the whole way without a second companion:

“He was supremely detached, silent and indifferent to everything. Fearing talk of worldly things, he always lived
alone.” (CC, Madhya 4.179) <!-- F108 F109 -->

## Disciples and Followers
Among Madhavendra's disciples the biographies, drawing on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” and the “Charitamrita,” name
Ishvara Puri, Advaita Acharya, Nityananda (a disputed point, as we have seen), Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Paramananda Puri,
Sri Ranga Puri, the Sanodiya brahmana, Raghupati Upadhyaya, Keshava Bharati, Brahmananda Puri and Brahmananda Bharati,
Keshava Puri, Krishnananda Puri, Nrisimha Tirtha, Sukhananda Puri and Ramachandra Puri. <!-- F111 F112 -->

The dearest of them, in Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's words, was Ishvara Puri, a brahmana from Kumarahatta. He received
initiation and sannyasa from Madhavendra and gave himself wholly to the service of his guru; by the time of the
meeting with Nityananda he was already his teacher's first servant and constant companion. Mahaprabhu accepted him as
His guru, says Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in order to reveal his glory. <!-- F113 F114 -->

Paramananda Puri, a brahmana from Tirhut, likewise received initiation from Madhavendra; the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”
(Antya 3.178) calls him a dear disciple of Madhava Puri, full of the taste of divine love. For this reason Mahaprabhu
honoured him as a dear elder, and on first seeing him exclaimed, “Today My sannyasa is fulfilled — it is as if
Madhavendra himself has appeared to Me!” (CB, Antya 3.168–175). Later, at the devotees' request, it was he who
interceded with the Lord on behalf of Chhota Haridasa. In the tree of devotion of the “Charitamrita” he is the middle
one of the nine roots; the other roots are Keshava Bharati, Brahmananda Puri, Brahmananda Bharati, Vishnu Puri,
Keshava Puri, Krishnananda Puri, Nrisimha Tirtha and Sukhananda Puri — and above them are the first sprout,
Madhavendra, the second, Ishvara Puri, and the trunk, Mahaprabhu (CC, Adi 9.13–16). <!-- F115 F116 F117 F118 Q23 -->

Mahaprabhu met Sri Ranga Puri in Pandharpur, in the house of a brahmana where he was staying, and fell at his feet in
love:

“Seeing Sri Chaitanya before him offering dandavat, Sri Ranga Puri was amazed and exclaimed: ‘O Shripada, please
rise!’” (CC, Madhya 9.288)

“‘O Shripada! You are surely connected with my teacher, Sri Madhavendra Puri. Were it not so, there would not be even
a particle of such divine love in You.’” (CC, Madhya 9.289)

Learning that the Lord was a disciple of Ishvara Puri, Ranga Puri was filled with tenderness towards Him, and for
seven days they spoke of Krishna. From him the Lord first heard that Shankararanya, Vishvarupa, had attained
perfection right there in Pandharpur. Then Ranga Puri left for Dvaraka; it is said that later, when the Lord returned
from the south, they met again, and Ranga Puri spent the rest of his life with Him in Purushottama-kshetra.
<!-- F119 F120 F121 Q17 -->

Pundarika Vidyanidhi, the guru of Gadadhara Pandita, was also a disciple of Madhavendra; he is believed to have
received initiation at the same time as Advaita. Mahaprabhu always honoured him as Madhavendra's disciple, a
godbrother of His own guru (GGD 56; “Prema-vilasa” 22). <!-- F122 F123 F124 Q24 -->

In Mathura Madhavendra came to the house of the Sanodiya brahmana, gave him the mantra and accepted a meal from him.
This brahmana came from a family of priests of the Sanvar caste, associated with goldsmiths. Bhaktivinoda Thakura
explains that among the western vaishyas the Agravals are considered pure, while the Kanvars and Sanvars are
considered fallen; their priests are called Sanodiya brahmanas, and sannyasis do not eat at their houses. But
Madhavendra, seeing in him a Vaishnava, accepted food from him — and thereby showed what daiva-varnashrama is: a
Vaishnava is not judged by caste. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls the Sanodiya brahmana a pure devotee: although by caste
rules it was forbidden to drink water from his hands, he stood on daiva-varnashrama, and those who judge devotees by
worldly caste or despise maha-prasada the Lord called wicked fools. <!-- F83 F125 F128 I6 -->

By Madhavendra's mercy love for Krishna awoke in this brahmana. Many years later, already an old man, he saw the Lord
dancing in ecstasy in Mathura, at the temple of Keshavaji, fell at His feet and joined in His dance; the people,
seeing such love, decided that before them was an incarnation of Krishna, while the brahmana recognised in Him the son
of the king of Vraja and understood at once that He was connected with Madhavendra. In private Mahaprabhu called him a
venerable, simple-hearted old brahmana and asked him where he had obtained such a treasure:

“The brahmana answered: ‘Once the venerable Shripada Sri Madhavendra Puri came to Mathura during his pilgrimage.’”
(CC, Madhya 17.166)

By his causeless mercy Madhavendra came to his house, accepted him as a disciple and even consented to dine with him
(CC, Madhya 17.167).

“‘Then Sri Gopala revealed Himself to the world, and Madhavendra Puri established His worship. From that day this
Deity has been served on Govardhana Hill.’” (CC, Madhya 17.168)

Hearing this, the Lord, as before a godbrother of His guru, bowed down at his feet: “You are My guru, I am your
disciple, and it is not fitting for a guru to bow to his disciple.” And the brahmana replied that such love does not
exist without a connection with Madhavendra: without him there is not even a scent of it (CC, Madhya 17.170–173). He
asked Balabhadra Bhattacharya the name of the Lord's guru and rejoiced; the Lord accepted bhiksha from him. The
Sanodiya brahmana became His companion in His circuit of the twelve forests of Vraja and, together with Balabhadra,
considered how to lead the Lord on to Soro-kshetra and Prayaga. <!-- F126 F127 F279 Q18 -->

Raghupati Upadhyaya of Tirhut, Mithila, a famous scholar, was also a disciple of Madhavendra (according to the
“Gaudiya-bhashya” on CB, Adi 9.154); his verses are included in the “Padyavali” (99, 127). In Prayaga, in the presence
of Vallabha Bhatta, he met the Lord, recognised Him as Krishna and spoke with Him about the degrees of excellence (CC,
Madhya 19.99–100). <!-- F129 -->

Keshava Bharati, of the Bharati order going back to Shankara, head of the matha in Katwa — the one who gave sannyasa
to Nimai[^11] — was, in the opinion of some, a mantra-disciple of Madhavendra and a Vaishnava sannyasi. Brahmananda
Puri is called by some an initiated disciple of Madhavendra, by others his follower; he was present at the meeting
with Nityananda. Brahmananda Bharati, and also Keshava Puri, Krishnananda Puri, Nrisimha Tirtha and Sukhananda Puri,
are among the nine sannyasis who followed Madhavendra; according to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (97–100), they
belong to the eight siddhas and nine Yogendras. Some call Keshava Puri the elder brother of Vallabhacharya, but this
report has not been verified. <!-- F130 F131 F132 F133 -->

Through his disciple's disciple Madhavendra is connected with Ramananda Raya as well: according to the
“Bhajana-nirnaya,” Ramananda was a disciple of Raghavendra Puri and thus a disciple of Madhavendra's disciple. With
Vallabhacharya (Vallabha Bhatta) he is linked by a chain of teachers: Vallabha was a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita,
Gadadhara of Pundarika, and Pundarika of Madhavendra. According to the “Nij-varta,” in early childhood Vallabha
studied the Vedas with Madhavendra in Kashi, and the “Vallabha-digvijaya” calls Madhavendra his teacher in the
Vaishnava scriptures. Sanatana Gosvami, in the auspicious opening verses of his work, honours “Sri Madhava Puri, king
of sannyasis, surrounded by disciples, through whom the immortal tree of devotion to Krishna sprouted in the world.”
<!-- F135 F136 F137 -->

Among those who called themselves his disciples was also Ramachandra Puri. Where he came from is unknown. Formally he
was a sannyasi of one of the ten orders of Shankara; he inclined to dry knowledge and the philosophy of Mayavada — a
disciple in appearance only. The Lord and Paramananda Puri honoured him as a disciple of Madhavendra, and in Benares
Mahaprabhu announced that He was staying with him so as to avoid reproach. What came of his discipleship is seen in
Madhavendra's last days. <!-- F134 -->

## Departure
Towards the end of his life Madhavendra grew weak and could no longer move. He chanted the names of Krishna and wept,
for he was in deep separation — in the mood of Radha after Krishna's departure for Mathura. Like every pure devotee,
he felt that he had not obtained Krishna's mercy, because he had not become worthy of it. <!-- F138 F139 F140 -->

“When his teacher, the great saint Madhavendra Puri, was on the point of death, Ramachandra Puri came to see him.”
(CC, Antya 8.16)

“Madhavendra Puri ceaselessly chanted the holy name of Krishna. At times he prayed with tears in his eyes: ‘O my Lord,
I have never attained shelter in Mathura.’” (CC, Antya 8.17)

“Then Ramachandra Puri foolishly instructed him. Without a trace of shame or fear he addressed his teacher:” (CC,
Antya 8.18)

“‘You are full of spiritual bliss and should always remember Brahman alone. For if you have realised Brahman, what
could make you weep?’” (CC, Antya 8.19) <!-- F141 -->

Madhavendra grew angry: “Away, away, you sinner! I am dying of grief that I have not obtained Krishna's mercy and have
not reached Mathura, and you have come to add to my torment. Do not show me your face, go wherever you like: if I die
looking at you, an evil destination awaits me. This fool is teaching me about Brahman!” (CC, Antya 8.20–23).
<!-- F142 -->

“Thus Madhavendra Puri rejected his disciple Ramachandra Puri. This offence against his teacher awakened in
Ramachandra Puri a multitude of worldly desires.” (CC, Antya 8.24)

“Dry, detached realisation of the Absolute does not develop a person's spiritual relationship with Krishna. On the
contrary, such an impersonalist philosopher soon becomes attached to offences and begins to criticise everyone
indiscriminately.” (CC, Antya 8.25)

Ramachandra reviled everyone, and everyone reviled him. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya” that
Ramachandra did not recognise the transcendental nature of his guru's separation; he took him for an ordinary man and
his feeling for a worldly one, born of some lack, and set about teaching him the oneness of Brahman. Seeing his
disciple's foolishness and disrespect, Madhavendra ceased to wish him well and drove him away. The “worldly desires”
here are the craving for dry knowledge, from which grows the tendency to revile devotees. Such is the fruit of
disregard for the guru: step by step the offence reaches even the Lord Himself (CC, Antya 8.97–98) — later Ramachandra
reviled Mahaprabhu too: finding no fault in Him, he noticed a line of ants in His room, suspected that the Lord had
been eating sweets and slandered Him — and fell, as every reviler of the guru and the Vaishnavas falls.
<!-- F143 F144 F145 F280 Q21 I12 -->

Quite differently did Ishvara Puri serve his teacher:

“Whereas another of his disciples, Ishvara Puri (the teacher of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu), personally tended
Madhavendra Puri, cleaning away his urine and stool with his own hands.” (CC, Antya 8.26)

“Ishvara Puri constantly helped his teacher to remember Krishna, telling of Krishna's lilas and chanting His holy
name.” (CC, Antya 8.27)

“Fully satisfied with Ishvara Puri, Madhavendra Puri embraced him and blessed him: ‘May you obtain all the wealth of
divine love for Krishna.’” (CC, Antya 8.28)

Of all the disciples he served best. Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami remarks that perfection is attained by the guru's
mercy, and the apparent infirmity of a Vaishnava only gives his disciples an opportunity to serve. <!-- F146 I9 -->

“Thus Ishvara Puri became like an ocean of love for Krishna, whereas Ramachandra Puri became an inveterate critic.”
(CC, Antya 8.29)

“Thus these two disciples became evidence of the results of the blessing and the rejection of a true saint. Through
them Madhavendra Puri taught a lesson to the people of the whole world.” (CC, Antya 8.30)

One who pleases a great soul becomes an ocean of love; one who angers him becomes a reviler. A disciple who satisfies
his guru obtains every good, while the guru's displeasure brings only misfortune. <!-- F147 I1 -->

Bestowing love for Krishna on the world, Madhavendra Puri, the teacher of the whole world, departed with a single
verse on his lips:

*ayi dīna-dayārdra nātha he*  
*mathurā-nātha kadāvalokyase*  
*hṛdayaṁ tvad-aloka-kātaraṁ*  
*dayita bhrāmyati kiṁ karomy aham*

“O Lord, whose heart melts with pity for the wretched! O Lord of Mathura, when shall I see You? Not seeing You, my
heart wanders in anguish. Beloved, what am I to do?” (CC, Antya 8.32) <!-- F28 F148 -->

“With this verse Madhavendra Puri teaches us all how we may obtain pure love for Krishna. Only acute separation from
Krishna can raise a devotee to the level of this sacred love.” (CC, Antya 8.33)

“Before his departure Madhavendra Puri planted in this world the seed of unalloyed love for Krishna. Soon that seed
became the glorious tree named Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.” (CC, Antya 8.34)

With this verse, the “Charitamrita” says in another place too (Madhya 4.196), Madhavendra attained perfection at the
end of his life. His day of departure is honoured on the twelfth lunar day of the bright fortnight of Phalguna — Govinda in the
Vaishnava calendar (phalguna shukla-dvadashi).[^21] Where it took place the “Charitamrita” does not say.[^12] All that is clear is that by Phalguna of
1431 Shaka, when Mahaprabhu told of him in Remuna, Madhavendra had already withdrawn from the eyes of the world.[^13]
<!-- F149 F150 F151 F152 F153 F154 Q20 -->

## The Day of Remembrance in Advaita's House
Every year Advaita Acharya celebrated the day of remembrance of His guru (CB, Antya 4.441–468).[^14] Once this day fell
at the time when the Lord, already a sannyasi, was staying with Him at Shantipur (CB, Antya 4.396–397). Advaita
joyfully spent all He had, and provisions poured in from every side. Gaurasundara with all His companions was happy.
Ai — Shachi — together with the wives of the Vaishnavas took on all the cooking, Nityananda the reception and
honouring of the Vaishnavas; some ground sandalwood, others wove garlands, carried water, swept the courtyard, washed
the guests' feet, hung up flags and canopies; the sankirtana never ceased, and Advaita's house became Vaikuntha.
<!-- F155 F158 Q8 -->

The Lord went round the storerooms and inspected the provisions: rooms full of rice, mountains of food, pots, mung
beans, cloth, leaf-plates, chira, thousands of bunches of bananas, coconuts, betel, vegetables, thousands of pots of
yoghurt and milk, oil, salt and ghee. All the Vaishnavas, anointed with sandal paste and wearing garlands, sang and
danced. Nityananda danced like a child, Advaita and Haridasa without tiring, and at last the Lord stood everyone in a
circle and Himself danced in the middle. Then all sat down to the meal; the Lord sat in the centre, “like ten million
moons among the stars,” and spoke all the time of Madhava Puri: “Whoever eats here on the day of Madhavendra's worship
obtains devotion to Govinda” (CB, Antya 4.492–508). <!-- F156 F157 -->

## Madhavendra's Verses
Rupa Gosvami preserved five of Madhavendra's verses in the “Padyavali.” In one (79) he takes leave of his former
duties: “Farewell, sandhya-vandana! Obeisances to you, ritual baths! Gods and forefathers, forgive me: I can no longer
offer you libations. Wherever I sit, I remember the Enemy of Kamsa, the ornament of the Yadu dynasty — and that, I
think, is enough.” In another (96) he prays that there may appear in his mind that cowherd boy, expert in the games of
love, who longs for the rasa dance with the maidens of Vraja. The third (104) is a sigh: “When shall I see the son of
Nanda, with a garland of kadamba flowers and tilaka on His brow?” In the fourth (286) sounds the flute of Hari, full
of the sweetness of the nectar of His lips: it delights the deer, the doe-eyed maidens and the sages. The fifth (330)
is “ayi dīna-dayārdra nātha,” the very verse that the “Charitamrita” quotes twice (Madhya 4.197 and Antya 8.32).
<!-- F159 F160 Q29 -->

At Remuna, having told His companions about Madhavendra, Mahaprabhu Himself recited this verse — and became so
intoxicated with love that Nityananda had to catch Him and hold Him up (CC, Madhya 4.197–199). Before that, He spoke
of the verse thus: <!-- F110 -->

“As the fragrance of Malaya sandalwood grows stronger with rubbing, so the meaning of this verse, when one reflects on
it, begins to shine with ever new facets.” (CC, Madhya 4.192)

“As the Kaustubha gem is acknowledged the best of precious stones, so this verse is the best in the poetic description
of the divine rasas.” (CC, Madhya 4.193)

“This verse was spoken by Srimati Radharani Herself, and by Her mercy it came forth from the lips of Sri Madhavendra
Puri.” (CC, Madhya 4.194)

“Sri Chaitanya could relish its secret meaning in full. A fourth who could do so simply does not exist.” (CC, Madhya
4.195) <!-- F161 Q16 -->

Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains where the power of this verse lies. The best way to worship Krishna is to follow the
mood of Radhika's great love in separation, after His departure for Mathura. A devotee of this taste, considering
himself insignificant, calls out to the “Lord merciful to the wretched”; the feeling of separation is the natural
bhajana of the soul. It is akin to Radha's feelings on seeing Uddhava, revealed in Mahaprabhu. <!-- I7 -->

A Sanskrit hymn, the “Yugalashtakam,” is also ascribed to Madhavendra: a manuscript of it, one leaf in Bengali script,
is kept at the Bangiya Sahitya Parishad in Calcutta (no. 513 in its catalogue of Sanskrit manuscripts). <!-- F162 -->

## Gopala after Madhavendra: Shrinathji
The Gopala revealed by Madhavendra was also called Sri Govardhana-natha, the Lord of Govardhana; in time He became
famous as Sri Govardhananathji, or Shrinathji for short. Under this name He is served by the descendants and followers
of Vallabhacharya, whereas the Gaudiya authors call Him, as Madhavendra did, Gopala. He is that very two-armed Krishna
of Madhavendra of the Madhva sampradaya; under the authority of Vallabha's son He was taken away from Mathura-mandala,
and today, in the hills of Rajasthan, the Vallabha sampradaya serves Him with great splendour. His history after
Madhavendra is long and tangled; the Gaudiya and Vallabhite traditions tell it differently, and both sides are given
below. <!-- F163 F194 -->

### Gopala in Mahaprabhu's Day
It is said that in 1498 the henchmen of Sikandar Lodi began to persecute the temples of Vishnu in Vraja. The Gaudiya
pujaris then carried Gopala off to the dense forest of Taker-ghona, some three miles away, and served Him there in
secret — of this, tradition says, Kumbhandasa sings: “You love the dense forest of Tonda.” When Lodi's men, having
ravaged the temple on Govardhana, had gone, Gopala was moved to Shyamadhak, about a mile from Giriraja, and served in
a grass hut. There Vallabhacharya saw Him and, it is said, on the second lunar day of the bright fortnight of Chaitra
1499 installed Him again on Giriraja. <!-- F166 -->

Vallabhacharya, who had previously worshipped with the mantra of Bala-Gopala, received by the mercy of Gadadhara
Pandita initiation into the mantra of Kishora-Gopala (CC, Antya 7.144–145). In 1434 or 1435 of the Shaka era
(1512–1513) Mahaprabhu came to Arail, where Rupa and his brother Vallabha (Anupama), the father of Jiva, were then
living, and showed His mercy to Vallabhacharya; Vallabha with his whole family drank the water that had washed the
Lord's feet. His elder son Gopinatha, born in 1432 Shaka (1510), was then two or three years old, while the younger,
Vitthala, was not yet born: he came into the world in 1437 Shaka (1515), as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta also states in the
“Anubhashya” (on CC, Madhya 18.47 and 19.108). According to another account, both sons were small, and Vallabha laid
them at the Lord's feet (CC, Madhya 19.84–112). Vitthalanatha's horoscope, cast by Govindasvami, gives a Friday, the
ninth lunar day of the dark fortnight of Pausha, 21 dandas 25 palas, the year 1572 of the Vikrama era — 1437 Shaka,
1515; the Vallabhite author Televala writes that he was born at Charanat near Allahabad and spent his childhood at
Adel.[^15] <!-- F196 F197 F198 F207 Q28 -->

Coming to Vraja, Mahaprabhu, after Radha-kunda and the darshana of Harideva, wished to see Gopala, but He had vowed
not to climb Govardhana. Then Gopala, “as if out of fear of the Muslims,” came to Him Himself. A rumour of a Pathan
raid spread through Anor, and the Gaudiya pujaris at once carried Gopala off to Gathuli, to a spot above Gulala-kunda,
then dense forest, and served Him in secret; Mahaprabhu, who had come from Nilachala, received His darshana just at
that time. For three days Gopala stayed above the kunda, and on the fourth, seeing that no raid came, the pujaris
returned Him to the temple on Giriraja. He was then served by two Bengali brahmanas, disciples of Madhavendra (BR
5.812–813). Gopala displayed this lila — the move to Gathuli — later as well; Sanatana Gosvami too saw Him there.
<!-- F164 F165 -->

Having received the mercy of Gaurasundara and Gadadhara Pandita, Vallabhacharya completely restored Gopala's temple on
Govardhana and on the third lunar day of the bright fortnight of Vaishakha 1441 Shaka (1519), amid sankirtana,
installed Gopala in it; below, at the foot of the hill, he built himself a hut for bhajana. Vallabha's followers tell
of the new temple in more detail. According to the “Nij-varta,” on the night before the second lunar day of the bright
fortnight of Chaitra 1556 of the Vikrama era (1499) Govardhana-natha commanded in a dream a wealthy kshatriya Rajput,
Puranmal of Ambala, to build a great temple. Puranmal went in search of the Deity installed by Vajranabha and found
through a dream by Madhavendra, and found Him on Giriraja; He was then served by Ramadasa Chauhan, and Sadu Pande, the
headman of Anor, told him to wait for Vallabhacharya. Through a dream an architect from Agra, Hiramani, came as well;
the foundation was laid on a Sunday, the third lunar day of the bright fortnight of Vaishakha, under the nakshatra
Rohini, in 1556 Vikrama, and twenty years later, in 1576 Vikrama (1441 Shaka, 1519), Vallabha installed the Deity in
the new temple.[^16] Yadunatha's “Vallabha-digvijaya,” in the section on Vallabha's third journey, likewise says that
the merchant Puranmal, at the Lord's command, became the acharya's disciple, began digging the foundation on the
bright tritiya of the month of Madhava, and on the Vaishakha tritiya Govardhana-dhara was installed in the new temple.
<!-- F167 F177 F180 -->

### Krishnadasa and the Expulsion of the Bengalis
Vallabhacharya entrusted the management of the temple to his disciple Krishnadasa, a shudra from Gujarat, seeing his
practical ability. Vallabha himself at the end of his life passed through all the stages of sannyasa — kutichaka,
bahudaka, hamsa and paramahamsa — and, having taken sannyasa thirty-nine days before his departure, left the world in
1587 of the Vikrama era (1452 Shaka, 1530). <!-- F168 F169 -->

After his departure Vallabha's sons, Gopinatha and Vitthalanatha, confirmed Krishnadasa as manager of the temple and
returned to Adail. Krishnadasa appointed a Gujarati brahmana, Ramadasa Chauhan, as pujari. Ten years after Vallabha's
departure Gopinatha went to Puri; his son Purushottamji died young, and Vallabha's seat passed to Vitthalanatha.
<!-- F181 -->

Krishnadasa, however, wanted sole power over the temple. The Gaudiya pujaris, following the precept of their guru
Madhavendra, gave away all of Gopala's prasada and wealth, hoarding nothing, while Krishnadasa wanted to accumulate.
He became attached to a dancer, Gangabai, brought a dancing girl from Agra and tried to replace the Vaishnava kirtana
at Gopala's awakening with her singing and dancing. Krishnadasa went to Adail (Arail) to Vitthalanatha, Vallabha's
son, who was the patron of the Gaudiya pujaris, and turned his mind; it is said that Vitthalanatha believed him and
wrote to Raja Todarmal and Birbal asking them to help him. Then Krishnadasa gathered bad men in Mathura and, while the
pujaris were serving on Govardhana, set fire to their houses above Rudra-kunda — the whole village burned down; he
seized the temple and drove out and wounded the pujaris with sticks. He very nearly closed the temple even to
Vitthalanatha himself. <!-- F170 F171 F181 -->

The pujaris went to Vrindavana, to Rupa and Sanatana, who were then living in burning separation from Gaura, and then
to the royal court in Mathura; but Krishnadasa bribed the officials. Rupa and Sanatana, hearing that the service of
Gopala, the treasure of Madhavendra's heart, might pass to royal officials, handed the service and the temple over to
Vitthalanatha and duly provided for the Gaudiya pujaris. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.814–817) puts it thus: when the two
brahmanas, Madhavendra's disciples, were no more, a certain fortunate man served for a while, and then, after
consultation with Dasa Gosvami and others, the right of service was given to Vitthaleshvara — who after his father's
departure lived in seclusion in Mathura, was deeply moved by the lilas of Gaurachandra and always served Gopala with
zeal. The Gosvamis headed by Rupa and Raghunatha approved his service because he acknowledged the authority of Krishna
Chaitanya and His companions. <!-- F172 F173 F281 Q25 -->

The Vallabhite “Chaurasi Vaishnavan ki Varta” of Gokulanatha tells the same story differently. At first Shrinathji was
served by Bengalis, and Vallabhacharya appointed Krishnadasa as manager. Krishnadasa was incited to expel the Bengalis
by Avadhuta Dasa,[^17] to whom Shrinathji Himself supposedly complained that the Bengalis were troubling Him by
secretly offering food first to a little goddess hidden in a knot of hair. Vitthalanatha said that within a year the
Bengalis had carried off to their guru nearly a lakh of rupees collected in the east for ornaments, but after all it
was the Acharyaji himself who had appointed them. Krishnadasa obtained letters to Todarmal and Birbal, set fire to the
Bengalis' huts at Rudra-kunda and beat them with a stick. Rupa and Sanatana kept silent: “You know, and He knows.” The
judge of Mathura ordered that the Bengalis be left alone, but Krishnadasa refused, citing Vitthalanatha, and
Vitthalanatha told the Bengalis: “You abandoned the service when your houses were burning — the fault is yours.”
<!-- F174 -->

The same varta relates that Krishnadasa became infatuated with a courtesan in Agra and brought her to Nathdvara, and
at the Lord's awakening she sang instead of the kirtaniyas. Once Gangabai glanced at the offering, and the service
could not take place; Shrinathji told the servitor, “I have not eaten,” and Vitthalanatha himself cooked and offered
Him food. Krishnadasa forbade Vitthalanatha to climb up to the temple, and Birbal imprisoned Krishnadasa, but
Vitthalanatha refused to eat until he saw him, and he was released. Krishnadasa's end was not a happy one: given three
hundred rupees for a well, he buried a hundred under a tree, and while inspecting the finished well he slipped, fell
into it and died. “The tamasic go downwards,” said Ramadasa. This happened soon after 1567. <!-- F175 F176 -->

Televala, himself a follower of the Pushtimarga, writes that while Madhavendra lived there were no disputes: the
service was conducted by Bengali Vaishnavas under the supervision of Krishnadasa Adhikari. Gopinatha brought about a
lakh of rupees from Gujarat, and under Vitthaleshvara wealthy Gujaratis brought gifts; the Bengalis supposedly carried
these off secretly to their gosvamis in Vrindavana. A certain Anantadasa rose up against this, and Krishnadasa, with a
letter to Birbal, set fire to the Bengalis' huts. Akbar by a firman granted Vitthaleshvara the village of Jatipura;
the inner service was restricted to his kinsmen, and of the brahmanas only Girnaras and Sachoras were admitted. It was
then, Televala concludes, that Vitthaleshvara lost the traces of the “thought of Chaitanya” he had acquired at
Jagannatha Puri in 1616 of the Vikrama era. <!-- F192 -->

### Mathura and Satghara
In 1562 Akbar's soldiers attacked Adail. Vitthalanatha withdrew to Bandhogarh, then to Garha, where Queen Durgavati
honoured him, and in 1565 moved to Vraja, to Gokula, to land bought long before by Vallabha. After a dispute with the
zamindar of Mahavana he moved to Satghara in Mathura — north of Holi Gate (Harding Gate), on the way to Vishrama-ghata
through Tulasi-chabutara. “Satghara” means “the houses of the seven brothers”: Durgavati built them for the seven sons
of Vitthaleshvara — Giridhara, Govinda, Balakrishna, Gokulanatha, Raghunatha, Yadunatha and Ghanashyama. <!-- F182 -->

Leaving the house in the care of his eldest son, Giridhara, Vitthalanatha went to Govardhana to Gopala, was grieved by
Krishnadasa's outrages and forbade them. Giridhara, who was close to Birbal, with the help of the royal officials of
Mathura moved Gopala to his own house in Satghara: according to Televala, Shrinathji was brought there from Govardhana
on the seventh lunar day of the dark fortnight of Phalguna 1623 of the Vikrama era (early 1567) for nearly two months.
<!-- F183 F192 -->

Hearing that Gopala was in Mathura, Rupa Gosvami came with his companions from Vrindavana and stayed a whole month in
Mathura, in Vitthala's house at Satghara, beholding Gopala:[^18]

“Sri Rupa with his companions also spent a whole month in Mathura, beholding this Deity.” (CC, Madhya 18.48)

“He was accompanied by Gopala Bhatta Gosvami, Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami, Raghunatha Bhatta Gosvami and Lokanatha Dasa
Gosvami.” (CC, Madhya 18.49)

“Bhugarbha Gosvami, Sri Jiva Gosvami, Sri Yadava Acharya and Govinda Gosvami.” (CC, Madhya 18.50)

“Sri Uddhava Dasa, Madhava, Sri Gopala Dasa and Narayana Dasa.” (CC, Madhya 18.51)

“Also the great devotee Govinda, Vani Krishnadasa, Pundarikaksha, Ishana and Laghu Haridasa.” (CC, Madhya 18.52)

“After a month Sri Gopala went to His temple, and Sri Rupa Gosvami returned to Vrindavana.” (CC, Madhya 18.54)

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.1333) preserves the same. Rupa and his companions were so delighted with Vitthalanatha's
service that they lived with him for a month; Raghunatha Dasa praised this service of Gopala in the
“Gopala-raja-stotra” (13–14), and Rupa himself at Gathuli rejoiced at how Vitthala served the Deity of Krishna
Chaitanya (BR 5.804–805). Vitthaleshvara's house in Satghara, where Rupa saw Gopala, and the places of service of his
seven sons were later destroyed by men of another faith. <!-- F184 F185 F186 F187 Q19 Q26 Q27 -->

On the fourth lunar day of the bright fortnight of Vaishakha 1567 (974 of the Bengali era) Gopala, at Vitthalanatha's
direction, was returned to the temple on Govardhana. Vitthalanatha himself departed on the seventh lunar day of the
dark fortnight of Magha 1642 of the Vikrama era (1507 Shaka, 1585; 991 of the Bengali era). After him his seven sons
declared themselves the sevaits of Gopala and introduced a new order of worship, contrary to the views and customs
both of Madhavendra and of the Gosvamis headed by Rupa. <!-- F188 F189 -->

### The Road to Nathdvara
In 1668 Aurangzeb began to persecute the temples of Vishnu in Vraja, and Vallabha's descendants, with the help of the
Vrajavasis, secretly carried Gopala away in a bullock cart on the full-moon day of Ashvina. His road was long —
through Agra, Kota, Bundi, Jodhpur, Chapa, Seni and Krishnagarh to Sinhar in the dominions of Udaipur. There Rana
Sujan Singh built Him a temple and granted lands, and around the temple grew a town — Shrinatha-dvara, Nathdvara. In
1801, when Holkar of Indore attacked Nathdvara, Shrinathji was taken to Ghasiyar, and in 1807 brought back to
Shrinatha-dvara, where Gopala, the treasure of Madhavendra's heart and the Lord of the Gaudiyas, resides to this day.
<!-- F190 F191 -->

In brief, Gopala's wanderings were these:
- Govardhana: Madhavendra finds Gopala in the thicket and installs Him on the hill.
- 1498–1499: for fear of Sikandar Lodi's men — the forest of Taker-ghona, then Shyamadhak; in 1499 — back on Giriraja.
- In Mahaprabhu's day: three days at Gathuli, above Gulala-kunda.
- 1519: the new temple on Govardhana, consecrated by Vallabhacharya.
- 1567: about two months at Satghara in Mathura, where Rupa Gosvami sees Gopala; in Vaishakha, return to Govardhana.
- 1668: flight from Aurangzeb; Sinhar, the future Nathdvara.
- 1801–1807: Ghasiyar, then Nathdvara again.

In Agrahayana 1333 of the Bengali era (December 1926) Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, having visited Govindaji in
Jaipur, travelled via Chittorgarh and Mauli station (Nathdvara Road) to Nathdvara, to see Gopaladeva, the Lord of
Madhavendra's heart. In a letter to the journal “Gaudiya” (year 5, nos. 22–23) he related that the master of the
temple, Sri Govardhanlal Gosvami, was like a feudal prince with his own guard; that the worship was richer than any in
India — some fifteen hundred coins a day for offerings, some fifteen hundred cows, its own market and library; and
that the chief manager was still called “Krishnadasji” — the name had become the title of the office. Gosvami Maharaja
received the guests, spoke with them about Hari for half an hour, arranged darshana for them at the evening arati,
which he performed himself, and sent them maha-prasada. Shrinathji is Madhavendra's Deity, whom Mahaprabhu saw and
honoured, and the Gaudiyas come before Him with the deepest reverence. <!-- F193 I11 -->

### The Traditions of Vallabha's Followers
Vallabhacharya himself and his son Vitthalanatha always honoured Madhavendra. But later many stories entered the books
of their sampradaya which the Gaudiya tradition considers groundless, historically self-contradictory and born of
envy: in them both Madhavendra, the first architect of the rasa of loving devotion, and Gaurasundara Himself are
belittled. <!-- F199 -->

The “252 Vartas” (varta 251) relate that Madhavendra, a sannyasi of the Madhva sampradaya, lived at Arail, and
Vitthalanatha studied with him daily: he would leave his books behind, yet recite his lessons ten times better than
his teacher. In a dream Shrinathji revealed to Madhavendra that He had appeared in the form of Vitthalanatha to teach
service, and Madhavendra asked the youth for one thing as his teacher's fee — to make him his servant. Vitthala
refused — “You are my teacher” — but at the command of Navanitapriya placed him in the service of Shrinathji together
with the Bengali servitors. Madhavendra gave away all the gifts to the Bengali brahmanas; Shrinathji told him to bring
Malaya sandalwood, and when he had gone south, Krishnadasa Adhikari expelled the Bengalis. <!-- F200 -->

The Gaudiya authors answer this with the words of the Vallabhite books themselves: Vitthala was born in 1437 Shaka,
and when the Lord came to Arail he was not yet born or was an infant, while Madhavendra had long since withdrawn.
Madhavendra was the guru of the guru of the guru of Vitthala's father; to make him Vitthala's disciple is both a
spiritual offence and historical suicide. And by calling Vitthala Purna Purushottama, the Lord Himself, the authors
show themselves to be ahamgrahopasakas — those who worship a man as God. Moreover, the “Vallabha-digvijaya” itself
calls Madhavendra Vallabha's teacher in the Vaishnava scriptures; hence the story that Vitthala made his father's
teacher his own disciple is false both historically and spiritually: a teacher of knowledge, as the “Bhagavatam” and
the “Narada-pancharatra” teach, is to be honoured on a par with Vishnu. <!-- F201 F204 Q32 -->

According to the “Nij-varta” (episodes 21–22), immediately after 1576 Vikrama (1441 Shaka) Vallabha — since the
kinsmen of the headman Sadu Pande did not know how to serve — called the brahmanas of Sri-kunda, servants of Krishna
Chaitanya, and placed Madhavendra Puri at the head of the service: “a Telugu brahmana, an acharya of the Madhva
sampradaya, the guru of Krishna Chaitanya, with whom the Acharyaji studied the Vedas in Kashi”; Vitthala was then four
years old. Vallabha kept Madhavendra's disciples in the service, made Krishnadasa manager and gave the kirtana to
Kumbhandasa. The Bengalis served for fourteen years, and then, at Shrinathji's “command,” Avadhuta Dasa and
Krishnadasa expelled them for “theft,” and the service passed to Gujarati brahmanas. According to the
“Vallabha-digvijaya,” at the new temple “Madhava with his disciples” was appointed to the service.
<!-- F178 F179 F180 -->

The Gaudiya side regards this appointment as pure invention: the testimony of the “Charitamrita” deserves more
credence. Vallabha's followers wished to present Gopala as theirs “from the very beginning” and portrayed Vallabha's
aged teacher, a detached paramahamsa, as a hired priest. And if the wealthy kshatriya who built the temple was
Puranmal, then the Vallabhite dates are wrong; probably the first temple for Madhavendra was built by some other
wealthy kshatriya of Mathura, while Puranmal of Ambala, having later become Vallabha's disciple, erected a larger one.
<!-- F209 F210 -->

The “Chaurasi Baithak” (baithak 21) moves the place of Gopala's appearance to Bhandiravana and relates that the head
of the Madhva seat, Vyasatirtha, told Vallabha there: “Madhavendra Puri is my disciple, and his disciple is Krishna
Chaitanya,” and offered him his own seat; but at night Vallabha's servants beat Vyasatirtha, and he became Vallabha's
disciple. In other versions — the “Sampradaya-pradipa” of Gadadhara Dasa Dvivedi, the “Vallabha-digvijaya” (2), the
“Nij-varta” (4) — this is a debate at Vidyanagara before Krishnadeva Raya: Vyasatirtha wished to give Vallabha
sannyasa, but Bilvamangala (or Shrinathji) in a dream told him not to accept the Madhva path; in the “Nij-varta” there
is no beating. The Gaudiya authors see in all this a fabrication meant to belittle the teachers of the
Brahma-Madhva-Gaudiya sampradaya, and remark with sorrow that it is lamentable to print tales of violence as history:
of the fate of those who revile Vaishnavas the “Bhakti-sandarbha” speaks (265, citing the “Skanda Purana”). The tale
of the beating, they suggest, arose because the Tattvavadi Vyasatirtha had routed the doctrine of shuddhadvaita.
<!-- F202 F203 Q31 -->

The “Sampradaya-pradipa” calls Chaitanya “a sub-sampradaya of Vishnusvami”; the Gaudiyas reply that the Lord called an
interpretation contrary to Shridhara Svami “the opinion of a harlot,” and Vallabha accepted the correction. The
“Chaurasi Baithak” (10) asserts that Chaitanya prostrated Himself before Vallabha at Manasi-Ganga and by his mercy saw
her “milky form,” and baithak 4 that Gopala Bhatta, on Chaitanya's advice, received the appearance of Radha-ramana
from Vallabha and in his next birth became a Pushtimargi, “Gopala Naga.” But Radha-ramana appeared in 1599 of the
Vikrama era (so in the “Sri Radha-ramanera seva-prakata,” a book seen in the possession of Banamali Lal Gosvami
Vidyabhushana) — twelve years after Vallabha's departure (1587); and the Lord met Vallabha only at Arail and at
Nilachala. <!-- F205 F206 -->

The Gaudiya authors also reject the charge of greed against the Gosvamis: Rupa and Raghunatha gave up ministerial
posts, slept under the same tree for one night only and ate dry chickpeas; Rupa in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu”
accepted the path of pushti of the early Vallabha; the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (110) sees in Vallabha Bhatta Shuka,
and the Gaudiyas always honoured both Vallabha and Vitthala. The Pushtimarga, for its part, portrays Vitthalanatha as
“the guru of his own parama-guru”; according to historians, the cult of enjoyment in it began under Vitthala's fourth
son, Gokulanatha (F. S. Growse, “Mathura,” 1880). Because an offence against Madhavendra's feet had been committed in
the Pushtimarga from the time of its forefathers, the Gaudiyas say, the seed of it grew into the worldly sahajiya of
householders (cf. CC, Antya 8.97); neither Vallabha nor Vitthala encouraged anything of the kind.
<!-- F208 F211 F212 -->

### Govardhana Today
The places connected with Madhavendra survive at Govardhana to this day. On 18 October 1932 they were visited by a
disciple of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta. Govinda-kunda lies south of Anor (Annakuta-grama) at the foot of Govardhana, and on
its southern bank they show the spot where Madhavendra sat. The place where he cleared the thicket and found Gopala
also survives; at the entrance is a stone tablet: “Appearance of Sri Nathaji — samvat 1535, Vaishakha, dark fortnight,
ekadashi, nakshatra Dhanishtha,” and on the veranda an inscription in Hindi: “Built by order of Purushottamji Maharaja
(Vallabha sampradaya) by the Vaishnava Govinda Dasa Garsola, samvat 1721.” An outcrop of Giriraja can be seen there.
<!-- F84 -->

From below, pilgrims bow to the ruins of the temple of Gopalaji on Giriraja, associated with Madhavendra. West of
Anor, on the other side of Govardhana, lies the village of Gopalapur, where Gopala appeared; here the Vrajavasis, at
Krishna's word, worshipped Govardhana, and nearby on the stone of the hill can be seen the marks of a pot of curd and
of Krishna's lotus; not far from the place of appearance is Annakuta-sthana. Old Vrajavasis say that Yatipura was
formerly called Gopalapur, after Gopala, and was renamed after His devotee — Madhavendra Yati, who sought not a drop
of fame, yet whose fame surpassed the name of the Lord Himself. According to another explanation, Vallabhacharya
bought land at the foot of Govardhana and built houses there, and the place came to be called Yatipura because
Madhavendra, “the best of yatis,” had lived there. <!-- F85 F86 -->

From Giriraja one can see in the distance the ruins of the temple where Madhavendra's two disciples from Gauda served
Gopala. Later, at Gopala's command in a dream, the kshatriya merchant Puranmal built a temple beside it — from afar
the two look like a pair of shrines, the main one and a chamber for rest. Its spire, it is said, was destroyed by
Aurangzeb, and Dvarakesha Maharaja of the Vallabha sampradaya restored it about a hundred years ago. <!-- F87 -->

## Around Shrinathji and Govardhana
Over the centuries many traditions grew up around Madhavendra's Gopala, and by no means all of them are friendly to the
Gaudiyas. Above they were mentioned briefly; here the most telling of them are gathered — together with what the
Gaudiya biographies answer. The Vallabhite works are reported as the books themselves tell them, and the judgements as
the biographies of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas give them.[^22] <!-- F271 F272 F273 F274 F275 F276 F277 F278 Q37 -->

### Vallabha, Vyasatirtha and Vishnusvami
Even the meeting at Arail the Vallabhite books tell in their own way. According to the “Nij-varta” and the
“Sampradaya-pradipa,” Vallabha, recognising his guest as Krishna Chaitanya and seeing Krishna Himself in His body, told
his wife to feed Him rice not offered to the Deity, and to her doubt replied: “Krishna, who dwells in His heart, will
surely taste it”; after staying a few days, the Lord went on “to His own place,” Vrindavana. The Gaudiya biographies do
not quarrel over details: one way or another, then or later, Vallabha and his sons received the mercy of the Lord and
His associates. The trouble lies in the later books of the sampradaya: exalting Vallabha, they spared neither
Madhavendra, whom Vallabha and Vitthala always honoured, nor Gaurasundara Himself — and they agree poorly with one
another besides. <!-- F213 F214 F215 -->

Nowhere is this plainer than in the stories about Vyasatirtha. According to the “Chaurasi Baithak,” when Vallabha was
reading the “Bhagavatam” for seven days at Bhandira-vana, the head of the Madhva seat came of his own accord to offer
him his place, a hundred thousand servants and a lakh of rupees, boasting that Madhavendra was his disciple and Krishna
Chaitanya Madhavendra's; Vallabha promised to answer the next day, and at midnight four of his servants with cudgels
beat the old man, who in the morning himself asked to become Vallabha's disciple. In the “Sampradaya-pradipa” (it is
ascribed to Gadadhara Dasa Dvivedi, “called a disciple of Vitthalanatha”) everything takes place at Vidyanagara:
Vallabha defeats a Mayavadi before whom the Tattvavadis could not stand for six days, King Krishnaraya bathes him from a
golden vessel (this kanakabhisheka is described in detail in the “Nij-varta” and the Gujarati “Kanakabhisheka ka
Itihasa”), and Vyasatirtha wants to give him sannyasa and make him acharya of his sampradaya. But in a dream Bilvamangala
appears to Vallabha, the last of seven hundred acharyas of Vishnusvami: for seven hundred years — ever since Rudra, by the
Lord's will, descended as Shankaracharya and turned people away from bhakti — he had waited here to pass the teaching on
to Vallabha, the incarnation of the fire of the Lord's mouth, who together with his sons was to save lakhs of souls by
the path of bhakti. In the “Vallabha-digvijaya” this command is given by Bhagavan Himself; in the “Nij-varta”
Vyasatirtha only urges Vallabha at night, there is not a word about any beating, and Bilvamangala brings a letter from
Vishnusvami himself, after which Govardhananathji appears: “Never accept the sampradaya of Madhvacharya,” — and Vallabha,
with certificates of victory, leaves Vidyanagara for Vraja. <!-- F216 F217 F218 F219 F220 F221 F222 F223 -->

The Gaudiya biographies see in all this an invention born of envy: printing robbers' tales as history, the writers make
a venerable acharya the patron of thugs, and the author of the “Nyayamrita,” which like the Sudarshana in Vishnu's hand
shattered the arguments of the Mayavadis, their victim. Were these thugs perhaps hired by the Mayavadis? And the books
contradict themselves: as a child Vallabha studied the Vedas under Madhavendra, the disciple of Vyasatirtha's disciple,
while Vitthala supposedly made his own teacher his disciple — whereas the “Bhagavatam” (11.10.12) likens teacher and
disciple to the two pieces of wood between which the fire of knowledge is born, and the “Narada-pancharatra” bids one
honour the guru who gives knowledge as Vishnu. Vallabha's meeting with Bilvamangala, who lived centuries before him, the
devotees do not dispute: great souls are not bound by time; but making earlier saints the disciples of later ones, or
having them “beaten by the hands of robbers” — this those who know the truth cannot accept. Nor does the
“Vyasayoga-charitam,” cited by Televala, confirm the story: there Krishnadeva Raya honours Vallabha “under the
presidency” of Vyasatirtha, which speaks rather of honour paid to Vyasatirtha himself. Nor does it fit the dispute over
the “Nyayamrita”: Madhusudana Sarasvati objected to it in the “Advaita-siddhi,” Ramacharya Tirtha answered him in the
“Tarangini,” Brahmananda Giri tried to refute the answer, and Vanamala Mishra demolished him — and yet according to the
“Nij-varta” Vallabha at Prayaga listens with pleasure to that very Madhusudana. Could the victor over Vyasatirtha's
opponent have delighted in the conclusions of a kevala-advaitin? Hence some say plainly: Vyasatirtha routed Vallabha's
shuddhadvaita, and for that the story of his beating was invented. <!-- F224 F225 F226 F227 F228 F229 F230 F231 F232 Q35 Q36 I16 -->

Hence, too, the dispute about sampradaya. The Vallabhite books regard Vallabha as a late member of the sampradaya of the
third of three Vishnusvamis (the first they name Devatanu, the second Rajagopala, the teacher of Bilvamangala's teacher,
the third Andhra-Vishnusvami; the teaching of Vishnusvami's “Sarva-sukta” is known outside their books as well — to
Sayana-Madhava and to Shridhara Svami), and Chaitanya as a “sub-sampradaya of Vishnusvami,” since He honoured Shridhara
Svami as jagad-guru. The Gaudiyas recall what happened at Nilachala: when Vallabha wished to read to the Lord his
commentary written against Shridhara Svami, Gaurasundara called an opinion opposed to the Svami “the opinion of a
harlot,” and Vallabha, bowing his head, accepted the correction. To derive a “sub-sampradaya” from this is, in the
logicians' phrase, an argument with the fault of ativyapti: far too broad. <!-- F233 F234 F235 F236 -->

### Other Traditions and the Gaudiya Answer
Of the same kind are the stories of the “Chaurasi Baithak”: how the Lord, who lived six months at Manasi-Ganga under a
vow not to speak until He had completed a hundred and twenty-five thousand names in the day, supposedly prostrated
Himself before Vallabha and by his mercy, together with the Vaishnavas, saw the milky form of Manasi-Ganga — or how
Vallabha manifested Radha-ramana from Gopala Bhatta's shalagrama, yet declined to become his guru: “In another birth
you will enter the Pushtimarga,” — and in his next life Gopala Bhatta became “Gopala Naga.” Was it not later
storytellers, the biographies ask, who made up these absurdities to amuse women? For Vitthalanatha together with Rupa
arranged the honouring of Gopala Bhatta at Mathura, and Vallabha himself drank the water that had washed Mahaprabhu's
feet; such would-be disciples only wounded his heart. Nor do these stories stand the test of dates: according to an old
book on the service of Radha-ramana, He appeared only after the departure of Vallabha, whose year the Vallabhite books
themselves give; and the Lord and Vallabha met, by all the books, only at Arail and at Nilachala — when the Lord was
wandering through Vraja, Vallabha was at Charanat, and when He returned from there, at Arail. <!-- F237 F238 F239 F240 F241 F242 Q38 -->

To the charges of self-interest that the Pushtimargi lawyer Televala levels at the Gaudiya Gosvamis, the biographies
answer with a saying: the lustful man sees the whole world full of objects of lust. Rupa and Raghunatha gave up
ministerial posts to live only for Krishna's pleasure; whereas stories contrary to history, logic and plain morality were
composed in illicit rivalry with Rupa's followers, to strengthen a householder sampradaya with its tamasic bhakti, proud
of wealth and numbers of followers. And if Televala is offended by “the opinion of a harlot,” it is only because such a
word troubles one who lacks loyalty to the guru and the succession; Vallabha himself accepted it with bowed head. And
Vallabha himself the Gaudiyas always honoured: just as Sarvabhauma, once a teacher of Mayavada, became an associate of
Gaura, so Vallabha, who had once displayed a scholar's reasoning, was honoured by Gaura's associates as an acharya, and
Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (110) called him Shuka, the son of Dvaipayana. To seek no honour for
oneself but to honour others is the eternal nature of Chaitanya's followers. <!-- F252 F253 F254 F255 F256 Q39 I15 -->

Televala himself, however, admits what Vitthala owed to the Gaudiyas. Living at Puri, Vitthaleshvara came close to
Chaitanya's followers, and his stotras to Radha — the “Svamini-ashtaka,” the “Svamini-stotra,” such as Vallabha himself
has none of — like his commentary on the “Krishna-premamrita” and the “Shringara-rasa-mandana,” arose perhaps under
their influence. He admits also the price of the shrine's passing: when Giridhara moved it to his own house in Mathura
(in samvat 1623) and Vitthaleshvara elaborated the service, it became the exclusive possession of the Vallabhites, the
Bengali Vaishnavas forgot their early connection with it, and the mass of Vaishnavas lost access to it — though the aim,
according to Televala, was only to remove the Bengalis, and hardly did Vitthaleshvara or “Vallabhacharya”[^19] wish for
such an outcome. <!-- F243 F244 F245 F250 F251 -->

### At Govinda-kunda and After
How the Gaudiyas fared at Govardhana later on can be seen from the stories that a disciple of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta heard
at Govinda-kunda on 18 October 1932. Manohara Dasa Babaji there called the bhajana of the holy name “bhakti turned
outward,” and hearing of the lilas “bhakti turned inward.”[^20] His disciple Ananta Dasa related that the Vallabhites,
who held sway at the kunda, mocked the Gaudiyas, nicknaming them “an-magi,” until Manohara Dasa began to teach boys
grammar and the “Bhagavatam” so that they might live by expounding it — then the gosains yielded the Gaudiyas a little
room; and when Ramasvarupa Mishra, priest of the gosain Ranchhorlalji, took initiation from him and the Vallabhites were
enraged, Manohara Dasa, so that his disciple should not lose his only livelihood, told him to go on adhering to them.
Another babaji, Ramakrishna Dasa, who before an assembly of Vallabhites had called Vallabha a disciple of Gadadhara
Pandita, was nearly beaten, and later, after a festival at his ashrama, night visitors of robber-like appearance,
demanding money, beat him until he bled. <!-- F246 F247 F248 F249 -->

What the gosains' customs degenerated into the biographies show in the words of the English historian F. S. Growse
(“Mathura,” 1880): the cult of enjoyment in the Pushtimarga began under Gokulanatha, Vitthala's fourth son, kinship with
whom is claimed by the chief gosain at Bombay as well. According to Growse, the gosains are family men and worldly, “the
Epicureans of the East”; Gokulanatha taught the complete identity of the gosain with the Deity, which is conferred by
birth and lost through no vice whatever, and the initiate surrenders to the gosain not only the first fruits of his
substance but the virginity of his newly wedded wife; these practices were exposed by a libel case in the Supreme Court
of Bombay in 1862, and the glorification of immorality is the whole point of many stories of the “Chaurasi varta.” All
this is Growse's own; the biographies quote it without additions. <!-- F257 F258 F259 F260 F261 F262 -->

From here the biographies draw a lesson for the Gaudiyas themselves. In Vallabha's “Sannyasa-lakshana” a sannyasa imbued
with bhava is accepted, but his present-day followers live as householders, whereas among Mahaprabhu's devotees
renunciation is the chief thing; and those Gaudiyas infected with worldly sahajiya ideas reap the poisonous fruits of the
same way of thinking. Mahaprabhu, being a sannyasi, cherished the mood of the gopis who prayed that the Lord might ever
appear in the minds of them who live at home (“Bhagavatam,” 10.82.48) — yet the worldly sahajiyas decided that a
renunciant's dress is incompatible with the vision of the Lord. The sakhi-bhekis, misunderstanding the words “be firm
within, and outwardly behave as people do,” fell away from renunciation and took to acting out humility “lower than the
grass”; but imitation without inner service is hypocrisy, not bhajana, and this difference is taught by Raya Ramananda's
conversation with Mahaprabhu and Sanatana's questions about sadhana and sadhya. In the same way they distort the
instruction of the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” (1.2.295) on serving both in the form of a sadhaka and in the siddha form,
following the residents of Vraja, excusing themselves on the ground that the corrupted society of Gaudiyas thinks so —
and this betrays only inexperience in bhajana: remembrance of the eight periods of the day not awakened by kirtana is
artificial and is not the path of the Gosvamis.[^23] <!-- F263 F264 F265 F266 F267 F268 F269 F270 Q33 Q34 I13 I14 -->

## Significance
Madhavendra Puri opened in the line of Madhva what had not been in it before — love for Krishna in separation; and
ever since, wherever such love is found, it is sure to have a connection with him (so, according to the
“Charitamrita,” the Sanodiya brahmana understood on seeing the Lord; CC, Madhya 17.172–173). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
teaches that transcendental separation is the one practice that leads the soul to perfection. Worldly longing betrays
attachment to matter, whereas longing for Krishna is the best evidence of a desire to please Him. The example of
Madhavendra, the root of this movement, is a model for everyone who wishes to serve, and Mahaprabhu and His intimates
made it their standard. <!-- F29 I2 -->
