# Madhavendra Puri
*Мадхавендра Пури · @madhavendra-puri*

## The First Sprout of the Tree of Devotion
Madhavendra Puri lived in the fourteenth century, long before Sri Chaitanya appeared, and yet in His lila he stands at the very source. Before coming into the world, the Lord sends ahead those whom He will honour as parents, teachers and elders, so that each of them may serve Him in his own way:

“Before descending to the Earth, Krishna sends here many of His devotees, who accept the roles of His venerable forebears, teachers and elders.” (CC, Adi 3.92)

“Madhavendra Puri, Ishvara Puri, Srimati Shachimata and Jagannatha Mishra appeared together with Advaita Acharya.” (CC, Adi 3.94)

Elsewhere the “Charitamrita” (Adi 13.52–55) names this circle of elders in greater detail: “Madhava Puri,” Shachi, Jagannatha Mishra, Keshava Bharati, Ishvara Puri, Advaita, Srivasa, Acharyaratna, Pundarika Vidyanidhi and Haridasa. Thus Gaurasundara, in whom Radha and Madhava are united, revealed on earth His elders in the chain of teachers even before His own appearance in Sridham Mayapur-Navadvipa. Lochana Dasa adds in the “Chaitanya-mangala” that together with the Lord, with Nityananda — Balarama — and Advaita — Shiva — and His other eternal companions, both Ishvara Puri and Madhavendra Puri descended. <!-- F1 F2 F3 F4 Q4 -->

Vrindavana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 9.155–160) speaks of him in a way that leaves little to add: his body is woven of divine love, and all who are near him are full of it; he knows no food but the taste of Krishna; Krishna Himself plays within his body; and if one more proof of his love is needed, it is enough to say that his disciple was Advaita Acharya. Gaurachandra would say again and again, “Madhavendra is the first architect (adi-sutradhara) of the rasa of devotion,” and He called him the original fountainhead of jubilant love for Krishna. <!-- F23 F24 Q1 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja, describing Sri Chaitanya's tree of devotion, begins with him:

“All glory to Sri Madhavendra Puri, the treasury of unalloyed loving service to Krishna! In him this wish-fulfilling tree of bhakti first sprouted.” (CC, Adi 9.10)

“Then this seed of the wish-fulfilling tree of bhakti took the form of a new shoot — Sri Ishvara Puri. And soon the gardener Himself, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, became the main trunk of this tree of bhakti.” (CC, Adi 9.11)

The gardener and the trunk of this tree is Mahaprabhu Himself; it was planted in Navadvipa and grew up in Puri and Vrindavana. Madhavendra is its first sprout, the one in which the seed of devotion first bore fruit; Ishvara Puri is that same sprout grown strong and tall; higher up the trunk divides into two — Advaita and Nityananda (CC, Adi 9.21) — while below it is held by nine roots, the sannyasis of whom we shall speak further on (CC, Adi 9.13–15). <!-- F25 F26 Q2 Q3 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.2272–2274) calls Madhavendra loving devotion embodied: the mere remembrance of his name brings every perfection, and Ishvara Puri, Ranga Puri and his other disciples are intoxicated with the taste of bhakti; and he had many disciples firm in such devotion throughout Bengal and Orissa. The “Charitamrita” extols him as the spiritual teacher of the whole world, the giver of divine love (CC, Antya 8.31). The Gaudiya tradition sees in him an intimate companion of Sri Vrishabhanu-nandini — Radha, the original abode of love — and of Sri Radha-Giridhari; even eternally perfect devotees worship his feet. He is the parama-guru of Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita, playing the part of a revered preceptor, and both Vallabhacharya and his son Vitthalanatha always worshipped him. <!-- F27 F28 F30 Q10 -->

He bore several names. Most often he is called Madhavendra Puri or Madhava Puri; his disciples and intimates said “Puri Gosani,” “Puri Gosvami,” “Shripada,” “Puripada”; in the writings of Vallabha's followers he is Madhavendra Yati or Madhava Yatindra, and the “Vallabha-digvijaya” calls him simply “the brahmana Madhava.” <!-- F5 -->

## Heir to the Line of Madhva
In the age of Kali the world is purified by four Vaishnava lines of succession — the sampradayas of Brahma, Sri, Rudra and the Sanakas. Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” that the Vaishnavas who teach pure devotion on the basis of the Vedanta are divided into these four branches. Madhavendra belonged to the Brahma, or Madhva, sampradaya: he accepted the line of Sri Madhvacharya and Vaishnava sannyasa and became a guru within it. <!-- F6 F7 -->

The succession of Madhva is recorded in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” in the “Prameya-ratnavali” and by Gopala Guru Gosvami; almost the same verses, with small variant readings, are given in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.2149–2162). According to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (22), the chain runs thus: the Lord of the spiritual world — Brahma — Narada — Vyasa — Shuka; from Vyasa Madhvacharya received the mantra of Krishna, and after him came Padmanabha, Narahari, Madhava-dvija, Akshobhya, Jayatirtha, Jnanasindhu, Mahanidhi, Vidyanidhi, Rajendra and Jayadharma; from Jayadharma's circle also came Vishnu Puri, the author of the “Bhakti-ratnavali.” Then the line passes through Purushottama[^1] to Vyasatirtha, the author of the “Vishnu-samhita,” from him to Lakshmipati Tirtha, and from Lakshmipati to Madhavendra Puri. <!-- F8 F9 F10 -->

Vyasatirtha was an ascetic of the Vyasaraya Matha and stood fourteenth in descent from Madhva in the Madhva-Gaudiya chain. According to one opinion, his disciple was Lakshmipati Tirtha, and Madhavendra was the follower of the latter; Madhavendra, then, was a disciple of Vyasatirtha's disciple. Vyasatirtha left the “Nyayamrita,” the “Tatparya-chandrika,” the “Tarka-tandava,” the “Bheda-ujjivana,” the three “Khandana-traya-mandara-manjaris” and the “Tattva-viveka-mandara-manjari”; even the foremost Mayavadis acknowledged the “Nyayamrita.” An old Bengali verse says the same: Vyasatirtha was Madhva's servant, Lakshmipati was Vyasa's servant, from him came Madhavendra Puri, and among the disciples of the great Madhavendra were Ishvara, Nityananda and Advaita; and a Sanskrit stanza honours his disciples — Ishvara, Advaita and Nityananda, the gurus of the world — and Sri Chaitanya, the disciple of Ishvara. <!-- F14 F15 -->

The “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” adds that it was Madhavendra who set this dharma in motion — and his path lives on to this day. There he is also called an incarnation of the wish-fulfilling tree that grows in Vraja, bearing four fruits: servitude (dasya), friendship (sakhya), parental love (vatsalya) and conjugal love (ujjvala). His disciple Ishvara Puri accepted the fruit of shringara, Advaita two fruits, dasya and sakhya, and Sri Ranga Puri took shelter of vatsalya (GGD 23–24).[^2] <!-- F11 F12 F13 Q5 -->

Of his homeland only Vallabha's followers give any account. Yadunatha's “Vallabha-digvijaya” (section 1, 1658 of the Vikrama era) calls him “Madhavendra Yati, an Andhra, a follower of the Madhva sampradaya, an incarnation of the rishi Shandilya”; the “Nij-varta” calls him a Telugu brahmana, an acharya of the Madhva sampradaya and the guru of Krishna Chaitanya; the “252 Vartas” — a sannyasi of the Madhva sampradaya who lived at Arail. <!-- F22 -->

What was new in this ancient line was what Madhavendra himself brought into it. Bhaktivinoda Thakura writes that the disciple of his disciple was Sri Chaitanya, and that before him there is no evidence of loving devotion (prema-bhakti) in the line of Madhva; from Madhvacharya down to Lakshmipati, Madhavendra's guru, it knew no devotion filled with the taste of conjugal love — what the spirit of Madhva's followers then was can be seen from Mahaprabhu's conversation with the Tattvavadis in the south. Madhavendra, by composing the verse “ayi dīna-dayārdra nātha,” sowed the seed of such devotion, and in that verse lies the germ of Mahaprabhu's entire teaching. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati calls him the first sprout of the wish-fulfilling tree of divine love to rise from the line of Madhva: before him there was not a trace of the conjugal mood in it. <!-- F16 F17 F18 -->

Before Madhavendra, down to Lakshmipati Tirtha, this line worshipped Krishna alone; from Madhavendra there descended into it love for Krishna in separation (vipralambha), rooted in wholehearted service to Sri Radha. Only those in whom a taste for it has awakened, and who are bound to him by dear ties, are eligible for it. The great souls say: Madhavendra is the root of the tree of shringara-rasa, Ishvara Puri its shoot, Mahaprabhu the main trunk, and His followers the branches and twigs. And in our own time Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha, in a speech at Udupi, recalled that the root of the Gaudiya sampradaya is the succession of Madhva, and that Madhavendra, Mahaprabhu's parama-guru and the first artist of loving devotion, belonged to it, while Mahaprabhu is the chief preacher of that devotion in the mood of separation. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta visited Udupi more than once, collected Madhva literature and invited a swami from there to teach Madhva philosophy at the head matha in Mayapur. <!-- F19 F20 F21 -->

## Intoxicated with Love
Even before the Lord's appearance Madhavendra lived in a state that the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 4.402–412) describes with wonder. Trembling, tears, hair standing on end, roaring and thunderous cries, loud laughter, stupor and perspiration never left him. Absorbed in Govinda, he was unaware of the outer world: walking along a road he would break into dance of his own accord, calling out to Hari; at times he lay unconscious for two or three praharas; in separation he wept in torrents like the Ganga, and in a moment of bliss he would forget even his clothing. Day and night, intoxicated with love for Krishna, he would laugh, weep and cry “Hai, hai!” like a madman (CB, Adi 9). At the mere sight of a cloud — it reminded him of Krishna — he would fall senseless. <!-- F31 F32 F33 -->

He lived by the vow of ayachaka-vritti: he never asked for food and ate only what was given unasked; if nothing was given, he fasted. He felt neither hunger nor thirst, for the nectar of love sustained him, and he remained detached and indifferent to everything worldly (CC, Madhya 4.123–124, 4.180). <!-- F34 -->

## Grief for the World and the Meeting with Advaita
Happy in Krishna, Madhavendra suffered deeply to see people without devotion. Before Sri Chaitanya's appearance the world was bereft of love for Vishnu and sunk in passion and ignorance: people revelled in songs about Yogipala, Bhogipala and Mahipala, even sannyasis called themselves “Narayana,” and the “men of knowledge, yogis and ascetics” only argued and did not accept the form of Krishna (CB, Adi 4.416–436). He had no one to talk to, and he stopped speaking with sannyasis. “Better to go and live in the forest,” he decided; “there at least there is no talk with non-Vaishnavas.” <!-- F32 F35 F36 -->

Day after day he thought of the good of the people, and his mind was turned to one thing: the descent of Krishna. Vrindavana Dasa shows how Madhavendra, seeing the wretched state of the world, longed ardently for Krishna to come, and on meeting Advaita passed on to him the whole force of that longing. <!-- F37 I10 Q6 -->

So, grieving for the world, he came to Shantipur, to the house of Advaita Acharya. Advaita, who himself grieved for the people, was then teaching the “Gita” and the “Bhagavatam,” revealing their meaning as devotion. Seeing in his guest the signs of a Vaishnava, he at once fell at his feet; Madhavendra embraced him and bathed him in tears. Absorbed in talk of Krishna, both forgot their bodies: at the sight of a cloud Madhavendra fell senseless, at the name of Krishna he roared. Advaita rejoiced to see such devotion to Vishnu and accepted instruction from him (CB, Adi 4.419–440). Madhavendra, delighted by Advaita's love for Krishna, gave him initiation into the mantra of Krishna, instruction and all that goes with them.[^3] Advaita is himself a manifestation of the Supreme Lord; and yet, convinced by his guest's ecstatic love that here was his guru, he underwent the rite of initiation to show conditioned souls that no one can do without a guru: he played the part of Madhavendra's disciple.[^4] <!-- F38 F39 F40 F41 F42 Q13 -->

The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Antya 4.398) says that Madhavendra and Advaita are not different from each other, and yet Advaita is his disciple. That is why the Lord honoured Advaita as a guru, while Advaita himself saw in Chaitanya the Lord and regarded himself as His servant — His principal limb (CC, Adi 6.36–41). <!-- F43 F44 Q7 Q22 -->

At about the same time Madhavendra, with his disciple Sri Ranga Puri, a resident of Pandharpur, also visited Sridham Mayapur, the house of Jagannatha Mishra. Many years later Ranga Puri told the Lord in Pandharpur:

“They had taken their meal then in the house of Jagannatha Mishra, and he still remembered the indescribable taste of the banana-flower curry.” (CC, Madhya 9.296)

“Jagannatha Mishra's wife had stayed in his memory as a deeply devoted and chaste woman. She showed them such care, as though she were the mother of the whole universe.” (CC, Madhya 9.297)

Shachi fed the sannyasis as if they were her own sons. She and Jagannatha had a worthy son who took sannyasa under the name Shankararanya and attained perfection in Pandharpur. <!-- F45 F46 -->

## The Meeting with Nityananda
Wandering as he pleased, together with disciples who relished the taste of love for Krishna, Madhavendra once met Nityananda by chance as the latter was touring the holy places of western India.[^5] On seeing each other, both fell senseless and grew stunned with love; the disciples, led by Ishvara Puri, wept. Coming to themselves, the two embraced and rolled on the ground, roaring with love for Krishna; rivers of tears flowed from their eyes, and the earth thought itself blessed; trembling, tears, hair on end — “Chaitanya was playing in both their bodies,” says the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 9.158–168). <!-- F47 F48 F49 -->

“However many holy places I have visited,” said Nityananda, “only today have I gained their true fruit, in seeing your feet. Nowhere have I seen such love.” And Madhavendra embraced him, took him on his lap and could not utter a word for tears — he only held him to his breast. <!-- F50 F51 -->

Madhavendra's disciples — Ishvara Puri, Brahmananda Puri and others — at once came to love Nityananda. They had met many “sadhus” before, but found love for Krishna in none of them: all were seeking something else — the fruits of karma, knowledge, yoga, vows and austerities — and the disciples had wandered through the forests in sorrow. Now their grief vanished. Ishvara Puri, seeing how dear Nityananda was to his guru, was himself, like the others, filled with great love for him. <!-- F52 F53 -->

For several days Nityananda wandered with Madhavendra, and through the forests they seemed to be enacting a search for Krishna. Neither knew day from night, the disciples sang the name of Hari unceasingly, and what the two spoke of only Krishna knows. “Nowhere have I seen such love,” Madhavendra would say. “Where it is, there are all my holy places. Krishna is merciful to me, since He has given me such a friend. Where there is Nityananda's company, there are all the tirthas and Vaikuntha itself. Whoever hears of such a devotee will surely attain Krishna; and whoever bears Nityananda even the slightest ill will is not dear to Krishna, even if he is a devotee.” So day and night he spoke of Nityananda and served him with love (CB, Adi 9.169–185). Madhavendra treated him as a friend and an equal, while Nityananda always honoured him only as his guru — for he, the teacher of all the world's teachers, was teaching people thus (CB, Adi 9.187–188; BR 5.2330–2334). <!-- F54 F55 F56 Q9 -->

At last they parted: Nityananda went to Setubandha, Madhavendra to see the Sarayu. Both were so absorbed in Krishna that they did not remember their bodies — that is why they survived the separation; had they kept their outer consciousness, they could not have borne it (CB, Adi 9.189–192). In this mystery — of a life that does not break off even in unbearable separation — lies the foundation of Gaudiya Vaishnava dharma. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that life is preserved because service gives an unbroken inner bliss; such love, in the words of the “Charitamrita,” is not to be found in the world of men (CC, Madhya 2.43). And whoever hears with faith the story of Nityananda and Madhavendra obtains the treasure of love for Krishna. <!-- F57 F58 I8 Q30 -->

Whose disciple Nityananda himself was, the traditions tell differently: some consider him a disciple of Madhavendra, others of Lakshmipati Tirtha, and so Madhavendra's godbrother; the “Prema-vilasa” names Ishvara Puri as his guru.[^6] Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, listing Madhavendra's principal disciples, names Ishvara Puri, Nityananda and Advaita. <!-- F59 -->

[^2]: In one of the texts of the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” cited by the biographies, the line about the tree with four fruits is absent, and in place of the line about Ranga Puri there is another: Gaura accepted Ishvara Puri as His guru and flooded the worlds with love.

According to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 4), it took place at their first meeting, when Madhavendra was wandering in grief for the world, before the Lord's appearance. According to the “Charitamrita” (Madhya 4.110–111), Madhavendra initiated Advaita later, on his way from Vraja to fetch sandalwood for Gopala, after two years of serving Him. One of the biographies combines the two accounts: it was at the time when Advaita was teaching the “Gita” and the “Bhagavatam” that Madhavendra dreamt of Gopala's command, and on the way he initiated Advaita.

According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” the meeting took place at a holy place on the western coast of India; one of the biographies places it in South India, and another says that Nityananda toured the holy places of both North and South and in these travels met Madhavendra, Ishvara Puri and Brahmananda Puri.
[^6]: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta remarks in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” (on CB, Adi 9.187–188) that the question of godbrotherhood is inseparable from the question of the guru and is in essence the same.
