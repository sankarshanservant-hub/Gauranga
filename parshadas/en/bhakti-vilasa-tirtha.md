# Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha Gosvami Maharaja
*Шрила Бхакти Виласа Тиртха Госвами Махарадж · @bhakti-vilasa-tirtha*

## Kunja Babu
Among the disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, one of the first and closest to him was Kunjavihari
Vidyabhushana — the one whom the guru himself called the chief pillar of his mission. His father named him Umapati; in
the world he bore the name Kunjavihari (Kunjabihari), and in the Gaudiya Math he was simply Kunja Babu. He was honoured
with the titles “Acharyatrika” and “Paravidyabhushana Bhagavata-ratna”; in the chronicles of the mission he is
“Maha-mahopadeshaka,” the great instructor; and those close to him called him “Prabhupada-preshtha,” dear to
Prabhupada, as the disciples called their guru. For many years he was the secretary and manager of the Gaudiya Math. In
1948 he accepted sannyasa with the name Tridandi Swami Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha Gosvami Maharaja, and to the end of his days
he remained the acharya of the Sri Chaitanya Math; his disciples called him Srila Tirtha Maharaj, Acharyadeva and
Acharyapada. The calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math marks his days as those of “Tridandi Swami Srimad Bhakti
Vilas Tirtha Maharaj,” and “Our Teachers” names him among those great disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta who themselves
became teachers. <!-- F1 F119 -->

## Appearance and Childhood
He appeared in the village of Chanchri-Purulia (Chanchuri-Purulia), Kalia police station, Narail subdivision, Jessore
district, in present-day Bangladesh. The day of his appearance is gaura-saptami, the seventh day of the bright fortnight
of the month of Vishnu (Chaitra): so the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math observes it. The year, by our
reckoning, is 1895.[^1] <!-- F2 F3 -->

His father, Sri Raicharan Dasa, was a Vaishnava, and the household worshipped the Deities Sri Gaura-Nitai and Sri
Giridhari. Before Them, on an auspicious day at the start of his fifth year, the boy was seated to learn his first
letters. There were two sons in the family; the younger was named Sri Ganapati. Raised by Vaishnava parents, from
childhood he knew no food but vegetarian, kept the Vaishnava fasts and holy days, chanted the holy Name morning and
evening, and watered the tulasi. <!-- F4 F5 F6 F9 -->

## The Search for a Guru
In 1911 he passed his entrance examination and came to Calcutta to study at college. But a few days later his father
left this world, and college had to be given up. The young man took on the care of his aged mother, of the house and of
his younger brother's studies, and became an assistant in an office of the postal department. <!-- F7 F8 -->

His soul was searching for a true guru. He went to Pahari Baba, to the “Ramakrishna Mission” and elsewhere, but a real
devotee of the Lord he did not find. On the advice of a close relative he came to Navadvipa and stayed in the house of a
certain gosai, to whom an old acquaintance, Sri Sakhicharan Ray, had introduced him. Seeing that meat was being cooked
there, he left at once. <!-- F10 F11 -->

In 1914, at Kuliya, he was blessed with the darshan of the feet of Om Vishnupada Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji
Maharaja. Babaji Maharaja had only one disciple — Sri Varshabhanavi-dayita Dasa, the future Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati — and to see him the young man came to Mayapur on the next phalguni-purnima, in 1915. There, at
Vraja-pattana, he first met his future guru. He was explaining to his listeners the lines of the “Charitamrita”:

“Wandering through the universe, some fortunate jiva, by the mercy of guru and Krishna, obtains the seed of the creeper
of devotion (*bhakti-lata*).” (CC, Madhya 19.151)

What he heard struck the young man. For the first time he saw with his own eyes, among Vaishnavas, a knower of the
scriptures who was guileless and pure in character, and he was drawn to this faith; he wanted to know more.[^2]
<!-- F12 F13 F14 Q1 F122 -->

## Initiation
In that same year, 1915, on the day of Utthana Ekadashi, Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji entered the eternal lila at
Koladvipa. Kunjavihari came to bid him farewell and saw Srila Bhaktisiddhanta for the second time.[^3] Two days later, at
Vraja-pattana, he listened for two hours to *hari-katha* from his lips — and was so attracted that he took two months'
leave from his work to hear him in seclusion. <!-- F15 F16 -->

The guru's word was not an easy one. Explaining what true devotees, bhagavatas, are like, he spoke also of those who only
call themselves devotees of Krishna — and it turned out that people whom the young man had revered as great souls did
not stand even on the first rung of devotion, that of the *kanishtha-adhikari*. At first this displeased him, and he
decided to leave. But Sri Paramananda Vidyaratna asked him to stay a few more days, and when he had heard the guru out,
Kunjavihari felt such reverence for Srila Bhaktisiddhanta that he asked him for initiation. <!-- F17 F18 -->

He received diksha in the first week of January 1916, and the guru's mercy entered his heart with special power. Then,
full of joy, he said to the guru: “How many will be able to come here and be fortunate enough to hear this message of Sri
Chaitanya, unequalled in its power? If you lived in Calcutta, the faithful from many places would be blessed by your
*hari-katha*.” <!-- F19 F20 -->

## The Bhaktivinoda Asana
The guru corresponded with him, and the plan was approved. In November 1918 (in the month of Agrahayana, 1325 of the
Bengali era) Kunjavihari rented, for fifty rupees a month, the two-storeyed house at No. 1 Ultadanga Junction Road,
near the Pareshnath temple, and opened there the “Sri Bhaktivinoda Asana.” Upstairs were the altar and Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta's room; downstairs lived Kunjavihari himself and a few devotees. Earlier that same year he had lived in
Calcutta at No. 7 Gauribere Lane, and the guru visited him there. Sundarananda Vidyavinoda, the future compiler of the
mission's chronicle “Sarasvati-jayashri,” came to him for two or three months to hear *hari-katha*, won over by his
learning, intelligence and manner, and on the night of Gaura-purnima 1918 Kunjavihari told him to go to Mayapur.[^4]
<!-- F21 F115 F116 -->

All this rested on one man — a government employee of small means and low salary. He took upon himself the whole care
of preaching his guru's message and, when money ran short, served even on borrowed money. His devotion to his guru and
his tireless zeal for preaching *hari-katha* opened to the world, as was later said, “a mine of priceless gems”: thus
the first foundation stone of the Sri Gaudiya Math was laid — the great sacrificial ground of pure *hari-kirtana*. Few
can compare with him in such service to the guru. <!-- F22 F23 F24 I1 -->

It was in the Bhaktivinoda Asana that the “Sri Gaudiya Math” — the first branch of the Sri Chaitanya Math — first
opened, and from here too began to appear the weekly spiritual paper “Gaudiya,” founded by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta; its
first issue came out on 19 August 1922, edited by Sri Haripada Vidyaratna. <!-- F25 -->

## Secretary of the Gaudiya Math
Kunjavihari himself left a written account of the early years of the mission, beginning from 1914; Sundarananda
Vidyavinoda included it in the second part of “Sarasvati-jayashri” (1934). In that chronicle “Acharyatrika Sripada
Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana” is the guru's constant companion: on 29 April 1922, together with Sripada Bhakti Pradipa
Tirtha and others, he preached in Nalpur and Manikpur, and he accompanied Srila Bhaktisiddhanta at the meetings in
Jessore and Khulna. <!-- F114 F117 -->

On 18 May 1920, without telling his guru, he left for Basra. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta was deeply grieved, and on 8 January
1921 he wrote him a letter in which he revealed the spiritual nature of them both.[^5] <!-- F108 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta made him general secretary of the mission: Kunja Babu conducted all its affairs and was manager of
the Gaudiya Math temple. On 5 March 1934 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta led a large preaching party to his native village,
Chanchuri-Purulia, and for five days preaching went on there without pause. On 4 November 1935 the guru installed the
Deities in the Kunja-bihari Math at Radha-kunda.[^6] On 15 March 1936 Kunja Bihari Vidyabhushan was among those who
accompanied Srila Bhaktisiddhanta to Sarbhog station, together with Paramananda Vidyaratna, Vasudeva Prabhu and others;
according to one of the later biographies, on 24 October of that year he travelled with the guru from Calcutta to Puri.
Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Goswami recalled that, living in the Calcutta math, he asked “Kunja-da” only for the cloth he
needed and nothing more.[^7] <!-- F100 F101 F102 F104 F122 -->

## “The Second Form of the Guru”
How highly Srila Bhaktisiddhanta himself valued him is seen from his own words. In 1932, for the fifty-eighth
anniversary of his appearance, he wrote an address for the Vyasa-puja and said in it of Kunjavihari: such a matchless
inclination to serve is rarely found among people. On the day when his own guru, Srila Gaurakishora, left this world,
Kunjavihari appeared to him as a second form of the guru, and from then on did not let him serve by himself, taking for
himself every opportunity of service. Thus he gave full scope to the blessing Srila Bhaktisiddhanta had received from his
guru: “May your inclination to serve Krishna increase.”[^8] <!-- F26 Q2 -->

In the same address Srila Bhaktisiddhanta plays on his name. Kunjavihari — “He who sports in the groves” — is a name of
Krishna, and the guru says that “in his kunja” the community of the servants of the Gaudiya Math, together with the holy
dhama, has obtained the service of Sri Gandharva-Giridhari — Radha and Krishna. One of Kunjavihari's undertakings became
the chief aim of that year: the exhibition “Gaudiya Math Pradarshani,” in which the truth appeared to people suffering
under the three gunas visibly, as the Lord appears in His worshipable form (*archa-murti*). And further the guru says:
watered with hearing and chanting, the “Sri Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha,” through the branches of this embodiment of
selfless love, will grow a kunja where Kunjavihari — Krishna Himself — together with Sri Gandharva-Giridhari will
manifest the wish-fulfilling tree of which the beginning of the “Charitamrita” sings:

“I remember Sri Sri Radha and Sri Govindadeva, seated on a throne in a beautiful temple of jewels beneath a
wish-fulfilling tree in the radiant forest of Vrindavana, served by Their dearest companions.” (CC, Adi 1.16)

For this kunja, surrounded by *tamala* trees, he prays: “*tamalibhih parivritam idam eva yache*” — “this alone I ask.”
And he ends thus: “If I could become a dog in the abode of this embodiment of love, I would consider my life
blessed.”[^9] <!-- F27 F28 Q2 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta carried out his work relying on the inspiration, zeal and energy of Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana. He
described him in eight phrases: Acharyatrika Sri Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana, secretary of the Sri Gaudiya Math, is its
chief pillar; the cause of the life and prosperity of all the service of the Gaudiya Math; the first architect of the
Sri Gaudiya Math; the central, chief gem in the necklace of the best bhagavatas, the crown jewels among the devotees of
the Gaudiya Math; the embodied model of service to the guru and the guru's dear form (*preshtha-murti*); the first among
the preachers who teach service to the guru, the crown of patience and diligence; a sentinel-bhagavata, like a morning
bell that wakes the people of the whole world to the service of Sri Guru and Sri Hari; the best friend of all Gaudiya
Vaishnavas. Not without reason is it said of him that by his tireless and pure service he became as it were another
form of Sarasvati Thakura, and that without consulting him the guru undertook nothing. <!-- F30 F31 Q4 I2 -->

## The Guru's Last Days
On 27 December 1936, on Sunday evening, at a gathering in memory of the departed Srila Jagabandhu Bhakti-ranjan, Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta gave his last address in his manifest pastimes. So that all would understand that every good would
come to them if they followed the example of Sripada Kunjavihari, he said before everyone: “May Sripada Kunjavihari
Vidyabhushana be victorious in everything! If those who help him follow him, great good fortune awaits them.”
<!-- F29 Q3 -->

The disciples took turns keeping watch at the guru's bedside. The day before his departure he asked Srila B. R. Sridhar
Maharaj to sing Narottama Dasa's song “Sri Rupa-manjari-pada.” Sridhar Maharaj, by his own account, was not yet used to
leading kirtan and hesitated, and then Kunja Babu — the secretary and manager of the Gaudiya Math and the guru's close
servant — asked a brahmachari with a good voice to sing. Sridhar Maharaj himself recalled: “Sriyukta Kunja Babu, later
Sripad Bhakti Vilas Tirtha Maharaj, asked a brahmachari: ‘You sing.’ He began to sing, but Prabhupada was displeased. He
said: ‘I do not need correct intonation or sweet sounds.’ And I had to sing ‘Sri Rupa-Manjari-pada’” (“Our Teachers”).
<!-- F118 -->

On Thursday, 31 December 1936, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta dictated in English his “Guiding Principles”: “Form a Governing
body of 10 to 12 persons for Management of Mission work but Kunja Babu will manage so long as he lives. Kunja Babu’s
sympathy for me brought me in connection with so many persons. His intelligence excelled all. His sympathy for me knows
no bound. I advise you (Kunja Babu) to be courageous and callous, as I am callous to all. This should be your guiding
principle. I told the other day and again I say Kunja Babu should be respected by all as long as he lives. Not to
quarrel with one another.” And he added in Bengali: “Let Vasudeva write something; let Sundarananda help Professor
Babu.”[^10] The next morning, 1 January 1937, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta left this world. <!-- F32 Q5 -->

## The Division of the Mission
Soon after the guru's departure a dispute arose among the disciples over the leadership of the mission. The matter went
to the Calcutta High Court, and by its decision the maths were divided. Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana and Sri Paramananda
Vidyaratna received the headquarters of the mission — the Sri Chaitanya Math at the birthplace of Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu, all the maths of Mayapur and many others; they took possession in February 1948. In time Kunjavihari became
president-acharya of the Sri Chaitanya Math and its branches, the Sri Gaudiya Maths.[^11] <!-- F33 F34 F35 F36 -->

Earlier, in the first years after the guru's departure, he, together with Bhakti Prakash Aranya, Bhakti Sarvasva Giri,
Bhakti Svarupa Parvat, Bhakti Prasun Bodhayan, Krishna Keshava and Sundar Gopal Brahmachari, urged Hayagriva
Brahmachari to accept sannyasa; and on Phalguni Purnima 1944, when he accepted it at the Tota-Gopinatha temple in Puri
and became Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava, Kunja Bihari Vidyabhushan was present together with Paramananda Vidyaratna,
Parvat Maharaj and Bodhayan Maharaj. <!-- F103 -->

Having received the Sri Chaitanya Math in 1948, he found himself in very difficult circumstances. In a letter of those
days he wrote that since his guru's departure in 1937, protecting the Sri Chaitanya Math, he had been living as if in a
pit of fire, yet was at peace: his whole life was an unceasing struggle, but he was so absorbed in preaching the great
teachings of his guru and in establishing the glory of the Sri Chaitanya Math that no thought other than service to the
guru entered his mind; he had courage enough to rely on himself, yet in every trouble he looked only to the guru's
mercy. <!-- F37 Q6 -->

He himself accepted sannyasa on the very first Gaura-jayanti after receiving the Sri Chaitanya Math — 25 March 1948,
Gaura-purnima — and from then on was called Tridandi Swami Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha Gosvami Maharaja. From whom he accepted
sannyasa the biographies do not say. <!-- F38 -->

## Acharya of the Sri Chaitanya Math
Wherever a religious meeting was called, Tirtha Maharaj was asked to speak. Once, in the grounds of Deshapriya Park in
Calcutta, at a festival in honour of Mahaprabhu's appearance, more than two hundred thousand people gathered, and when
he rose to speak, silence fell over that sea of people.[^12] Living in Calcutta, he spoke *hari-katha* tirelessly from
dawn to midnight. Of the math's money he used to say: of every rupee donated, twelve annas go to preaching and
publications and only four to the upkeep of the math; and his brahmacharis give heart and life to preaching.
<!-- F39 F40 F41 Q7 -->

He made it his rule to visit every Gaudiya Math in the country at least once a year, with large meetings and festivals,
and all year round he sent parties of sannyasis and brahmacharis to the remotest villages of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.
At his request his godbrother, Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Goswami Maharaj, served for seven years as head pujari of the
Yogapitha temple — the birthplace of Mahaprabhu. <!-- F72 F105 I4 -->

## Publications and Presses
On taking up the service of acharya he arranged the publication of the “Gaudiya” — now a monthly journal from
Mayapur;[^13] from Calcutta appeared the “Gaudiya” in Hindi, from Madras in English, and from Guntur a monthly in
Telugu. For the extensive publications of the math he set going again the “Nadia Prakash Printing Works” press at the
Sri Chaitanya Math, and when it was no longer enough he opened another press in Calcutta; in Madras many books were
printed in English and the local languages. Under his editorship the Sri Chaitanya Math also brought out the fifth
edition of the “Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita.” <!-- F42 F43 F121 -->

## Temples and Maths
Over his guru's samadhi in Mayapur he raised the samadhi temple and dug before it a pond — Shyama-kunda. Fulfilling
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's wish, he built the temple of Gadadhara at Srivasa-angana, next to the “Advaita-bhavana.” With
the help of the local authorities he paved about eight miles of the road to Sri Dhama Mayapur. <!-- F45 F46 F47 -->

On 15 July 1951 he laid the foundation of the Sri Purushottama Gaudiya Math in Puri, near the samadhi of Srila
Haridasa Thakura, and on 2 September of the same year the foundation stone of the temple of the Ramananda Gaudiya Math
at Kovvur on the Godavari. On 22 July 1956 he opened a math in Calcutta, at 29/A/1 Chetla Central Road. The mission
had a fine math at Radha-kunda, but he saw that a math was needed in Vrindavana as well, and received one from Srimad
B. S. Siddhanti Maharaj (the Gaudiya Sarasvata Ashrama); the documents were completed on 1 June 1960.
<!-- F48 F49 F54 F50 -->

The south of India asked on all sides for a Gaudiya Math, but there were not enough workers, and the requests could not
be met; still, on 12 September 1958 a math opened in Guntur (Andhra Pradesh). The Madras Gaudiya Math grew greatly and
became one of the notable religious centres of the city. In 1975 a branch of the Sri Chaitanya Math appeared at
Diamond Harbour (South 24 Parganas), and the temple of Sri Radha-Vinoda-jiu in Vrindavana and the Sri Gaudiya Math in
Basirhat joined the Sri Chaitanya Math as its branches. <!-- F44 F53 F51 F52 -->

## The Sri Chaitanya Research Institute
One of his chief achievements was the Sri Chaitanya Research Institute in Calcutta. The need for such an institute for
the wide preaching of Mahaprabhu's teaching was first felt by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta. In October 1957, before a great
preaching tour of the south, Tirtha Maharaj saw in a dream a huge building where, in a large library, his gurudeva was
helping researchers. <!-- F56 F57 -->

The plot at 70-B Rashbehari Avenue was obtained on 15 January 1958 and registered on 14 March; on 28 December of the
same year, at a large gathering, the foundation stone was laid by the then Vice-President of India, Dr. S.
Radhakrishnan. On 29 June 1964 the same Dr. Radhakrishnan, now President of the country, opened the institute; the
opening was performed with *nama-sankirtana*. In his speeches the guest spoke of preserving the heritage of which
Mahaprabhu was the interpreter, and said that the institute should seek what true religion is.[^14]
<!-- F55 F58 F59 -->

## Mahaprabhu's Footprints
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta installed the footprints of Mahaprabhu (*charan-chihna*) in many places in India where the Lord
had walked, but in Tirupati, Srirangam and Udupi, the chief centre of the Madhva sampradaya, he did not have time to do
so. Fulfilling his guru's wish, Tirtha Maharaj installed Mahaprabhu's footprints in Srirangam in 1958 and in Tirupati in
1965, at places indicated by the authorities of the maths there. So even after his guru's departure he went on
fulfilling his will: the samadhi temple, the temple of Gadadhara, the pada-pithas, and the institute conceived by Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta himself. <!-- F60 I5 -->

## Preaching throughout India
His preaching knew no boundaries between classes and circles of society. He invited prominent politicians to his large
meetings — and with them came hundreds of their followers. India, he explained, must regain her lost glory in order to
live, and will regain it not by material prosperity but by developing inner, spiritual strength. “The death of a
nation will come on the day,” he said, “when spirituality disappears and materialism increases.” Politicians, he held,
should not be kept away from religion: they should be rightly instructed in our philosophy and religion, on which the
nation stands. <!-- F73 Q9 I3 -->

Year after year he travelled. In 1952, after the Vraja-mandala parikrama, he gave a learned address in Sitapur and then
spoke in Allahabad; in 1953 in Madras, and in 1954 again in Madras, where Hindus, religious societies and the editor of
the journal “Fellowship of Faiths,” S. M. Fazil, invited him to speak about Mahaprabhu's message of love. In 1955 he
toured North India: from 19 to 21 April he spoke on “Bhagavata-dharma” in the Theosophical Society hall in Sitapur,
from 24 to 26 April in the Hanuman temple in Gonda and at Thomson College; from 10 May he spent seven days in Kandi and
on 11–12 May spoke at a large meeting in Baharampur; in Calcutta, on 9 September, he spoke at the women's college in
Bidhan Sarani, and on 16 October at Charu Chandra College, presided over by the Chief Minister P. C. Sen. On 10 October
1955 in the Dhaka math he was heard by the Chief Minister of East Pakistan, Abu Hossain, and the ministers Basanta
Kumar Das and Madhusudan Sarkar. At the “All-India Conference of All Religions” in Ujjain he was heard by more than ten
thousand people.[^15] <!-- F75 F76 F77 -->

When the Purushottama Gaudiya Math in Puri was opened, many wished to hear from him about Sri Chaitanya's religion of
love: the mahant of the Emar Math, Srimad Gadadhara Ramanuja Dasa; the mahant of the Balananda Math in Deoghar,
Mohananda Maharaj; Prankishore Goswami; the local officials — the deputy magistrate G. C. Parija, the subdivisional
officer Satyavadi Panda and the town magistrate Patnaik; the former minister of Mayurbhanj, Vidyadhar Mahanta; and
also two guests from America. <!-- F74 -->

In 1956 the Madras math celebrated its silver jubilee. Tirtha Maharaj spent seven days in Madras and seven in Srirangam
and Madurai. In Srirangam the citizens presented him with an address of welcome in the municipal hall, and in Madurai
likewise. At the Minakshi temple the priests met him at the gate with full pitchers and Vedic hymns; a golden staff and
a velvet umbrella were carried, two decorated elephants greeted him with raised trunks, and the chief priest tied a silk
cloth on his head. From 31 October to 16 November of that year he toured Sitapur, Gonda, Lucknow, Bareilly and
Naimisharanya with a large party of devotees, everywhere speaking before hundreds and thousands of listeners. At Lucknow
University he was welcomed by the Vice-Chancellor, Dr. Radhakamal Mukerjee: Uttar Pradesh, he said, is doubly indebted
to Mahaprabhu's movement — for Vrindavana and for the teaching of love that rejected Shankara's advaita. In Bareilly, a
great northern centre of the Ramanuja sampradaya, a meeting was held on 11 November; on the 13th he spoke at Bareilly
College, on the 14th at a large meeting in the house of Dr. P. N. Guha, on the 15th in the house of Dr. S. N.
Chatterjee, and on the 16th at the Rotary Club. <!-- F78 F79 F80 -->

Many cities gave him civic receptions: the Madras Corporation on 2 August 1955, Madurai on 7 September 1956, Tanjore on
27 October 1956, Kumbakonam on 26 October 1957, Udupi on 7 November 1957, Guntur on 15 January 1959, Mysore on
6 September 1959, Vijayawada on 21 September 1959; in the north, Kanpur, Sitapur, Porbandar and others. <!-- F61 -->

## “My Aim”
Before a great preaching and pilgrimage tour of the south in 1957 he issued a message, “My Aim.” The aim of his life, he
wrote, is to establish the Lord in the pure hearts of people. To teach not only worship with flowers and sandalwood,
but worship of the Lord with devotion — chanting the Name of Krishna, free from all connection with maya. In an impure
heart full of sin worship is impossible. Everyone has the right to worship the Lord, whatever his birth and position,
but the worship must be pure, otherwise it does not please the Lord. Genuine religious teachers purify the heart and
mind — this is the first condition of worship — and one must take up the role of true acharyas. As long as there is no
purity in everyday deeds and thoughts, neither the life of society nor peace can be established.[^16] <!-- F81 Q10 -->

On that tour he was received in Madras under the chairmanship of the Governor, Dr. P. V. Rajamannar; the chief guest was
the Chief Justice of the Madras High Court, P. Rajagopalan. The route passed through Sriperumbudur, Kanchipuram,
Chidambaram, Kumbakonam, Tanjore, Srirangam, Rameshvaram, Madurai, Trivandrum, Cape Comorin, Udupi, Tirupati and other
places. <!-- F82 -->

## At Udupi
On 7 November 1957 Udupi honoured him on behalf of its citizens. The chairman of the municipality, K. K. Pai, said: the
followers of Madhvacharya live all over Udupi, and the light of his knowledge has burned here for about seven
centuries; Mahaprabhu, attracted by him, conversed here with an eminent sannyasi of the Madhva sampradaya, and since
then the swamis of the Gaudiya Math and of the eight maths of Udupi have been connected.[^17] <!-- F83 -->

Tirtha Maharaj replied: he had given all he had to the service of Svayam Bhagavan Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, and
therefore the honour shown to him is honour shown to Mahaprabhu. The connection with Udupi is an old story: the Gaudiya
sampradaya comes by succession from the Madhva sampradaya. Srila Madhavendra Puri, Mahaprabhu's parama-guru and the
first architect of the special prema-bhakti, was in the Madhva sampradaya, and Mahaprabhu is the chief preacher of this
prema-bhakti in the mood of separation (*vipralambha*). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta visited Udupi more than once, collected
the literature of the Madhva sampradaya and invited one of the swamis from here to teach Madhva-darshana at the main
math in Mayapur. <!-- F84 Q11 -->

The connection continued. Once, on 5 April, the Pejavar Swami of Udupi was honoured at the Sri Chaitanya Research
Institute (the chairman was Dr. Krishnagopal Goswami, head of the Sanskrit department of Calcutta University); the next
day the swami came to Mayapur and said with tears that this place was heavenly, that his pilgrimage was fulfilled, that,
inspired, he dreamed of founding a new mission, and that nowhere in the holy places of India had he seen such an
atmosphere.[^18] <!-- F90 -->

## The Years 1958–1964
On 20 November 1958 he was warmly met at the station in Sitapur, on 6 December by a large crowd at the station in Kanpur;
in Prayaga he spoke at the Narayana Mandir, Sanatana Dharma College, the Bar Association and the Dvarakanatha Mandir; on
the 20th he was in Vijayawada with a party of devotees, and on 23 January 1959 the city honoured him (the chairman of
the municipality was Dr. J. Dakshinamurti).[^19] On 26 March 1959 he presided over a huge Sri Chaitanya-jayanti festival
in Calcutta, attended by about two hundred thousand people; the organizing committee was headed by the West Bengal Food
Minister P. C. Sen, and the treasurer was the Mayor of Calcutta, Dr. Triguna Sen. <!-- F85 F66 -->

In 1959, on 1 April, he spoke at the International Club in Lucknow, on the 2nd at a satsanga-bhavana, on the 3rd at the
palace of Ranilila Ramkumar Bhargava; on 19 August he was in Coimbatore and on the 30th received many visitors; then in
Mysore, where the city gave him a reception; on 6 November in Dehradun, then in Bareilly. On 22 November he was met at
the Kanpur station and escorted with sankirtana to the Sri Sanatana Dharma hall; for two weeks he spoke in Kanpur — at
Christ Church, D.A.V. and Sanatana Dharma colleges, the law college, the Bagla Building, in Marupnagar and Ashoknagar,
at the Jyotirbrahma Ashrama and the Hari Sabha — and the whole city was stirred; on 28–29 November he was again in
Lucknow. <!-- F86 -->

On 22 February 1960, at the Ranji Stadium in Calcutta, he opened the second World Conference of Religions — eight days,
about eight thousand delegates from all over the world. In the same year the President of India, Dr. Rajendra Prasad,
came with the Governor of Madras, Bishnuram Medhi, to the Madras Sri Gaudiya Math for Krishna-jayanti; also present
were the Chief Minister of Madras, K. Kamaraj, the Chairman of the Legislative Council, Dr. P. V. Cherian, and others.
On 13 August 1960 his address was broadcast by Madras radio. <!-- F87 F62 F63 -->

From 23 to 25 December 1961 the Madras Sri Gaudiya Math held the All-India Vaishnava Conference — a new chapter in the
Vaishnava movement. The idea and organization belonged to his disciple Nandadulal Brahmachari, but everything was done
with the guru's blessing, and he himself called the conference a wonderful event. Representatives of the main
Vaishnava sampradayas shared their teachings; the four main sampradayas — of Ramanuja, Madhva, Vishnusvami and
Nimbarka — and also the followers of Vallabha, the Swaminarayanis and others called Tirtha Maharaj their friend,
brother, patron, fearless preacher and standard-bearer of Vaishnava philosophy, and the heads of all the sampradayas
unanimously elected him chairman. The exhibition at the conference was opened by the Chief Justice of the Madras High
Court, Dr. P. V. Rajamannar; in seventeen days more than three hundred thousand people visited it, and the souvenir
volume ran to a hundred and twenty pages.[^20] <!-- F64 F65 F110 -->

In April 1962, at the Kumbha Mela in Hardvar, an exhibition was held at the Sarasvata Gaudiya Math, a branch of the Sri
Chaitanya Math; he arrived on 5 April, and on the 8th the Governor of Uttar Pradesh, Dr. B. Ramakrishna Rao, visited the
exhibition. On 16 November 1964 he presided over a large meeting of the Vaishnava sampradayas of Vrindavana at the
Radha-raman Ghera. <!-- F88 F89 -->

## Jubilees
From 7 February to 6 March 1969 the Sri Chaitanya Math celebrated its golden jubilee.[^21] On the day of Vyasa-puja,
7 February, Tirtha Maharaj, carrying his gurudeva's padukas on his head, led a festive procession. Until 25 February
bhajans and *hari-katha* went on every day; then more than ten thousand devotees went in procession around Navadvipa
Dhama, and from 2 to 4 March the All-India Religious Conference was held — more than ten thousand delegates, among the
speakers philosophers of many universities, scholars and judges; the film division of the central government filmed
the celebrations. At the jubilee the exhibition “Sri Chaitanya-Sarasvata Bhagavata-pradarshani” was held.
<!-- F67 F69 -->

Welcoming the guests, he recalled: in 1918, with no more than twenty-five faithful disciples, his gurudeva began his
mission from here and founded more than sixty-four maths and temples in India and abroad; from Kurukshetra to Madras,
from Bombay to Puri, hundreds of educated young men became his disciples, and from here Mahaprabhu's message spread
throughout India and the world. <!-- F68 Q8 -->

From 13 August to 13 September 1970, at the Madras Sri Gaudiya Math, a programme was held in commemoration of the Sri
Chaitanya Math in which more than a million people took part. <!-- F91 -->

The centenary of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, founder of the Sri Chaitanya Math, was celebrated for a whole year. It was
opened in Mayapur on 22 February 1973 by the Governor of Orissa, B. D. Jatti. More than five hundred devotees set out by
special train through the cities of India and the four dhamas, with processions and sankirtana; according to his
disciple, the train ran from 7 May to 30 June 1973 from Howrah through Bhubaneswar, Puri, Kovvur, Guntur, Madras,
Trivandrum, Kanyakumari, Srirangam and other places.[^22] The pilgrims were received by the Governor of Orissa at the Raj
Bhavan in Bhubaneswar, by a former Chief Minister of Orissa at the Veda Bhavan in Puri, and by the citizens of Madras,
Madurai and Bangalore. From 22 December 1973 to 4 January 1974, under his direction, a spiritual exhibition and an
“All-India Conference on Culture and Religion” were held in Kalighat Park in Calcutta: the exhibition was opened by the
Governor of West Bengal, A. L. Dias, the conference on 26 December by the Vice-President of India, Gopal Swarup Pathak;
the chairman was Vishvanath Das, former Governor of Uttar Pradesh and Chief Minister of Orissa, and the chief guest was
the Chief Justice of West Bengal, Shankar Prasad Mitra. <!-- F70 F71 F111 -->

## Disciple and Teacher
Among Tirtha Maharaj's disciples one was especially dear to him: Nandadulal Brahmachari, later Srila Bhakti Prajnana
Yati Gosvami Maharaja. He recalled: he first came to Mayapur in 1950 but found neither the acharya nor the president of
the math; the second time was at the end of 1951. For a full ten months his gurudeva himself taught him Gaudiya
Vaishnava philosophy, and in 1952 sent him to Madras. Nobody so devoted, practical and disciplined as his gurudeva, the
disciple said, had he ever met: not a single superfluous word, his whole life by the clock; he was never a second late
anywhere and never spoke longer than necessary, and until the age of eighty he worked on books eight to ten hours a
day. <!-- F106 F107 -->

The guru wrote him letters: on 22 November 1957 — about the unprecedented organization of preaching in the south; on
7 December 1968 and 19 March 1969 — about the golden jubilee; on 13 July 1970 — that a great responsibility for the
mission would fall on the disciple and that this was Srila Prabhupada's blessing. When Yati Maharaj wrote on 25 July
1970 that missionary work had never interested him, the guru replied the next day: “Yati Maharaj, this judgement is
wrong,” and blessed him in English: “You have opened a new chapter in the annals of Gaudiya Vaishnavism. Srila
Prabhupad guides you,” adding that all his service was wholly one with the guru's own way of thinking. <!-- F109 -->

## The Last Journey
His health had long been weak, but in 1976, moved by great zeal for preaching, he went to the Ramananda Gaudiya Math at
Kovvur on the Godavari with forty-two sannyasis and brahmacharis. He arrived on 14 July, and on 15 July, at the place
where the footprints of Sri Chaitanya remain, he installed the *vigrahas* of Raya Ramananda and Sriman
Mahaprabhu.[^23] <!-- F92 F120 -->

From his arrival in Kovvur devotees came to him in an endless stream, and he spoke *hari-katha* to them. His frail body
could not bear the strain; by evening he fell ill and was treated by his personal physician, but he did not improve,
and he was brought to Calcutta. For about two months his body, like a ship on a stormy sea, withstood the onslaught of
illness. <!-- F93 -->

## Departure and Samadhi
On 10 September 1976, at 3.20 in the afternoon, he left this world. The biography says: a brightly shining meteor
passed from the material world into the spiritual world. By our calendar the day of his departure is the
krishna-dvitiya of the month of Padmanabha; he was eighty-one. <!-- F94 -->

His sacred body was taken to Mayapur and given samadhi where he had first met his gurudeva — at Vraja-pattana, next to
the samadhi temple of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta: so that, as the biography says, hand in hand with his guru he might remain
in loving service to Sri Sri Radha-Govinda. <!-- F95 -->

The news of his departure spread throughout India. Newspapers wrote, meetings were held, hundreds of letters and
telegrams arrived — from the Vice-President of India, governors, judges, high officials; leaders and heads of maths and
sampradayas came in person to express their grief, and memorial meetings were held almost everywhere. The “Calcutta
Municipal Gazette” devoted a lengthy editorial to him. <!-- F96 F97 -->

After his departure preaching and building in the mission came almost to a standstill for ten years. The “Sri
Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha” ceased to meet and gathered again only on 9–11 March 1990. The centenary of his appearance
was celebrated by the Sri Chaitanya Math from 25 to 28 March 1994, together with the meeting of the
“Navadvipa-dhama-pracharini Sabha,” and the year-long celebration concluded on 24–28 October 1994 in the
natya-mandiras of the Sri Chaitanya Math and the Yogapitha. <!-- F112 F113 -->

## Significance
Tirtha Maharaj's life is above all service to the guru. A postal clerk of small means, he rented a house on borrowed
money so that his guru would have a preaching centre in Calcutta, and with this the whole history of the Gaudiya Math
began. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta himself called him the pillar and first architect of the mission, the life-force of its
service, and said that he worked relying on his zeal. <!-- I1 I2 -->

All his life — under his guru and after him — he bore the burden of management, but did not let it hide what mattered
most: *hari-katha* from dawn to midnight, tours of the maths, preaching in remote villages and before the rulers of the
country, who, he was convinced, should not be kept away from religion but instructed. And, having become acharya, he
above all fulfilled his guru's will: he raised the samadhi temple and the temple of Gadadhara, installed Mahaprabhu's
footprints in Srirangam and Tirupati, and founded the institute Srila Bhaktisiddhanta had conceived. Other Vaishnava
sampradayas also recognized him, electing him chairman of their common conference. And so he is remembered: “a
sentinel-bhagavata, like a morning bell,” calling the people of the whole world to serve Sri Guru and Sri Hari.
