# Srila Bhakti Vilas Tirtha Goswami Maharaj
*Шрила Бхакти Вилас Тиртха Госвами Махарадж · @bhakti-vilasa-tirtha*

## Kunja Babu
Among the disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, one of the first and closest to him was Kunjavihari
Vidyabhushana — the one whom the guru himself called the chief pillar of his mission. His father named him Umapati; in
the world he bore the name Kunjavihari (Kunjabihari), and in the Gaudiya Math he was simply Kunja Babu. He was honoured
with the titles “Acharyatrika” and “Paravidyabhushana Bhagavata-ratna”; in the chronicles of the mission he is
“Maha-mahopadeshaka,” the great instructor; and those close to him called him “Prabhupada-preshtha,” dear to
Prabhupada, as the disciples called their guru. For many years he was the secretary and manager of the Gaudiya Math. In
1948 he accepted sannyasa with the name Tridandi Swami Bhakti Vilas Tirtha Goswami Maharaj, and to the end of his days
he remained the acharya of the Sri Chaitanya Math; his disciples called him Srila Tirtha Maharaj, Acharyadeva and
Acharyapada. The calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math marks his days as those of “Tridandi Swami Srimad Bhakti
Vilas Tirtha Maharaj,” and “Our Teachers” names him among those great disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta who themselves
became teachers. <!-- F1 F119 -->

## Appearance and Childhood
He appeared in the village of Chanchri-Purulia (Chanchuri-Purulia), Kalia police station, Narail subdivision, Jessore
district, in present-day Bangladesh. The day of his appearance is gaura-saptami, the seventh day of the bright fortnight
of the month of Vishnu (Chaitra): so the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math observes it. The year, by our
reckoning, is 1895.[^1] <!-- F2 F3 -->

His father, Sri Raicharan Dasa, was a Vaishnava, and the household worshipped the Deities Sri Gaura-Nitai and Sri
Giridhari. Before Them, on an auspicious day at the start of his fifth year, the boy was seated to learn his first
letters. There were two sons in the family; the younger was named Sri Ganapati. Raised by Vaishnava parents, from
childhood he knew no food but vegetarian, kept the Vaishnava fasts and holy days, chanted the holy Name morning and
evening, and watered the tulasi. <!-- F4 F5 F6 F9 -->

## The Search for a Guru
In 1911 he passed his entrance examination and came to Calcutta to study at college. But a few days later his father
left this world, and college had to be given up. The young man took on the care of his aged mother, of the house and of
his younger brother's studies, and became an assistant in an office of the postal department. <!-- F7 F8 -->

His soul was searching for a true guru. He went to Pahari Baba, to the “Ramakrishna Mission” and elsewhere, but a real
devotee of the Lord he did not find. On the advice of a close relative he came to Navadvipa and stayed in the house of a
certain gosai, to whom an old acquaintance, Sri Sakhicharan Ray, had introduced him. Seeing that meat was being cooked
there, he left at once. <!-- F10 F11 -->

In 1914, at Kuliya, he was blessed with the darshan of the feet of Om Vishnupada Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji
Maharaja. Babaji Maharaja had only one disciple — Sri Varshabhanavi-dayita Dasa, the future Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati — and to see him the young man came to Mayapur on the next phalguni-purnima, in 1915. There, at
Vraja-pattana, he first met his future guru. He was explaining to his listeners the lines of the “Charitamrita”:

“Wandering through the universe, some fortunate jiva, by the mercy of guru and Krishna, obtains the seed of the creeper
of devotion (*bhakti-lata*).” (CC, Madhya 19.151)

What he heard struck the young man. For the first time he saw with his own eyes, among Vaishnavas, a knower of the
scriptures who was guileless and pure in character, and he was drawn to this faith; he wanted to know more.[^2]

## Initiation
In that same year, 1915, on the day of Utthana Ekadashi, Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji entered the eternal lila at
Koladvipa. Kunjavihari came to bid him farewell and saw Srila Bhaktisiddhanta for the second time.[^3] Two days later, at
Vraja-pattana, he listened for two hours to *hari-katha* from his lips — and was so attracted that he took two months'
leave from his work to hear him in seclusion. <!-- F15 F16 -->

The guru's word was not an easy one. Explaining what true devotees, bhagavatas, are like, he spoke also of those who only
call themselves devotees of Krishna — and it turned out that people whom the young man had revered as great souls did
not stand even on the first rung of devotion, that of the *kanishtha-adhikari*. At first this displeased him, and he
decided to leave. But Sri Paramananda Vidyaratna asked him to stay a few more days, and when he had heard the guru out,
Kunjavihari felt such reverence for Srila Bhaktisiddhanta that he asked him for initiation. <!-- F17 F18 -->

He received diksha in the first week of January 1916, and the guru's mercy entered his heart with special power. Then,
full of joy, he said to the guru: “How many will be able to come here and be fortunate enough to hear this message of Sri
Chaitanya, unequalled in its power? If you lived in Calcutta, the faithful from many places would be blessed by your
*hari-katha*.” <!-- F19 F20 -->

## The Bhaktivinoda Asana
The guru corresponded with him, and the plan was approved. In November 1918 (in the month of Agrahayana, 1325 of the
Bengali era) Kunjavihari rented, for fifty rupees a month, the two-storeyed house at No. 1 Ultadanga Junction Road,
near the Pareshnath temple, and opened there the “Sri Bhaktivinoda Asana.” Upstairs were the altar and Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta's room; downstairs lived Kunjavihari himself and a few devotees. Earlier that same year he had lived in
Calcutta at No. 7 Gauribere Lane, and the guru visited him there. Sundarananda Vidyavinoda, the future compiler of the
mission's chronicle “Sarasvati-jayashri,” came to him for two or three months to hear *hari-katha*, won over by his
learning, intelligence and manner, and on the night of Gaura-purnima 1918 Kunjavihari told him to go to Mayapur.[^4]
<!-- F21 F115 F116 -->

All this rested on one man — a government employee of small means and low salary. He took upon himself the whole care
of preaching his guru's message and, when money ran short, served even on borrowed money. His devotion to his guru and
his tireless zeal for preaching *hari-katha* opened to the world, as was later said, “a mine of priceless gems”: thus
the first foundation stone of the Sri Gaudiya Math was laid — the great sacrificial ground of pure *hari-kirtana*. Few
can compare with him in such service to the guru. <!-- F22 F23 F24 I1 -->

It was in the Bhaktivinoda Asana that the “Sri Gaudiya Math” — the first branch of the Sri Chaitanya Math — first
opened, and from here too began to appear the weekly spiritual paper “Gaudiya,” founded by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta; its
first issue came out on 19 August 1922, edited by Sri Haripada Vidyaratna. <!-- F25 -->

## Secretary of the Gaudiya Math
Kunjavihari himself left a written account of the early years of the mission, beginning from 1914; Sundarananda
Vidyavinoda included it in the second part of “Sarasvati-jayashri” (1934). In that chronicle “Acharyatrika Sripada
Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana” is the guru's constant companion: on 29 April 1922, together with Sripada Bhakti Pradipa
Tirtha and others, he preached in Nalpur and Manikpur, and he accompanied Srila Bhaktisiddhanta at the meetings in
Jessore and Khulna. <!-- F114 F117 -->

On 18 May 1920, without telling his guru, he left for Basra. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta was deeply grieved, and on 8 January
1921 he wrote him a letter in which he revealed the spiritual nature of them both.[^5] <!-- F108 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta made him general secretary of the mission: Kunja Babu conducted all its affairs and was manager of
the Gaudiya Math temple. On 5 March 1934 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta led a large preaching party to his native village,
Chanchuri-Purulia, and for five days preaching went on there without pause. On 4 November 1935 the guru installed the
Deities in the Kunja-bihari Math at Radha-kunda.[^6] On 15 March 1936 Kunja Bihari Vidyabhushan was among those who
accompanied Srila Bhaktisiddhanta to Sarbhog station, together with Paramananda Vidyaratna, Vasudeva Prabhu and others;
according to one of the later biographies, on 24 October of that year he travelled with the guru from Calcutta to Puri.
Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Goswami recalled that, living in the Calcutta math, he asked “Kunja-da” only for the cloth he
needed and nothing more.[^7] <!-- F100 F101 F102 F104 F122 -->

## “The Second Form of the Guru”
How highly Srila Bhaktisiddhanta himself valued him is seen from his own words. In 1932, for the fifty-eighth
anniversary of his appearance, he wrote an address for the Vyasa-puja and said in it of Kunjavihari: such a matchless
inclination to serve is rarely found among people. On the day when his own guru, Srila Gaurakishora, left this world,
Kunjavihari appeared to him as a second form of the guru, and from then on did not let him serve by himself, taking for
himself every opportunity of service. Thus he gave full scope to the blessing Srila Bhaktisiddhanta had received from his
guru: “May your inclination to serve Krishna increase.”[^8] <!-- F26 Q2 -->

In the same address Srila Bhaktisiddhanta plays on his name. Kunjavihari — “He who sports in the groves” — is a name of
Krishna, and the guru says that “in his kunja” the community of the servants of the Gaudiya Math, together with the holy
dhama, has obtained the service of Sri Gandharva-Giridhari — Radha and Krishna. One of Kunjavihari's undertakings became
the chief aim of that year: the exhibition “Gaudiya Math Pradarshani,” in which the truth appeared to people suffering
under the three gunas visibly, as the Lord appears in His worshipable form (*archa-murti*). And further the guru says:
watered with hearing and chanting, the “Sri Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha,” through the branches of this embodiment of
selfless love, will grow a kunja where Kunjavihari — Krishna Himself — together with Sri Gandharva-Giridhari will
manifest the wish-fulfilling tree of which the beginning of the “Charitamrita” sings:

“I remember Sri Sri Radha and Sri Govindadeva, seated on a throne in a beautiful temple of jewels beneath a
wish-fulfilling tree in the radiant forest of Vrindavana, served by Their dearest companions.” (CC, Adi 1.16)

For this kunja, surrounded by *tamala* trees, he prays: “*tamalibhih parivritam idam eva yache*” — “this alone I ask.”
And he ends thus: “If I could become a dog in the abode of this embodiment of love, I would consider my life
blessed.”[^9] <!-- F27 F28 Q2 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta carried out his work relying on the inspiration, zeal and energy of Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana. He
described him in eight phrases: Acharyatrika Sri Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana, secretary of the Sri Gaudiya Math, is its
chief pillar; the cause of the life and prosperity of all the service of the Gaudiya Math; the first architect of the
Sri Gaudiya Math; the central, chief gem in the necklace of the best bhagavatas, the crown jewels among the devotees of
the Gaudiya Math; the embodied model of service to the guru and the guru's dear form (*preshtha-murti*); the first among
the preachers who teach service to the guru, the crown of patience and diligence; a sentinel-bhagavata, like a morning
bell that wakes the people of the whole world to the service of Sri Guru and Sri Hari; the best friend of all Gaudiya
Vaishnavas. Not without reason is it said of him that by his tireless and pure service he became as it were another
form of Sarasvati Thakura, and that without consulting him the guru undertook nothing. <!-- F30 F31 Q4 I2 -->

## The Guru's Last Days
On 27 December 1936, on Sunday evening, at a gathering in memory of the departed Srila Jagabandhu Bhakti-ranjan, Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta gave his last address in his manifest pastimes. So that all would understand that every good would
come to them if they followed the example of Sripada Kunjavihari, he said before everyone: “May Sripada Kunjavihari
Vidyabhushana be victorious in everything! If those who help him follow him, great good fortune awaits them.”
<!-- F29 Q3 -->

The disciples took turns keeping watch at the guru's bedside. The day before his departure he asked Srila B. R. Sridhar
Maharaj to sing Narottama Dasa's song “Sri Rupa-manjari-pada.” Sridhar Maharaj, by his own account, was not yet used to
leading kirtan and hesitated, and then Kunja Babu — the secretary and manager of the Gaudiya Math and the guru's close
servant — asked a brahmachari with a good voice to sing. Sridhar Maharaj himself recalled: “Sriyukta Kunja Babu, later
Sripad Bhakti Vilas Tirtha Maharaj, asked a brahmachari: ‘You sing.’ He began to sing, but Prabhupada was displeased. He
said: ‘I do not need correct intonation or sweet sounds.’ And I had to sing ‘Sri Rupa-Manjari-pada’” (“Our Teachers”).
<!-- F118 -->

On Thursday, 31 December 1936, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta dictated in English his “Guiding Principles”: “Form a Governing
body of 10 to 12 persons for Management of Mission work but Kunja Babu will manage so long as he lives. Kunja Babu’s
sympathy for me brought me in connection with so many persons. His intelligence excelled all. His sympathy for me knows
no bound. I advise you (Kunja Babu) to be courageous and callous, as I am callous to all. This should be your guiding
principle. I told the other day and again I say Kunja Babu should be respected by all as long as he lives. Not to
quarrel with one another.” And he added in Bengali: “Let Vasudeva write something; let Sundarananda help Professor
Babu.”[^10] The next morning, 1 January 1937, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta left this world. <!-- F32 Q5 -->

## The Division of the Mission
Soon after the guru's departure a dispute arose among the disciples over the leadership of the mission. The matter went
to the Calcutta High Court, and by its decision the maths were divided. Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana and Sri Paramananda
Vidyaratna received the headquarters of the mission — the Sri Chaitanya Math at the birthplace of Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu, all the maths of Mayapur and many others; they took possession in February 1948. In time Kunjavihari became
president-acharya of the Sri Chaitanya Math and its branches, the Sri Gaudiya Maths.[^11] <!-- F33 F34 F35 F36 -->

Earlier, in the first years after the guru's departure, he, together with Bhakti Prakash Aranya, Bhakti Sarvasva Giri,
Bhakti Svarupa Parvat, Bhakti Prasun Bodhayan, Krishna Keshava and Sundar Gopal Brahmachari, urged Hayagriva
Brahmachari to accept sannyasa; and on Phalguni Purnima 1944, when he accepted it at the Tota-Gopinatha temple in Puri
and became Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava, Kunja Bihari Vidyabhushan was present together with Paramananda Vidyaratna,
Parvat Maharaj and Bodhayan Maharaj. <!-- F103 -->

Having received the Sri Chaitanya Math in 1948, he found himself in very difficult circumstances. In a letter of those
days he wrote that since his guru's departure in 1937, protecting the Sri Chaitanya Math, he had been living as if in a
pit of fire, yet was at peace: his whole life was an unceasing struggle, but he was so absorbed in preaching the great
teachings of his guru and in establishing the glory of the Sri Chaitanya Math that no thought other than service to the
guru entered his mind; he had courage enough to rely on himself, yet in every trouble he looked only to the guru's
mercy. <!-- F37 Q6 -->

He himself accepted sannyasa on the very first Gaura-jayanti after receiving the Sri Chaitanya Math — 25 March 1948,
Gaura-purnima — and from then on was called Tridandi Swami Bhakti Vilas Tirtha Goswami Maharaj. From whom he accepted
sannyasa the biographies do not say. <!-- F38 -->
