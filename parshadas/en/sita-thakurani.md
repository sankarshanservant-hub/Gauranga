# Sita Thakurani
*Сита Тхакурани · @sita-thakurani*

## Mother of the Universe
The primary sources speak of Sri Advaita Acharya's wife with special reverence. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls her “Sita
Thakurani, revered by all” and “the Acharya's wife” (*acharyani*); Vrindavana Dasa calls her “Advaita's wife”
(*advaita-grihini*), “devoted to her husband” (*pati-vrata*), “mother of the universe” (*jagan-mata*), “the great chaste
lady” (*mahasati*) and “mistress of yoga” (*yogeshvari*). Advaita Himself affectionately calls her “Krishnadasa's mother,”
after their son. She is also called Sitadevi and Sri Sita Devi. <!-- F1 -->

Vaishnavas honour her just as they honour Shachi Devi, the Lord's mother: she is the mother of the universe and
Advaita's eternal consort. A song in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.3753–3756), glorifying Advaita, says that Advaita is
non-different from Gauranga, and His consort Sita is the mother of the universe. All her life her heart was given to
motherly love, *vatsalya-prema*, for Sri Gaurasundara: she saw Him as her own child, fed Him, protected Him and blessed
Him. This is no worldly attachment but her eternal nature, and the Lord answered her in kind — He called her “mother”
and honoured her just as He honoured His own mother. <!-- F9 F10 Q5 -->

## The Daughter of Nrisimha Bhaduri
Sita's father was Nrisimha Bhaduri (Baduri), a devoted brahmana. She had a sister named Shri, and Nrisimha with great joy
gave both daughters to Advaita; the two wives of the Acharya, honoured by the world, are known everywhere as Shri and
Sita. So the “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells (in its twelfth wave); neither the “Charitamrita” nor the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”
names her father. One of the biographies explains why Advaita had two wives: Vishnu has three shaktis — Shri, Bhu and
Nila — and without them Advaita's person would not have been manifest in its fullness. The wedding, according to the
same biography, took place in Phulia-nagara, after which Advaita moved to Nadia and settled in Shantipura. <!-- F2 F3 F4 F5 Q4 -->

Where and when she was born the biographies do not say; by a late tradition her birthplace was Narayanapura near
Saptagrama (of which more below). Her appearance day is kept in our calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math) on
Gaura-panchami, the fifth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Hrishikesha (Bhadra), as “the Appearance Day of
Sri Sita Devi, consort of Sri Advaita Prabhu”; the year of her appearance the sources have not preserved.

## Yogamaya
Who she is in the Lord's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Bhagavati Yogamaya is now His [Advaita's] wife, descended in the form of Sita; and by her manifestation, the one named
Shri.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 86)[^1]

Yogamaya is the Lord's internal, spiritual energy, who arranges His pastimes and serves Him in them. She should not be
confused with Mahamaya, the external energy that rules the material world: Yogamaya belongs to the Lord Himself, and the
Lord is never subject to her — she only serves His lila. And Sita's sister Shri, according to this verse, is her
manifestation, her *prakasha*. Some writers call Sita Paurnamasi or Katyayani in Krishna's pastimes; on this see the
note.[^2] <!-- F6 F7 F8 Q3 F52 -->

## Gifts for the Infant
When the Lord appeared, Advaita was in Shantipura. In the evening, on the day of the lunar eclipse, He and Haridasa
Thakura suddenly began to dance for joy and bathed in the Ganga; taking the eclipse as an occasion, He gave generously to
the poor and sent many gifts for the Infant through Sita.[^3] With her husband's leave Sita travelled from Shantipura to
Navadvipa, to the house of Jagannatha Mishra in Mayapura:

“Advaita Acharya's wife, the noble lady revered by all the world, was named Sita Thakurani. With the Acharya's leave she
set out with gifts to see that jewel among children.” (CC, Adi 13.111)[^4] <!-- F11 F12 -->

According to the “Gaura-krishnodaya” (2.20–24), Advaita Himself also set out with His wife and the gifts, but the
palanquin-bearers went so fast that they brought Sita to Mishra's house the next day, ahead of her husband. The Infant
was then refusing the breast, and Sita found the women downcast; wise and compassionate, she said not a word, and the
brahmana ladies paid her honour. <!-- F52 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” draws attention to the words “in His own house” (Adi 13.99):
although Advaita also had a house in Mayapura, at that time He lived in Shantipura. Advaita's Navadvipa house stood not
far from the house of Srivasa, and Advaita sometimes stayed there and talked of Krishna until dawn with the devotees
headed by Srivasa. <!-- F13 -->

Sita came in a curtained palanquin with her maidservants, and Krishnadasa Kaviraja lists her gifts lovingly, one by one:

“She brought a little golden waist-chain and anklets of silver coins, golden armlets and bracelets, fine conch-shell
bangles for both arms, silver ankle-rings and necklaces of gold coins. There was also a tiger's claw set in gold, a
silken waist-cord, every kind of ornament for hands and feet, many-coloured silk saris, a little shirt woven with a silk
border, and a wealth of gold and silver coins. Riding in a curtained palanquin with her maidservants, she came with
baskets full of clothes and ornaments, and plates heaped with auspicious things — durva grass, paddy, gorochana,
turmeric, saffron and sandal.” (CC, Adi 13.112–114)

The biographies add silver ornaments for the feet, golden *churi* and *bala* bracelets, an *ananta* necklace, a
*ghunsi* waist-string and fragrances; the “Anubhashya” explains what these ornaments are. <!-- F14 -->

Entering Shachi's house, Sita stood still in wonder:

“Bringing many loads of sweets and other foods, she arrived at Shachi's house. When she saw the child, He was Kana of
Gokula Himself — only His colour was the opposite. Every limb was finely formed, He seemed an image of gold, and every
limb bore auspicious marks. Seeing the child's divine radiance, she was filled with delight, and her heart melted with a
mother's love.” (CC, Adi 13.115–116)

She was struck by the Infant's superhuman form: she recognized in Him Kana Himself, Krishna of Gokula, only of another,
golden colour. <!-- F15 -->

## The Name “Nimai”
Then Sita blessed the Infant, tied a protective amulet (*raksha-kavacha*) on Him and gave Him a name:

“She placed durva grass and paddy on His head and blessed Him again and again: ‘May the two brothers live long!’
Fearing the witches and evil spirits, she gave Him the name Nimai.” (CC, Adi 13.117)

“The two brothers” are Vishvarupa and Vishvambhara. The witches and evil spirits are, in Bengali, *dakini* and
*shankhini*: malevolent spirits in female form in the train of Parvati and Mahesha, lesser deities (*apadevatas*). As
the “Anubhashya” explains, they dare not approach the sacred nimba (neem) tree or anything connected with it — hence the
“bitter” name Nimai.[^5] The Lord Himself, of course, needs no protection: the amulet, the durva grass and the name that
wards off harm are a custom in which tender motherly love found its expression. Kaviraja says just this: she gave the name
“out of fear” — the fear for a beloved child that only a loving heart knows. <!-- F16 F17 -->

When mother and son first bathed and came out of the birthing room, Sita presented them with clothes and ornaments:

“On the day the mother and child were bathed, she gave them clothes and ornaments and honoured Mishra together with his
son. Then, honoured in turn by Shachi and Mishra, Sita Thakurani returned home glad at heart.” (CC, Adi 13.118)

The “Anubhashya” explains that this is the day of *nishkramana*, the first going out of the house, and that “home” means
Shantipura. <!-- F18 Q1 -->

## Beside Shachi
From that day on, according to the biographies, Sita often came from Shantipura to Mayapura and taught Shachimata how to
care for the Infant and bring up children, gave her much advice and then went home again.[^6] At the festival of the
Child's birthday Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi gave the honoured Sita the privilege of being the first to offer Him new
clothes, and all the devotees together with Advaita and Sita prayed that the Child might live long and happily in
Mayapura. Sita and Shachi were of one heart: Nimai was their life and soul. And when Nimai's elder brother Vishvarupa took
sannyasa, Advaita and Sita consoled Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi and helped them bring up Nimai.[^7]
<!-- F19 F20 F21 F22 F23 -->

Narahari Chakravarti, too, sings of those days in his padas. At the birth festival Sita Devi and Malini perform the
auspicious rites; Sita gazes unblinking at Gaura's smile, weeps, instructs Shachi and blesses the Child; and
Shachi, clasping her feet, begs her to bless Nimai with long life, so that He will not leave home as Vishvarupa did.
<!-- F42 -->

And in the “Namamrita-samudra,” where Narahari calls upon the Lord's associates one after another, Sita stands among
the guardians of Gaura — after Shachi and Jagannatha, beside Malini:

“O Madhavi, Malini, Damayanti, O Sri Sita! Who but you protects Gauranga?” (“Namamrita-samudra” 10)
<!-- F43 -->

## The Message from Ramai
When the Lord revealed Himself to the devotees in Navadvipa, He sent Sri Rama Pandita, Ramai, to Shantipura with a
message for Advaita: let Him come with His wife and bring everything for worship. The message filled Advaita's house with
tears of joy:

“Hearing that the Lord had come, Advaita's chaste wife, the mother of the whole universe, wept for joy. Advaita's son
Achyutananda, though still very small, also began to weep without restraint. So Advaita, His wife and son, and all the
servants gathered there shed streams of tears.” (CB, Madhya 6.40–42) <!-- F24 -->

Advaita told His wife to gather quickly everything for worship, and she, knowing Who had come to Navadvipa, took all that
the Lord loves:

“Advaita's chaste wife knew the true glory of Sri Chaitanya. She took sandal paste, flower garlands, cloth and incense.
She also took what the Lord loved to eat: thickened milk, yogurt, cream, butter, camphor and betel.” (CB, Madhya
6.53–54)

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.1796) says the same: Sitadevi gathered everything for the Lord's worship. Advaita set out for
Navadvipa with His wife and the gifts, but, wishing to test the Lord, first hid in the house of Nandana Acharya. When the
Lord called Him, Advaita together with His wife prostrated Himself before Him in joy from afar, offering prayers, and both were struck
speechless as they beheld His majesty (CB, Madhya 6.73–103). When the Lord spoke to them, they raised their arms and wept
for joy (6.99), and when He placed His feet on their heads, their long-cherished desire was fulfilled:

“So Advaita together with His wife received the Lord's divine feet upon their heads, and their innermost desire was
fulfilled.” (CB, Madhya 6.138)

Joyful, they stayed there with the devotees (6.178). According to one of the biographies, Advaita brought Sita from Shantipura to Mayapura, to the kirtana in the
house of Srivasa, and was the first of all to bow to Sri Chaitanya's lotus feet. <!-- F24 F25 F49 -->

Even before His sannyasa the Lord would at times come with Nityananda to Advaita's house in Shantipura; a festival was
held there, and kirtana went on day and night. Parameshvari Dasa Thakura, in one of his padas included in the
“Pada-kalpataru” (23), tells of such a day: the Lord, Nityananda and Advaita were jokingly discussing the festival when
Sita Thakurani came in, smiling; the Lord turned to her and told her to invite all the Vaishnavas of the neighbourhood,
and at noon the kirtana began. <!-- F31 Q7 -->

## The Chastisement of Advaita
Once Advaita, expounding the “Yoga-vasishtha” in Shantipura, began to extol the path of abstract knowledge, hostile to
bhakti. It was a ruse: Advaita longed to be punished by the Lord, for punishment from the Lord is the guru's abundant mercy. The Lord understood His design, came with Nityananda to Shantipura and showed His anger against the
detractor of bhakti (CB, Madhya 19.118–127). <!-- F26 -->

Seeing the Lord in such a mood, Advaita's wife grew anxious and bowed to Him in her mind:

“Advaita's wife offered obeisances to the Lord within her mind. She felt great anxiety, seeing the mood the Lord was
in.” (CB, Madhya 19.129)

Outwardly she did not bow to Him or greet Him openly, but in this there is neither disrespect nor pride: such is the
reserve proper to a married woman, and in her heart, casting off all self-importance, she humbly submitted to the Lord.
<!-- F26 -->

“Which is higher — knowledge or bhakti?” the Lord asked. “Knowledge,” Advaita answered, “and the one who knows has no
need to keep to the path of bhakti.” Then the Lord, to teach the people a lesson, dragged Advaita from His seat into the
courtyard, threw Him to the ground and began to beat Him (CB, Madhya 19.131–134). And Sita interceded for her husband:

“Advaita's chaste wife, the mother of the whole universe, understood what was happening, yet still she tried to stop the
Lord: ‘He is an old brahmana! Spare His life! Whom will You teach, and what, by punishing Him? This brahmana is so old.
What more do You mean to do to Him? If anything happens to Him, You will be answerable for it.’ Hearing these words of
Advaita's chaste wife, Nityananda smiled. Haridasa Prabhu in fear began to call on Krishna's name within his mind.”
(CB, Madhya 19.135–138)[^8]

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.1974) says the same: the all-knowing Sita, mother of the universe, speaks gentle words in
alarm. Her alarm is not a wife's worldly fear for her husband: she, in Vrindavana Dasa's words, knew the whole truth and
only showed alarm, as the pastime required. For the Lord's anger here is also a pastime: He beats Advaita to teach people
the superiority of bhakti over knowledge, and this punishment is the very mercy Advaita had been seeking. <!-- F27 I2 -->

The Lord's anger soon subsided. “Nityananda, if I was too hot-tempered, forgive Me,” He said, and all four looked at one
another and laughed (CB, Madhya 19.225–226). Sita the Lord always called mother:

“Whenever Vishvambhara Mahaprabhu addressed Advaita's chaste and virtuous wife, He called her mother. The Lord said to
her: ‘Go and prepare an offering for Krishna. Then we shall eat.’” (CB, Madhya 19.227–228) <!-- F28 F29 -->

The Lord Himself went with Nityananda, Advaita and Haridasa to bathe in the Ganga, and on returning sat down to the meal,
and the great chaste lady, the mistress of yoga, served the food:

“Advaita's chaste and virtuous wife, an exalted devotee, silently repeated the names of Lord Hari as she brought them
the food.” (CB, Madhya 19.239) <!-- F29 -->

After the meal Sita had cooked, Nityananda, overcome by His childlike mood, scattered rice all over the house (CB,
Madhya 19.242 ff.). And when the Lord embraced Advaita, Achyuta, Haridasa and Sita rejoiced; the “Bhakti-ratnakara”
(12.1979) says that Advaita, Sita and all their household wept. <!-- F30 -->

## Shantipura after the Sannyasa
When the Lord took sannyasa and, half-mad with love, rushed through the forests towards Vrindavana, Sita spent four days
with Shachi, who, sunk in the darkness of separation, lay on the ground as if at death's door. <!-- F32 -->

But the Lord came to Shantipura, to Advaita's house — and to Advaita and Sita it was as if life had returned. The Lord had
fasted for three days (CC, Madhya 3.38), and now a great feast was prepared for Him in Advaita's house:

“The Acharya's wife had done the cooking beforehand, and the Acharya Himself offered the food to Vishnu.” (CC, Madhya
3.41)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta gave this passage of the “Anubhashya” just such a heading: “Sita Thakurani cooks, and the Acharya
Himself offers the bhoga.” Sita laid out the huge feast on whole banana leaves, and after His fast Gaurasundara accepted
the prasada prepared by her hands, while Advaita talked and joked at the meal with Him and with Nityananda. So too the
“Gaura-krishnodaya” (6.38–54) tells that in Shantipura after the sannyasa Sita cooked a great feast for the Lord. And Kavi Karnapura in his drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” (Act 6) says that, to
gladden His mother and the devotees, the Lord stayed in Shantipura for three days and ate food cooked by Shachi and by
“Achyutananda's mother,” that is, Sita. <!-- F33 Q6 F41 F51 -->

## Feasts in Puri
While the Lord lived in Nilachala, Advaita Acharya, Sita Thakurani and their son Achyutananda went there to Him every
year. Before setting out, Sita would cook Gaurasundara's favourite dishes and take them with her, and in Puri she would
invite Him and feed Him.[^9] Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells how in the third year the Bengali devotees went to Puri with
their wives:

“That year all the devotees set out together with their wives, including the wife of Sri Advaita Acharya, the mother of
Achyutananda.” (CC, Madhya 16.21)

“Wishing to feed Mahaprabhu, the wives of all the Vaishnavas brought from home His favourite foods and sweets.” (CC,
Madhya 16.25) <!-- F48 -->

Vrindavana Dasa tells of one such meal. Advaita went home and made everything ready for the
feast:

“Advaita's chaste wife, a manifestation of Lakshmi, joyfully set to work. She had brought from Bengal the foodstuffs
Lord Chaitanya loves and gave them to Advaita. Then Sri Advaita Mahashaya sat down and began to cook, meditating on Lord
Chaitanya. Following the longing of her heart, His chaste wife prepared various vegetables for the Lord's feast. She knew
that the Lord greatly likes shak, and so she brought and gave Advaita ten kinds of shak. Advaita Acharya cooked, and His
chaste wife helped Him. So they floated in an ocean of bliss.” (CB, Antya 9.19–24)

And Advaita, sharing His concern with her, says: “O mother of Krishnadasa, listen…” (Antya 9.25). <!-- F34 Q2 -->

The Lord, ever absorbed in Krishna's name, accepted their invitations only for their joy. Sita, full of motherly love,
treated Him as her own son, and the Lord regarded her with the same reverence and love as Shachi. Murari Gupta in his
“Kadacha” tells how in Puri the Vaishnavas' wives cooked for the Lord from the provisions they had brought, while Advaita
Himself cooked sweet rice and, together with His wife, brought it to the Lord and fed Him in private (4.19.11–15).
<!-- F35 F41 -->

## Her Sons
Advaita's eldest son, Achyutananda, is Sita's son; the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” finds him still very small when Ramai's
message came to the house (Madhya 6.41). The biographies name three sons of hers — Achyutananda, Krishna Mishra and
Gopala, devoted followers of the Lord; according to the “Advaita-charita,” which one of them and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” (on Adi 12.13) cite, these are Gaura's
“three jewels,” members of His circle, while Advaita's other sons are Balarama, Svarupa and Jagadisha. The “Charitamrita”
names Achyutananda, Krishna Mishra and Gopala among the branches of Advaita's tree but does not mention their mother
(Adi 12.13–26), and according to the “Prema-vilasa” (24.348) Sita had five sons and Shri one.[^10] <!-- F36 Q8 F50 -->

When, at Shachi's word, Nityananda came with His wives to Shantipura, Sita, seeing Vasudha and Sri Jahnava, was
overjoyed and embraced them as tenderly as Shachi had embraced them. <!-- F37 -->

## Her Last Years
The Lord withdrew from sight, and after Him, still before Sita, so did Advaita. Sita, in separation from the Lord, could barely
remain in her body. Then Srinivasa came to Shantipura: having received Vishnupriya's blessing and the mercy of the
Navadvipa devotees, he was passing through Shantipura on his way to Khardaha. Narahari Chakravarti tells of this
meeting:

“In Mother Sita's body life barely remained; she called Srinivasa into the inner apartments. Srinivasa went and bowed to
her feet; in her mercy she placed her feet on his head. Tears stream incessantly from both her eyes; in a faltering voice
she says to Srinivasa: ‘O my son Srinivasa, I have been waiting for you; it is good that you have come — I am happy to
see you. Live long on the earth: through you good will come to living beings. You will distribute this rare prema-bhakti
and spread the scriptures of bhakti, the whole treasure of the devotees.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.70–75)

She foretold that he would have many followers and be immersed in sankirtana, and that in Vrindavana he would become a
disciple and his desire would be fulfilled; she told him to go there quickly, entrusted him to Madana-Gopala and
introduced him to her sons and servants; her motherly love for him, says Narahari, cannot be described (4.76–80).
Narahari says the same in the “Narottama-vilasa” (2.90): in Shantipura Srinivasa saw Mother Sita, and her motherly love
cannot be described. Later, in Vrindavana, while embracing Narottama,
Srinivasa regained his composure by remembering the words of Mother Sita (4.416). The “Anuraga-valli” (3.28–33) also
says that Srinivasa was blessed with the darshana of Sita Thakurani's feet, and Jayananda in his Uttara-khanda recalls
the grief of Sita and Advaita for Chaitanya and Nityananda. <!-- F38 F46 F53 -->

When and where Sita left this world the sources do not say, and our calendar has no day of her departure. According to one
of the biographies, when Jahnava, on her way to Kheturi, came to Shantipura, neither Advaita nor Sita Thakurani was any
longer in this world: Jahnava was received by their sons, Achyutananda and Gopala.[^11] <!-- F39 F53 -->

## In Songs and Traditions
Lochana Dasa in his “Chaitanya-mangala,” listing who from the eternal world appeared together with the Lord, says:

“Katyayani took birth on earth and bears the name Sita in the society of brahmanas.” (“Chaitanya-mangala,”
Sutra-khanda 8.614)

He remembers her further on as well: the Lord takes a meal at the house of Advaita and Sita (“The Truth about Advaita,”
Madhya-khanda), and in the account of the Lord's coming to Shantipura after the sannyasa “Sita-devi and Narayani floated
in bliss.”[^12] <!-- F40 F54 -->

Later works have preserved traditions about her. The “Prema-vilasa” (24.322–368) tells that Nrisimha Bhaduri was a
brahmana from Narayanapura near Saptagrama; the girls' mother had left the world, leaving them behind, and of the two sisters Sita was the
elder and Shri the younger; Sita is Yogamaya, and Shri is her manifestation. In a dream Nrisimha saw that Advaita would
be the husband of both; then came the meeting with Bada Shyamadasa in Kulia and the wedding in Phulia, the expenses of
which were borne by Hiranya and Govardhana. On the day the bride first served food, the wind blew away her veil, and the
brahmanas saw Sita four-armed. Both sisters received initiation from Advaita. There it is also said that Chhota
Shyamadasa honoured Sita as his mother and saw her four-armed, and that two of her maidservants, Nandini and Jangali,
received the Krishna mantra from her: Nandini served her feet, while Jangali practised austerities in the forest — the
tradition of the “Jangali-tota” is connected with her name. In another chapter (4.273–315) Sita finds Srinivasa weeping
and feeds him, together with Gopala, with the remnants of Advaita's meal, and, speaking of Kamadeva Nagara, says that she
rejected both him and her maidservant Nandini. The “Advaita-prakasha,” ascribed to Ishana Nagara, tells that Nrisimha
found Sita in a lotus, that she received the Krishna mantra in a dream from Madhavendra Puri, that on the day of the swing festival she foresaw Krishna's
appearance in the form of Radha and came to bless Gauranga; it gives the birth dates of her sons, says that she
initiated Krishna Mishra and his wife, grieved for Gaura and went to Him in Nilachala, raised Ishana himself, fainted on hearing of the Lord's disappearance, and after
Advaita's departure told Ishana to marry. These traditions are late, and we give them only as traditions.[^13]
<!-- F44 F45 F55 -->

Among the books on Advaita's life one of the biographies also names a Bengali “Sita-charitra”; it is evidently a late
work. The verse “Sita-advaita-charita,” for its part, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” (on Adi 12.13) calls the
work of one Lokanatha Dasa of the “sakhi-bheki” prakrita-sahajiyas and points out that it contradicts the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata.” <!-- F47 F50 -->

## Her Significance
Sita Thakurani is Yogamaya, the Lord's internal energy, descended as Advaita's consort, and one of Gaura's mothers. She
saw in the Infant Krishna of Gokula, gave Him the name Nimai, watched over His childhood, fed Him in Shantipura and in
Puri, and to the end of her days lived by her love for Him. Her motherly love is of the same kind as Shachi's, and the
Lord answered her as He answered Shachi: He called her mother and honoured her as a mother. That is why Vaishnavas
revere her equally with Shachi, as the mother of the universe. <!-- F6 F9 F35 I1 -->

And in Shantipura, when the Lord was chastising Advaita, she showed how a true devotee behaves: outwardly reserved, in
her heart she was humbly submissive to the Lord, and when she interceded for her husband she knew that the Lord's
punishment was mercy and a lesson to the world on the superiority of bhakti over knowledge. <!-- I2 -->
