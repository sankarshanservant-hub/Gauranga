# Gangamata Gosvamini
*Гангамата Госвамини · @gangamata-gosvamini*

## The Princess of Putiya
Gangamata Gosvamini lived long after Sri Chaitanya's disappearance, yet her life is bound to Him by the closest of ties:
she entered the spiritual succession of Sri Gadadhara Pandita and restored in Puri the house of Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya,
where Mahaprabhu had once been a guest. Neither the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” nor the other primary sources tell of her;
her name and her place in the succession are given by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the “Anubhashya”, and everything
else is known from tradition and from the records of her abode, the Gangamata Matha in Puri. The name her father gave her
was Shachi Devi, or simply Shachi. She came to be called Gangamata only later, after the miracle of
the Ganga described below; the name of her matha comes from the same event[^1]. <!-- F1 -->

Shachi was the daughter — according to one of the biographies, the only daughter — of Raja Sri Naresha Narayana of Putiya,
in the district of Rajshahi (now in Bangladesh). Her father held wide lands: one biography calls him a great zamindar, or
raja, another the king of a whole realm. That she was a princess of Putiya is also stated by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the
“Anubhashya”; the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” of Haridasa Dasa records it with the words “it is said”. <!-- F2 -->

From childhood her heart belonged to the Lord, and she remained indifferent to worldly things. She began her studies
early: first grammar and poetry, and, having mastered them, she gave herself wholly to the shastras and studied them with
deep attention while still in her father's house. The princess was beautiful, and young men were captivated by her, but
her mind inclined to no one: it was full of Madana-gopala. When her parents began to prepare her marriage, Shachi said
that she would take as husband no mortal born in this world of death. This was not contempt for the world but fidelity:
her only Lord was Krishna. Her parents grieved, but could not change her mind; according to one account, they lamented it
into their old age. <!-- F3 F4 -->

When her mother left this world, Shachi left her home and went to wander among the holy places[^2]. She went first to
Sri Kshetra, to Puri, and from there to Vrindavana-dhama. One of the biographies relates that in her wanderings she
found no peace of heart anywhere and resolved to seek a true guru; in Puri, after the darshan of Jagannatha, she felt
within her heart His command to set out for Vrindavana — and then, as this account puts it, her good fortune rose like
the moon. <!-- F5 F6 -->

## The Succession of Gadadhara Pandita
In Vrindavana Shachi took shelter of Haridasa Pandita Gosvami, and through him her spiritual line goes back to Gaura's
own shakti, Sri Gadadhara Pandita. Gadadhara's disciple was Ananta Acharya, and Ananta Acharya's dear disciple was
Haridasa Pandita, who in the days of Krishnadasa Kaviraja had charge of the service in the temple of Sri Govinda in
Vrindavana. The “Charitamrita” says of him:

“The superintendent of that service is Sri Haridasa Pandita, whose fame and virtues are known throughout the world. He is
gentle, patient, serene, generous and grave, sweet in speech and sweet in conduct, and steady beyond measure. He honours
everyone and works for the good of all; his heart is free of crookedness, envy and malice. The fifty good qualities of
Krishna that are shared in part by others all dwell in his person.” (CC, Adi 8.54–57)

And further:

“Ananta Acharya was the disciple of Pandita Gosani; his very body was made of love for Krishna, and he was generous and
noble in every way. Who could recount his endless virtues? And this Haridasa Pandita is his dear disciple.”
(CC, Adi 8.59–60) <!-- F7 Q1 -->

Who Ananta Acharya is in the eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Anantacharya Gosvami is she who was Sudevi in Vraja.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 165)

Sudevi is one of the eight principal sakhis of Sri Radha. Citing this verse in the “Anubhashya” to CC, Adi 8.59–60, Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta adds that the well-known Gangamata Matha in Purushottama is a branch of Ananta Acharya's line; in the
guru-parampara of that matha Ananta Acharya himself is called Vinoda-manjari, and his disciple Haridasa Pandita Gosvami,
otherwise called Sri Raghu-gopala, is Sri Rasa-manjari. <!-- F8 Q2 Q3 -->

In the same passage of the “Anubhashya” the succession of the matha is then given name by name. The disciples of
Haridasa Pandita are Sri Lakshmipriya, the wife of Gangamata's maternal uncle, and Sri Gangamata, a princess of Putiya;
after them come Sri Vanamali, Bhagavan Dasa (a Bengali), Madhusudana Dasa (an Oriya), Nilambara Dasa, Narottama Dasa,
Pitambara Dasa, Madhava Dasa and, finally, his disciple — the mahanta of the Gangamata Matha in Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's
own day[^3]. <!-- F9 -->

## The Disciple of Haridasa Pandita
When she saw Haridasa Pandita in Vrindavana, Shachi longed with all her heart to receive initiation from him. It seemed to
her that this meeting alone had made her whole pilgrimage worthwhile. One of the biographies says that Haridasa Pandita's
devotion to Gauranga and Nityananda was unequalled, and that, seeing his spiritual power and austerity, she was beside
herself with joy. She fell at his feet in tears, begging for his mercy. <!-- F10 -->

Haridasa Pandita, however, was in no hurry: he knew that before him stood a princess, the daughter of a wealthy house.
According to one account, he reflected for several days and then, to test her as a disciple, told her that a king's
daughter could not live in Vrindavana as a poor devotee — in bhajana, half-starved, without any comforts; better for her to
worship the Lord at home. Shachi understood what these words meant, but did not turn back. With unshakeable resolve she
continued her bhajana and her service to the guru, and little by little gave up her costly garments and ornaments. Seeing
her renunciation and her thirst for bhajana, Haridasa Pandita gave her shelter. <!-- F11 -->

The same account goes on: one day the guru permitted her, putting aside pride and fear, to go about Vraja begging alms as a
renounced devotee should, and blessed her. In great joy, in worn-out clothes, she went from door to door through Vrindavana,
taking only a little from each — like a bee that gathers a drop from many flowers; such begging is called *madhukari*. The
Vaishnavas of Vrindavana marvelled at her renunciation. She grew thin and weak, but did not notice it. Tired from her
service, she would fall asleep on the bank of the Yamuna, and in the morning she would again clean the temple of the Lord,
visit the holy places, watch the arati of the Deity and hear the “Bhagavatam”. Then Haridasa relented and said with a
smile, “A princess — and you labour so for the worship of Krishna… Soon I shall give you the mantra.” All this was
for the sake of bhajana and service to the guru, not for the sake of a feat: sincere renunciation in this way helps one
find the guru and keep his mercy. <!-- F12 I3 -->

Shachi received initiation in the temple of Sri Govindaji, on the shukla-ekadashi of the month of Chaitra (according to
one of the biographies, on a Wednesday): her guru initiated her with the eighteen-syllable mantra. After that she lived
on *madhukari* alms in strict renunciation, begging a morsel of bread at one door after another[^4]. <!-- F13 F14 -->

## Radha-kunda
After a year in Vrindavana, Shachi, at her guru's bidding, settled at Radha-kunda together with her elder guru-sister,
Srila Lakshmipriya Devi, who was also her relative. Lakshmipriya was gentle, devoted to bhajana, an exalted Vaishnavi,
and every day she chanted Hari's names three hundred thousand times. Together the two of them set an example of bhajana;
every day they went round Giriraja Govardhana together[^5]. So, after several years at Radha-kunda, Shachi grew mature
in bhajana — and then her guru called her. <!-- F15 F16 F17 -->

## In the House of Sarvabhauma
Now Haridasa Pandita sent his disciple to Puri: to live in the house of Sri Sarvabhauma, to perform her bhajana there,
to restore that house, and to bring to people the teaching of Sri Chaitanya that she had learned. Shachi took her guru's
order as everything she had in life, came to Puri, and took the vow of *kshetra-sannyasa* — never to leave the holy land
of Purushottama. This was not a formal vow of renunciation for the sake of liberation, but the resolve to live in the
Lord's dhama and serve Him. One of the biographies notes that by then nearly all of Mahaprabhu's intimate associates had
already entered His eternal, unmanifest pastimes. <!-- F18 F19 -->

The house of Sarvabhauma had fallen into ruin: it had long stood empty. All that was left was one dilapidated building,
where Sri Damodara-shalagrama abided, whom Vasudeva Sarvabhauma himself had once served; there was no longer anyone to
worship Him. Shachi established His service according to the rules of the shastras; the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana”
calls Him Sri Radha-Damodara. In this house Mahaprabhu, in mahabhava, first accepted hospitality in Nilachala: that
Sarvabhauma's house is now known as the Gangamata Matha is stated by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” to CC,
Madhya 6.8. Sarvabhauma then lived, he says, south of the Sri Mandira, in Balukhanda, on the bank of Markandeya-saras;
the biographies, for their part, place his house and Gangamata's abode on the bank of the Shvetaganga[^6].
<!-- F20 F21 -->

Soon Shachi became famous in Puri as a reader of the “Srimad-Bhagavatam”. She had learned to read and explain the
“Bhagavatam” while still at Radha-kunda, discussing it with the Vaishnavas, and had studied it thoroughly in Vrindavana.
Now, in the house of Sarvabhauma, she read the “Bhagavatam” to the faithful, and people came from afar to hear her: her
wonderful explanations, full of bhakti and true to siddhanta, and her Vaishnava qualities drew a great many devotees. The
“Abhidhana” calls her simply a *kathaka*, a narrator of sacred histories. <!-- F22 -->

She maintained the service of the Lord on alms. To restore the house of Sarvabhauma she began to preach; for the needs of
the Deities' service she had to beg, and so it remained even after she received the place at the Shvetaganga. The
biographies speak of this without a trace of regret: where the Lord is truly loved and His service is felt to be one's own
dearest concern, hardships are not felt as hardships — on the contrary, the devotee rejoices at every chance to serve.
<!-- F23 I1 -->

## King Mukundadeva and the Shvetaganga
Shachi's fame grew, and the king of Puri at that time, Sri Mukundadeva, came to hear Shachi read the “Bhagavatam”. Her
reading and her original explanations of siddhanta charmed him; he became her devoted follower and longed in his heart
to present her with some gift. <!-- F24 -->

That very night Sri Jagannatha appeared to the king in a dream and commanded that Shachi be given a place by the
Shvetaganga. Next morning the overjoyed king came and related the Lord's command to her. Shachi was wholly free of
worldly interest, but she kept in mind the order of her guru — to restore a lost holy place, the house of Sarvabhauma —
and for that reason she accepted the gift[^7]. <!-- F25 F26 -->

The Shvetaganga is a sacred tank of Puri, and it has its own history, which the biographies retell from the
“Utkala-khanda” of the “Skanda Purana”. In the Treta-yuga lived King Shveta, devoted to Jagannatha as Indradyumna was,
who arranged offerings of food to the Lord. One morning, during the puja, he saw thousands of divine gifts brought by
the gods, and stood at the gate of the temple with bowed head, in doubt: would Jagannatha accept the paltry offerings of
so insignificant a man as he? And then he saw that Lakshmi Devi Herself was serving his offerings to Jagannatha, and
that Jagannatha and the Vijaya-vigrahas were accepting them with great joy. The king felt that his life had been
fulfilled, and for a long time he served the Lord with devotion. So does the Lord joyfully accept a humble offering. By
Jagannatha's gift, in the mukti-kshetra between the sea and the Akshaya-vata, before Matsya-madhava, the name of the
devoted king became famous as “Shveta-madhava”, and the tank was called the Shvetaganga. Shveta-madhava and Lord
Matsya-madhava abide on its bank, and there too are the forms of the nine planets (the navagrahas); the tank is lined
with marble steps[^8]. <!-- F27 Q4 -->

## The Ganga in Jagannatha's Temple
Once, on a krishna-trayodashi, the auspicious hour of the Mahavaruni bath arrived, and Shachi longed for a bath in the
Ganga. Many of her friends invited her to go with them. But her guru had told her not to leave Sri Kshetra, and she
could abandon neither her vow nor her service of the Deities. Remembering her guru's word, she gave up her wish.
<!-- F28 -->

What followed is told by the tradition of the matha. That same night Jagannatha said to her in a dream, “Shachi, do not
be troubled. When the day of the Varuni bath comes, take your bath in the Shvetaganga: Ganga Devi, desiring your
company, will come there.” Shachi herself had not meant to go, but Jagannatha Himself arranged her bath in the Ganga.
Gladdened by the dream, at midnight, unseen by anyone, she went to the Shvetaganga. No sooner had she entered the water
than Ganga Devi appeared: the waters of the tank rose, a current that seemed to come from nowhere ran before her all the
way to the Jagannatha Mandira and carried her inside the temple. <!-- F29 F30 -->

There she saw the Ganga before her eyes and the people of the Kshetra bathing in it, among them her friends from Puri; on
every side rang the joyful noise of bathing and prayers, and she bathed together with all. This was no miracle for
miracle's sake: the Ganga flows from the feet of the Lord, she is His servant, and she herself sought the company of a pure
devotee. <!-- F31 -->

The noise woke the gatekeepers of the temple. Hearing that someone was inside, they informed the temple overseers (the
*parichhas*), who informed the king, and the king ordered the temple opened. But inside there was no crowd and no noise
— only Shachi Devi alone. Jagannatha's servants were at first bewildered, and then concluded that she had concealed
herself in the temple to rob the Lord of His jewels and now stood exposed. Some, says one of the biographies, had their
doubts: “It cannot be — there is some mystery here.” Even so, she was at once put in prison to await trial for theft.
Her mind did not waver: in confinement she chanted the name of Krishna without cease[^9]. <!-- F32 -->

Suspicion and abuse of a great devotee did not pass without consequence for the servants: they were struck by illness and
misfortune, and the service of Jagannatha fell into disorder. So it is with an offence against a Vaishnava
(*vaishnava-aparadha*): it is not revenge or the Deity's punishment, but the consequence of the offence itself, and only the
forgiveness of the one offended can remove it. And the Lord stands up for His devotee. <!-- F33 -->

Jagannatha appeared in a dream to King Mukundadeva. He said that, conquered by her pure devotion, He Himself had made
the Ganga flow from His lotus feet and had bathed her in the streams of His *charanamrita*. According to one account,
the Lord angrily ordered her immediate release: “I Myself wished to bathe her holy feet — that is why the Ganga brought
her into My temple.” If the servants begged her forgiveness and accepted a mantra from her, their offence would vanish;
and if the king desired his own good, let the pandas and pujaris fall at her feet, and let him take initiation from her
himself. So does the Lord glorify His devotee. <!-- F34 -->

The king came to her with his courtiers, guards, parichhas and all the servants of Jagannatha. She was released; all
prostrated themselves before her and begged forgiveness, and the king implored her to pardon the offence done to the feet
of a devotee and related Jagannatha's command to accept her as guru. Understanding that all this was Jagannatha's lila,
Shachi was filled with joy and love for Krishna, laid her hand upon the king's head and blessed him. <!-- F35 -->

From then on Shachi Devi became famous as Gangamata — she whom Jagannatha bathed with the Ganga flowing from His feet — and
the place of Vasudeva Sarvabhauma came to be called the Gangamata Matha. <!-- F36 -->

## Disciples
King Mukundadeva and the servants of Jagannatha asked her for a mantra. But, obedient to Jagannatha's command, she
initiated the king alone[^10]. The king wanted to offer her land as guru-dakshina — she refused. Again and again the king
begged to be allowed to serve in some way, and then, for the sake of serving the Vaishnavas, she agreed to one thing only:
that after the midday dhupa each day two pots of mahaprasada, a pot of vegetables, one prasada cloth and two panas of
cowries — one hundred and sixty pice — be sent to the matha. By this arrangement prasada and royal bhogas are sent regularly
to the Gangamata Matha to this day. <!-- F37 F38 -->

One day Mahiratha Sharma of Dhananjayapura, a learned brahmana of the smarta school, arrived at the Shvetaganga to
perform tarpana for his forefathers. He heard how great Gangamata was and went to behold her feet. She received her
guest with honour, gave him a seat and inquired what had brought him. Artlessly the brahmana explained his purpose, and,
seeing his simplicity, she explained to him the siddhanta of the “Bhagavatam”. Enchanted, he dived, as it were, and
surfaced again and again in the nectarean flow of her speech, and he begged for shelter at her feet; when an auspicious
day came, she gave him initiation in the Radha-Krishna mantra. At her bidding he carried the name and prema of Sri
Gaura-Nityananda across the district of Ganjam[^11]. <!-- F39 -->

For the sake of preaching pure bhakti, says the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana”, she accepted other disciples as well. Those
who continued her line are named in the “Anubhashya” (see above); the service of her Deity, Sri Rasika Raya, she entrusted
to Banamali Dasa. <!-- F40 -->

## Sri Rasika Raya
To the last days of her life Gangamata served Sri Rasika Raya. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in the “Anubhashya” to CC,
Adi 8.59–60, says of her that she brought the Deity of Sri Rasika Raya from Krishna Mishra of Jaipur and began His service
in Purushottama, in the house of Sarvabhauma. The biographies tell it in their own way: Rasika Raya came to her Himself, by
Jagannatha's will, while Gangamata, poor and living on alms, at first did not dare to accept His service until He Himself
asked her to feed Him[^12]. Rasika Raya is Krishna Himself in the form of the Deity: He is “hungry”, He “has come to accept
service” not out of need but out of love for the service of His devotee. <!-- F41 F42 -->

For many years Gangamata served Him with what she received as alms. In her old age the service became hard for her, and
Rasika Raya Himself arranged for wealthy merchants to bring the provisions for Him. Fearing shortcomings, she begged His
forgiveness, and in a dream He answered her, “I am pleased with your service — serve a little longer.” Later she told Him
that she wished for one thing only: to depart with His name on her lips. And Rasika Raya answered, “Give My service into the
hands of a worthy disciple and come to My abode.” Then she entrusted His service to the gentle devotee Banamali Dasa. In
the succession of the matha given in the “Anubhashya”, Sri Vanamali is named immediately after Gangamata; in all likelihood
this is he[^13]. <!-- F43 -->

## The Gangamata Matha
Today five Divine Couples reside in the Gangamata Matha: Sri Sri Radha-Rasikaraya, Radha-Shyamasundara,
Radha-Madana-mohana, Radha-Vinoda and Radha-Ramana. Here too are Sri Damodara-shalagrama, whom Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya
served, the dancing Sri Gaura and Naru-Gopala (Laddu-Gopala). The “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” adds that in the house of
Sarvabhauma Gangamata also established the service of Sri Sri Gaura-Gadadhara. <!-- F44 -->

The matha has branches: in Puri, the Haveli Matha and the Gopala Matha, and in the district of Cuttack, at the place called
Tangi, the Sri Gopala Matha. According to the “Abhidhana”, gosvamis of her line serve Radha-Madhava at Kalikapura near
Katwa, in the district of Bardhaman; in Balagarh live gosvamis known as “Gangamata-vamshis”, descendants of her line; and in
her matha in Nilachala a washerman from Faridpur, healed of leprosy, found his service — the “Abhidhana” tells of him in its
article on Siddha Baba. The matha lived on in the time of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta as well: gathering materials in Puri for
the “Vaishnava-manjusha”, he spoke, among others, with Bihari Dasa, a pujari of the Gangamata Matha. <!-- F45 F46 F47 -->

## Departure
According to the records of the Gangamata Matha itself, Gangamata appeared on the jyeshtha-shukla-dashami of 1601 and in
1721 entered the eternal pastimes. One of the biographies adds that she left this world at the age of one hundred and
twenty, on the ashvina-shukla-ekadashi, contemplating the feet of Sri Rasika Raya and gazing on the sweetness of His
form — in all likelihood in Puri, in His service. The Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math observes the
day of her appearance — the Gaura-dashami of the month of Trivikrama (in 2026, 24 June, the same day as Dashahara, the
Ganga-puja); the day of her departure is not in the calendar[^14]. <!-- F48 -->

## Significance
Shachi, the princess of Putiya, left a palace for Krishna, took shelter of a guru and gave herself entirely to bhajana
and to her guru's will. And the Lord glorified her: Jagannatha Himself bathed her with the Ganga from His feet, the king
of Puri became her disciple, and Rasika Raya Himself came to accept her service. Her form did not diminish her position
as guru. Goda Devi, Jahnava, Gangamata, Hemalata Thakurani and others, though they appeared in a woman's body, are
*jagad-gurus*, teachers of the whole world. She gave initiation not in defiance of her times, but by the mercy of her
guru and at the direct command of Jagannatha, as a Vaishnavi guru. <!-- F49 -->

A devotee of Hari, in whatever caste, varna, family or body he may appear, is the best of men and deserves the honour of
all, and Gangamata is an example of this. The biographies recall others too: the wives of the Vedic brahmanas, who for
the sake of bhakti disregarded their husbands' prohibition and went to serve Krishna, and, in the Kali-yuga, a fallen
woman who by the mercy of Haridasa Thakura became a renowned Vaishnavi, to whom great devotees came. <!-- F50 I2 -->

    after her.
[^14]: The years of her appearance and departure are known only from the records of the matha; the hundred and twenty years
