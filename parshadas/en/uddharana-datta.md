# Uddharana Datta Thakura
*Уддхарана Датта Тхакур · @uddharana-datta*

## Subahu, a Cowherd Boy of Vraja
Uddharana Datta is the companion of Nityananda in whose house on the bank of the Triveni the Lord lived at Saptagrama,
who cooked for Him and accompanied Him to the holy places of Gauda. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him “the best of great
devotees” and “Uddharana Datta Thakura”; Vrindavana Dasa calls him “the fortunate Uddharana Datta,” “Datta-Uddharana”
and “a great, generous Vaishnava”; Narahari Chakravarti calls him “Uddharana-Thakura” and “Nityananda's beloved.” The
biographies call him Sri Uddharana Datta Thakura, Srila Uddharana Thakura and Uddharana Thakura Mahashaya, and in the
Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math he is named Srila Uddharana Dasa Thakura. <!-- F1 -->

Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Subahu, a cowherd of Vraja, is Uddharana Datta; Mahabahu, a friend in Vraja, is Sriman Mahesha Pandita.” (GGD 129)

Subahu is a cowherd boy (*gopa*) of Vraja. He is one of the twelve gopalas —
Balarama's principal companions in the service of friendship (*sakhya-rasa*), who came into Gaura's pastimes as the
associates of Nityananda.[^1] In the lists of the twelve gopalas he stands now second, now eleventh: the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” does not number them outright, and Lochana Dasa says simply: “Kala Krishnadasa and Uddharana
Datta — the twelve cowherd boys of Vraja.” <!-- F2 F3 F5 Q1 -->

The biographies explain how this came about. Krishna, taking the mood and radiance of Radha, appeared as Mahaprabhu in
Mayapur, in the house of Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi, and with Him, to make His pastimes complete, came His
associates. So too Baladeva, Krishna's first expansion, took the mood of a devotee and appeared as Nityananda in
Ekachakra (Birbhum), and with Him appeared His associates. Nityananda is Baladeva Himself, Shesha, Maha-Sankarshana,
the source of the three purusha-avataras; Uddharana is His eternal friend and servant from Vraja. The nature of the
Lord and His devotees cannot be grasped by worldly senses: it is revealed to a surrendered heart — and, as one of the
biographies says, the spiritual nature of Uddharana can be understood only by his own mercy. <!-- F4 I3 -->

## A Family of Gold Merchants
Uddharana appeared in Saptagrama (today in Hooghly district, West Bengal), not far from Trishbigha station, in the
year 1403 of the Shaka era (1481). The sources do not name the day of his appearance, nor is it found in the calendar of
the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. The biographies name his father as Sri Kara Datta (Shrikara) and his mother as
Bhadravati.[^2] The family belonged to the suvarna-vaniks, the dealers in gold; the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” speaks of it
simply as “the merchant community.” <!-- F6 F7 F8 -->

The family in which a Vaishnava appears is sanctified, the land of his birth is blessed, and his mother attains the
goal of life. So Uddharana's appearance sanctified his whole community. By the social measure of the time the
suvarna-vaniks were counted a low caste. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati writes in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” that Uddharana,
born in that milieu, became a vessel of Nityananda's mercy, and that, following his example, people born in “low”
castes were able to give up pride in their birth; other trading communities too — the Kaloaras and Bhangaris — turned
to the devotees of Hari. One of the biographies, following the “Gaudiya-bhashya,” speaks severely of the community's
former state — want of learning, want of good sense, and a heart given over to thoughts of gold — and says that
Nityananda, in the days of His manifest pastimes, delivered the whole community. What raised it was not any merit of
birth nor any change of custom, but the mercy of Nityananda, which came through His eternal servant. <!-- F11 F12 F13 -->

The same author adds with sorrow that later some of those born in this community, calling themselves devotees, set
themselves against Nityananda and turned away from Hari. This is said not to reproach anyone for his birth but as a
warning: mercy is not inherited — it is kept by faithfulness. <!-- F14 -->

Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda wished to show the world that a great devotee can appear in any family, and so some of
Their associates came in families that society ranked low — just as Haridasa Thakura appeared in a Muslim family: by the Lord's will, says Vrindavana Dasa, he was born in a low
family to show that birth and social standing are worth nothing in themselves (CB, Adi 16.237). Krishnadasa Kaviraja says:

“One of low birth is not unfit for the worship of Krishna, nor is a brahmana of good family fit for it by that alone.
Whoever worships is great; the non-devotee is low and contemptible. In the worship of Krishna there is no consideration
of caste, family and the like.” (CC, Antya 4.66–67)

And the “Padma Purana” calls it a hellish vision to see caste in a Vaishnava — as it is to see stone in the Deity, or an
ordinary man in the guru. Subahu merely took birth in a family of gold merchants; he is an eternal associate of the
Lord, beyond the modes of material nature. <!-- F15 Q6 -->

## Diwan of the Raja of Naihati
Uddharana was very wealthy. Outwardly he served as diwan — steward — to the raja of the village of Naihati, a mile and a
half north of Katwa.[^3] But even this service was only a pastime he showed the world: at heart he belonged to
Nityananda. Near Daihat (Dainhat) station the ruins of that family's palace can still be seen, and the place where
Uddharana lived while serving the raja is called Uddharanpur to this day. There he installed Deities of Nitai-Gaura;
according to tradition, They were later taken away to the capital of Banwaribad. <!-- F26 F27 F28 F29 -->

His enormous wealth he gave up for Nityananda — gave up all that worldly life can offer — and with all his senses, in
every way, he served His feet. The biographies hold him up as the very pattern of such service. Krishnadasa Kaviraja
says of him among the branches of Nityananda's tree:

“Uddharana Datta, the best of great devotees, served the feet of Nityananda in every way.” (CC, Adi 11.41)

## Nityananda at Saptagrama
When Mahaprabhu sent Nityananda to preach in Gauda, He spent some days at Khardaha and then came with His companions to
Saptagrama. This is an ancient holy place at the Triveni, where the Ganga, the Yamuna and the Sarasvati meet.
Nityananda bathed in the Triveni with great joy and stayed with Uddharana. Vrindavana Dasa relates:

“On the bank of the Triveni, in the house of the fortunate Uddharana Datta, the Lord stayed. With body, mind and words
Datta-Uddharana worshipped Nityananda's feet without guile. Uddharana obtained the right to serve Nityananda-svarupa —
how great his fortune! Birth after birth Nityananda-svarupa is his Lord; birth after birth Uddharana is His servant.”
(CB, Antya 5.449–452)

And he adds: through Uddharana the whole merchant community was purified — of this there is no doubt (CB, Antya
5.453). <!-- F16 Q2 -->

Nityananda had come to deliver that community, and He gave the merchants the right to pure love of God. Nitai Chand
Himself led kirtana in the homes of all the merchants of Saptagrama; they all took shelter at His feet and served Him
with all their hearts, and people marvelled at the love for Krishna that filled them. So the pastimes of sankirtana
went on at Saptagrama, and, in Vrindavana Dasa's words, they could not be described in a hundred years (CB, Antya
5.454–460). By Nityananda's mercy the vaniks of that day, though counted low and unlearned, attained prema-bhakti, and
even a rebellious yavana took shelter at His feet; everywhere Nityananda preached love for the holy name. According to
one of the biographies, after the chida-dadhi festival at Panihati Mahesha Pandita too followed Nityananda to Saptagrama. <!-- F17 F18 F19 -->

Narahari Chakravarti in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” describes the same visit thus. Having lived at Khardaha and wishing to
settle there, Nityananda went to Saptagrama to Uddharana and, showing him His mercy, entered his house with His
companions. The townspeople came running; in Uddharana's house and in other homes there was great jubilation, and
Nityananda sang and danced in ecstasy. For Uddharana's sake the merciful Nitai bestowed indescribable mercy on the
vaniks. Elsewhere the same book says that Nityananda made Uddharana His own; intoxicated with prema, he served the Lord
with a joyful heart and, living near Him at the Triveni, witnessed many of His pastimes (BR 12.3722–3752; 8.168–186, 199).
<!-- F24 F25 -->

Conquered by his pure love, Nityananda ate with great pleasure the rice, vegetables and other dishes that Uddharana
cooked for Him.[^4] The “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” tells how the brahmanas once gathered and half in jest asked Him how Sripada's meal
was prepared each day — did He cook Himself, or did He have a brahmana cook:

“The Lord said: ‘Sometimes I cook Myself, and when I cannot, Uddharana cooks and takes it off the fire.’”
(“Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” 1.164)

The brahmanas were astonished that Nityananda's food was cooked by a gold merchant. And the “Prema-vilasa” calls
Uddharana “the best of devotees, whose food cooked in ghee Nitai eats” (24.616). <!-- F20 F46 -->

Murari Gupta tells how Nityananda, singing kirtana, came to Saptagrama, to the bank of the Triveni, where the Ganga, the
Yamuna and the Sarasvati meet and where the seven rishis meditate on the Lord's feet. There, house after house of the
merchants, He held a great sankirtana of the name of Krishna Chaitanya, and the joy once known in Navadvipa came here
too by His mercy (Murari, 4.22.14–21). From Saptagrama Nityananda went to Shantipura to Advaita, and Uddharana was with
Him:

“Having stayed in the house of Uddharana, the Teacher of the world, immersed in the rasa of Gaurachandra, then went
with him to Shantipura.” (Murari, 4.22.22)

Nityananda came to Saptagrama once more, after His marriage. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” He came with all His
company from Baragachhi to Nadia, where Shachi welcomed Vasudha and Jahnava with boundless affection; then, with her
leave, He went by way of Shantipura to Saptagrama, and from there, at the devotees' wish, to Khardaha, where He settled
His wives (BR 12.4009–4013). One of the biographies adds that while He was staying with Vasudha and Jahnava at
Advaita's house in Shantipura, Uddharana invited Him to his home; for several days a great sankirtana festival went on
at Saptagrama, and from there Nityananda went on to Borogram.[^5]

## With Nityananda through Gauda
The vandanas glorify Uddharana as the one who went round all the holy places together with Nityananda. Jiva writes:
“I praise Uddharana Datta, who together with Nityananda wandered through all the holy places — pure of soul, seeking
nothing for himself” (“Vaishnava-vandana,” pt. 1, 134). The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says the same: together with the Lord he
went round all the holy places of Gauda and, wholly absorbed in Nityananda's qualities, served His feet without
ceasing. <!-- F43 F25 -->

Later and less reliable narratives also connect Uddharana with the arranging of Nityananda's marriage. According to the
“Advaita-prakasha,” when Nityananda went to Gauda at Mahaprabhu's command, Uddharana was with Him, “a vessel of the
Lord's mercy, who served his Lord day and night” (20.3). In Ambika Surya Dasa Pandita, struck by Nityananda's
appearance, asked who He was; Uddharana answered that He was the best of brahmanas, foremost among masters of the
shastras, by title Nyaya-chudamani, by name Nityananda (20.8–9); later Nityananda, seated on the bank of the Ganga,
laughingly spoke with Uddharana about the highest truth (20.78). The “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” relates that
Nityananda went to Ambika taking only one servant, Uddharana, and sent him into the house of Surya Dasa (1.88–90). And
in the “Prema-vilasa” Uddharana, coming to Nityananda at Dogachhia, in the house of Hoda Krishnadasa, reports that he
proposed the marriage of Surya Dasa Sarakhela's two daughters to Him and that Surya Dasa flared up in anger
(24.615–620). <!-- F45 -->

One more tradition has come down to us from Uddharana's own lips: in the “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” Virachandra retells
the account he heard from him of how Nityananda awakened Shridama — Abhirama (10.55). <!-- F47 -->

Uddharana was also present at the chida-dadhi festival at Panihati, where Raghunatha Dasa fed Nityananda and His
associates. Having listed those who sat with Nityananda, Krishnadasa Kaviraja concludes:

“Uddharana Datta and all His other intimate associates sat on the platform. Who could count them?” (CC, Antya 6.63)
<!-- F42 -->

Vrindavana Dasa, listing the servants of Nityananda, says of him:

“Uddharana Datta is a great Vaishnava, generous; to him belongs the right to serve Nityananda.” (CB, Antya 5.743)[^6]

## Departure
Uddharana Datta disappeared — left this world — in the year 1463 of the Shaka era (1541/42), on the thirteenth lunar
day of the dark fortnight of the month of Pausha (krishna-trayodashi).[^7] In the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat
Math this is the krishna-trayodashi of the month of Narayana; it next falls on 5 January 2027, and on the same day the
departure of Srila Mahesha Pandita is honoured. His samadhi is to the west of the temple at Uddharanpur, or, according to
some, in Vrindavana; so Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, too, writes. <!-- F31 F30 -->

Bereft of him, Saptagrama was orphaned. Narahari Chakravarti relates that when Narottama, on his journey through Gauda,
entered Saptagrama full of love and asked where Uddharana's house was, someone answered in tears: “There it is. Burning
in separation from the Lord, he disappeared not long ago, and without him Saptagrama has sunk into darkness” (BR
8.200–205). The townspeople, whom Nityananda had once blessed with His mercy and who were all great devotees, felt as
though they had lost their sight. Narottama came to his house, found the devotees in the anguish of separation from
their guru, bowed down to them and went on to Khardaha. <!-- F33 -->

Jahnava Devi, too, visited his shripata. Returning from her pilgrimage, she stopped at Uddharana's house; the vaniks of
Saptagrama, young and old, came out to meet her, and she, remembering his life, could not contain her feelings. For
one night a sankirtana festival went on there, and then she returned by boat to Khardaha. “Not everything can be
told,” Narahari Chakravarti concludes, “in the accounts of Nityananda's beloved” (BR 11.773–779).[^8] <!-- F32 -->

Uddharana's son was named Shrinivasa Datta Thakura — according to one of the biographies, his only son. When
Virachandra set out for Vrindavana at his mother's command, the first place he visited was Uddharana's house in
Saptagrama; Shrinivasa Datta received him with honour, and after staying there two days Virachandra went on to
Shantipura. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” also says that Virachandra spent two days at Saptagrama in the house of “a supremely
virtuous vanik,” absorbed in kirtana, gave the jewel of bhakti to the fallen and the afflicted, and went on to
Shantipura (BR 13.283–287). Descendants of the family still live in Hooghly, in Calcutta and in many other places. One of the
biographies calls those born in this family undoubtedly fortunate and prays that, leaving aside a merely outward,
family tie with Uddharana, they may gain a spiritual tie with him. <!-- F9 F34 F51 F10 -->

## The Shripata at Saptagrama
Saptagrama stands on the river Sarasvati; in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries it was the chief trading town of
Bengal on the Ganga, and Triveni grew out of it. Formerly the name Saptagrama covered seven settlements together —
Vasudevpur, Banshberia, Krishnapur, Nityanandapur, Shivpur, Shankhanagar and Saptagrama itself.[^9] <!-- F35 -->

In Uddharana's shripata is the six-armed (*shadbhuja*) form of Mahaprabhu, whom he installed and served with his own
hands. To His right is Nityananda, to His left Gadadhara; on another throne are Radha-Govinda and a shalagrama. Beneath
the base of the throne an image (*alekhya*) of Uddharana himself is worshipped. Before the present temple stands a large
natmandir; on the stone tablets of its pillars are carved the names of those who built the temple and renewed the
shripata. In front of the natmandir is a cool, shady madhavi bower, and on either side of it stand two pillars: on one
Uddharana is depicted, on the stone tablet of the other are the four taraka-brahma-namas, the holy names of the four
yugas. <!-- F36 F37 -->

The shripata has known neglect as well. Nitai Dasa, a babaji who lived on alms, obtained twelve bighas of land for it in
the Bengali year 1283 (1876);[^10] afterwards, through the care of some devotees, the service went on but fell into
disorder. In 1306 (1899), by the joint efforts of Balarama Mallik, a former sub-judge of Hooghly, and many well-to-do
suvarna-vaniks of Calcutta, order was partly restored. More than half a century ago the wooden shri-murti of Uddharana
stood in a dilapidated hut; today it is no longer in the shripata — his image is worshipped instead. The former murti is
kept in the house of Sri Madana Datta in Bali (Hooghly), and the shalagrama whom the Thakura served is in the same
village, in the house of Shrinatha Datta.[^11] One of the biographies calls for the buildings of the shripata to be
renewed, so that people may come there again: learning of Uddharana's glory and seeking his mercy, they will attain the
highest good. <!-- F38 F39 F40 -->

Uddharana's temple is honoured by the whole suvarna-vanik community. As a child Srila A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami
Prabhupada visited it with his parents, and in 1967 he came here at the invitation of the temple's custodians together
with his American disciples. <!-- F41 -->

## Deliverer of the Most Fallen
Nityananda, playing the part of a devotee, is by nature the Lord Himself, and His associates are embodiments of His
merciful potency. He is the deliverer of the fallen, and His intimate associates are deliverers of the most fallen of
the fallen (*parama-parama-patita-pavana*): Nityananda gives His mercy through His devotees. Krishnadasa Kaviraja says
of Him:

“Intoxicated with love, Nityananda is the avatara of mercy; He makes no distinction between the high and the low. He
delivers whoever falls before Him; and so He delivered even a sinner like me.” (CC, Adi 5.208–209)

Therefore whoever takes shelter of Uddharana will easily be freed from the round of births and will attain service at
the feet of Nityananda and Gauranga; for sinners and offenders there is no other way than Nityananda's mercy.
<!-- I1 Q5 -->

One of the biographies leads up to this as follows: even the shadow of the holy name (*namabhasa*) destroys sins, but
offences rob it of its fruit; Chaitanya and Nityananda take no account of offences, and one who takes shelter of Them in
faith is quickly cleansed of them (CC, Adi 8.24, 8.31, as explained by Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura). Whoever worships
Uddharana, the dearest associate of Nityananda, serves him and pleases him, quickly obtains Nityananda's mercy, the
right to love for Krishna and the fulfilment of life. So too Krishnadasa Kaviraja received Nityananda's mercy for taking
the side of His servant Minaketana Ramadasa. Whoever tries to serve a devotee is given by that devotee the strength to
serve. <!-- I4 I2 I5 -->
