# Uddharana Datta Thakura
*Уддхарана Датта Тхакур · @uddharana-datta*

## Subahu, a Cowherd Boy of Vraja
Uddharana Datta is the companion of Nityananda in whose house on the bank of the Triveni the Lord lived at Saptagrama,
who cooked for Him and accompanied Him to the holy places of Gauda. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him “the best of great
devotees” and “Uddharana Datta Thakura”; Vrindavana Dasa calls him “the fortunate Uddharana Datta,” “Datta-Uddharana”
and “a great, generous Vaishnava”; Narahari Chakravarti calls him “Uddharana-Thakura” and “Nityananda's beloved.” The
biographies call him Sri Uddharana Datta Thakura, Srila Uddharana Thakura and Uddharana Thakura Mahashaya, and in the
Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math he is named Srila Uddharana Dasa Thakura. <!-- F1 -->

Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Subahu, a cowherd of Vraja, is Uddharana Datta; Mahabahu, a friend in Vraja, is Sriman Mahesha Pandita.” (GGD 129)

Subahu is a cowherd boy (*gopa*) of Vraja, a friend of Krishna and Balarama. He is one of the twelve gopalas —
Balarama's principal companions in the service of friendship (*sakhya-rasa*), who came into Gaura's pastimes as the
associates of Nityananda.[^1] In the lists of the twelve gopalas he stands now second, now eleventh: the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” does not number them outright, and Lochana Dasa says simply: “Kala Krishnadasa and Uddharana
Datta — the twelve cowherd boys of Vraja.” <!-- F2 F3 F5 Q1 -->

The biographies explain how this came about. Krishna, taking the mood and radiance of Radha, appeared as Mahaprabhu in
Mayapur, in the house of Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi, and with Him, to make His pastimes complete, came His
associates. So too Baladeva, Krishna's first expansion, took the mood of a devotee and appeared as Nityananda in
Ekachakra (Birbhum), and with Him appeared His associates. Nityananda is Baladeva Himself, Shesha, Maha-Sankarshana,
the source of the three purusha-avataras; Uddharana is His eternal friend and servant from Vraja. The nature of the
Lord and His devotees cannot be grasped by worldly senses: it is revealed to a surrendered heart — and, as one of the
biographies says, the spiritual nature of Uddharana can be understood only by his own mercy. <!-- F4 I3 -->

## A Family of Gold Merchants
Uddharana appeared in Saptagrama (today in Hooghly district, West Bengal), not far from Trishbigha station, in the
year 1403 of the Shaka era (1481). The sources do not name the day of his appearance, nor is it found in the calendar of
the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. The biographies name his father as Sri Kara Datta (Shrikara) and his mother as
Bhadravati.[^2] The family belonged to the suvarna-vaniks, the dealers in gold; the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” speaks of it
simply as “the merchant community.” <!-- F6 F7 F8 -->

The family in which a Vaishnava appears is sanctified, the land of his birth is blessed, and his mother attains the
goal of life. So Uddharana's appearance sanctified his whole community. By the social measure of the time the
suvarna-vaniks were counted a low caste. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati writes in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” that Uddharana,
born in that milieu, became a vessel of Nityananda's mercy, and that, following his example, people born in “low”
castes were able to give up pride in their birth; other trading communities too — the Kaloaras and Bhangaris — turned
to the devotees of Hari. One of the biographies, following the “Gaudiya-bhashya,” speaks severely of the community's
former state — want of learning, want of judgement, and a heart given over to thoughts of gold — and says that
Nityananda, in the days of His manifest pastimes, delivered the whole community. What raised it was not any merit of
birth nor any change of custom, but the mercy of Nityananda, which came through His eternal servant. <!-- F11 F12 F13 -->

The same author adds with sorrow that later some of those born in this community, calling themselves devotees, set
themselves against Nityananda and turned away from Hari. This is said not to reproach anyone for his birth but as a
warning: mercy is not inherited — it is kept by faithfulness. <!-- F14 -->

Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda wished to show the world that a great devotee can appear in any family, and so some of
Their associates came in families that society ranked low — just as Haridasa Thakura appeared in a Muslim family.
Krishnadasa Kaviraja says:

“One of low birth is not unfit for the worship of Krishna, nor is a brahmana of good family fit for it by that alone.
Whoever worships is great; the non-devotee is low and contemptible. In the worship of Krishna there is no consideration
of caste, family and the like.” (CC, Antya 4.66–67)

And the “Padma Purana” calls it a hellish vision to see caste in a Vaishnava — as it is to see stone in the Deity, or an
ordinary man in the guru. Subahu merely took birth in a family of gold merchants; he is an eternal associate of the
Lord, beyond the modes of material nature. <!-- F15 Q6 -->

## Diwan of the Raja of Naihati
Uddharana was very wealthy. Outwardly he served as diwan — steward — to the raja of the village of Naihati, a mile and a
half north of Katwa.[^3] But even this service was only a pastime he showed the world: at heart he belonged to
Nityananda. Near Daihat (Dainhat) station the ruins of that family's palace can still be seen, and the place where
Uddharana lived while serving the raja is called Uddharanpur to this day. There he installed Deities of Nitai-Gaura;
according to tradition, They were later taken away to the capital of Banwaribad. <!-- F26 F27 F28 F29 -->

His enormous wealth he gave up for Nityananda — gave up all that worldly life can offer — and with all his senses, in
every way, he served His feet. The biographies hold him up as the very pattern of such service. Krishnadasa Kaviraja
says of him among the branches of Nityananda's tree:

“Uddharana Datta, the best of great devotees, served the feet of Nityananda in every way.” (CC, Adi 11.41)

## Nityananda at Saptagrama
When Mahaprabhu sent Nityananda to preach in Gauda, He spent some days at Khardaha and then came with His companions to
Saptagrama. This is an ancient holy place at the Triveni, where the Ganga, the Yamuna and the Sarasvati meet.
Nityananda bathed in the Triveni with great joy and stayed with Uddharana. Vrindavana Dasa relates:

“On the bank of the Triveni, in the house of the fortunate Uddharana Datta, the Lord stayed. With body, mind and words
Datta-Uddharana worshipped Nityananda's feet without guile. Uddharana obtained the right to serve Nityananda-svarupa —
how great his fortune! Birth after birth Nityananda-svarupa is his Lord; birth after birth Uddharana is His servant.”
(CB, Antya 5.449–452)

And he adds: through Uddharana the whole merchant community was purified — of this there is no doubt (CB, Antya
5.453). <!-- F16 Q2 -->

Nityananda had come to deliver that community, and He gave the merchants the right to pure love of God. Nitai Chand
Himself led kirtana in the homes of all the merchants of Saptagrama; they all took shelter at His feet and served Him
with all their hearts, and people marvelled at the love for Krishna that filled them. So the pastimes of sankirtana
went on at Saptagrama, and, in Vrindavana Dasa's words, they could not be described in a hundred years (CB, Antya
5.454–460). By Nityananda's mercy the vaniks of that day, though counted low and unlearned, attained prema-bhakti, and
even a rebellious yavana took shelter at His feet; everywhere Nityananda preached love for the holy name. After Him
Mahesha Pandita too came to Saptagrama, following the chida-dadhi festival at Panihati. <!-- F17 F18 F19 -->

Narahari Chakravarti in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” describes the same visit thus. Having lived at Khardaha and wishing to
settle there, Nityananda went to Saptagrama to Uddharana and, showing him His mercy, entered his house with His
companions. The townspeople came running; in Uddharana's house and in other homes there was great jubilation, and
Nityananda sang and danced in ecstasy. For Uddharana's sake the merciful Nitai bestowed indescribable mercy on the
vaniks. Elsewhere the same book says that Nityananda made Uddharana His own; intoxicated with prema, he served the Lord
with a joyful heart and, living near Him at the Triveni, witnessed many of His pastimes (BR 8.168–186; 12.3722–3752).
<!-- F24 F25 -->

Conquered by his pure love, Nityananda ate with great pleasure the rice, vegetables and other dishes that Uddharana
cooked for Him.[^4] The “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” tells how the brahmanas once gathered and half in jest asked Him:

“‘How is Sripada's meal prepared each day? Do you cook yourself, or is there a brahmana?’ The Lord said: ‘Sometimes I
cook myself, and when I cannot, Uddharana cooks and takes it off the fire.’” (“Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” 1.163–164)

The brahmanas were astonished that Nityananda's food was cooked by a gold merchant. And the “Prema-vilasa” calls
Uddharana “the best of devotees, whose food cooked in ghee Nitai eats” (24.616). <!-- F20 F46 -->

From Saptagrama Nityananda went to Shantipura to Advaita, and Uddharana was with Him. Murari Gupta writes:

“Having stayed in the house of Uddharana, the Teacher of the world, immersed in the rasa of Gaurachandra, then went
with him to Shantipura.” (Murari, 4.22.22)

Nityananda came to Saptagrama once more, after His marriage. When He was staying with Vasudha and Jahnava at Advaita's
house in Shantipura, Uddharana invited Him to his home; for several days a great sankirtana festival went on at
Saptagrama, and from there Nityananda went on to Borogram.[^5] <!-- F44 F22 F23 -->
