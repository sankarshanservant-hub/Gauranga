# Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Goswami Maharaj
*Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Goswami Maharaj · @bhakti-dayita-madhava*

## A Companion of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Goswami Maharaj was one of the closest companions of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and
the founder of the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math, whose temples and ashrams spread throughout India. His disciples
count him the tenth in the line of succession descending from Sri Krishna Chaitanya. His worldly name was Heramba
Kumar Bandyopadhyaya; at initiation he became Hayagriva Dasa Brahmachari, and on accepting sannyasa,
Parivrajakacharya Tridandi Swami Srimad Bhakti Dayita Madhava Goswami Maharaj. <!-- F1 F3 -->

One of his biographies was written by his disciple and successor, Srila Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha Maharaj, who
dedicated his book to his “beloved gurudeva” and to his shiksha-guru, Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Maharaj, and first
printed its articles in the journal “Sri Chaitanya Vani,” which his guru had founded. The biography opens with
Sanskrit pranama-mantras. They glorify Srila Madhava Maharaj as dear to the followers of Sri Rupa, as
magnanimity itself; as one who, out of love for his guru and godbrothers, revealed the glory of Ishodyan in Mayapur
and restored to the world Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's birthplace in Puri; as the best of the teachers of the Holy Name
and a teacher of the whole world. <!-- F4 Q10 F101 -->

## Appearance and Family
He appeared in 1904, at eight in the morning, on the Utthana Ekadashi of the month of Damodara, in the village of
Kanchanpara in Faridpur district, East Bengal.[^1] The same lunar day also marks the departure of Srila Gaurakishora
Dasa Babaji, the guru of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta. Utthana Ekadashi is the day on which Lord Vishnu rises from His
sleep, and his biographer sees a sign in the coincidence: one had come into the world who would awaken suffering
souls. <!-- F5 F6 I1 -->

Kanchanpara lies right at the mouth of the Padma, which is called the Kirtinasha there. Upstream is Prema-tali, where
Nityananda Prabhu bathed in the river and left prema in its waters for Narottama. Kanchanpara was the home not of his
father's family but of his mother's: here lived her brothers, the wealthy Bandyopadhyaya talukdars, who had received
from the British the title “Raja-chakravarti,” so that their estate was known as the Chakravarti-bari. Srila Madhava
Maharaj's disciples still lovingly call it the home of Gurudeva's uncles. <!-- F7 F8 -->

His father's family lived in the village of Bharakar in Bikrampur, Dhaka district. His grandfather, Chandra Prasad
Devasharma Bandyopadhyaya, and his father, Nishikanta, were known as pious men; his mother, Shaibalini Devi, was
devoted to the service of the Lord, the brahmanas and the sadhus. His father named the boy Heramba Kumar, but at home
he was called Ganesh. He was only four when Nishikanta died; his mother returned to her brothers, and the orphan grew
up in Kanchanpara surrounded by his uncles' affection. <!-- F2 F9 F10 F11 -->

## Childhood and Schooling
From early childhood he never told a lie, and he taught his friends to be truthful. Worldly amusements held no
attraction for him; he loved order and discipline and was always quick to help others, even at his own cost. His
mother recalled that whenever he was given a sweet, he would eat it only after sharing it with his friends. Once at
school he fell while running and was badly hurt, yet it was he who comforted his frightened teachers: whatever the
Lord does is for the best, and for past sins it might have been worse. <!-- F12 F13 F14 -->

In high school he set up a free library for his poorer classmates. Tall and strong, he was a leader among his peers,
captain of the school teams and an actor in its plays; nor did he stand aside from the independence movement, and he
read Gandhi's paper “Young India.” But it was his mother who shaped what was deepest in him: she read the scriptures
with him, and at her wish he read the “Bhagavad-gita” every day, so that by the age of eleven he knew all seven
hundred of its verses by heart. He studied in Kanchanpara and Bhatagram, and went to Calcutta for higher education.

## The Search for a Guru
In Calcutta he lived with a relative and childhood friend, Narayan Chandra Mukhopadhyaya — the same who many years
later would take sannyasa from him and become Bhakti Prasun Bodhayan Maharaj. Narayan Chandra saw his friend weeping
at night as he called out to the Lord; the young man ate once a day, and only havishyanna. Once, in a dream, Narada
Muni gave him a mantra and promised that it would bring him to perfection; but on waking he could not recall it in
full, and he grieved for a long time. <!-- F18 F19 -->

Resolving to leave home, he went for the blessing of his widowed mother, who was then living in Gosain-Durgapur in
Nadia, and with her blessing set out for the Himalayas. Through Hardwar he climbed into the mountains and for three
days, without food or water, called upon the Lord. The answer came from above: the guru was not to be sought here —
he would appear where you came from. A sadhu in Hardwar told him the same: go back; at home you will meet your true
guru. <!-- F20 F21 F22 -->

In Hardwar, by the Brahma-kunda, a wealthy childless merchant couple from North India noticed him. They began urging
him to become their adopted son and heir. But he saw in this only a snare of maya, refused, and left for Calcutta.
This was around 1925. So, his biographer observes, a heart drawn to the Lord as iron to a magnet can be held back
neither by family nor by wealth. <!-- F23 I2 -->

## At the Feet of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
That same year he came to Mayapur for the first time with Narayan Chandra and some friends, and saw Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta. At a lecture he heard that one must learn to see the Deity — not with eyes blinded by desires, but
with eyes of love. That day he witnessed the initiation of Dr. S. N. Ghosh and his wife. Back in Calcutta he began
going to the Gaudiya Math on Ultadanga Road to hear hari-katha, studied the scriptures and, without telling anyone,
gave the math a good deal of money and goods. <!-- F24 F25 -->

In 1927, on the day of Radhashtami, at the Ultadanga Math, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta gave him harinama and mantra and
named him Hayagriva Dasa Brahmachari; the fire sacrifice was performed by Acharya Dasa Deva Sharma. Soon Hayagriva
moved into the math and took the vow of lifelong celibacy, naishthika-brahmacharya. He quickly became one of the
leading disciples, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta called his zeal “volcanic energy.” He was among the chief collectors of
the donations on which the whole mission depended, including its preaching in the West. He was so close to his guru,
and even looked so much like him — tall and fair — that he was often taken for Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's son.
<!-- F26 F27 F28 F37 -->

## Madras and the South
At his guru's wish he lived for many years — some ten — at the Madras Gaudiya Math. He was responsible for buying the
land and building the temple, the natamandira and the living quarters; the temple rose through the labours of Srila
Bhakti Rakshak Sridhar Maharaj, Srila Bhakti Hridaya Bon Maharaj, himself and others, and his senior godbrothers
always encouraged him. The land was given by Sadashiva Iyer, whose son Ramachandra Iyer advised him to learn Tamil.
After three days of lessons a telegram came from Puri: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta said that love for Krishna is not
conveyed by language — preach in the languages you know. In Madras he became close to many prominent people.
<!-- F29 F80 -->

He was also one of the chief builders of the Ramananda Gaudiya Math, raised in memory of Ramananda Raya: in Andhra,
at Goshpada-tirtha, where Ramananda met Mahaprabhu, he bought the land and supervised the construction. He took part
in all his guru's undertakings — festivals, parikramas of Vraja and Navadvipa, the opening of maths and pada-pithas,
publishing — and was usually sent ahead to prepare the ground. He began nothing without Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's
direct instruction; and for this complete surrender, his biographer says, he inherited his guru's power to preach.
His fidelity to his guru remains an example to this day. <!-- F31 F30 F32 I3 -->

## Sarbhog
In March 1936 the Deities of Sri Sri Guru-Gauranga-Gandharvika-Giridhari were to be installed at the Sarbhog Gaudiya
Math in Assam, near Barpeta — one of the sixty-four maths of that time. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sent him ahead with
Srila Sridhar Maharaj, Janaki-Vallabha Dasa Brahmachari and others; the math was in the charge of Bhakti Vijnan
Ashram Maharaj. A few days before the festival nothing was ready, and Hayagriva hurriedly arranged lodgings for the
guests. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta himself arrived at Sarbhog station on the morning of 15 March with Kunja Bihari
Vidyabhushan, Paramananda Vidyaratna, Krishna Keshava Brahmachari and other companions; for three days thousands of
people received mahaprasad. <!-- F33 F34 -->

At his guru's instruction Srila Sridhar Maharaj led the installation ceremony; he had dressed and adorned the Deities
beforehand. Seeing Them, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta said, “The Deities are already installed,” and Sridhar Maharaj was
saddened, thinking he had committed an offence. He asked Hayagriva to write to their guru begging forgiveness on his
behalf. The reply came: one who is surrendered commits no offence — the Lord does not see his faults. Srila Madhava
Maharaj would often tell this story to his disciples as a lesson in surrender. <!-- F35 F38 -->

At the same time, pressed by Ashram Maharaj, he complained to his guru about a brahmachari named Nimananda. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta was at first angry, then praised him kindly and instructed him: you alone are answerable for the
service of the guru, and you should be grateful to anyone who helps — just as Sri Radhika, the “manager” of service
to Krishna, is grateful to every helper. <!-- F36 I4 -->

## Errands for His Guru
In 1930, during the month-long Janmashtami festival at the Bagbazar Gaudiya Math, the students of the celebrated
scientist C. V. Raman asked that he be invited to preside over an evening meeting, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta gave the
task to Hayagriva. They spoke in English, in the laboratory on Circular Road. Raman demanded, “Show me God.”
Hayagriva answered with an example from Raman's own life: to understand his science one must study under him; so
too the truth reveals itself only to one who goes through the process. Raman had no reply, but he declined the
invitation. <!-- F42 F43 -->

He and two of his godbrothers were chosen to preach in England; photographs had been taken and passports made. But
the rajarshi Sharadindu Narayan Ray advised against sending young and handsome men overseas, and Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta sent Bhakti Pradip Tirtha in his place, entrusting him instead with raising funds. He was only glad:
what he feared most was a long separation from his guru. <!-- F39 -->

In 1936 his guru sent him to Pandit Panchanan Tarkaratna of Bhatpara, who had been attacking daiva-varnashrama. He was
told to go without tilaka or Vaishnava dress, as an ordinary brahmana. In the morning he arrived with Praphulla
Chandra Chattopadhyaya of Kanthalpara, found the pandit's son Jiva Nyayatirtha there, and for two hours discussed the
scriptures. His conclusion was this: the pandit's learning is immense, but without the company of a pure devotee no
right conclusions are possible. A record of the conversation appeared as the leading article in the Bengali weekly
“Gaudiya.” His resourcefulness in such debates, his biographer explains, was the fruit not of book learning but of
his guru's grace. <!-- F40 F41 I5 -->

## After His Guru's Departure
Departing in 1937, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta charged his disciples to preach the message of Sri Rupa and Raghunatha
together. The united mission soon broke apart; his biographer sees the will of the Lord even in this: capable
disciples, acharyas in their own right, came to preach each on his own, and to revile some while extolling others is
an offence against Vaishnavas. In those years he himself went from house to house seeking out disciples who had gone
back to family life and brought them back to the math; some of them later became acharyas. He helped any godbrother
in trouble — for love of one's godbrothers is the measure of love for one's guru, as love for the devotees is the
measure of love for Krishna. <!-- F44 F52 I6 -->

In 1942, together with his godbrothers Srila Bhakti Kumud Santa Maharaj and Srila Bhakti Bichar Jajabar Maharaj, he
founded a math in Medinipur and named it after Shyamananda, the first preacher of those parts — the Shyamananda
Gaudiya Math. Local men of means helped buy land and a two-storey house in the Shiva-bazar quarter. Among the people
of Medinipur was a miser, Govardhan Piri, whom the neighbours advised not even to visit. He would not give the math
his family Deities of Radha and Krishna, but he paid for new Deities, ordered from Jaipur, and for Their
installation, dress, service and the festival feast; he began to hear hari-katha, gave up his sinful habits and
accepted initiation, and his wife thanked Gurudeva for giving her husband back to her. A sadhu does not fear being
insulted, Srila Madhava Maharaj used to say; the fruit of preaching is that the wicked become good. <!-- F45 F46 -->

Later he also told his disciples of meeting Vamshidasa Babaji in Medinipur. The Babaji was passing through with
pilgrims and promised to visit the Shyamananda Gaudiya Math, but stopped under a pipal tree, taking it for the
Vamshi-vata. So Gurudeva went to him himself, and the Babaji, out of love for Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, treated him to
sweet rice — “very tasty.” <!-- F97 -->

## Sannyasa
Before his sannyasa he preached in the districts of Bankura and Medinipur — at Kether-Danga, Onda, Jhanti-Pahari,
Bankura and Gardevata; with him were Krishna Keshava Brahmachari, Rama Govinda Brahmachari, Kunjalal, Hari Govinda and
others, and they were helped by Radha Govinda Sit of Kether-Danga and Avinash Pal of Onda. <!-- F47 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta had meant to give him sannyasa but did not: the mission needed his gift for collecting too
much. After their guru's departure his godbrothers — Kunja Bihari Vidyabhushan, Bhakti Prakash Aranya, Bhakti
Sarvasva Giri, Bhakti Svarupa Parvat, Bhakti Prasun Bodhayan Maharajas, Krishna Keshava and Sundar Gopal
Brahmacharis — persuaded him to accept sannyasa so as to fulfil their guru's wish. In 1944, on Phalguna Purnima — the
day of Mahaprabhu's appearance — on the grounds of the Tota-Gopinatha temple in Puri, he accepted sannyasa from Srila
Bhakti Gaurava Vaikhanasa Maharaj; he was about forty years old.[^2] Present were Kunja Bihari Vidyabhushan,
Paramananda Vidyaratna, Parvat Maharaj and Bodhayan Maharaj. From then on he was called Bhakti Dayita Madhava
Goswami. When he returned to the Shyamananda Gaudiya Math, the Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha received him with honour
and presented him with an address praising his fearlessness, the power of his words and his love for the devotees.
<!-- F48 F49 F50 F51 -->

## East Pakistan
After sannyasa, before and after 1947, he travelled long on preaching tours through India and East Pakistan. With him
went Mihir, Sankarshan, Krishna Keshava Brahmachari, Trailokya, Mahendra, Sri Brahma, Pyari Mohan Brahmachari,
Yajneshvar Dasa Babaji and others; in the districts of Dhaka and Mymensingh they visited Baliati, Nawabganj, Kalakopa,
Jamuki, Pakulla and Churain. <!-- F53 -->

At Jamuki-Pakulla he lectured in a pandal by the school before a thousand Hindus and Muslims. The police warned him
that a word against Pakistan could land him in prison. Wary of impure prison food, he asked that questions be held
until the end. Still a mullah asked him about “idol worship.” Srila Madhava Maharaj replied that God is all-powerful
and can appear in any form. An idol is a product of the mind and of hands working with matter; the sri-vigraha is
the form the Lord Himself accepts out of love for His devotee. It is wholly spiritual and is seen only by the eyes of
bhakti. <!-- F54 -->

## Assam
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta had once told him directly to preach in Assam: the Assamese are simple-hearted and trust sadhus.
And so he went there — to Sarbhog, with Bhuvana, Uddharan and Krishna Keshava Brahmachari.[^3] Because of the
troubles within the Gaudiya Math he stayed not at the math but in the house of Krishna Keshava's brother. There was a
flood; they lived on a bamboo platform, and Krishna Keshava's mother cooked for the Vaishnavas. The war with Japan was
on; the house was requisitioned for troops, and Maharaj moved to a village near Sarbhog, where he preached for seven
days, and afterwards stayed with Gopal Prabhu in Sarbhog itself. <!-- F55 -->

In Sarbhog he spoke on the “Bhagavata” daily in the house of the schoolteacher Chinta Haran Patgiri, and his first
disciples there were Gopal Dasa Adhikari and his wife, Shivananda Dasa Adhikari, Khagen Dasa Adhikari and Achyutananda
Dasa Adhikari. A brahmana youth, Kamala Kanta Goswami, followed him to Bhavanipur-Tapu; his father, Ghana Kanta, took
him home, believing he had lost caste by eating the Vaishnavas' food, but the young man came back, though his
initiation had to be postponed because of his family. The Marwari merchants of Tapu served the Vaishnavas, and
Shivananda gave his son Lokesh to the service. Harinama was received in the house of Tularama Babu in Tapu by Lohita
and Lokesh, then by Kamala Kanta, Ram Prasad and Bhavananda; and full initiation in Medinipur was accepted by Lalita
Charan, Lokanath and Krishna Prasad Brahmachari — later the sannyasis Bhakti Lalita Giri, Bhakti Suhrid Damodar and
Bhakti Prasad Ashram Maharajas. <!-- F56 F57 -->

In Gauhati, thanks to Krishna Keshava and Chinta Haran, he met prominent people — the Chief Minister of Assam,
Gopinath Bardoloi, Durgeshvar Sharma, Kumudeshvar Goswami and others — and spoke on the “Bhagavata” in the Chief
Minister's house. Bardoloi likened his preaching to Gandhi's “Ram-dhun.” Maharaj reminded him that Gandhi had written
in “Young India” that he was ready to sacrifice the Ram-dhun for the country — “but we are ready to sacrifice the
country for the Ram-dhun.” The Absolute is a Person, and everything exists for Him. Bardoloi wished to leave
politics and live in the math, but his colleagues would not let him go, and soon afterwards he died. <!-- F58 F59 -->

He preached also at Bijani, Bhatipara, Hauli and Barpeta. At Hauli, before a gathering of many thousands, a maulvi
asked whether anyone had ever seen the soul or God. Maharaj took up a book in Urdu: one who does not know the
language cannot read even its title — but does it follow that it has none? There are two kinds of sight, the sensory
and the Vedic, veda-drik; the truth reveals itself to one who surrenders. There Rameshvar Varman took shelter, becoming
Rameshvar Dasa Adhikari. In 1945 he walked from Hauli to Barpeta with Krishna Keshava, Gopal Krishna Dasa Adhikari,
Trailokyanath, Madhavananda Vrajabasi and Bhuvana Mohan Dasa Adhikari. In Barpeta he stayed with Amiya Kanti Dasa and
Hare Krishna Dasa, who received initiation as Aghadaman Dasa and Hari Dasa. He was honoured too by the satradhikari of
Shankaradeva's school, Narayan Deva Mishra, who served as priest at a large gathering in Barpeta and invited
Maharaj to his own seat. <!-- F64 F66 -->

Jiveshvar Goswami of Tihun had grown cold towards the Gaudiya Math after the harsh preaching of a certain
householder; having heard Srila Madhava Maharaj, he admitted that he said exactly the same, but in a way that did
not wound. <!-- F67 -->

In 1947 he came again to Assam, to Goalpara, at the invitation of Radha Mohan Dasa Adhikari — a householder, a
disciple of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta and a former brahmachari, known in Goalpara as “Ram Mohan-da.” With him were
Krishna Keshava, Uddharan Brahmachari, Madhavananda Vrajabasi and Ratharudha Dasa Brahmachari. The Harisabha meeting
was chaired by the advocate Khiroda Sen. <!-- F60 -->

In Radha Mohan's house he was first seen by Kamakhya Charan Guha-Ray, son of Dhirendra Guha-Ray, who had come with his
friend Devavrata; later he became Krishna Vallabha Brahmachari, and after sannyasa, Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha Maharaj.
As he bowed at Maharaj's feet, the young man felt his blessing so strongly that his hair stood on end. He asked:
would he lose his family if he chanted the Name? Maharaj answered with the parable of the geese and the swan from
Lake Manasa-sarovara: one attached to what is low cannot fly to the Lord. And he cited the “Bhagavata”: “The wise
will leave bad company and cling to the saints, whose words cut the knots of the mind's attachments” (SB 11.26.26).
<!-- F61 Q1 -->

Kamakhya's vartma-pradarshaka-guru — the one who first showed him the path — was Radha Mohan, and it was to his
address that Gurudeva's letters came. Maharaj advised the young man to read Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura's
“Jaiva-dharma” and wrote that on the path of renunciation, nivritti, government service is not needed, while on the
path of pravritti one may take it. Kamakhya resolved to leave home. That same year, 1947, he completed his master's
degree in philosophy at Calcutta University and from then on gave his life to the service of his guru; in time he
became secretary of the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math, one of Maharaj's first sannyasi disciples (taking sannyasa in
1961), and after his departure his successor-acharya. <!-- F62 F100 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta had once visited Goalpara and told the householder Nimananda to found a math on Hulukanda Hill
by the Brahmaputra — the Prapannashram; it was later lost. When Sharat Kumar Nath offered a house and land, Srila
Madhava Maharaj saw in this the fulfilment of his guru's wish and in 1969 opened a branch of the Sri Chaitanya
Gaudiya Math there. <!-- F63 -->

Almost every year he returned to Assam with disciples and godbrothers, travelling on foot and by bullock cart, and
hundreds became his disciples. He preached in the districts of Goalpara (Dhubri, Basugaon, Bilasipara), Kamrup and
Barpeta (Gauhati, Sarbhog, Chakchaka-bazar, Pathshala, Rangia, Nalbari, Amingaon), Darrang (Tezpur, Mangaldai),
Cachar (Silchar, Hailakandi), Sibsagar and Lakhimpur, and also in Shillong. The surrendered have nothing to fear, his
biographer says: their selfless service cannot be stopped. As the “Bhagavata” says: “Your devotees, O Madhava, bound to
You by ties of love, never fall from the path; protected by You, O Lord, they stride fearlessly over the heads of the
enemy's commanders” (SB 10.2.33). In Assam he opened maths in Tezpur, Gauhati and Goalpara.[^4] <!-- F65 Q2 I7 F68 -->

## The Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math
He left the Sri Chaitanya Math when already mature in years; in his biographer's words, Mahaprabhu Himself led him out
of a constricted position — and in a short time he built large centres all over India. No efforts of the envious can
hide a true guru, as a wisp of cloud cannot hide the sun, and to oppose him is an offence against a Vaishnava. In a
difficult time his “right and left hands” in managing the temples were two Calcuttans, Manikantha Mukhopadhyaya and
Dr. S. N. Ghosh, head of the Homeopathic Factory — the same whose initiation he had witnessed in Mayapur in 1925;
later the advocate Jayanta Kumar Mukhopadhyaya took on much of the management. <!-- F69 I8 F70 -->

He preached in the Punjab and in Hyderabad, those strongholds of mayavada, and hundreds there accepted Mahaprabhu's
teaching. Atul Krishna Goswami of the Radha-Ramana temple in Vrindavana called him a peerless spiritual giant. At his
inspiration many educated and influential men accepted tridandi-sannyasa. Warm, tolerant and forbearing, he drew
people of every station — and that, says his biographer, is why the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math grew so quickly.
<!-- F71 F72 F75 I9 -->

He acquired the land and buildings at Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's birthplace in Puri. The house on Grand Road near
Narayan-chata where his guru was born stood on the property of the Dakshina-parshva Math; in 1974 Srila Madhava
Maharaj recovered it, and today a Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math with a temple stands there. He was also the chief
organiser of the all-India celebration of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's centenary, in which all his godbrothers took part.
<!-- F73 F74 -->

In Jashra, in Nadia, the Deities of Jagadisha Pandita — Lord Jagannatha and Gaura-Gopala — entrusted Themselves to his
service. Jagadisha's descendants, Vishvanath Goswami and Shambhunath and Mrityunjaya Mukhopadhyaya, could no longer
maintain the worship or the crumbling temple; on the advice of Panchu Thakura of Jashra and Santosh Kumar Mallik of
Ranaghat they handed the temple over to him unconditionally. His biographer compares this to Gopala waiting for
Madhavendra Puri: the Deities Themselves offered Themselves to the one who would serve Them. Maharaj repaired the
temple, brought in electricity, built a guesthouse for pilgrims, and in the first year personally seated and fed
several thousand people with mahaprasad. <!-- F95 -->

The maths and ashrams he founded are listed in the biography year by year. The first was the Shyamananda Gaudiya Math
in Medinipur (1942); then came maths in Tezpur (1948) and Gauhati (1953), in Calcutta on Rash Behari Avenue (1955),
and the main math at Ishodyan in Sri Mayapur (1956). In Vrindavana there arose the Sarveshvar-haveli (1956), a math
on Mathura Road (1960) and the Sri Vinoda-vani Gaudiya Math at Kaliya-daha (1967); in Krishnanagar, a math at
Goari-bazar (1960); in Calcutta, the math on Satish Mukherjee Road (1961; the new temple opened in 1967). In
Mathura-mandala came the Sri Gaudiya Sevashram at Madhuvana (1961) and an ashram at Gokula-Mahavan (1975); in
Hyderabad, two maths (1962 and 1972); in Jashra, the shripat of Jagadisha Pandita (1962). These were followed by
Goalpara (1969), Chandigarh (1970),[^5] Puri (1974), the Jagannatha temple in Agartala (1976) and Dehradun (1977).
In 1955 two more maths passed into his care — in Sarbhog, and the Sri Gadai-Gauranga Math in Baliati. <!-- F92 -->

He also opened schools: a chatushpathi in Medinipur (1946), a primary school and a Sanskrit vidyapitha at Ishodyan
(1959), the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Vidyamandir, a school of Western languages and a Sanskrit college in Calcutta
(1961–1968), and a Sanskrit school and a school of Indian languages in Chandigarh (1972); libraries in Calcutta (of
comparative religion) and in Chandigarh; and free dispensaries at Ishodyan, Chandigarh and Puri. In 1961 he founded
the monthly “Sri Chaitanya Vani,” and in 1964 the Sri Chaitanya Vani Press. That same year, 1964, he invited Srila
Bhakti Pramoda Puri Maharaj to head the journal's editorial board, where he served for thirty-three years; earlier
still, after Puri Maharaj's sannyasa in 1946, the two of them, with Bon Maharaj, had travelled across India.
<!-- F93 F94 F99 -->

He told his disciples that it would be enough to translate Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura's books into the languages of
the world. And Srila Sridhar Maharaj he regarded as his shiksha-guru, as did many other disciples of Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta — Bhaktivedanta Swami, Bhakti Prajnana Keshava, Bhakti Saranga Goswami, Bhakti Pramoda Puri.
<!-- F96 F98 -->

## A Talk on Guru-puja Day
In 1967 his disciples celebrated his appearance day, Utthana Ekadashi — also the departure day of Srila Gaurakishora
Dasa Babaji — at the Calcutta temple on Satish Mukherjee Road. In the evening Maharaj spoke to them and, among other
things, recalled what “revered Puri Maharaj” had told of the Babaji. <!-- F76 -->

He had, he said, three gurus: the Lord, who dispels ignorance; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, who drew him into service; and
all the Vaishnavas, his own disciples included. Guru and disciple are to each other both the object of service and
the one cared for — as Yashoda served Gopala by binding Him. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta addressed all his disciples as
“prabhu” and with the respectful “apni,” using the familiar “tumi” with only a few — yet he instructed them and
corrected them sternly. <!-- F77 F78 -->

Whoever honours him, Maharaj said, in truth honours his guru. In his guru he had seen nothing but the desire to
please the Lord; otherwise his guru would not have kept him in the math. Without love for the guru, for Krishna and
Radha, renunciation is false; true renunciation is yukta-vairagya, offering everything in service. He cited the
“Upadeshamrita”: one who has mastered the urges of speech, mind, anger, tongue, belly and genitals can instruct the
whole world; the “Gita”: the taste for sense objects leaves only when the Supreme is seen (BG 2.59); and the
“Bhagavata”: by whatever means, fix the mind on Krishna (SB 7.1.32) — which is attained through the association of
saintly devotees (SB 7.7.30). <!-- F79 Q3 Q4 Q5 -->

## Last Instructions
In 1976 he registered the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math as a society under West Bengal law. In December of that year he
signed a letter — “Tridandi-bhikshu Sri Bhakti Dayita Madhava”: his health was failing; all the maths were
registered; they were governed by twelve trustees, removable only for grave misconduct; as president and acharya
after himself he appointed Tridandi Swami Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha. <!-- F81 F82 -->

On the morning of 30 December 1978, at the Calcutta math, at Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha's request and against his doctor's
orders not to talk much, he gave instructions to a Western devotee from Chandigarh. Devotion must be exclusive, given
to Krishna alone, as a faithful wife is devoted to her husband; the demigods are not to be reviled, but neither are
they to be set on a par with Krishna: “krishnas tu bhagavan svayam” — Krishna is Bhagavan Himself (SB 1.3.28). The
“Chaitanya-charitamrita” says the same: “Only the original Lord of all that exists, the source of all Avataras and
manifestations of God, is worthy to be called Svayam Bhagavan” (CC, Adi 2.88). <!-- F83 Q6 -->

He spoke too of succession. The math is no one's property; there is one acharya, and he is also the president. The
acharya is not elected by vote — he is indicated by the Lord, from above; so it is in all four sampradayas. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta, he recalled, rejected a constitution with an elective procedure drawn up by the advocate
J. N. Basu — he himself and two or three others were present. Having consulted his senior godbrothers, he appointed
Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha as his successor and president. No one should be expelled from a math for quarrelling, but
reasoned with; if that fails, give him money and a letter and transfer him to another math; and obey the appointed
head. <!-- F84 F85 -->

Three obstacles stand on the path: money, women and the thirst for fame; that is why Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sang that
a Vaishnava is one who has given up gold, women and prestige. What is collected in a day must be handed to the math
that same day; the sick must be treated, even on credit — he himself had once secretly borrowed money for
provisions, and only Uddharan knew; a householder, Govinda Babu, helped then. He recalled collecting together with
“Sripad Goswami Maharaj” and Sripad Nemi Maharaj, while Bhakti Pradip Tirtha, Jajabar Maharaj and Sridhar Maharaj went
with him; he never spent temple money on himself and asked “Kunja-da” only for the cloth he needed. And fame, when
fled from, follows love for Krishna of itself: “Fearing universal honour, Sri Madhavendra Puri left Remuna. But fame
followed him, born of his pure love for Krishna” (CC, Madhya 4.147). So in his last instructions he once more said
what mattered most: the acharya is appointed from above, devotion belongs to Krishna alone, and money, women and fame
are the three hindrances. <!-- F86 Q9 F87 Q7 I10 -->

Tirtha Maharaj, he said, cannot always be here — let Jagamohan Prabhu manage the math. He asked forgiveness if he had
been harsh, and closed with obeisance to the Vaishnavas: “I bow to the Vaishnavas — wish-fulfilling trees, oceans of
mercy, deliverers of the fallen.” <!-- F88 Q8 -->

## Departure and Samadhi
Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Goswami Maharaj left this world in 1979, on the Gaura-pratipad of the month of Govinda,
at nine in the morning, to the chanting of the Holy Names, in his room at the Calcutta math on Satish Mukherjee
Road; in his biographer's words, he entered the morning pastimes with Sri Radha. The departures of Srila Jagannatha
Dasa Babaji and of Rasikananda Deva Gosvami are commemorated on the same day.[^6] <!-- F89 -->

At four that afternoon his body was carried to Mayapur with kirtan and laid in samadhi according to the scriptures;
the rites were performed by Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Maharaj. Two days later a memorial festival was held at the
main math in Ishodyan, and on 22 March many of his godbrothers gathered in the sankirtana hall of the Calcutta math;
the meeting was led by Srila Bhakti Hridaya Bon Maharaj, and the guest of honour was Tushar Kanti Ghosh, publisher of
the “Amrita Bazar Patrika.” <!-- F90 F91 -->
