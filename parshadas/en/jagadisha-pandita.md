# Jagadisha Pandita
*Джагадиша Пандит · @jagadisha-pandita*

## A Branch of Two Trees
Sri Jagadisha Pandita is one of those companions of Sri Chaitanya whom the Lord blessed with His mercy already in His
childhood pastimes. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, counting the branches of Chaitanya's tree, speaks of him together with
Hiranya:

“Jagadisha Pandita and Hiranya Mahashaya received the compassionate Lord's mercy when He was still a child.” (CC, Adi 10.70)

“In these two houses, on the day of Ekadashi, the Lord begged for the food offered to Vishnu and ate it Himself.” (CC, Adi 10.71)
<!-- Q3 -->

Yet the same Kaviraja names him also among the branches of Nityananda's tree:

“Sri Jagadisha Pandita purifies the whole world; like a heavy monsoon rain, he pours down the nectar of love for Krishna.” (CC, Adi 11.30)
<!-- F6 Q4 -->

He is therefore counted a branch of both Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda. Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, listing the companions
of Nityananda, says of him:

“Jagadisha Pandita is the supreme abode of radiant holiness; Nityananda with His companions was his wealth and his life.” (CB, Antya 5.736)
<!-- F5 F7 Q5 -->

The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” calls him Jagadisha and Sri Jagadisha Pandita, the “Charitamrita” Jagadisha Pandita; Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” calls him both Jagadisha Bhatta and Jagadisha Pandita Goswami, and the
biographies call him Sri Jagadisha Pandita Prabhu.[^1] <!-- F1 -->

## Chandrahasa
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes is revealed by Kavi Karnapura:

“Chandrahasa, the dancer of Vraja, a knower of *rasa*, is the lover of dance Sri Jagadisha Pandita.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 143)

In Vraja he is Chandrahasa, a dancer who knows the taste of *rasa*; and now too, in Gaura's pastimes, Jagadisha loves to
dance in the Lord's kirtana. As “the dancer” the vandanas — songs in glorification of the Vaishnavas — also remember
him. <!-- F2 Q1 -->

Elsewhere in the same list Karnapura speaks of him and of Hiranya:

“Other wives of the sacrificial priests are Sri Jagadisha and Hiranyaka, whose food the Lord asked for and ate on
Ekadashi.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 192)

The wives of the brahmana priests engaged in sacrifice (*yajna-patni*) fed Krishna in His pastimes; now they appeared as
Jagadisha and Hiranya to nourish the pastimes of Gaura — and again the Lord asked them for food. The biographies cite the
two verses in one order — first 192, then 143 — as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta does in the “Anubhashya.”[^2] <!-- F3 Q2 F4 -->

## From Gauhati to the Ganga
Jagadisha appeared in the east of India, in Gauhati — ancient Prag-jyotishapura, now in Assam. His father's name was
Kamalaksha Bhatta; according to the “Anubhashya,” he came of the line of Bhatta-narayana.[^3] His father and mother
were householders and great devotees of Vishnu. <!-- F8 F9 F10 F11 -->

When his parents left this world, Jagadisha, with his wife Dukhini and his brother, left his native land and came to
the Ganga, to Sri Mayapur. There he built a house next to the house of Jagannatha Mishra and settled in it. Which
brother came with him the sources tell differently: according to some, Hiranya Pandita; according to Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta, his younger brother Mahesha, who also wished to live by the Ganga in the company of Vaishnavas.[^4]
<!-- F12 F13 F14 F15 -->

A close, heartfelt friendship bound Jagadisha to Jagannatha Mishra. Vrindavana Dasa writes of him and Hiranya:

“The Panditas Jagadisha and Hiranya were great Vaishnavas, one in life with Jagannatha Mishra.” (CB, Adi 6.26)
<!-- F16 -->

Jagadisha's wife was named Dukhini; she is also called Dukhini-mata. He and Dukhini loved Nimai just as Jagannatha
Mishra and Shachi loved Him — with parental love, *vatsalya*. According to one of the biographies, Dukhini was like a
mother to Nimai and at times even nursed Him at her breast. Such fortune falls only to the Lord's eternal associates: to
treat God Himself as one's own child is possible only for those whom He Himself has called into this *rasa*.
<!-- F17 F18 F19 I5 -->

Jagadisha showed the pastime of a householder. His son was Ramabhadra Goswami; according to the “Anubhashya,” his only
son. <!-- F20 -->

Jagadisha was born before the Lord, and not by chance. Vrindavana Dasa speaks of the eternal associates who came to
Nadia ahead of Mahaprabhu:

“By the order of Lord Chaitanya many of His eternal associates — Sri Chandrashekhara, Jagadisha, Gopinatha Acharya,
Shriman Pandita, Murari Gupta, Sri Garuda Pandita and Gangadasa Pandita — took birth in Nadia, hidden, before His
coming.” (CB, Adi 2.98–99)

The biographies quote the same verses in their chapters on Chandrashekhara and Gangadasa.[^5] <!-- F21 Q10 -->

## Ekadashi
How dear Jagadisha was to Him, the Lord showed while still an infant, in a childhood pastime. <!-- F22 -->

The day of the Lord's appearance, Ekadashi and certain Dvadashis are called “Sri Hari-vasara,” the days of Hari. On
these days the servants of Hari set aside their affairs and serve Him by fasting. But fasting is the service of the
servants; the Lord Himself is not bound by it. This time He did not show the pastime of the fast obligatory for the
servants, but showed another pastime — accepting the offering prepared for Himself. <!-- F23 -->

Mahaprabhu is the father of sankirtana, and already as an infant He made everyone chant the holy name. When He cried,
Shachi and the neighbouring women would clap their hands and sing the names of Hari — and He would grow quiet:

“There was, however, one sure remedy: as soon as kirtana began, the Lord stopped crying. Whenever someone began to clap
and sing the names of Hari, the Lord became quiet and docile. To keep the child content, everyone sang the holy names of
God constantly, and the house of Jagannatha Mishra was like Sri Vaikuntha.” (CB, Adi 6.13–15)
<!-- F24 -->

But one day, on Ekadashi, Nimai would not be calmed, however much everyone sang. His anxious elders lovingly asked what
He wanted, promising to bring Him anything at all. And He answered:

“The Lord replied: ‘If you want to save My life, go at once to the house of two brahmanas. Jagadisha and Hiranya are
great devotees, and what I desire is with them. Today, on Ekadashi, they are both fasting, but they have prepared an
offering for Lord Vishnu. When I eat it, I will stop crying and go out to play.’” (CB, Adi 6.20–23)
<!-- F25 -->

Hearing this, Shachi began to lament:

“‘What you ask for is permitted neither by people nor by the Vedas.’” (CB, Adi 6.24)

But the people around laughed and promised the child to bring Him prasada. <!-- F26 -->

When Jagadisha and Hiranya learned of Nimai's wish, they saw in it neither a child's whim nor a breach of the fast.
Vrindavana Dasa relates:

“Learning of the child's wish, these brahmanas were filled with joy. The brahmanas exclaimed: ‘What a wonderful
request! We have never heard of so wise a child. How did He know that today is Ekadashi? How does He know that we have
prepared many different dishes for the Lord? Now we understand why this child is so beautiful: surely Gopala-Krishna
Himself is acting in His body.’” (CB, Adi 6.27–30)

“Then these brahmanas, with great joy, brought all the dishes. They said: ‘Child, taste these delicacies. Today our
desire to render service to Krishna has been fulfilled.’” (CB, Adi 6.32–33)

The eternal associate recognized in Mishra's son Gopala, the son of Nanda, who wished to taste his offering in the form
of Nimai, and gave Him everything without hesitation. This is no breach of Ekadashi: the brahmanas gave the Lord His own
*bhoga*, and in this the purpose of all their service to Krishna was fulfilled.[^6] <!-- F27 F28 F29 Q7 -->

Having received the offering from His devotees, Nimai stopped crying and began to eat with great joy, tasting a little
of each dish, while the people sang the names of Hari, and He ate and danced (CB, Adi 6.37–39). <!-- F30 -->

One of the biographies retells this pastime in its own way: Shachi struck her forehead in amazement, the women promised
to fulfil the child's wish if He would stop crying, and Jagadisha and Hiranya, who had known before that Hari Himself
had been born as Mishra's son, fed Him to His fill and said in jest, “You have eaten what was meant for Krishna!” While
Nimai ate, this biography says, there appeared to them the form of the infant Krishna — the colour of a fresh rain cloud,
radiant, with a peacock feather — and in ecstasy they began to sing “Hari! Hari!” It also says that the little Lord
came to their house Himself, and adds to His words: the devotees fast, but Vishnu is not bound by the fast and eats what
He likes.[^7] <!-- F31 F32 F33 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja recalls this pastime in his account of the Lord's childhood:

“On the day of Ekadashi, pretending to be ill, He ate the food offered to Vishnu in the house of Jagadisha and
Hiranya.” (CC, Adi 14.39)

And Vrindavana Dasa, much later, when Jagadisha and Hiranya set out for Nilachala, recalls again that in childhood the
Lord had eaten the grain they offered to Krishna in their home on Ekadashi (CB, Antya 8.29). The Ekadashi story is told
in the sixth chapter of the Adi-khanda of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata.”[^8] <!-- Q11 -->

The biographies draw a lesson from this pastime: the Lord Himself snatches and eats what belongs to His devotee, but
will not even glance at the gifts of one without devotion. Vrindavana Dasa says the same elsewhere:

“Thus the Lord enjoys the gifts of His devotees and pays no attention to millions of offerings from those devoid of
devotion.” (CB, Madhya 9.185)

Nimai's request on Ekadashi is no breach of the fast but a pastime of God Himself: the servants keep the fast, and the
Lord accepts what they have prepared for Him, and so shows how dear they are to Him. <!-- I1 I2 Q6 -->

The house of Jagadisha and Hiranya in Navadvipa where this took place is shown to pilgrims on the circuit of the dhama
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.1114, 12.2186). According to Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura's “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya,” it stood
about a *krosha* to the south-east of Mishra's house — no child could have known what was being cooked there. <!-- F34 -->

## In the Kirtanas of Navadvipa
When the Lord began sankirtana, Jagadisha was among the *parshadas* with whom He sang and danced every night in the house
of Shrivasa, and sometimes at Chandrashekhara's, only with His associates. Vrindavana Dasa names him in the list of those
present at these kirtanas (CB, Madhya 8.115). <!-- F35 -->

The Lord was conquered by Jagadisha's pure devotion, and in the same way Nityananda, non-different from the Lord, was
conquered by his love: He regarded Jagadisha as His own and loved him dearly. <!-- F36 -->

After the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai, Jagadisha was among the devotees who sported with the Lord in the waters of
the Ganga (CB, Madhya 13.336–339); on the day the Kazi was subdued, among those who went with the Lord in the night
procession of kirtana through the town (CB, Madhya 23.150).[^9] And when in Shridhara's house the Lord drank water from
his old iron pot, Jagadisha wept with joy together with the others:

“Jagadisha, Gopinatha, Nandana Acharya, Shuklambara, Garuda and many other devotees shed tears.” (CB, Madhya 23.452)
<!-- F37 F38 F39 Q8 Q9 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” and the vandanas glorify him among the Lord's associates; in Narahari's songs quoted in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara,” Jagadisha sings in the Lord's kirtana in Navadvipa and in Shrivasa's courtyard (BR 12.1888, 12.2702,
12.3150); his name stands also in the lists of the “Narottama-vilasa.” <!-- F74 -->

## Jagannatha from Purushottama
By the Lord's order Jagadisha went to Nilachala to preach krishna-bhakti and the religion of the age — the sankirtana of
the names of Hari. When this was is not known for certain.[^10] Krishna, the son of Nanda, and Gaurasundara, the son of
Jagannatha Mishra, are the same Supreme Truth as Lord Jagannatha. Seeing Jagannatha in Purushottama, Jagadisha was
filled with prema, and on the way back he was overcome by longing in separation from Jagannatha, and wept.
<!-- F40 F41 F42 -->

Many see Jagannatha in Puri, but few, on leaving, long for Him in separation. One of the biographies observes: such
longing is a sign of Jagannatha's mercy, and without it His mercy has not truly been received. <!-- I3 -->

Seeing Jagadisha's tears, Jagannatha, full of mercy, appeared to him in a dream and told him to take His Deity and serve
Him. At the same time He ordered the king of Orissa of that day to give Jagadisha that form of His which, at the
Nava-kalevara, the renewal of Jagannatha's body, had been “buried” (*samadhistha-vigraha*); according to one of the
biographies, He appeared to the king, too, in a dream. This is not a form whose service had ended: it is Jagannatha
Himself, who wished to accept Jagadisha's service. And the king considered himself fortunate to have met such a devotee,
and held it a great honour to give him the Deity. <!-- F43 F44 F45 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” that Jagadisha prayed to Jagannathadeva and in answer was allowed to
take the Deity away; one of the biographies, citing him, writes that Jagadisha, having asked Jagannatha's permission,
took His Sri-murti to Yashora and installed Him there. <!-- F46 -->

Jagadisha asked how he could carry the heavy Deity. Jagannatha answered that He would become light as shola pith
(according to another biography — as cork); Jagadisha should cover Him with new cloth and carry Him on a pole on his
shoulder, and wherever he wished to install Him, there he should set Him down. One of the biographies adds: wherever He
was set down on the ground, there Jagadisha would have to remain for ever. <!-- F47 -->

With the help of the brahmanas who accompanied him (according to one of the biographies, two, who carried the Deity in
turns), Jagadisha brought Jagannatha to Yashada on the Ganga, within Chakdaha, in present-day West Bengal. There he left
Him on the shoulder of a brahmana servant and went to bathe in the Ganga and perform *tarpana*. Then Jagannatha suddenly
became very heavy; the servant could not hold Him and set Him down on the ground. Returning, Jagadisha understood:
Jagannatha wished to remain here. The Lord Himself becomes light when He wishes to be carried, and heavy when He chooses
His place. <!-- F48 F49 -->

There is a tradition that Jagadisha brought the Deity from Puri on a pole; this pole — “the staff of Jagadisha
Pandita” — is kept to this day in the temple of Jagannatha and shown to pilgrims. <!-- F50 -->

Chakdaha is an ancient holy place. In Puranic times it was called Rathavarma; at the end of the Dvapara-yuga Pradyumna,
Krishna's son, slew the demon Shambara here, and the place came to be called Pradyumna-nagara; and when Bhagiratha led
the Ganga to deliver the descendants of Sagara, the wheel (*chakra*) of his chariot sank here — hence the name
“Chakradaha,” now Chakdah.[^11] <!-- F51 -->

The news that Jagannatha had come from Purushottama to Yashada spread far, and countless people began to flock to Him.
Since Jagannatha had remained in Yashada, Jagadisha decided to live there and never returned to Mayapur. According to
one of the biographies, Jagadisha and Hiranya moved from Mayapur “probably” after the Lord's sannyasa. <!-- F52 F53 -->

## The Shripat at Yashada
At first Jagannatha stood under a banyan on the bank of the Ganga. Then Raja Krishnachandra of Goari-Krishnanagara
helped to build a temple, and when it fell into disrepair it was restored by Mokshada Dasi, the wife of Umeshachandra
Majumdar; according to the “Anubhashya,” this was in the year 1324 of the Bengali era, as an inscription on a stone
slab records. <!-- F54 -->

The temple has no dome (*chura*) and looks like an ordinary house. In it are Jagannathadeva, Radha-vallabha and
Gaura-Gopala. Jagannatha's Snana-yatra is celebrated here every year with special splendour: a platform is set up on a
wide field, and on the day of the Snana-yatra Jagannatha proceeds to it from the temple; the fair on this field is famous
to this day. There is no Ratha-yatra here, according to one of the biographies. A dilapidated five-hundred-year-old
*dola-mancha* also survives: on Dola-purnima Radha-vallabha is brought out onto it. <!-- F55 F56 F57 -->

The shripat stands in the village of Yashada (Yashora, Jashra) in the Nadia district, within a mile of Chakdaha station.
In Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's time the Ganga had receded almost a *krosha* from it; the servitors were Vijayachandra
Goswami and then Lalitamohana Goswami, of the Bandyopadhyaya line, and in Yashada they sang the *suchaka-gana*, a song
of Jagadisha's life composed by the poet Gadadhara. For some time the siddha Bhagavan Dasa Babaji of Kalna lived and
performed bhajana in Yashada; after settling in Kalna he would still come here from there. <!-- F61 F73 F60 -->

The devotees donated much land to Jagannatha, but, according to one of the biographies, in time Jagadisha's descendants
sold it to maintain the service. Later, when they could no longer carry it on, the temple passed to Srila Bhakti Dayita
Madhava Maharaja, and since 1962 the shripat at Jashra has been one of the centres of the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math.
The biographers see a familiar pattern in this: just as the Gopala of Madhavendra Puri, so the Jagannatha and
Gaura-Gopala of Jagadisha seemed Themselves to “offer Themselves” to Bhakti Dayita Madhava Maharaja — the Lord finds
various ways to accept the service of the one He Himself has chosen. <!-- F58 F59 I4 -->

## Gaura-Gopala
Drawn by the parental love of Jagadisha and his wife, the Lord and Nityananda came to Yashada twice, held sankirtana
there and celebrated a festival.[^12] <!-- F62 -->

When the Lord was about to leave Yashada for Nilachala, Dukhini wept in separation from Him. To console her, the Lord
agreed to remain in Yashada in the form of the Deity — Gaura-Gopala. According to one of the biographies, this was after
a festival of many days in Jagadisha's house, and Gaurasundara said to her: “I shall live for ever in your house in the
form of the Deity.” Since then He has been served. Gaura-Gopala is golden in colour; according to the “Anubhashya,” He is
a wooden Gopala, and Dukhini-mata herself installed Him. Gaura-Gopala is not a memorial image or a likeness: He is the
Lord Himself, who remained in Yashada out of love for His devotee. <!-- F63 F64 -->

## With Nityananda's Companions
Jagadisha was among Nityananda's companions at the chida-dadhi festival in Panihati:

“Dhananjaya, Jagadisha, Paramesvara dasa, Mahesha, Gauridasa, Krishnadasa Hoda.” (CC, Antya 6.62)
<!-- F65 -->

Together with the Gauda devotees he went to the Lord in Nilachala. Vrindavana Dasa writes how he and Hiranya set out
together with Advaita:

“Sri Jagadisha Pandita and Hiranya Bhagavata also came joyfully, both intoxicated with the sweetness of the *rasas* of
divine love for Krishna.” (CB, Antya 8.28)

Murari Gupta says the same (4.17.10); according to one of the biographies, Jagadisha went with the Gaudiyas to the
Ratha-yatra every year. <!-- F66 -->

Probably it was he who was among the devotees when, after His sannyasa, the Lord met Shachi in Shantipura: Vrindavana
Dasa names in the list “Haridasa, Murari, Shrigarbha, Narayana, Jagadisha and Gopinatha” (CB, Antya 4.273). And when at
the festival in Kheturi the Lord appeared with His associates, the “Bhakti-ratnakara” names Jagadisha too among the
dancers (10.551; so also the “Narottama-vilasa” 7.110). <!-- F67 F68 -->

His dearest disciple was Raghunatha, or Raghunathacharya, the son of the lame Bhagavan; the “Narottama-vilasa” tells
how he met Jahnava-devi on her journey (6.214–215), and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls him a disciple of Jagadisha Pandita
Goswami. <!-- F72 -->

## Appearance and Disappearance
In our calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math the appearance day of Srila Jagadisha Pandita is Gaura Ekadashi of
the month of Narayana (in 2027, 18 January),[^13] and his disappearance day is Gaura Tritiya of the same month (in 2027,
10 January). The biographies and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta also name the shukla-tritiya of the month of Pausha as the day of
his departure; on this day a festival is held every year in Yashada. The sources do not give the year of his appearance
or departure, nor do they say plainly where he left this world.[^14] <!-- F69 F70 F71 -->

## Significance
Jagadisha Pandita is Chandrahasa, the dancer of Vraja, and one of the priests' wives who fed Krishna; in Gaura's
pastimes he is a branch of both Chaitanya and Nityananda, the one who, in Krishnadasa Kaviraja's words, purifies the
whole world and pours down the nectar of love for Krishna like a monsoon rain. <!-- F2 F3 F5 F6 -->

In his life the Lord revealed two sides of His love for His devotees. As an infant He Himself demanded his offering on
Ekadashi, showing that fasting is the service of the servants, while the Lord accepts what is prepared for Him with love
and joyfully eats what belongs to His devotee. And Jagannatha, seeing his longing in separation, Himself wished to go with
him from Purushottama, Himself became light and Himself chose His place; and Gaura, moved by Dukhini's tears, Himself
remained in her house as Gaura-Gopala. Such parental love for the Lord is accessible only to His eternal associates, and
the Lord finds ways to accept the service of those whom He Himself has chosen. <!-- I1 I2 I3 I4 I5 -->
