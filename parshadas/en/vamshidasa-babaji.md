# Srila Vamshidasa Babaji Maharaja
*Шрила Вамшидас Бабаджи Махарадж · @vamshidasa-babaji*

## A Paramahamsa in the Guise of an Avadhuta
Srila Vamshidasa Babaji Maharaja was a saint of Navadvipa in the first half of the twentieth century, whom the
disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati remembered with reverence and with caution. The journal “Gaudiya” called
him Om Vishnupada Sri Srila Vamshidasa Babaji Maharaja, or more often simply Srila Babaji Maharaja or Baba; the best
of avadhutas (avadhuta-vara) and the crown jewel of the line of paramahamsas, the foremost of maha-bhagavatas, a great
soul free in every respect (sarva-tantra-svatantra). Others called him Srila Vamshi Dasa Babaji, Vamshi Dasaji or
Mahatma Vamshi Dasa, and memory has kept for him the name “the Paramahamsa Avadhuta”. <!-- F1 -->

He was a paramahamsa Vaishnava who lived as an avadhuta — a wanderer not bound by outward rules. An avadhuta has shaken
off every worldly feeling and obligation, takes no account of social custom, above all of the rules of varnashrama,
and is unusual in his conduct; Sri Nityananda too is often called an avadhuta. The little we know of him has been
preserved in old articles of the journal “Gaudiya” and in the accounts of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta and his disciples, who
knew Babaji personally. <!-- F5 F6 -->

The chronicle of the “Gaudiya” sees in him a boundless mercy dependent on nothing. For the sake of souls who have
turned away from the Lord, are bound by maya and tormented by the threefold miseries, he displayed the pastime of
wandering through holy places, towns and villages. The dust of his feet purified the earth, and simple people who saw
him and served him, “the beloved of Vaikuntha”, received true good fortune. <!-- F3 -->

## Homeland and Former Life
He was born in the village of Majidpur near the town of Jamalpur in East Bengal (now Bangladesh); before the partition
of India this area belonged to the district of Mymensingh. According to Haridasa Dasa, his father's name was Sanatana
Malobrahma, and before leaving the world he himself was called Bhairava. The year and day of his birth have not been
preserved. <!-- F7 F8 F2 -->

Of his former life he spoke sparingly and always with self-effacement: “In those days, in my former ashrama, I lived on
the other bank of the Brahmaputra.” “Without serving father or mother, I took a kaupina and left.” “I know neither
the work of the Vedas nor the work of the injunctions.” And once he sighed: “I am in accord with none of the four
sampradayas — what will become of me?”[^1] <!-- F9 F11 -->

He also recalled the bad company in which he had once lived: he named his former companions, who bore the name of
Vaishnavas, and told of their unworthy deeds, but added that while living among them he never mixed with them and
performed his bhajana as it should be done. He had little love for his native land. When devotees from Majidpur
invited him there, he yielded to their eagerness, but found no joy in the journey and said that this was a place the
Pandavas had disdained. He said the same in Bhagalpur: “Majidpur is a land the Pandavas left behind: the people are
ill-natured, they eat anything, there is no Harinama. I have no place of my own; like the Pandavas in exile, I live
unrecognised in the forest.” Only with a fellow countryman, Gokula Dasa Babaji, an elder godbrother of Srila Bhakti
Pramoda Puri Goswami Maharaj, did he gladly speak of Krishna in his native Mymensingh dialect. <!-- F10 F12 F13 -->

## Beneath a Tree by the Ganga
From East Bengal he came to Navadvipa and settled on the bank of the Ganga. He lived in the highest renunciation: at
first simply under a tree, later in a hut by the river. “In Navadvipa I lived on alms,” he said. “To eat another's
rice is good, but to live under another's roof is not.” He wore only a kaupina and neither cut nor groomed his hair
or beard. He was so tall that he could pick fruit for puja from the upper branches without a ladder; but once he fell
from a tree — and from then on he showed the form of a lame man. <!-- F14 F19 F18 -->

He seemed not to notice the offerings people brought. They brought him rice, fruit, vegetables, flour and bananas; when
much had gathered, he offered it all to his Deities in his mind and distributed the remnants to whoever happened to be
near. The door of his hut was never locked, though well-wishers begged him to lock it, and thieves again and again
carried off everything inside. When asked about it he replied that guarding the house and its things was the concern
of Gaura-Nitai — and at times he himself scolded the Deities for not stopping the thieves[^2]. <!-- F22 F24 -->

Once he gathered fruit by his hut for his Deities and allowed no one to touch it. In the night a thief carried off the
Deities' utensils, and in the morning a cow wandered in and ate all the fruit; no one could drive her out. But Babaji
laughed and clapped his hands: “One thief gives, another thief takes!” — for the greatest thief is Krishna Himself. A
disciple named Purna (or Punya) lived with him. <!-- F25 F26 -->

## Deities in Two Cloth Bags
His Deities lived in two cloth bags: in one, Nitai-Gaura; in the other, Radha-Govinda. Regularly he took Them out,
served Them in his mind with mantras, and carefully put Them back; and at times he left Them outside so that people,
too, could have Their darshana. The chronicle of the “Gaudiya” calls Them the Deities of his bhava-seva — service in
divine feeling; Babaji called his Deity simply “Thakura” or “Sri Gopala”. <!-- F20 -->

He spoke more with his Deities than with people: he laughed with Them, and at times wept in despair; at night he talked
with Thakura and joyfully sang to Him. In temples, too, he spoke quietly with the Deities, revealing to Them his
innermost heart. At times he offered tobacco in a hookah to Radha and Govinda — but never to Nitai-Gauranga[^3].

## Srila Gaurakishora and the Acharyas of the Gaudiya Math
The chronicle of the “Gaudiya” calls him a follower of Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji Maharaja: in his life one sees
the same examples of restraining and instructing worldly people as in the life of Srila Gaurakishora. <!-- F4 -->

When the young Srila Bhaktisiddhanta came to Navadvipa, Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura introduced him to Babaji. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta was drawn by his Vaishnava conduct and his complete disregard for social convention. He himself bowed
to Babaji from a distance, but did not allow his disciples to associate with him: a beginner might misunderstand his
indifference to rules and offend his feet. The rules of sadhana, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's disciples explained, exist
above all for the pleasure of Radha-Govinda and for the growth of the beginner; whoever measures a perfected Vaishnava
by the standards of a sadhaka risks committing an offence and falling from the path of devotion. <!-- F15 F16 F17 I1 -->

He allowed no one to touch his feet. Only once — on the day after Phalguna Purnima, when the festival Jagannatha Mishra
held for the birth of Gauranga was being remembered — he forgot his rule in ecstasy, and Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri
Goswami Maharaj received the dust of his feet; on another occasion he also received the remnants of his prasada. Srila
Puri Maharaj remembered other words of his as well. Hearing someone sing the line “tyajiya shayana-sukha, vichitra
palanka…” — “leaving the comfort of sleep on an ornate bed…” — Babaji said: “You are only singing a song. It reveals
itself only to one who has himself been opened”[^4]. And when someone sang in his presence a newly composed “mantra”,
contrary to siddhanta and to the spirit of devotion, he cut him short: “Such a name is not allowed here.”

## To Vraja
He wandered much through holy places, and everywhere his mind was steeped in the rasa of devotion to Krishna. Everything
around him reminded him of Krishna's pastimes — above all the pipal and bata, the banyan. On seeing such a tree he
would sit beneath it, seeing in it the Vamshi-vata, the banyan under which Krishna played His flute, and it was not
easy to move him from there. <!-- F28 -->

On Monday, 12 Phalguna 1347 of the Bengali era (24 February 1941), he set out from Koladvipa — the town of Navadvipa —
for Vrindavana, now on foot, now by cart, now by train. In Katwa he spent two days under a banyan by the station; in
Bhagalpur, a day under a banyan by the station and four days by the Ganga; in Gaya, three days by the Phalgu; in
Benares, three days in a boat at Dashashvamedha Ghata; in Ayodhya, three days by the Sarayu, three hours of them under
a banyan; in Prayaga, ten days at the Triveni; in Mathura, two days at Vishrama Ghata. In Vrindavana he stayed eight
days at Vamshi-vata, nine at Madhyachura on the Yamuna, a day at the temple of Govindaji and two at Kalidaha. In
Nandagrama he sat eight days under a tamala on the eastern bank of Surya-kunda, two days at Pavana-sarovara and four
at the roots of a pilu tree; and then he spent nine more days at Vamshi-vata Ghata in Vrindavana. After three months,
in Jyaishtha, he returned to Navadvipa-dhama. <!-- F29 -->

His companions related that in Vraja he would now sing of Krishna's pastimes, now glorify Navadvipa-dhama, now laugh
like a madman, now speak indistinctly, now keep silent for a long time — and his absorption captivated their hearts.

## The Journey in the Chronicle of the Journal “Gaudiya”
The weekly “Gaudiya” recorded his journeys in later years too, from 1943 to 1947: to Ambika-Kalna, Kharagpur,
Baleshvar, Soro, Bhadrak, Khurda Road and Purushottama (Puri), and then again to Gaya, Kashi, Saidpur, Patna, Munger
and other places. One of these journeys, from Kharagpur to Bhagalpur, the servants of the Gaudiya Math recorded almost
day by day, from 26 August to 1 January — with his words and songs. The chronicle does not name the year; apparently
it is 1943–1944[^5]. <!-- F31 F33 -->

### Kharagpur and Medinipur
He arrived in Kharagpur on 26 August, having eaten nothing for the two days before. He told his servants to look for a
“vamshi-vata”, and his tent was pitched under a banyan right in front of the station; there he spent the night. At
first he wanted to go to Vrindavana by passenger train via Gomoh, but then he had two bullock carts brought and, however
much the respected townsmen urged him to go by train, he held his ground. By rickshaw and the cart of a Hindu driver he
reached a banyan by Upendra Maiti's road at Nutan Bazar in Medinipur and stayed there four days. <!-- F34 F35 -->

At that time Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Goswami Maharaj was living in Medinipur, at the Shyamananda Gaudiya Math.
Learning that Babaji was passing through the town by cart, he sent a disciple to invite him to the math. Babaji
promised to come, but on entering the town he saw a pipal, recognised in it a Vamshi-vata — and stayed beneath it. Then
Srila Madhava Maharaj came to him himself, and Babaji, remembering his love for Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, treated him to
sweet rice. Apparently this meeting took place in just those four days by Upendra Maiti's road[^6]. <!-- F32 -->

### Gaya
He spent one night in the village of Godapiyasal, in the morning was at Shalboni and asked whether there was a station
there. By a train at three in the morning he reached Gomoh and stayed on the platform, and the next day, with two
servants, by the Delhi Mail, he arrived in Gaya-dhama at about half past one in the afternoon. Here he wished to stay
near the temple of Sri Vishnu-pada. Local people invited him to the veranda of an open dharmashala by the Phalgu, but
he remained in his tent on the sand of the bank. The whole day of Radhashtami he spent in supreme joy. And after two or
three days he was speaking of Vrindavana again. <!-- F36 F37 F39 -->

In Gaya he sang: “I will not go where Nitai is not; I will not look upon the faces of those who oppose Nitai.” The
pandas of Gaya asked him to offer pinda to his ancestors. He asked where Gayasura had come from and heard out the whole
legend; and the pandas added: “Here, during Ramachandra's exile, Sri Sita Devi offered pinda to Maharaja Dasharatha —
you too should offer it.” Babaji replied: “People perform the dharma of giving gifts — and where do they go once they
have crossed the Vaitarani? I cross it on a cart! I am kept outside the rules of the Vedas: I do neither the work of
the Vedas nor the work of the injunctions — how am I to offer pinda to my parents?” And pointing to his Deities, he
added: “They here will not let me.” “Ask your Thakuras and do as They say,” the pandas insisted, but he only repeated:
“Hari, lover of His devotees!” <!-- F38 F40 F41 -->

A wrestler laid a “Hanuman-yantra” amulet before him and asked him to return it with a blessing. “You have come to
wheedle a blessing!” Babaji said sternly. “Take it — why did you throw it here?” — and the man picked up the amulet
himself and left. And to Sri Gopala Babaji complained: “In Puri there are pandas, and here there are pandas, but no
Harinama. I know nothing — now You Yourself deal with the pandas. You have left both Vedic and worldly dharma — who will
drink water from Your hands? Gayanatha has no home, and Kashinatha has none either.” <!-- F42 F43 -->

### Kashi
From 25 Ashvina he lived in Kashi-dhama on the bank of the Ganga — at “the field where Sri Sanatana received
instruction”; of his days there the servants of the Sri Sanatana Gaudiya Math told. To his Thakura he said: “You have
seen Gayanatha — now see Kashinatha. In Puri You saw Lokanatha — here he is Kashinatha. You will go to Prayaga — there
are troops there, but there are sadhus too, only he does not reveal himself to everyone. You will go to Vrindavana, and
then where? In the rains, when the Ganga rises, in Ashadha, You will return to Navadvipa.” <!-- F45 F46 -->

Almost every day he sang Srila Narottama Dasa Thakura's song “Nitai-pada-kamala” — “The lotus feet of Nitai”. At the
line “When Nitai's mercy comes, you will attain Radha-Krishna in Vraja” he would say: “There is talk of Vraja, but
whether I shall go I cannot say.” At the words that Nitai's feet are cooler than ten million moons he sighed: “Then why
have I not been cooled?” At night he talked with Thakura and sang joyfully. <!-- F44 F23 Q6 -->

Once, smelling a stick of incense, he said: “Nothing has any fragrance — or the fragrance is there, but the nose does
not catch it.” And he recalled a saying from his former life beyond the Brahmaputra: “What is a washerman to do in a
land of naked sannyasis?” — so too a speaker without listeners. “When Mahaprabhu came, hundreds of thousands flocked to
Him; let us see how many come now. The Mayavadis of Kashi reviled Mahaprabhu then, and Tapana Mishra and Chandrashekhara
suffered for it; let them come now — we shall see who is able.” And another time he said: “Wherever I go, sorrow goes
with me — the prema of separation.” <!-- F47 F48 -->

On his very first day in Kashi he himself chose the place for his tent. An old woman would not let him have it: “This
is my place, I have sat here twenty years!” He drove her off: “Her place! Her purchased ghata! So Nitai's own estate is
lost!” — and he sang: “Yama, run, run! In the domain of Nitai-chanda your turn is over.” The old woman tried to have
him driven out with the help of worldly people. The chronicle remarks: such an evil thought takes hold of a soul that
only pretends to live in Kashi and offends the feet of Shambhu — the king of Vaishnavas — and of the dhama itself; she
offended the feet of a paramahamsa, one of Gaura-Nityananda's own, as Ramachandra Khan once offended the feet of
Nityananda. <!-- F49 F50 -->

He sang: “One who has no connection with this — his life has passed in vain” — and added: “I have nothing to do with
all this; I see none of it.” This line he repeated later too. He sang also of intimate service: “Following the sakhis,
I shall take up loving service”, “The sakhi in the form of the guru is on the left; I shall fan with a chamara Him who
stands in the threefold-bending pose.” <!-- F51 -->

On 3 Karttika he suddenly wished to go straight to Vrindavana, ordered transport to be arranged and even agreed to the
train — on condition that, apart from him, not a single passenger travel on the entire train. When they came to say the
carriage had been booked, he made no reply, and long afterwards said: “I will not go now.” — “But everything is booked,
the money is paid!” — “Why did you pay? How should I know whether I shall go? You paid — very well; I will not go.”
That day from morning he smiled to himself and spoke much; with tears of joy he sang “Nitai and Gaura dance in the house
of Gauridasa”, and at times he spoke of Kalna and its Amli-tala. <!-- F52 F53 -->

When a bather cried out “Sita-Rama!”, Babaji remarked: “‘Sita-Rama’ lives on the throne, and ‘Hare-Rama’ on the bank of
the Sarayu.” And with a laugh: “All the sadhus get stuck at Gopala. There is no madhura-rasa now — I have no
madhura-rasa; here it is shanta, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, up to that.” And looking at a banana (rambha) before Thakura,
he played on the word: “Rambha — Rambhavati, Kalavati, Lilavati — that is in Puri.” When a servant brought water for
the service, he said to himself: “Without service the pure is not attained; Raya says: service is the way to the pure.”
And suddenly: “What use is the thread? Mahaprabhu Himself said:
“If a person has realised the truth about Krishna, he is a true Guru, whether he be a brahmana, a sannyasi or a
shudra”. (CC, Madhya 8.127)
And again: “In the service of Krishna birth or eminence is not taken into account at all”. (CC, Antya 4.67)”
<!-- F54 F55 Q1 Q2 -->

Once in Kashi a prakrita-sahajiya who had earlier sung lila-kirtana before him came to him. Chewing betel and laughing,
he shouted loudly “Jaya Nitai, Jaya Nitai!”, as if glorifying Babaji, and, breaking the flow of hari-katha, struck up
“The cowherd boy of Vraja has come”. A servant of the Sanatana Gaudiya Math stopped him and asked him to listen to
Babaji; feigning embarrassment, the man began to take his leave. Babaji raised his hand before his face: “Excessive
devotion is the mark of a thief!” — “How have I become a thief?” — “With your lips you say ‘Hari’, but in your mind
there is something else.” The mask fell, and the hypocrite left. <!-- F56 -->

A stout woman in saffron, assuming the air of a venerable elder, said: “Baba, I saw you at Baral Ghata in Navadvipa!
Bless me to chant the name of Hari much; I am a daughter-in-law of Advaita's family.” He replied: “Above three locks —
six, above them — sixteen, and still the thief breaks in and steals. A family has only one lock: what can it do? Go,
go to your Baral Ghata.” <!-- F57 -->

To a woman carrying bel leaves for the worship of Shiva he said: “I have seen, I have seen — this is in Navadvipa too,”
and, pointing to a Shiva-linga: “Here it is all the same. When Gaura was small, He used to go naked to the bathing
ghata of the Ganga. The girls would mould a Shiva-linga there and worship it, and He would joke with them: ‘Rather than
Shiva, won't you worship Me?’”[^7] <!-- F58 -->

When people asked him: “What will become of us, what are we to do?” — he sang: “Worship Nitai and you will attain
Gaura; joylessness will go, and the highest joy will dawn.” To some he answered only: “Hari, lover of His devotees!”
And once he said with a smile: “I have searched the whole world and not found a man after my own heart.” <!-- F59 -->

On Tuesday, 9 Karttika (26 October), he sang: “In Vrindavana You were the little butter thief — the stealer of cream,
butter, clothes and hearts,” and asked his servants: “Do you see the house of Chandrashekhara? Mahaprabhu lived there,
and now no one can even tell. This is Kashi of stone — Vishvanatha is not here.” On 11 Karttika (28 October) he said:
“This One (Sri Krishna) is a robber, and That One (Sri Gaurasundara) is the root of all cheats: He will not be caught!
From Gaya He came to Kashi; I came to Kashi — and He went back to Gaya. He has no home: He became a vairagi, left His
native land, dressed as a fakir — rags on His body, a kaupina about His loins.” <!-- F61 F62 -->

To an old woman in saffron he said: “The Doms herd pigs and keep them within bounds by a mantra — not one leaves the
herd, except perhaps one or two. So too are worldly people: they cannot leave the world, and if they do leave it, they
go to live in Kashi. But Vishvanatha is not in Kashi — this is Kashi of stone; he lives in the spiritual, golden Kashi,
and now roams the cremation grounds. Here on the ghata they have seated Hara-Gauri, set out a plate — and the coins pour
in! I saw the same in Navadvipa.” <!-- F63 -->

On Saturday, 13 Karttika (30 October), the day of his departure, he said: “Whoever hopes in You, You ruin utterly; to
one You give money, from another You take it away. ‘There is no escape from offence to Your feet’ — how will you stop
it? Who will save? To whom shall I speak, who will listen? I have not a drop of attachment to the Vaishnavas”[^8]. And
before setting off: “Renunciation and enjoyment, majesty and sweetness… I smell nothing — no fragrance: the flowers have
no scent, nor the fruits. ‘One moon dispels the darkness’ — what can many stars do? Is there a fragrant flower blooming
anywhere?” All that day he fasted, and at eleven at night the boat left Kashi. <!-- F64 F65 F60 -->

### Down the Ganga to Patna
On 14 Karttika (31 October) the tent was pitched under a banyan on the bank of the Ganga near the village of Saidpur,
twenty miles from Kashi. On 15 and 16 Karttika the boat drifted downstream by night. On 16 Karttika (2 November) he
said: “Mercy to living beings, taste for the Name, service to the Vaishnavas — apart from this there is no eternal
dharma.” To the Deity: “You have seen the Ganga of Kashi — now You will see the Ganga of Patna.” Near the village of
Ghoshpur in Ghazipur district milk was obtained for Thakura; a pumpkin without its stalk, brought by a villager, he did
not accept. Cooking after sunset, he sang to Thakura: “In sorrow and in joy we live in a boat: now ghee and honey, now
fasting. I shall bring wild greens and cook them without salt — put two or three morsels in Your mouth.” That day he
himself honoured prasada of only rice and boiled vegetables. <!-- F66 F67 -->

### Patna
On the morning of 18 Karttika (4 November), at Chhapra, the boat was tied to a steamer, and after three in the
afternoon he reached the ghata of the Engineering College in Patna; here the tent was pitched, and he stayed in Patna
until 15 November. On 19 Karttika he said: “They have locked Him up in prison. How much He used to eat — and now rice
of three chhataks. The same smell as in Kashi I have caught in Patna too.” On 20 Karttika, when a servant complained
that rats had eaten food left uncovered, he remarked: “Now they steal in order to eat, but give with respect — and they
will not eat.” He questioned a Ramanuji sadhu from Patna about Harihara-chhatra and wanted to go there, but the next
day changed his mind. <!-- F68 F69 F70 -->

On 21 Karttika (7 November) he taught: “Do not eat tasty food, do not wear fine clothes, do not chew betel, do not smoke
tobacco,” and he sang: “Leaving the comfort of sleep on an ornate bed, when shall I be covered with the dust of
Vraja?” Seeing an old sadhu after his bath, he joyfully quoted the words of Hanuman:
“The Lord of Sri and the Lord of Janaki are one and the same Supreme Soul; and yet my all in all is lotus-eyed Rama”.
And he told a legend: when Kali came, Krishna told Narada that it was time for Him to depart. “But what of the souls?”
asked Narada. “They will attain Me by feeding a brahmana sixteen dishes.” — “And the poor?” — “With flowers, water and
tulasi.” — “And those crippled from birth?” — “By uttering My name once at the end of the day with all their heart.”
<!-- F71 Q3 -->

A resident of Patna asked him for instruction. “What instruction?” Babaji replied. “A mother looks at her son's belly —
is he fed? A wife looks at her husband's waistband — has he brought money? From worldly life Krishna is far away.” —
“How then can one leave worldly life?” — “Sadhus live by the Sarayu and say ‘Sita-Rama’: there is joy there, no
joylessness. On Duryodhana's side is joylessness, on Yudhishthira's side happiness. Happiness and sorrow are two
brothers; enjoyment and renunciation — one enjoys, another renounces.” <!-- F73 -->

On 28 Karttika (14 November) he called out loudly: “Hari bol, Hari bol! Say ‘Gaura-Nitai’! So they say in Navadvipa and
in Mayapur. Around the temple in Mayapur there are houses, and the service is under a nim tree. Once I went to Mayapur
with a torn blanket and a water pot. Shachinandana Gosai came and took my pot; I sat. A little later Shachinandana Gosai
returned the pot and left; and I left too.” Shachinandana Gosai is Mahaprabhu Himself. <!-- F74 -->

On Monday, 29 Karttika (15 November), the day of departure, he offered balya-bhoga to the Deities in the morning, closed
the tent, quickly cooked, offered to Thakura and honoured prasada at three o'clock. The servants of the Gaudiya Math
bowed at his feet and sang songs of Sri Pancha-tattva — “Nitai-pada-kamala” and others — and he listened joyfully. Then
he boarded the boat with his Deities and set off at about half past six in the evening. On 30 Karttika the boat lay at
anchor near the village of Rupas, and on 2 Agrahayana (18 November) he was in great joy at the sight of the Ganga and in
the evening reached the ghata of Munger. <!-- F75 F44 -->

### Munger
On 3 Agrahayana (19 November) he had the tent pitched at Chandi Ghata, away from the town, and quoted a line from
Srila Narottama's “Prema-bhakti-chandrika”: “Hearing Govinda's roar, all will flee of themselves, as elephants flee at
the roar of a lion”[^9]. He asked Sri Gopala: “Where will You get curd and butter?” No curd could be found, and that day
he did not offer balya-bhoga but cooked greens. “In the house of Srila Narottama Thakura there are six thieves,” he
said, meaning the six Deities of Kheturi — Sri Gauranga, Sri Ballavikantha, Sri Krishna, Sri Brajamohana, Sri
Radhakanta and Sri Radharamana, — “and in Mayapur there is one thief, Sri Vishvambhara.” <!-- F76 Q4 -->

The next day he said: “Maya has seized all these people. The birds, too, eat and drink — and in them too is the urge of
the senses. There it is, maya!” And on 5 Agrahayana (21 November) he recalled the Lord's words: “The devotee is the
friend of My life, listen, Arjuna: through the mouth of the devotee I take a second meal,” and asked: who, then, eats
and who gives? These words he repeated later too. “You are going to the land the Pandavas left behind — we shall see.
Six Gosvamis — and six enemies, six thieves; six thieves in Narottama's house, one in Mayapur.” After the midnight
prasada he sang “Having conquered maya, you cannot leave her…”, “Be merciful, O Vaishnava Gosai…”, Narottama's song
“Gora pahun na bhajiya mainu” — “Not worshipping Gaura, my Lord, I perished…” — and others. <!-- F77 F78 Q6 -->

### Bhagalpur
On 6 Agrahayana (22 November) he sang joyfully from the morning, had a boat brought for Bhagalpur and set off at about
half past two, and at noon on 7 Agrahayana, Tuesday 23 November, he landed at Mayaganj Ghata in Bhagalpur[^10]. Here,
on the bank of the Ganga, under a vamshi-vata, a cloth tent was pitched, and in it he lived together with the Deities
of his bhava-seva. <!-- F60 F79 F28 -->

On 9 Agrahayana he asked how many days' journey it was from Bhagalpur to Godavari Ghata and what holy places lay along
the way. At night, at nearly one o'clock, he made an offering to Thakura, honoured prasada and talked long with Thakura.
And in the morning, seeing palms all around, he remembered Vrindavana: “Here is Talavana, here Khajuravana — stay a
little while in Bhagalpur,” and he sang of Talavana, Khajuravana and Madhuvana. <!-- F80 F23 -->

11 Agrahayana (27 November): “He performs ashtanga-pranama. Where there is fearlessness, there is fear too; it is hard
to be fearless.” And he sang: “I will not take, I will not take any other burden; I want neither gems nor ornaments”;
“Once I saw You in the womb — and now I seek and do not find You, Hari,” — “Hari, when shall I see You again?”
15 Agrahayana (1 December): “The intimate and the external: the intimate one does what is inward, the external one what
is outward — to each according to his mind. With Yashoda — up to shanta, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya. Where now in
Vrindavana are Yashoda and Krishna? Where are the sixteen thousand gopis? Who churns curd and butter now?” And seeing a
forest he sang: “Who is it that plays the flute beneath the Vamshi-vata in Bhandiravana?” <!-- F81 F82 -->

17 Agrahayana (3 December): “Where shall I go? I see no path; wherever I go, there I shall sit. Who performs this
service? You have set up house. Srila Narottama Thakura set up house — he founded six services,” that is, the service
of six Deities. And the next day he spoke of Majidpur, the land the Pandavas left behind. <!-- F83 F84 -->

20 Agrahayana (6 December): “A dwarf from birth — what use pulling at him? Maya holds us fast,” and he sang: “I have
fallen into the snare of the wicked, the whale of lust has swallowed me — O Lord, save me!”, “Having become maya's
servant, I nurse countless desires, and the memory of Your feet has gone far away”, “Apart from the mercy of the sadhu I
see no other means,” — “Who is this sadhu?” 22 Agrahayana (8 December), remembering Navadvipa: “At Navadvipa the Ganga
bends to the right — and here to the right as well; ahead a sandbank can be seen, as from Navadvipa one sees the
sandbank of Mayapur.” <!-- F85 F86 -->

On 24 Agrahayana (10 December) he taught: “Do not live with unbelievers,” and quoted a verse of Chanakya: a friend who
ruins your affairs behind your back but speaks sweetly to your face the wise man will reject, like a pot of poison with
milk only at its brim. “Whose company do you keep? ‘I have fallen into the snare of the wicked.’ What a maya! How to be
free? ‘I see not a trace of deliverance’, ‘the more I see, the more I suffer’, ‘so weeps the helpless one’ — and now I
cannot even weep. I see no path. What shall I answer when Yama comes? He will not listen to words!” 28 Agrahayana
(14 December): “In this land there is no Harinama, and yet I came here; I do not know where to go now. ‘The senses have
fallen under the sway of enemies…’ — what remedy is there now?” <!-- F87 F88 -->

30 Agrahayana (16 December): “One thing after another; three days have passed — let us see how many more.” He sang:
“With a sick body one does not serve Krishna: where there is illness, there the mind is”; “Fifty-six dandas of bhajana,
four of rest, and in sleep the darshana of Radha-Krishna”; “Association with sadhus is the essence of all scriptures:
from a moment's association with sadhus comes the darshana of Krishna”[^11], — “association with sadhus goes on — where
is the darshana of Krishna?” “At Gauranga's sweet pastimes even stone melts”; “I say ‘Gauranga’ — and my body
trembles; I say ‘Hari, Hari’ — and tears pour from my eyes,” — “Does the body tremble, do tears pour? Neither. Only
singing? The singing goes on.” “Piety is the abode of happiness, but do not utter its name; reject both piety and
liberation,” — “and yet the mind wants happiness.” <!-- F89 Q5 -->

Once several Marwaris persistently pressed him: “How long will the war go on?” He answered with annoyance: “I know
nothing of all that,” and the businessmen went away empty-handed. <!-- F90 -->

In Bhagalpur everything reminded him of the holy lands. The palm and date groves, Bhandiravana all around — this was
Vrindavana; the flow of the Ganga and its sandbanks — Navadvipa-dhama. “It is good, I am in solitude,” he said on
3 Pausha (19 December). “In Jyaishtha there is the fair at Sri Ramakeli; I shall go there — there will be much joy. But
these months must be spent here. Here too are the outlines of Navadvipa: ahead, the sandbank of Sri Mayapur; on the
other bank, Svarupganj; the Ganga flows south, as in Navadvipa. Talavana, Khajuravana, Bhandiravana, a bamboo grove — I
am happy beneath the Vamshi-vata.” Now laughing, now weeping, he was immersed in the joy of his realisation.
<!-- F91 F94 F28 -->

For several days he displayed — to outward eyes — the pastime of a cold and a cough, and acted as if in defiance of the
illness: he did not shield himself from the cold but embraced it. He ate curd and sour things, early in the morning
poured ice-cold water over his head from a clay pot inscribed with the names of the Ganga and the Yamuna, and morning
and evening, in bitter cold, he stayed with his body uncovered. The chronicle remarks: his every act is wondrous and
beyond the ordinary, lying outside human understanding. On 1 Pausha (17 December), early in the morning, in the cold,
he cooked and offered to Thakura khichri and sweet rice: every year in the month of Pausha he offered khichri each
morning. <!-- F92 F93 I2 -->

On 9 Pausha (25 December) some Marwari women came to him: they left a coin or two, looked, and went away. “I go to see
with my own eyes the One I love,” he said. “They came, looked and left — is that love? They left a coin or two!” The
next morning he told a servant to clear and water the ground by the little date palms near his dwelling, but not to cut
the branches. And again he recalled the second meal through the mouth of the devotee: “How will the second meal be? Where
is a devotee to be found? Who speaks of ‘madhukari-bhiksha’ — who will give and who will take? What fruit does the giver
seek? Alms are given, yes — but how am I to take madhukari? They have seated me here.” And loudly he told his servants:
“Chant Harinama!” <!-- F95 F96 F78 -->

On 13 Pausha he said: “‘With His devotees, in the rasas of love, He speaks of love’ — but there is no bhajana at all:
what talk of love can there be?” 15 Pausha (31 December): “I shall dash my head on a stone, I shall enter fire — where
shall I find Gauranga, the treasury of virtues?”; “Worship Gaura and you will attain Nitai; worship Nitai and you will
attain Gaura”; “The snake is wicked and the scoundrel is wicked — both are wicked, but the scoundrel no means can
tame.” <!-- F97 F98 -->

The last entry of the chronicle is Saturday, 16 Pausha, 1 January. “‘The senses are under the sway of enemies, I have
forgotten Gaura's feet’ — let us see what happens when one forgets Gaura's feet… ‘The touch of the Ganga purifies in
time, but Your darshana at once: such is Your nature’ — you have travelled along the Ganga; have you been purified? It
is darshana that purifies.” And of Narottama's song “Jivante marana bhela”: “Narottama sang: ‘Living, I have died’; what
use remaining longer? Seeing the load of my sins, even Gaura went away. ‘Always reject bad company’ — I shall leave it;
perhaps You will take me. I will not call — You Yourself must come; if You cannot come alone, bring Nitai; if not that,
bring the devotees; if You come in secret, come at least once into my heart; come once, let me see You.” And he sang:
“Gaurachandra, Gaurahari, come just once!” <!-- F99 Q6 -->

## Answers to Questions
Usually he was absorbed in his service, interfered in nothing and spoke little. To questions he mostly kept silent or
answered indirectly. When he instructed, he seldom quoted scripture — he spoke from his own realisation, in two or three
short phrases that deeply moved the listener. Visitors wrote down some of his answers. <!-- F100 -->

One man came constantly and kept asking how to attain God. For a long time Babaji did not answer, and then one day
suddenly asked: “What do you want?” — “To find God.” — “Weep.” To others he said: “There is no joy here until you
worship Gaura-Nitai: that is our eternal world, and yours is illusion; happiness here is like a baby's laughing or
crying in its sleep.” “The mercy of Krishna is attained by weeping. But who weeps truly? Shed tears of love and you will
attain the Lord's mercy,” and he would quote the line: “With my lips I say ‘Hari’, but I do otherwise — and so tears of
love do not flow from my eyes.” The same thought he flung in the face of the sahajiya in Kashi. <!-- F101 F102 F56 -->

Among the recorded answers are also those he gave on the road. To the question “What are we to do?” — “Worship Nitai
and you will attain Gaura.” To the question how to know the mercy of Krishna and the Vaishnavas — the words about Him
who ruins those who hope in Him, and about the inescapable offence to His feet. To the question where happiness lies —
in renunciation or in enjoyment — the answer about Duryodhana and Yudhishthira. To the question whether he had been to
Mayapur — the story of Shachinandana Gosai and the water pot: “some call this place Mayapur, some Navadvipa”. And to the
question how to be freed from the sway of the senses — the line about Govinda's roar. <!-- F59 F64 F73 F74 F76 -->

One gentleman came often and kept begging for mercy. One day, losing patience, Babaji took off his kaupina and held it
out to him: “You want my mercy? Here it is — take it!” — and the man recoiled in fright. We hear that all perfection
comes by the mercy of the Vaishnavas, but we lack the sincerity to accept that mercy; nor is there real feeling in our
singing of the songs of the mahajanas — we shake off the dust of the holy places without knowing its worth.

## Departure
Srila Vamshidasa Babaji Maharaja left this world on the fourth lunar day of the bright half of the month of Shravana
(shravana shukla chaturthi); by our calendar this is Gaura-chaturthi of the month of Shridhara, falling in August.
Neither the year nor the place of his departure, nor the site of his samadhi, have been preserved by the sources.
<!-- F105 -->

## Significance
The life of Srila Vamshidasa Babaji is an example of devotion that does not fit the measures of the outer world. His
every act is beyond the ordinary and incomprehensible to the worldly mind, and in his wanderings he poured out causeless
mercy and purified the earth. His sharpness with hypocrites and petitioners — with the sahajiya, the “daughter-in-law of
Advaita's family”, the wrestler with the amulet, the gentleman begging for mercy — is the same restraint for the good of
souls as that of Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji. Therefore Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, bowing to him from a distance, taught
his disciples not to judge him by the rules of a sadhaka, but to honour him as a paramahamsa whose heart belongs
wholly to Gaura-Nitai and Radha-Govinda. <!-- I2 I3 F16 F3 F4 -->
