# Paramananda Puri
*Парамананда Пури · @paramananda-puri*

## A Brahmana of Trihut
Sri Paramananda Puri was a sannyasi, a disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri, and one of Mahaprabhu’s closest companions
in Nilachala. Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, listing those who came into the world before the Lord, says of him:

“Sri Paramananda Puri, the intimate companion of Sri Chaitanya in Nilachala, was born in Trihut.” (CB, Adi 2.43)

Trihut, or Tirhut, is the northern part of present-day Bihar — the districts of Muzaffarpur, Darbhanga, Sitamarhi,
Champaran and Chhapra; in ancient times it was a kingdom of its own. Another disciple of Madhavendra, the scholar
Raghupati Upadhyaya, also came from Tirhut. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati speaks of Paramananda Puri first of all
as “a brahmana born in Trihut,” and in his “Anubhashya” on the “Charitamrita” writes of him: “a brahmana from Trihut,
a disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri and very dear to Mahaprabhu.” <!-- F3 F4 F11 Q2 -->

Nothing more is known of his worldly life. The sources name neither his parents nor the year and day of his
appearance, nor when and where he left this world; nor are the days of his appearance and disappearance found in
the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. We know only that he outlived Mahaprabhu: after the Lord’s
disappearance Srinivasa Acharya still found him in Puri. <!-- F5 -->

The “Charitamrita” calls him Paramananda Puri and Puri Gosani, and sometimes simply “Puri”; Vrindavana Dasa calls him
Puri Gosani and Puri Gosvami; Kavi Karnapura, in the drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” calls him Paramananda
Puri-ishvara and Puri-ishvara, “the lord of the Puris”; the biographies add the honorifics “Puripada” and “Sri
Paramananda Puripada.” <!-- F1 -->

## Uddhava
Who he is in the eternal pastimes of Krishna, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Sri Paramananda Puri was formerly Uddhava.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 118)

He who was Uddhava in Krishna’s pastimes descended into the pastimes of Gaura as Paramananda Puri, to enrich them
with his service. This is no rebirth: an eternal associate of the Lord descends into the world together with Him and
by His will. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, too, calls him Uddhava, and so do the Vaishnava vandanas: the “Vaishnava-vandana”
ascribed to Jiva Gosvami praises “Sri Puri Paramananda, called Uddhava, dear to Hari” (1.58), Devakinandana praises
him “who is Uddhava by nature” (2.46), and the vandana ascribed to the “second” Vrindavana Dasa, “him whom they call
Thakura Uddhava.” The same Sanskrit vandana speaks of the sannyasi companions of the Lord “headed by Sri Paramananda
Puri” (1.141).[^1] <!-- F2 Q1 F69 -->

## A Disciple of Madhavendra Puri
Paramananda Puri received initiation from Sri Madhavendra Puri. Vrindavana Dasa says of him:

“Sri Paramananda Puri was a dear disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri, and he was brimming with the sweetness of divine
love.” (CB, Antya 3.178)

So too do Murari Gupta — “a disciple of Sri Madhava Puri named Paramananda” (3.15.19), Lochana Dasa (CM,
Shesha-khanda, pt. 2, v. 136) and Karnapura — “a disciple of the lord of sages, Madhavendra Puri”
(“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” act 8). Among Madhavendra’s other disciples are named Ishvara Puri, the guru of
Mahaprabhu, Advaita Acharya, Brahmananda Puri, Sri Ranga Puri, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Raghupati Upadhyaya and
others. <!-- F6 F7 Q3 -->

The title “Puri” is the name of one of the ten orders of sannyasis, but Paramananda Puri was no Mayavadi renunciate
seeking to merge into the impersonal Brahman. He was a Vaishnava sannyasi in the line of Madhavendra, and his whole
wealth was prema, love for Krishna: Vrindavana Dasa calls him “the abode of pure love for Sri Chaitanya” (CB, Antya
3.175). <!-- F6 -->

As a disciple of Madhavendra he was a godbrother of Ishvara Puri, the Lord’s guru. Therefore Mahaprabhu showed him the
highest respect: He treated him as a very dear elder and honoured him as His own guru. It was his connection with
Madhavendra Puri, the biographies say, that made him an object of such reverence for the Lord. The Lord Himself, the
Master of all, is above every guru; but, setting an example for the world, He honoured the guru-varga — the circle
of His elders — and taught others by doing so. <!-- F8 I4 -->

## The Central Root of the Tree
Describing the tree of bhakti grown by Sri Chaitanya, Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami says:

“Glory to Sri Madhava Puri, brimming with love for Krishna! He is the first sprout of the wish-fulfilling tree of
bhakti. In the form of Sri Ishvara Puri the sprout grew strong, and then the gardener, Sri Chaitanya Himself, became
the trunk of the tree. By His own inconceivable power the gardener became the trunk, the original support of all the
branches.” (CC, Adi 9.10–12)[^2]

Herein lies the supreme secret of the tree: the Lord is both its gardener and its trunk, and all this by the power of
His inconceivable energy. And at the base of the tree nine roots sprang up — nine sannyasis:

“At the base of the tree nine roots sprang up: Paramananda Puri and Keshava Bharati, Brahmananda Puri and Brahmananda
Bharati, Vishnu Puri, Keshava Puri, Krishnananda Puri, Sri Nrisimha Tirtha and Sukhananda Puri. On them the tree stood
immovable. The central root is Paramananda Puri, a man of great steadfastness, and on these nine roots the tree stood
secure.” (CC, Adi 9.13–16)[^3]

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his “Anubhashya” makes this a subheading: “Paramananda Puri, the middle root.”

## The Meeting on Rishabha Hill
Mahaprabhu first met Paramananda Puri on His pilgrimage to the South. Leaving Purushottama, the Lord passed through
Kurmasthana, Jiyada-Nrisimha, Vidyanagara, where He conversed with Ramananda Raya, the Gautami-Ganga, Mallikarjuna,
Ahobala, Siddhavata, Skanda-kshetra, Trimatha, Vriddha-Kashi, Bauddhasthana, Tirupati, Trimalla, Pana-Nrisimha,
Shiva-Kanchi and Vishnu-Kanchi, Trikalahasti, Vriddhakola, Shiyali-Bhairavi, the banks of the Kaveri,
Kumbhakarna-kapala and Srirangam, where He spent the four months of the rains with Venkata Bhatta and blessed his
whole family. After the chaturmasya He went on further south — and came to Rishabha hill. <!-- F12 -->

Rishabha hill lies in the South of India, near Madurai. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta describes the place thus: twelve miles
north of Madurai are the Anagadamalai hills, in the forests around Kutakachala, where, according to tradition,
Rishabhadeva perished in a forest fire; today it is known as Palni Hill, and the local people call it
Varaha-parvata.[^4] <!-- F13 Q13 -->

The “Charitamrita” relates:

“Coming to Rishabha hill, Sri Gaurahari bowed down before the Deity in the temple of Lord Narayana and offered many
hymns and prayers. Hearing that Paramananda Puri was staying for the four months in a nearby village, Mahaprabhu went
to see him. With all respect Sri Chaitanya touched the feet of Paramananda Puri, and he embraced Him with great love.”
(CC, Madhya 9.167–169)

Paramananda Puri was observing the chaturmasya there, living in the house of a brahmana. The Lord too stayed in that
same house, and for three days they spoke of Krishna, filled with the bliss of prema (Madhya 9.170). Krishnadasa
Kaviraja mentions this meeting in his summary of the pastimes as well: “Travelling through South India, He met
Paramananda Puri” (Madhya 1.111). <!-- F14 F15 F16 -->

On parting, Puri said that he was going to Purushottama to see Jagannatha, and from there to Gauda to bathe in the
Ganga. Then the Lord asked him:

“‘Please come back again to Jagannatha Puri afterwards. I shall reach Setubandha and soon come there too. I wish to be
always in your company, and you will show Me mercy if you return to Nilachala.’ Then, with Paramananda Puri’s
consent, Sri Chaitanya happily continued His journey through the South. So Paramananda Puri set out for Nilachala,
and Mahaprabhu for Sri Shaila.” (CC, Madhya 9.172–175)

The Lord wished Puri to come to Nilachala and stay there with Him for good. <!-- F17 Q5 -->

Other original sources tell of this meeting differently and with new details.[^5] According to Murari Gupta, the
Lord met Paramananda Puri on the road from Ranga-kshetra:

“Seeing the form of Gauranga, Sri Paramananda Puri remembered his guru’s words, and was adorned with tears of love
and thrilling hair. And the Lord too, the guardian of dharma, in supreme delight fell down like a stick, His head to
the ground, before Puripada and his servant. In alarm Puri said, ‘You should not do so! You alone are the Lord of the
worlds, the One who awakens consciousness in the world. I know who You are: You are Bhagavan Himself, who has taken
the form of a devotee of Sri Krishna; You have assumed the mood of Sri Radha and thirst for the rasa of sweetness.’
Hearing his words, Krishna laughed and replied respectfully, ‘Know that My heart is bound by your love — of this
there is no doubt. Go to the most delightful Kshetra and stay there until I return.’” (Murari Gupta, 3.15.20–25)

Lochana Dasa says that, looking at the Lord, Paramananda Puri remembered his guru’s prophecy. Madhavendra had once
told him: in the first twilight of Kali the Lord of Lakshmi will appear — a golden-bodied sannyasi dwelling near the
Daru-brahma; “I have not the good fortune to see Him with my own eyes; when you see Him, remember me.” And Puri
understood firmly: this is Bhagavan Himself. In tears he kept repeating “Madhavendra!” — and the Lord, hearing the
name of Madhavendra, danced and wept in bliss:

“‘Today My sannyasa has borne fruit: the name of Madhavendra has entered My ears.’” (CM, Shesha-khanda, pt. 2,
v. 142+4)[^6]

Puri bowed to Him, and the Lord, saying “What are you doing?”, lifted him up and embraced him tightly (vv. 134–144).
Lochana Dasa places the meeting after the chaturmasya, on the road from Trimalla; so too does a biography that
retells his poem: the Lord spent four months in Srirangam, then met Paramananda Puri, who offered prayers to Gaura.
<!-- F18 -->

Kavi Karnapura in his mahakavya makes a riddle of his name: the Lord met the best of ascetics, at the beginning of
whose name was “Parama,” in the middle “ananda,” and at the end “Puri”; then Paramananda went to Puri, and the Lord to
the Setu (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 13.14–16). Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” writes that on Rishabha
hill, near Narayana, “the ascetic Paramananda” spent the four months of the rains, and that the Lord met and honoured
him (10.12–13); the “Karcha” of Govinda Dasa likewise says, “On Rishabha hill lives Paramananda Puri,” and the Lord
hastened to see him (6.1100–1102). <!-- F18 F68 -->

## Through Gauda to Nilachala
After a few more days on Rishabha hill, Paramananda Puri went, as he had intended, to the Ganga, and, walking along
its bank, came to Navadvipa:

“Shortly before this Paramananda Puri had come from the South, walking from village to village along the bank of the
Ganga. He stayed in the house of mother Shachi, and she respectfully fed him.” (CC, Madhya 10.91–92)

He lived in Mayapur, in the house of Shachimata, and took his meals from her hands, and she looked after him with
great love. <!-- F19 F21 -->

Meanwhile Mahaprabhu had returned from the South to Puri. Nityananda, Jagadananda, Mukunda and Damodara resolved to
inform Shachimata and the Bengali devotees of His return, and, with the Lord’s permission, sent to Gauda Kala
Krishnadasa, the Lord’s companion on His southern journey (CC, Madhya 10.67–72).[^7] The news spread through Gauda,
and Advaita Acharya and the other devotees, taking leave of Shachi, began to prepare for the journey to Nilachala
(10.88). <!-- F20 -->

Paramananda Puri too heard the news. The devotees’ preparations were dragging on, and he could not wait to see the
Lord; so he hurried to Puri alone, taking with him only the Bengali brahmana Kamalakanta, a devotee of Sri Chaitanya
(CC, Madhya 10.93–94). Thus these two were the first from Bengal to come to Mahaprabhu. <!-- F22 -->

“Soon he met Mahaprabhu, and the Lord was overjoyed to see him. In the bliss of prema Sri Chaitanya offered prayers
at his feet, and Paramananda Puri embraced Him with love. Sri Chaitanya said, ‘Please show Me mercy and stay in
Jagannatha Puri, so that I may always be in your company.’ Paramananda Puri replied, ‘I too wish to be with You.
That is why I have come to Nilachala from Bengal. All the devotees of Bengal were very happy to hear of Your return
from South India. They are all preparing to come and see You, but their preparations were taking long, and I decided
to set out without delay.’” (CC, Madhya 10.95–100)[^8] <!-- F23 Q6 F22 -->

The Lord lodged him in a secluded room in the house of Kashi Mishra and gave him a servant (CC, Madhya 10.101). Soon
Svarupa Damodara came; having bowed to the Lord and to Nityananda, he respectfully bowed at the feet of Paramananda
Puri too, and Puri embraced him with love (10.128). And Ramananda Raya, returning to the Lord in Puri, paid his
respects to those gathered around Him — Nityananda, Paramananda Puri and others. <!-- F24 -->

## How the Lord Received Him in Puri
Vrindavana Dasa Thakura tells of Paramananda Puri’s coming in his own way. Some time later, having travelled to the
holy places, Paramananda Puri arrived; the Lord, catching sight of him from afar, respectfully rose to meet him.

“The Lord was overjoyed to see His dear devotee. He offered him due respect and began to dance in ecstatic love.
Raising His arms, the Lord cried out: ‘Hari! Hari! At last I have seen Paramananda Puri. Today My eyes and My whole
life have become perfect. Today I have gained the fruit of fulfilling all My religious duties.’ The Lord went on:
‘Today I have received the fruit of accepting sannyasa, for Madhavendra Puri has appeared before Me.’ Saying this,
the Lord embraced His dear devotee, whose body was at once drenched with the tears flowing from Mahaprabhu’s lotus
eyes. Paramananda Puri too was filled with bliss and forgot himself on seeing the moonlike face of the Lord. Thus
they offered respects to each other. Paramananda Puri was the abode of pure love for Sri Chaitanya.” (CB, Antya
3.169–175)[^9]

The Lord, supremely pleased, accepted him among His intimate companions, and Puri, having found his Lord, joyfully
served His feet (CB, Antya 3.176–177). One of the biographies says that from then on, whenever Mahaprabhu caught
sight of Puripada even from afar, He would respectfully rise.[^10] <!-- F25 F26 Q7 -->

Kavi Karnapura in his drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” paints this meeting differently. The Lord, thinking of
Paramananda Puri, calls him a disciple of Madhavendra “into whom, they say, has entered all the divine power” of His
elder brother Vishvarupa.[^11] In Karnapura, Puri comes from Varanasi and longs so for the Lord that he hurries to
Him before the darshan of Jagannatha, begging Jagannatha’s forgiveness: “You, the all-knowing, know Yourself what
this thirst is.” The Lord rises, bows to him and asks: “Svamin, are you Puri-ishvara?” (act 8). In the same act
Karnapura plays on his name: the call offstage about “the lord of Paramananda-puri” bearing his staff refers both to
Jagannatha, Lord of “the City of Supreme Bliss,” and to the sannyasi with his danda. <!-- F25 F61 -->

In Karnapura’s mahakavya, Paramananda Puri, wandering, comes to the Ganga, to Navadvipa, and stays in the Lord’s
house. Shachi, “the mother of the three worlds,” honoured him with alms as her own son; the next day he accepted alms
from Acharyaratna. Kamalananda, a beloved servant of the Lord, came there too, and after a few days they set out for
Puri together with Gadadhara Pandita and Jagadananda; and the Lord Himself bowed to Puri, “showing him great respect
as an elder” (13.110–125). Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” likewise says that he came before the Snana-yatra
with Kamalananda (11.28–29), and the “Karcha” of Govinda Dasa names him among the sannyasis who gathered around the
Lord in Nilachala (3.501–504).[^12] <!-- F25 F68 -->

## Life with the Lord
From then on Paramananda Puri lived constantly with Mahaprabhu. He spent every chaturmasya with Him and took part in
all His pastimes. At first he lived in Kashi Mishra’s house, and later settled in his own matha west of the
Jagannatha temple, where he also dug a well; Vrindavana Dasa calls the place “the matha of Puri Gosani” (CB, Antya
3.232). He lived for the Lord alone, and the Lord held him dear as life itself; every day Puripada went first to see
the Lord and only then turned to his other affairs. <!-- F27 F28 F29 -->

Vrindavana Dasa writes that among the sannyasis around the Lord none was so dear to Him:

“Among the sannyasi companions of Sri Chaitanya there was none dearer to the Lord than Paramananda Puri. Paramananda
Puri and Svarupa Damodara were the two most eminent devotees among all Mahaprabhu’s sannyasi companions. These two
were always inseparable from Mahaprabhu. They took sannyasa to assist His pastimes as a sannyasi. Paramananda Puri
was always absorbed in meditation, and Sri Svarupa always sang kirtana. These two sannyasis were like the arms of
Lord Chaitanya.” (CB, Antya 10.46–49)

Puri’s “meditation” is not impersonal contemplation but absorption in the remembrance of Krishna. And Svarupa, says
Vrindavana Dasa, the Lord loved just as He loved Paramananda Puri (10.42); elsewhere he calls the two of them “the
chief companions of the Lord in His final pastimes” (Antya 3.181), and already at the beginning of his book, in the
summary, he mentions how they settle in Puri to live with the Lord (Adi 1.161). <!-- F30 F59 Q7 -->

With Paramananda Puri the Lord had a special intimacy:

“The Lord and Paramananda Puri had a very confidential relationship, exactly like that of two close friends, Krishna
and Arjuna. The Lord always delighted in Paramananda Puri’s company, holding confidential conversations with him
about Krishna.” (CB, Antya 3.233–234) <!-- F31 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja says the same, listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya’s tree in Puri:

“Of all the devotees who lived with the Lord in Nilachala, two were foremost, the Lord’s very heart: Paramananda Puri
and Svarupa Damodara.” (CC, Adi 10.124–126)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta makes this a subheading of his “Anubhashya”: “The foremost — (1) Sri Paramananda and (2) Sri
Svarupa.”[^13] Paramananda Puri is named among those who settled with the Lord in Puri (Madhya 1.252–254) and among
the companions with whom the Lord went every morning for the darshan of Jagannatha (Madhya 15.184). <!-- F32 Q10 -->

And in the conversation with Ramananda Raya, Krishnadasa Kaviraja reveals which rasas the Lord tasted with His
closest ones:

“Sri Chaitanya relished parental love with Paramananda Puri, friendship with Ramananda Raya, servitude with Govinda
and others, and the rasa of conjugal love with Gadadhara, Jagadananda and Svarupa Damodara.” (CC, Madhya 2.78)

According to one of the biographies, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains here that Paramananda Puri, who was Uddhava in
Krishna’s pastimes, loved Mahaprabhu with parental love. Both Puri’s vatsalya and his friendly intimacy with the
Lord, like that of Krishna and Arjuna, are not worldly feelings of a “father” or a “companion,” but the pure rasas of
an eternal associate, which the Lord Himself relishes. <!-- F33 F31 Q10 -->

Vrindavana Dasa repeatedly glorifies the Lord precisely as the treasure of Paramananda Puri: “Glory to Him who has
become the wealth of the heart of Sri Paramananda Puri” (CB, Adi 14.2); “Glory to Gaurasundara, dearer than life
itself to Paramananda Puri and Svarupa Damodara!” (Madhya 6.4); “You are the very life of Sri Paramananda Puri!”
(Madhya 11.2); “All glory to Him who is the life and soul of Paramananda Puri!” (Antya 7.3). So too His devotees sing
of the Lord: Raghunatha dasa Gosvami in the “Shachisunu-ashtaka” praises the Lord “beloved of Paramananda Puri,
accepting Govinda’s service, surrounded by Svarupa’s love” (2–3), and Narahari in a song sees the Lord in Nilachala
holding sankirtana on the seashore “with Svarupa, Rupa, Ramananda, Govinda and Paramananda.” <!-- F57 F70 Q12 -->

## The Well Filled with the Ganga
Vrindavana Dasa, “the Vyasa of the Lord’s pastimes,” glorifies Paramananda Puri’s well especially in the third
chapter of the Antya-khanda of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata.” Puripada had a well dug by his matha, but its water turned
out to be bad, and this grieved him. One day the Lord came to the matha of Puri Gosani and sat down beside him. The
indwelling Lord of all, He knew Himself that the water was unfit, but, as if in passing, He asked Puri how the water
in the well was. And Puri replied: “This well is most unfortunate: its water has become like thick mud.”[^14]
<!-- F34 F35 Q7 -->

“Hearing this, Chaitanya said: ‘Alas, Lord Jagannatha has become a miser. Even the most sinful of sinners is
liberated if he touches the water of Paramananda Puri’s well. Therefore the maya of Lord Jagannatha has spoiled this
water, and now no one drinks it.’” (CB, Antya 3.238–240)

This is a loving jest of the Lord, and it glorifies the well and Puri himself. So holy is this devotee’s water that
whoever touches or drinks it will be purified and liberated — and so Jagannatha, as it were, “grew miserly”: He did
not want liberation to be had so easily, and made the water muddy Himself. “Jagannatha’s maya” here is not worldly
illusion with which the Lord might harm people, but His will and internal potency, yogamaya, which serves His
pastimes.[^15] <!-- F36 -->

“Then Mahaprabhu stood, raised His arms and said: ‘O Lord Jagannatha, grant Me this boon: let the Ganga enter this
well. Please command the Bhogavati Ganga, who flows through Patala, to enter this well.’” (CB, Antya 3.241–243)

So the Lord asked Jagannatha for mercy on the jivas. Hearing these words, the devotees loudly sang the names of
Hari, and the Lord went home, and all lay down to sleep.[^16]

“Then, obeying the Lord’s command, Gangadevi entered this well and filled it. The next morning all were astonished:
Paramananda Puri’s well was filled with crystal-clear water.” (CB, Antya 3.246–247)

The Ganga, who springs from the Lord’s feet, at His command invisibly entered the well of His devotee. The devotees
sang the name of Hari, and Puri Gosvami fainted with bliss. Understanding that the Ganga had come into the well, all
began to bow to it and walk around it (CB, Antya 3.248–249). <!-- F37 F38 Q7 -->

Hearing of this, the Lord hurried to the well and, seeing the clear water, was filled with great joy:

“The Lord said: ‘Listen, devotees: whoever bathes in the water of this well will certainly obtain the benefit of
bathing in the Ganga: he will attain pure loving service to Krishna.’” (CB, Antya 3.251–252)[^17]

The Lord Himself, in great jubilation, drank this water and bathed in it (3.254), and, as one of the biographies
says, did so often. And then He said of Puri Gosani: <!-- F39 F40 Q7 -->

“‘Know that I remain on earth only out of love for Puri Gosani. I belong to Puri Gosvami — have no doubt of it. If he
wishes, he may even sell Me. Anyone who sees Puri Gosvami even once will become worthy of love for Krishna.’” (CB,
Antya 3.255–257)[^18]

So speaks of Himself the Lord who is subject to the love of His devotees. He is the independent Master of all, and
no one can “sell” Him; but by His own will He gives Himself to those who love Him — this is His bhakta-vatsalya,
His tenderness towards His devotees, not dependence. <!-- F41 Q7 -->

“Having told everyone of the glory of Paramananda Puri and sanctified his well, the Lord returned home. Only the
Supreme Lord knows how to increase the glory of His devotees.” (CB, Antya 3.258–259)

Thus Lord Mahaprabhu sang the glory of His devotee. The Ganga in Puri’s well is not a miracle for its own sake, but
the Lord’s mercy towards His servant and towards all jivas. As the devotee glorifies the Lord, so the Lord glorifies
and exalts His devotee; to those indifferent to the Lord this glory is incomprehensible. The Lord glorifies His
devotees so that all may understand: without their company and their mercy the jiva cannot attain any good. <!-- I2 -->

Paramananda Puri’s well is in Puri to this day. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, according to one of the biographies, writes
in his commentary on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” that it lies not far from the Jagannatha temple, on the road leading
west, near the police station, and that Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura identified it as the very well of which
Vrindavana Dasa speaks.[^19] <!-- F42 Q13 -->

## Intercessor for the Junior Haridasa
Paramananda Puri’s seniority among the Lord’s companions is vividly seen in the story of the Junior Haridasa, a
singer with the Lord in Puri. For speaking with a woman Mahaprabhu rejected him and forbade him to enter His house:

“‘From now on My house is closed to the Junior Haridasa. Do not let him in here any more.’” (CC, Antya 2.113)

Haridasa fasted for three days. Svarupa Damodara and other close devotees came to plead for him, but the Lord
answered them sternly and told them never to speak of it again (CC, Antya 2.115–126). Then on the next day all the
Vaishnavas went to Paramananda Puri: the Lord honoured him, His guru’s godbrother, as a guru, and they hoped that
his word would help.[^20] <!-- F43 F44 -->

“Then Paramananda Puri went in person to Sri Chaitanya. Offering him respect, the Lord seated him beside Himself and
asked: ‘Tell Me, what brings you to Me? What can I do for you?’ And Sri Paramananda Puri began to beg Him to have
pity on the Junior Haridasa and forgive him. Hearing this request, Sri Chaitanya said: ‘My lord, listen! You stay
here with all the Vaishnavas, and allow Me to go to Alalanatha. I shall live there alone, with Govinda only.’ So
saying, Sri Chaitanya called Govinda, offered His respects to Paramananda Puri, rose and left the house.” (CC,
Antya 2.129–133)

The Lord did not grant the request, yet neither would He argue with an elder: honouring him, He chose rather to
leave Himself.[^21] Puri was abashed; he hastily stood before the Lord and humbly begged Him to come back into the
room:

“Paramananda Puri prayed to Sri Chaitanya: ‘O wholly independent Lord! You may do whatever You wish. Who can tell You
how to act? You do everything only for the good of all people. But we can never fathom what is in the depths of Your
heart.’” (CC, Antya 2.135–136)

With these words Puri went home. The Lord did not forgive Haridasa: from then on he could only see Him from afar,
when the Lord went to Jagannatha (2.137–142). <!-- F45 F46 Q11 -->

Puri’s humility in saying, “You are the independent Lord,” is not the failure of an intercessor but an example for
all: the elder whom the Lord honours as a guru himself bows before the Lord’s will. And the Lord’s severity is not
anger but a lesson to the world, as Krishnadasa Kaviraja says:

“Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is a boundless ocean of causeless mercy. But who can understand Him? Sometimes He punishes
His devotees, but only to establish in people’s hearts the highest standard of life, dharma.” (CC, Antya 2.143)
<!-- F46 -->

And by honouring Puri even while refusing him, the Lord taught something more: the guru’s godbrother deserves the
same reverence as the guru, and disregard for the elders, the guru-varga, is ruinous for bhakti. Of this the Lord
also speaks to Sanatana:

“‘Jagadananda Pandita is dear to Me, but you, Sanatana, are far dearer. Yet I cannot tolerate any transgression of
spiritual etiquette.’” (CC, Antya 4.166)[^22] <!-- F47 I1 Q8 -->

## At the Festivals of Nilachala
Paramananda Puri was beside the Lord in almost all His pastimes in Purushottama: at the cleansing of the Gundicha
temple, at the Ratha-yatra, in the water-sports on Lake Narendra, at the festival after the passing of Haridasa
Thakura. The Gaudiya Vaishnavas and the people of Puri all revered him as an elder worthy of worship. <!-- F48 F51 -->

When the Lord and His devotees washed the Gundicha temple before the Ratha-yatra, He did not ask the elders, the
guru-varga, to carry water: they washed the temple with water brought by others.

“All except Nityananda, Advaita, Damodara, Bharati and Paramananda Puri filled the pots and carried water.” (CC,
Madhya 12.109)

Damodara here is Svarupa Damodara, and Bharati is Brahmananda Bharati. <!-- F49 Q9 -->

At the Ratha-yatra, when the chariot stopped, the Lord honoured the elders before all others, applying sandal paste
to their foreheads with His own hand and adorning them with garlands:

“Paramananda Puri and Brahmananda Bharati, receiving garlands and sandal paste from the hands of Sri Chaitanya, felt
great bliss.” (CC, Madhya 13.30) <!-- F50 Q9 -->

In the water-sports on the Narendra, says Vrindavana Dasa, “Nityananda, Gadadhara and Puri Gosvami began to fight one
another in the water, but none of them was ever wholly defeated” (CB, Antya 8.122). Together with Nityananda and
Gadadhara, Puri went with the Lord to meet Advaita and the Gaudiya devotees, and Vrindavana Dasa names him in the
list of the Lord’s companions in Puri (CB, Antya 8.55–61). And at the festival in memory of Haridasa Thakura, “Sri
Chaitanya sat down beside Paramananda Puri and Brahmananda Bharati and began to eat. Seeing this, all the Vaishnavas
began to eat as well” (CC, Antya 11.87). <!-- F48 F59 -->

When the Gaudiya devotees had gone home after the Ratha-yatra, Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya invited the Lord and the ten
sannyasis who were always with Him to his home for a whole month: Paramananda Puri, Svarupa Damodara, Brahmananda
Puri, Brahmananda Bharati, Vishnu Puri, Keshava Puri, Krishnananda Puri, Nrisimha Tirtha, Sukhananda Puri and
Satyananda Bharati (CC, Madhya 15.193). Five days he set aside for the Lord Himself and five for Paramananda Puri;
Svarupa, Sarvabhauma’s close friend, would come both with the Lord and on his own, and the other eight sannyasis
twice each (15.194–196).[^23] For five days Paramananda Puri alone was feasted with mahaprasada in Sarvabhauma’s
house. <!-- F52 Q12 -->

The “Charitamrita” mentions him in many other scenes. He comes to the Lord soon after His return to Puri (Madhya
1.129); the sannyasis headed by him wait for the Lord to dine after His meeting with the Gaudiya devotees, and the
Lord eats together with him, Brahmananda Bharati and Nityananda (Madhya 11.33, 11.197–211); he is with the Lord at
the meal in the garden by the Gundicha (12.156); at the Netrotsava he and Brahmananda Bharati walk before the Lord
(12.208);[^24] he comes to Advaita’s feast (14.92); he is named first among the companions with whom the Lord sets
out from Puri to Gauda on the way to Vrindavana (16.127); on the Lord’s return from Vrindavana he and Brahmananda
Bharati are the first to bow to Him at the Narendra-sarovara (25.220). The Lord introduces Sanatana Gosvami to him
(Antya 4.104–108); the sannyasis headed by him partake of Vallabha Bhatta’s maha-prasada (Antya 7.62); the Lord sends
prasada to him and to Brahmananda Bharati (Antya 16.105); and the cloth-prasada from Jagannatha’s festival of the
cowherd pastimes the Lord, “on the advice of Paramananda Puri,” would send to His mother (Antya 19.12).[^25]
<!-- F58 -->

Murari Gupta names him among the companions who went out with the Lord to the Narendra-sarovara to meet the Gaudiya
devotees (4.17.17), among those who were with the Lord when a poor brahmana from Dravida came to Him, “in the circle
of Svarupa and his companions, Sri Paramananda Puri, Ramananda and others” (4.21.12), and among His companions in
Puri (4.24.13). Lochana Dasa adds that it was Puri Gosani who asked the Lord about this poor brahmana (CM,
Shesha-khanda, pt. 7, vv. 85–86). <!-- F63 F65 -->

In Karnapura’s “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” the Lord, returning from Vrindavana, meets Paramananda Puri and Svarupa and
addresses him: “Teacher!” (act 9); in the tenth act the Lord sits with Puri-ishvara, talks with him and Svarupa
about the king and about Advaita, goes with him to meet the Gaudiya devotees, and of Shivananda Sena’s little son
says to him: “Svami, this is your servant” — a hint at the boy’s name, Puri-dasa, “servant of Puri”: so the author
of the drama himself was called as a child. In Karnapura’s mahakavya Paramananda Puri at the Ratha-yatra drinks in
the beauty of the dancing Lord, bathed in tears (16.30). <!-- F62 F64 -->

## Ramachandra Puri
Once Ramachandra Puri, another disciple of Madhavendra, came to Puri. The Lord and Paramananda Puri honoured him as a
disciple of their guru:

“Paramananda Puri offered respects at the feet of Ramachandra Puri, and he embraced him tightly. Sri Chaitanya too
bowed to him, and he embraced Him as well, remembering Krishna. Then the three of them talked of many spiritual
matters. After this Jagadananda Pandita invited this sannyasi to his home.” (CC, Antya 8.6–8)

Ramachandra Puri, however, looked for faults in everything and reproached the Lord for eating much. The Lord began
to eat half as much as before. Then Paramananda Puri, together with all the devotees, humbly entreated Him:

“‘My godbrother Ramachandra Puri constantly criticises everyone, for such is his nature. What is the use of Your
giving up food because of his reproaches?’” (CC, Antya 8.70)

Ramachandra Puri, he said, himself makes people overeat and then reproaches them; of the two rules of the
“Bhagavatam” — not to praise and not to blame others (11.28.1) — he keeps only the first: he praises no one but
blames everyone; in one who has a hundred virtues he sees none. “Accept all our invitations as before,” Puri begged
(Antya 8.71–81). The Lord defended Ramachandra Puri, but, yielding to their entreaties, began to eat half of what He
had eaten before (8.82–84). Thus Paramananda Puri, while honouring his godbrother, saw his fault clearly and cared
only that the service of the Lord should not suffer. <!-- F53 F54 Q11 -->

## Chataka-parvata
Once, seeing the sand-hill Chataka-parvata in Puri, the Lord took it for Govardhana and ran towards it in ecstasy.
The devotees rushed after Him:

“Brahmananda Bharati and Paramananda Puri ran too. And even the lame Bhagavan Acharya ran slowly after all the
devotees.” (CC, Antya 14.90)

Puri and Bharati arrived later, when the Lord was already coming to Himself:

“Meanwhile Paramananda Puri and Brahmananda Bharati arrived there. Seeing them, Sri Chaitanya tried to pay them
respects. Coming fully to His senses, He at once offered them prayers, and they embraced Him with love. Then Sri
Chaitanya asked them: ‘Why have you come so far?’ Paramananda Puri answered Him: ‘To see Your dance.’ Mahaprabhu was
greatly embarrassed on hearing these words.” (CC, Antya 14.113–116) <!-- F56 Q12 -->

## On the Way to Gauda, and in Other Traditions
When the Lord set out from Puri for Gauda, Paramananda Puri went with Him. Karnapura in his mahakavya tells of this
journey in detail: with the Lord go Govinda, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita and Paramananda Puri (19.6); the
sanctified cloth received from Prataparudra the Lord sent to His mother, accepting Puri’s advice (19.9); at dawn
“Puri-prabhu” covered with a cloth the mark of a blow from a book on Damodara Pandita’s back (19.30); when the Lord
nearly fell on thorns, Puri and others caught Him (19.47); the Lord stays with him in a freshly plastered house
(19.55); the Lord and Puri walk in front, Ramananda behind (19.60); a local brahmana invites Puri to a meal (19.70).
And when people asked, “Where is Krishna-Chaitanya?”, the Lord and Puri in jest pointed at each other (20.7). From
Raghava’s abode the Lord sent “the elder” ahead to Navadvipa — apparently Paramananda Puri (20.13). <!-- F64 -->

Lochana Dasa relates that when, on this journey, the Lord unexpectedly turned back from Natashala Kanai, Paramananda
Puri asked Him the reason, and the Lord explained: the wish of Nrisimhananda’s mind had been fulfilled — he had paved
a road for Him in his mind from Purushottama to Mathura, but had brought it only as far as Natashala Kanai (CM,
Shesha-khanda, pt. 2, section “The Lord Sees Vrindavana,” v. 22). According to Lochana too, Puri Gosani, with the
devotees in Kashi Mishra’s house, begged the Lord to receive King Prataparudra, who had fasted for ten days, and the
Lord agreed (pt. 6, vv. 92–110).[^26] Lochana Dasa also praises him at the beginning of the poem — “Puri Paramananda
and Vishnupuri … I praise” (Sutra-khanda, pt. 1, v. 26) — and calls him “Paramananda Puri of pure conduct”
(Sutra-khanda, pt. 8, v. 624). <!-- F65 -->

Jayananda, in the opening vandana of his “Chaitanya-mangala,” bears on his head “Srinivasa, Paramananda Puri, Vishnu
Puri, Madhavendra Puri, Ishvara Puri,” and, describing the memorial festival for Madhavendra Puri at Advaita’s house
in Shantipura, says: “Damodara Svarupa and Paramananda Puri direct the feast, canes in hand.” The late
“Prema-vilasa,” to be approached with caution, reports that Madhava Acharya (Kavivallabha), who had fled from his
wedding to Vrindavana, took sannyasa from Paramananda Puri and afterwards learned bhajana from Rupa and Sanatana
(24.436; 25.891).[^27] <!-- F66 F67 -->

## After the Lord’s Disappearance
Paramananda Puri outlived Mahaprabhu. When, after the Lord’s disappearance, Srinivasa Acharya came to Puri, he stayed
with Gadadhara Pandita and met there Ramananda Raya, Sarvabhauma, Vakreshvara, Paramananda Puri, Shikhi Mahiti,
Govinda, Shankara, Gopinatha Acharya and others; all of them, plunged in separation from the Lord, had almost given
up food and sleep, and all of them blessed him (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 3). When and where Paramananda Puri himself
passed away is not known. <!-- F55 F5 -->

According to the “Sri Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” Paramananda Puri wrote a book, the “Govinda-vijaya.” Jayananda
speaks of it still earlier: “Sri Paramananda Puri Gosani, the venerable, set [this] out briefly in the
‘Govinda-vijaya.’” The book itself has not survived — at least, it is unknown to us. <!-- F60 -->

## Significance
Paramananda Puri is the Uddhava of Krishna’s pastimes, the central root of the tree of bhakti, and one of the “two
arms” of the Lord as a sannyasi. The Lord Himself says that He remains on earth out of love for him, and promises
love for Krishna to anyone who sees him even once. Together with Svarupa Damodara he was the very life of Sri
Chaitanya in Nilachala, and with him the Lord tasted the sweetness of parental love. <!-- F2 F9 F30 F32 F41 F33 -->

Through him the Lord showed the world how to honour one’s elders. His connection with Madhavendra Puri made
Paramananda Puri an object of the highest reverence for the Lord: the Lord bowed to him, rose to meet him, was the
first to adorn him with sandal paste and garlands, and even when refusing his request did not forget to honour him.
The guru’s godbrother deserves the same reverence as the guru, and disregard for the guru-varga is ruinous for
bhakti. <!-- I1 I4 -->

And through him the Lord showed how He glorifies His devotees. The Ganga in Puri’s well, the words “I belong to
him,” and the promise of love for Krishna to those who see him — all this the Lord says and does so that the world
may understand: without the company of devotees and their mercy the jiva has no good. And the supreme secret of the
tree of bhakti of which the “Charitamrita” speaks is that the Lord Himself is both the gardener and the trunk on which
all the branches rest; and among the roots on which the tree is established, the central one is Paramananda Puri.
