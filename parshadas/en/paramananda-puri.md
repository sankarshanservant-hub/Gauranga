# Paramananda Puri
*Парамананда Пури · @paramananda-puri*

## A Brahmana of Trihut
Sri Paramananda Puri was a sannyasi, a disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri, and one of Mahaprabhu’s closest companions
in Nilachala. Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, listing those who came into the world before the Lord, says of him:

“Sri Paramananda Puri, the intimate companion of Sri Chaitanya in Nilachala, was born in Trihut.” (CB, Adi 2.43)

Trihut, or Tirhut, is the northern part of present-day Bihar — the districts of Muzaffarpur, Darbhanga, Sitamarhi,
Champaran and Chhapra; in ancient times it was a kingdom of its own. Another disciple of Madhavendra, the scholar
Raghupati Upadhyaya, also came from Tirhut. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati speaks of Paramananda Puri first of all
as “a brahmana born in Trihut,” and in his “Anubhashya” on the “Charitamrita” writes of him: “a brahmana from Trihut,
a disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri and very dear to Mahaprabhu.” <!-- F3 F4 F11 Q2 -->

Nothing more is known of his worldly life. The sources name neither his parents nor the year and day of his
appearance, nor when and where he left this world; nor are the days of his appearance and disappearance found in
the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. We know only that he outlived Mahaprabhu: after the Lord’s
disappearance Srinivasa Acharya still found him in Puri. <!-- F5 -->

The “Charitamrita” calls him Paramananda Puri and Puri Gosani, and sometimes simply “Puri”; Vrindavana Dasa calls him
Puri Gosani and Puri Gosvami; Kavi Karnapura, in the drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” calls him Paramananda
Puri-ishvara and Puri-ishvara, “the lord of the Puris”; the biographies add the honorifics “Puripada” and “Sri
Paramananda Puripada.” <!-- F1 -->

## Uddhava
Who he is in the eternal pastimes of Krishna, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Sri Paramananda Puri was formerly Uddhava.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 118)

He who was Uddhava in Krishna’s pastimes descended into the pastimes of Gaura as Paramananda Puri, to enrich them
with his service. This is no rebirth: an eternal associate of the Lord descends into the world together with Him and
by His will. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, too, calls him Uddhava, and so do the Vaishnava vandanas: the “Vaishnava-vandana”
ascribed to Jiva Gosvami praises “Sri Puri Paramananda, called Uddhava, dear to Hari” (1.58), Devakinandana praises
him “who is Uddhava by nature” (2.46), and the vandana ascribed to the “second” Vrindavana Dasa, “him whom they call
Thakura Uddhava.” The same Sanskrit vandana speaks of the sannyasi companions of the Lord “headed by Sri Paramananda
Puri” (1.141).[^1] <!-- F2 Q1 F69 -->

## A Disciple of Madhavendra Puri
Paramananda Puri received initiation from Sri Madhavendra Puri. Vrindavana Dasa says of him:

“Sri Paramananda Puri was a dear disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri, and he was brimming with the sweetness of divine
love.” (CB, Antya 3.178)

So too do Murari Gupta — “a disciple of Sri Madhava Puri named Paramananda” (3.15.19), Lochana Dasa (CM,
Shesha-khanda, pt. 2, v. 136) and Karnapura — “a disciple of the lord of sages, Madhavendra Puri”
(“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” act 8). Among Madhavendra’s other disciples are named Ishvara Puri, the guru of
Mahaprabhu, Advaita Acharya, Brahmananda Puri, Sri Ranga Puri, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Raghupati Upadhyaya and
others. <!-- F6 F7 Q3 -->

The title “Puri” is the name of one of the ten orders of sannyasis, but Paramananda Puri was no Mayavadi renunciate
seeking to merge into the impersonal Brahman. He was a Vaishnava sannyasi in the line of Madhavendra, and his whole
wealth was prema, love for Krishna: Vrindavana Dasa calls him “the abode of pure love for Sri Chaitanya” (CB, Antya
3.175). <!-- F6 -->

As a disciple of Madhavendra he was a godbrother of Ishvara Puri, the Lord’s guru. Therefore Mahaprabhu showed him the
highest respect: He treated him as a very dear elder and honoured him as His own guru. It was his connection with
Madhavendra Puri, the biographies say, that made him an object of such reverence for the Lord. The Lord Himself, the
Master of all, is above every guru; but, setting an example for the world, He honoured the guru-varga — the circle
of His elders — and taught others by doing so. <!-- F8 I4 -->

## The Central Root of the Tree
Describing the tree of bhakti grown by Sri Chaitanya, Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami says:

“Glory to Sri Madhava Puri, brimming with love for Krishna! He is the first sprout of the wish-fulfilling tree of
bhakti. In the form of Sri Ishvara Puri the sprout grew strong, and then the gardener, Sri Chaitanya Himself, became
the trunk of the tree. By His own inconceivable power the gardener became the trunk, the original support of all the
branches.” (CC, Adi 9.10–12)[^2]

Herein lies the supreme secret of the tree: the Lord is both its gardener and its trunk, and all this by the power of
His inconceivable energy. And at the base of the tree nine roots sprang up — nine sannyasis:

“At the base of the tree nine roots sprang up: Paramananda Puri and Keshava Bharati, Brahmananda Puri and Brahmananda
Bharati, Vishnu Puri, Keshava Puri, Krishnananda Puri, Sri Nrisimha Tirtha and Sukhananda Puri. On them the tree stood
immovable. The central root is Paramananda Puri, a man of great steadfastness, and on these nine roots the tree stood
secure.” (CC, Adi 9.13–16)[^3]

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his “Anubhashya” makes this a subheading: “Paramananda Puri, the middle root.”

## The Meeting on Rishabha Hill
Mahaprabhu first met Paramananda Puri on His pilgrimage to the South. Leaving Purushottama, the Lord passed through
Kurmasthana, Jiyada-Nrisimha, Vidyanagara, where He conversed with Ramananda Raya, the Gautami-Ganga, Mallikarjuna,
Ahobala, Siddhavata, Skanda-kshetra, Trimatha, Vriddha-Kashi, Bauddhasthana, Tirupati, Trimalla, Pana-Nrisimha,
Shiva-Kanchi and Vishnu-Kanchi, Trikalahasti, Vriddhakola, Shiyali-Bhairavi, the banks of the Kaveri,
Kumbhakarna-kapala and Srirangam, where He spent the four months of the rains with Venkata Bhatta and blessed his
whole family. After the chaturmasya He went on further south — and came to Rishabha hill. <!-- F12 -->

Rishabha hill lies in the South of India, near Madurai. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta describes the place thus: twelve miles
north of Madurai are the Anagadamalai hills, in the forests around Kutakachala, where, according to tradition,
Rishabhadeva perished in a forest fire; today it is known as Palni Hill, and the local people call it
Varaha-parvata.[^4] <!-- F13 Q13 -->

The “Charitamrita” relates:

“Coming to Rishabha hill, Sri Gaurahari bowed down before the Deity in the temple of Lord Narayana and offered many
hymns and prayers. Hearing that Paramananda Puri was staying for the four months in a nearby village, Mahaprabhu went
to see him. With all respect Sri Chaitanya touched the feet of Paramananda Puri, and he embraced Him with great love.”
(CC, Madhya 9.167–169)

Paramananda Puri was observing the chaturmasya there, living in the house of a brahmana. The Lord too stayed in that
same house, and for three days they spoke of Krishna, filled with the bliss of prema (Madhya 9.170). Krishnadasa
Kaviraja mentions this meeting in his summary of the pastimes as well: “Travelling through South India, He met
Paramananda Puri” (Madhya 1.111). <!-- F14 F15 F16 -->

On parting, Puri said that he was going to Purushottama to see Jagannatha, and from there to Gauda to bathe in the
Ganga. Then the Lord asked him:

“‘Please come back again to Jagannatha Puri afterwards. I shall reach Setubandha and soon come there too. I wish to be
always in your company, and you will show Me mercy if you return to Nilachala.’ Then, with Paramananda Puri’s
consent, Sri Chaitanya happily continued His journey through the South. So Paramananda Puri set out for Nilachala,
and Mahaprabhu for Sri Shaila.” (CC, Madhya 9.172–175)

The Lord wished Puri to come to Nilachala and stay there with Him for good. <!-- F17 Q5 -->

Other original sources tell of this meeting differently and with new details.[^5] According to Murari Gupta, the
Lord met Paramananda Puri on the road from Ranga-kshetra:

“Seeing the form of Gauranga, Sri Paramananda Puri remembered his guru’s words, and was adorned with tears of love
and thrilling hair. And the Lord too, the guardian of dharma, in supreme delight fell down like a stick, His head to
the ground, before Puripada and his servant. In alarm Puri said, ‘You should not do so! You alone are the Lord of the
worlds, the One who awakens consciousness in the world. I know who You are: You are Bhagavan Himself, who has taken
the form of a devotee of Sri Krishna; You have assumed the mood of Sri Radha and thirst for the rasa of sweetness.’
Hearing his words, Krishna laughed and replied respectfully, ‘Know that My heart is bound by your love — of this
there is no doubt. Go to the most delightful Kshetra and stay there until I return.’” (Murari Gupta, 3.15.20–25)

Lochana Dasa says that, looking at the Lord, Paramananda Puri remembered his guru’s prophecy. Madhavendra had once
told him: in the first twilight of Kali the Lord of Lakshmi will appear — a golden-bodied sannyasi dwelling near the
Daru-brahma; “I have not the good fortune to see Him with my own eyes; when you see Him, remember me.” And Puri
understood firmly: this is Bhagavan Himself. In tears he kept repeating “Madhavendra!” — and the Lord, hearing the
name of Madhavendra, danced and wept in bliss:

“‘Today My sannyasa has borne fruit: the name of Madhavendra has entered My ears.’” (CM, Shesha-khanda, pt. 2,
v. 142+4)[^6]

Puri bowed to Him, and the Lord, saying “What are you doing?”, lifted him up and embraced him tightly (vv. 134–144).
Lochana Dasa places the meeting after the chaturmasya, on the road from Trimalla; so too does a biography that
retells his poem: the Lord spent four months in Srirangam, then met Paramananda Puri, who offered prayers to Gaura.
<!-- F18 -->

Kavi Karnapura in his mahakavya makes a riddle of his name: the Lord met the best of ascetics, at the beginning of
whose name was “Parama,” in the middle “ananda,” and at the end “Puri”; then Paramananda went to Puri, and the Lord to
the Setu (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 13.14–16). Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” writes that on Rishabha
hill, near Narayana, “the ascetic Paramananda” spent the four months of the rains, and that the Lord met and honoured
him (10.12–13); the “Karcha” of Govinda Dasa likewise says, “On Rishabha hill lives Paramananda Puri,” and the Lord
hastened to see him (6.1100–1102). <!-- F18 F68 -->

## Through Gauda to Nilachala
After a few more days on Rishabha hill, Paramananda Puri went, as he had intended, to the Ganga, and, walking along
its bank, came to Navadvipa:

“Shortly before this Paramananda Puri had come from the South, walking from village to village along the bank of the
Ganga. He stayed in the house of mother Shachi, and she respectfully fed him.” (CC, Madhya 10.91–92)

He lived in Mayapur, in the house of Shachimata, and took his meals from her hands, and she looked after him with
great love. <!-- F19 F21 -->

Meanwhile Mahaprabhu had returned from the South to Puri. Nityananda, Jagadananda, Mukunda and Damodara resolved to
inform Shachimata and the Bengali devotees of His return, and, with the Lord’s permission, sent to Gauda Kala
Krishnadasa, the Lord’s companion on His southern journey (CC, Madhya 10.67–72).[^7] The news spread through Gauda,
and Advaita Acharya and the other devotees, taking leave of Shachi, began to prepare for the journey to Nilachala
(10.88). <!-- F20 -->

Paramananda Puri too heard the news. The devotees’ preparations were dragging on, and he could not wait to see the
Lord; so he hurried to Puri alone, taking with him only the Bengali brahmana Kamalakanta, a devotee of Sri Chaitanya
(CC, Madhya 10.93–94). Thus these two were the first from Bengal to come to Mahaprabhu. <!-- F22 -->

“Soon he met Mahaprabhu, and the Lord was overjoyed to see him. In the bliss of prema Sri Chaitanya offered prayers
at his feet, and Paramananda Puri embraced Him with love. Sri Chaitanya said, ‘Please show Me mercy and stay in
Jagannatha Puri, so that I may always be in your company.’ Paramananda Puri replied, ‘I too wish to be with You.
That is why I have come to Nilachala from Bengal. All the devotees of Bengal were very happy to hear of Your return
from South India. They are all preparing to come and see You, but their preparations were taking long, and I decided
to set out without delay.’” (CC, Madhya 10.95–100)[^8] <!-- F23 Q6 F22 -->

The Lord lodged him in a secluded room in the house of Kashi Mishra and gave him a servant (CC, Madhya 10.101). Soon
Svarupa Damodara came; having bowed to the Lord and to Nityananda, he respectfully bowed at the feet of Paramananda
Puri too, and Puri embraced him with love (10.128). And Ramananda Raya, returning to the Lord in Puri, paid his
respects to those gathered around Him — Nityananda, Paramananda Puri and others. <!-- F24 -->
