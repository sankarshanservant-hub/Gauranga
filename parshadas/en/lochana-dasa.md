# Lochana Dasa Thakura
*Лочан Дас Тхакур · @lochana-dasa*

## The Singer of the “Chaitanya-mangala”
Lochana Dasa Thakura is the Vaishnava poet who composed the “Sri Chaitanya-mangala,” a Bengali poem on the life and
pastimes of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, and a disciple of Narahari Sarakara Thakura of Shrikhanda, an intimate associate of
the Lord. He never saw the Lord Himself: he was born when Mahaprabhu was already residing in Nilachala, and he composed
his poem after the Lord's disappearance — one of those who treasured the memory of His pastimes. In the poem and in his
songs he signs himself “Lochana Dasa”; later Vaishnava poets call him “Thakura Lochana,” and the “Prema-vilasa” calls him
Lochana or Lochana Dasa. The “Chaitanya-charitamrita” does not speak of him; nor is he found in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” and the sources are silent on who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes.[^1] All we know of his
life comes from his own words, from a few lines in later Vaishnava books, and from tradition. <!-- F14 F24 F26 -->

## Kogram: Family and Childhood
Lochana Dasa appeared in 1523 (1445 of the Shaka era) in the village of Kogram, or Ko-gram, in the Rarh country. The
village lies in the Katwa subdivision (*mahakuma*) of Bardhaman district, about ten miles — five kroshas — north of
Guskara station, near Jhamatpur; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati gives the place the same way: “in Kogram, in the Katwa
subdivision of Burdwan district.” One of the biographies adds that the Thakura's house stood not far from the river
Ajay.[^2] By our calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math) his appearance day is Gaura-pratipad, the first day of the bright
fortnight of the month of Narayana (≈ Pausha); in 2027 it falls on 8 January. One of the biographies, citing “some,”
gives the same day. <!-- F1 F2 F3 F57 -->

His family were vaidyas, physicians of Rarh (*radhiya-vaidyas*): so say Lochana himself, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, the
“Prema-vilasa” and the biographies.[^3] His father was Sri Kamalakara Dasa, his mother Srimati Sadanandi. At the end of
the “Chaitanya-mangala” Lochana himself tells of his family:[^4]

“Having completed the account in four parts, I have made it known. I was born in the vaidya caste and live in Kogram. My
pious mother is named Sadanandi: born from her womb, I long for Krishna. My father who gave me birth is Kamalakara Dasa:
by his grace I tell the tale of Gora's [Gaura's] glories.” (“Chaitanya-mangala,” Shesha-khanda, 94+59–61)
<!-- F4 F5 F6 Q7 -->

He goes on to recall his mother's family. Her people and his father's lived in one village; his grandmother was named
Abhaya Dasi, and his maternal grandfather was Sri Purushottama Gupta, “purified by many tirthas and content in
austerity.” “In my mother's family and my father's I am the only son,” Lochana writes. The only son was especially
loved: wherever he went he was petted, and no one could teach the spoiled boy. It was his grandfather who taught him his
letters — “by beating and strictness”; and Lochana gratefully bows at his feet: “by his grace I write the life of
Chaitanya” (ibid., 94+62–67). The biographies also say that he studied in his grandfather's house, and according to one
of them he spent most of his childhood with his grandparents. <!-- F6 F7 Q7 -->

From his very childhood his heart was given to Gauranga, and he remained indifferent to worldly things; according to one
of the biographies, while still a boy he was blessed with the company of Sri Gauranga's devotees. He was married very
young, as was the custom of the time. His father-in-law's house was in the village of Kakut near Amedpur. <!-- F8 F9 -->

## A Renounced Householder
Lochana became a householder, but the world held no attraction for him: he spent his time with Gaura's devotees, in talks
about Krishna. <!-- F10 -->

There is a wondrous tradition about his detachment. His wife had been given to him as a child, and she lived with her
parents. The girl was growing up and the time was approaching for her to move to her husband's house, but her parents
saw that their son-in-law was wholly detached from the world, and they grew anxious. They went to his guru, Narahari
Sarakara, and Narahari told his disciple to go to his father-in-law's house. Lochana had not been there for a long time
and could not find the house; he asked the way of a young woman he met in the village street and addressed her, as is
the custom in Bengal, as “ma,” “mother.” When he reached the house, he realized that he had asked the way of his own
wife. From then on, it is said, he saw his wife no longer as a spouse but as a mother, and to the end of his days,
renounced from the world, he served Sri Guru and Gauranga. <!-- F11 F12 F13 -->

There is no neglect of duty or coldness toward his wife in this story. It speaks of the *vairagya* of Narahari's
disciple: his heart was so full of Gaura that he saw a mother in every woman, and a word spoken out of custom became for
him a truth for life. <!-- F13 -->

## At Narahari's Feet
Lochana's guru was Narahari Sarakara Thakura of Shrikhanda, one of Gaura's best-known associates. In the bloom of youth
Lochana came to Shrikhanda, found his gurudeva and took shelter at his feet. Narahari came to love him and gave him
initiation (*diksha*); the biographies call Lochana Narahari's “dear” disciple, devoted to him alone. Lochana gladly
settled with his guru in Shrikhanda — the “Prema-vilasa” says just this: “he is a disciple of Sri Narahari and lives in
Shrikhanda.” Narahari taught him the art of kirtana, and later told him to write the life of the Lord. Lochana took
this order to heart, and so the “Sri Chaitanya-mangala” came to be, a holy narrative full of the nectar of Gauranga's
pastimes. In many places of the poem, as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta observed, Lochana calls himself a follower of Narahari.
<!-- F14 F15 F16 F17 -->

Love for the guru fills the whole book. At its very beginning Lochana writes:

“Thakura Sri Narahari, master of his servant's life — my hope is in his feet; low though I am, I long to sing the
virtues of Gaura — this is what Lochana Dasa trusts in.” (“Chaitanya-mangala,” Sutra-khanda 9)

“Without him I have no friend in the three worlds; I praise Narahari Dasa, ocean of Gaura's virtues,” he says further on
(ibid., 33); and, asking the Vaishnavas' blessing, he turns to his guru once more: “My Thakura, Prabhu Narahari Dasa — I
bow and humbly pray: fulfil my desire” (ibid., 61). So too the poem ends. In his closing word Lochana calls Narahari
Dasa “the Thakura of my life” and “the giver of prema-bhakti to me”: all he was able to tell is by his guru's grace
(Shesha-khanda, 94+42, 94+68–69). <!-- F18 Q2 -->

At the end of the Sutra-khanda Lochana glorifies his guru as an eternal associate:

“In Vrindavana he was named Madhumati, a dear companion of Radha, a treasury of sweetness. Now, in the Kali age,
Narahari, companion of Gaura, is the keeper of the treasury of the prema of Radha and Krishna.” (“Chaitanya-mangala,”
Sutra-khanda 645–646) <!-- F19 Q4 -->

As Narahari's disciple, Lochana says that Gadadhara Pandita and Narahari are Mahaprabhu's dearest associates: “Glory to
the Lord of the life of Narahari and Gadadhara!” (Sutra-khanda 12). More than once he paints Gaurasundara with Gadadhara
at His left, Narahari at His right and Raghunandana at His feet. He glorifies his guru's nephew, Raghunandana Thakura, as
well — both in the opening of the poem (Sutra-khanda 36) and at the end of the Sutra-khanda (647–651), where he recalls
how the boy Raghunandana fed the Deity a sweet ball. <!-- F20 F21 -->

## After the Guru's Departure
Narahari Sarakara left this world on the Krishna-ekadashi of the month of Agrahayana, and in memory of his departure a
great festival was held in Shrikhanda. Lochana served the Vaishnavas there. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells how he and
Yadunandana Thakura adorned the honoured guests, Krishna Mishra and Gopala, the sons of Sri Advaita:

“Sri Yadunandana and Sri Lochana — the two of them — took flower garlands and fragrant sandal.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara”
9.591)

One of the biographies adds that he distributed these garlands and sandal to all who had gathered (for the day of the
festival see “Bhakti-ratnakara” 9.513). Thus Lochana's name was preserved by Narahari Chakravarti, the author of the
“Bhakti-ratnakara”; one of the biographies takes care to note that this Narahari — a native of Renga in Murshidabad
district, known also as Ghanashyama Dasa — is not the Narahari of Shrikhanda who was Lochana's guru.
<!-- F22 Q10 F23 -->

The “Prema-vilasa,” a late book and not reliable in everything, speaks of the same days. After the departure of
Gadadhara Dasa and Narahari, Raghunandana, “taking Lochana with him,” came to Katwa (Katoa) to Yadunandana Chakravarti,
a disciple of Gadadhara Dasa, and together they fixed the day of the memorial festival; and some days before the
festival Raghunandana came to Katwa again “together with Lochana and others” (19.126, 136).[^5] To the great festival
at Kheturi Raghunandana came from Khanda, “bringing with him Lochana Dasa and other devotees” (19.322). <!-- F24 Q12 -->

Narahari Chakravarti remembers Lochana in the “Namamrita-samudra” as well, where he addresses a prayer to each of the
Lord's associates:

“Thakura Lochana, grant me always this one thing: may my mind drown in Gauranga's pastimes!” (“Namamrita-samudra” 69)
