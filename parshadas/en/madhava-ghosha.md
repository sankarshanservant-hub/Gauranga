# Madhava Ghosha
*Мадхава Гхош · @madhava-ghosha*

## "The Singer of Vrindavana"
Madhava Ghosha is one of the three Ghosha brothers, an eternal companion of Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda, a kirtaniya
to whose singing Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda danced. He is most often called Madhava Ghosha or, with reverence, Sri
Madhava Ghosha Thakura; but Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” and Vrindavana Dasa Thakura in the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” call him Madhavananda, and under this name too — Madhavananda Ghosha Thakura — he is known in the
biographies. <!-- F1 -->

The finest praise of him comes from Vrindavana Dasa. Telling how Nityananda wished to dance in Panihati and the best
singers gathered around Him, he names Madhava before all the rest:

“The pious Madhava Ghosha was the most skilled in singing kirtana. In this he had no equal in all the world. He was
known as the singer of the Lord's pastimes in Vrindavana, and he was very dear to Lord Nityananda.” (CB, Antya 5.257–258)

"The singer of Vrindavana" is not praise of a performer's craft. Madhava sang of the eternal pastimes of Vrindavana, and
there was nothing worldly in his singing; that is why the Vaishnavas called him so. One biography stresses that these
words come from Vrindavana Dasa himself, the servant of Nityananda — the one who knew best who was dear to his Lord.

## Rasollasa
Who the three brothers are in Krishna's eternal pastimes is revealed by Kavi Karnapura:

“Kalavati, Rasollasa and Gunatunga, who were in Vraja and sang the songs composed by Sri Vishakha, are now considered to
be Govinda, Madhavananda and Vasudeva respectively.” (GGD 188)[^1]

In Vraja, then, Madhava is the sakhi Rasollasa, one of those who sang the songs composed by Sri Vishakha. Song is his
eternal service; in Gaura's pastimes he continues it, glorifying the Lord in kirtana. <!-- F2 Q2 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” speaks of the brothers
more deeply still: Madhava, Vasudeva and Govinda are kaya-vyuha, personal expansions of the ashraya-vigraha of the sweet
madhura-rasa of Vraja, that is, of Sri Radha. Ashraya is the "abode" of love, she in whose heart love for Krishna
reaches its summit. Therefore, as one biography relates, all three brothers abide in madhurya-rasa, among those who have
accepted Srimati Radhika as the highest shelter of that love. This is not reincarnation in the worldly sense, nor
worldly femininity, but the eternal spiritual nature of a companion who descended together with the Lord. <!-- F3 -->

## The Three Brothers
Madhava is the brother of Govinda Ghosha and of Vasudeva (Vasu) Ghosha. Which of the three was the eldest the sources do
not say. Kavi Karnapura and Krishnadasa Kaviraja name them in the order "Govinda, Madhava, Vasudeva," and Vrindavana
Dasa in the order "Madhava, Govinda, Vasudeva." <!-- F6 -->

The brothers came from the kayasthas of Uttara-Rarhi — northern Rarh, the part of Rarh through which the Ganga does not
flow. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, too, writes of them as kayasthas of northern Rarh in the “Anubhashya”[^2]. The family was
prominent and respected and belonged to the "shaukra," kayasthas by birth. The family is linked with Shrihatta (Sylhet)
through the village of Buran, or Burangi; the brothers' father settled in Kumarahatta, and later the brothers moved to
Navadvipa. More on this is told in the article on Vasu Ghosha[^3]. <!-- F7 F8 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” calls the three brothers branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, and at
once he recalls their kirtana:

“Govinda, Madhava and Vasudeva were three brothers, and when they sang kirtana, Chaitanya and Nitai danced.”
(CC, Adi 10.115)[^4] <!-- F5 Q3 -->

But Madhava is a branch of Nityananda's tree as well. Listing Nityananda's companions, Kaviraja Gosvami says that
Abhirama and Gadadhara Dasa are counted in two communities at once — among the devotees of Sri Chaitanya and among the
devotees of Nityananda — and adds:

“Therefore they are counted in both groups, and so it is with Madhava and Vasudeva Ghosha.” (CC, Adi 11.15)

One biography explains: in serving Nityananda in Gauda, Madhava served Gaura as well, for he went to Gauda at the
command of the Lord Himself. <!-- F4 Q6 I2 -->

## In Nilachala
When the devotees of Gauda first came to the Lord in Puri, King Prataparudra watched them from the palace roof, and
Gopinatha Acharya named each of them for him. He pointed out the brothers too:

“Here are Govinda, Madhava Ghosha and Vasu Ghosha — three brothers whose kirtana delights the Lord.” (CC, Madhya 11.88)
<!-- Q8 -->

At the Ratha-yatra Mahaprabhu divided the singers into seven groups, and the brothers sang in the fourth:

“He formed another group with Govinda Ghosha as the leader, in which Haridasa, Vishnudasa and Raghava sang, with the two
brothers Madhava and Vasudeva Ghosha; and Vakreshvara Pandita danced there.” (CC, Madhya 13.42–43)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” on Adi 10.115 says the same: in one of the seven groups these three brothers
were the chief singers[^8]. <!-- F20 Q5 -->

When the Lord wished to dance Himself, He brought all seven groups together, and for His wild, leaping dance He put nine
devotees with Svarupa Damodara: Srivasa, Ramai, Raghu, Govinda, Mukunda, Haridasa, Govindananda, Madhava and Govinda (CC,
Madhya 13.72–74). One biography sees the Ghosha brothers in this Madhava and Govinda; the “Charitamrita” itself does not
specify[^9]. <!-- F21 -->

From then on, every year the three brothers went from Gauda to Nilachala — together with Shivananda Sena and the other
devotees of Bengal — and came for the Ratha-yatra; with their kirtana they increased the Lord's joy. Of the third year
of these journeys the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” says:

“Acharyaratna, Vidyanidhi, Srivasa, Ramai, Vasudeva, Murari, and Govinda with his two brothers set out. Raghava Pandita packed his bags (jhali) for the journey, and the residents of Kulina-grama came bringing silk ropes.” (CC, Madhya 16.16)
<!-- F19 Q6 -->

## With Nityananda in Bengal
Later Mahaprabhu sent Nityananda to preach in Gauda, Bengal. Madhava and Vasudeva went with Him, and Govinda stayed with
the Lord:

“At the Lord’s command Nityananda went to Gauda, and by the Lord’s command three devotees went with Him: Sri Ramadasa, Madhava and Vasudeva Ghosha. Govinda stayed with the Lord, and was overjoyed to do so.” (CC, Adi 10.117–118)

One biography, speaking of Nityananda's coming from Nilachala to Gauda, likewise reminds us that He went at Gaura's
command. <!-- F13 Q4 -->

One of the first places on this journey was Panihati. Nityananda stayed in the house of Raghava Pandita on the bank of
the Ganga, to the joy of Raghava, Makaradhvaja Kara and their families. Absorbed within Himself, He roared like a lion,
and when the desire to dance arose in His heart, the best singers gathered around, and first among them was Madhava.
Vrindavana Dasa goes on:

“The three brothers — Madhava, Vasudeva and Govinda — sang while Lord Nityananda danced.” (CB, Antya 5.259)

Nityananda danced so that the earth trembled under the weight of His body; He loudly sang the holy name, and whoever His
glance fell upon fell to the ground, overwhelmed with divine love (CB, Antya 5.260–262). To the singing of Madhava Ghosha,
the best kirtaniya on earth, Nityananda danced with a thunderous roar, and then He sat on the throne of Vishnu and
ordered Raghava to perform His abhisheka[^5]. Thus Madhava, together with his brothers, took part in one of
Nityananda's great festivals. <!-- F14 F12 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on the verses about Madhava, explains why the brothers were called
"singers of Vrindavana": they were wholly given to kirtana and did not look on it with worldly eyes, as worldly
kirtaniyas do. The inclination to serve Hari grows in the heart only when worldly conceptions have been destroyed
completely; such was the kirtana of the three brothers[^6]. <!-- F11 I1 -->

All the while Nityananda preached in Gauda, Madhava and Vasu Ghosha served Him with their singing: He danced, they led
the kirtana. Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks of Madhava in particular:

“Sri Madhava Ghosha was foremost among the kirtana singers, and Nityananda Prabhu would dance to his singing.”
(CC, Adi 11.18) <!-- F15 Q8 -->

## The Dana-khanda in the House of Gadadhara Dasa
Especially memorable is Madhava's kirtana in Ariadaha, in the house of Gadadhara Dasa. Nityananda came there with His
companions and, seeing the Deity of Bala-Gopala whom Gadadhara served, pressed Him to His chest and with a roar broke
into dance in the mood of a cowherd boy (CB, Antya 5.376–377). Then Madhava began to sing:

“Then Madhavananda Ghosha sang of the dana-lila, and the lion-like avadhuta Nityananda was deeply pleased. So sweet was
the voice of the fortunate Madhava that the jewel of avadhutas was filled with bliss.” (CB, Antya 5.378–379)

Dana-keli is Krishna's eternal pastime with the gopis: Krishna and His friends block the path of the girls carrying milk
products to market and demand a "toll" from them; Srila Rupa Gosvami described it in the “Dana-keli-kaumudi.” This song,
the dana-khanda, is what Madhava sang, and Nityananda, hearing of the dana-lila, danced so that it cannot be described;
His dance was adorned with every sign of divine love (CB, Antya 5.382–383). Thus Nityananda revealed this lila through
Madhava's kirtana and His own dance, and the “Charitamrita” says:

“Gadadhara Dasa was full of bliss in the mood of a gopi, and in his house Nityananda revealed the dana-keli pastime.”
(CC, Adi 11.17)

From that time the temple of Gadadhara Dasa became famous as the place of the dana-lila, for there, when Madhavananda
Ghosha sang of it, an extraordinary bhava appeared in Nityananda. Nityananda stayed with Gadadhara for several days (CB,
Antya 5.393), heard the dana-khanda from Madhavananda's lips and bestowed His mercy on many. This lila is no worldly
spectacle and no theatre: Madhava sang and enacted Krishna's pastime in kirtana, and Nityananda, listening, revealed it
in His dance[^7]. <!-- F16 F17 F18 Q7 -->

## Songs and the Memory of the Vaishnavas
All three brothers sang with sweet voices and composed padas. As a poet Vasudeva is the best known of them; of Govinda's
and Madhava's songs only a few have survived. <!-- F10 -->

Yet Madhava's name lives on in the obeisances of the Vaishnavas. Lochana Dasa at the beginning of the
“Chaitanya-mangala” writes: "To Govinda and Madhava Ghosha and to Vasu Ghosha, falling to the ground, with joined palms,
I bow" (Sutra-khanda 34), and listing the companions who descended with the Lord, he names them as well: "Govinda,
Madhava Ghosha and Vasu Ghosha — all came together and spread [bhakti] over the earth" (Sutra-khanda 629). Devakinandana
in the “Vaishnava-vandana” glorifies him thus: "I shall praise Madhava Ghosha — the object of the Lord's love, to whom
the Lord granted a voice that never breaks" (part 2, 81); the sweetness of his singing is the Lord's gift. In another
vandana he is called "the singer Madhavananda Ghosha Mahashaya" (part 4, 65), just as Vrindavana Dasa, in his list of
Nityananda's companions, says: "Madhavananda Ghosha Mahashaya was a singer" (CB, Antya 5.750). And Narahari Chakravarti
opens the “Bhakti-ratnakara” with "Glory to Sri Mukunda, Vasu, Madhava and Shankara!" — this Madhava, too, is Madhava
Ghosha. <!-- Q8 -->

One biography also tells of the Navadvipa days: when Advaita performed arati to Gauranga and Gadadhara beneath a tree,
"Madhava, Vasudeva, Purushottama, Vijaya, Mukunda and others" sang in various ragas. Whether this is the same Madhava the
book does not say; but Vasudeva is named beside him, and perhaps the Ghosha brothers are meant. <!-- F25 -->

## The Last Years
Little is known of Madhava's later years, and the accounts differ. His shripata is in Daihata, on the Ganga between
Navadvipa and Katwa; Vasudeva settled in Tamoluk, Govinda in Agradvipa. According to one biography, "some say" that the
brothers parted after the Lord's sannyasa, suffering in separation from Him; according to another, after Mahaprabhu's
disappearance[^10]. <!-- F22 -->

Neither the day of Madhava's appearance, nor the day of his departure, nor the year and place where he left this world,
nor the place of his samadhi is given by the sources; nor are his days in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya
Saraswat Math (it commemorates the departure of Govinda and of Vasudeva). <!-- F23 -->

Yet the brothers' kirtana did not cease even after the Lord's disappearance. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.572
ff.), when Narottama Dasa sang at the great festival in Kheturi, Sri Chaitanya appeared there with His companions, and
among them were Madhava Ghosha, Vasu Ghosha and Govinda Ghosha. <!-- F24 -->

## Significance
Madhava Ghosha is, in Vraja, the sakhi Rasollasa, who sang the songs of Sri Vishakha; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sees in him a
kaya-vyuha of Sri Radha. He descended into Gaura's pastimes to sing for the Lord. He belongs to both ganas: Mahaprabhu
sent him with Nityananda, and in serving Nityananda in Gauda he served Gaura; and every year he returned to Nilachala to
sing before the Lord and before Jagannatha's chariot. To his singing Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda danced, and his
dana-khanda in the house of Gadadhara Dasa revealed an extraordinary bhava in Nityananda. Vrindavana Dasa called him "the
singer of Vrindavana," and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta disclosed the meaning of these words: such is the kirtana of one in
whom worldly conceptions have been destroyed completely and only one desire grows — to serve Hari.
