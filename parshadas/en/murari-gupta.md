# Murari Gupta
*Мурари Гупта · @murari-gupta*

## Hanuman in the Pastimes of Gaura
Sri Murari Gupta was a physician of Navadvipa, a fellow student of Nimai Pandita and one of His closest associates —
the one who first set down the Lord's pastimes in writing. Kavi Karnapura's “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” reveals who he
was in the Lord's earlier pastimes:

“Murari Gupta is Hanuman; Angada is Sri Purandara; he who was called Sugriva is Govindananda.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-
dipika” 91)

So all the original sources see him. Lochana Dasa, at the opening of his “Chaitanya-mangala,” glorifies Murari as
Hanuman; the Lord Himself tells him, “I am Ramachandra, and you are Hanuman” (CB, Madhya 10.14) — and many years later,
in Puri, “You are the servant of Sri Ramachandra, Hanuman himself” (CC, Madhya 15.156). And Vrindavana Dasa says that
once Murari received the Lord's mercy “which before only Hanuman had been granted” (CB, Madhya 20.52). <!-- F3 Q1 -->

An eternal servant of Rama, in Gaura's pastimes too he remained the servant of his Ramachandra, and this fidelity, as
we shall see, became one of the most moving lessons Mahaprabhu taught the world. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, enumerating the
branches of the tree of Sri Chaitanya, says of him:

“Sri Murari Gupta is a branch, a storehouse of love; whenever the Lord heard his humble words, His heart would melt.”
(CC, Adi 10.49)

Humility is the ornament of a Vaishnava, and seeing it in Murari, the Lord could not hold back His feelings.
<!-- F6 Q6 I1 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja names him more than once among the very closest. Murari is one of those intimate associates who
“are learned men of the highest greatness, and service to Chaitanya makes every one of them mad” (CC, Adi 6.48–49);
together with Svarupa Damodara he is praised as one of the two moons that “have risen and driven all darkness away”
(Adi 13.4); the author places his feet on his own head beside those of Advaita, Nityananda, Shrivasa and Gadadhara
(Adi 4.227–228). And the Lord Himself, speaking in Puri with Vallabha Bhatta, recalls: “Kashishvara, Mukunda, Vasudeva,
Murari and many other Vaishnavas were born in the land of Bengal” (CC, Antya 7.49). <!-- F7 Q24 -->

The original sources call him by various names. The Lord addresses him affectionately simply as “Gupta” (CC, Madhya
15.138, 15.153; CB, Madhya 3.44; Antya 4.317, 4.342), and once calls him “Murari Pandita” (CB, Madhya 1.70). In his own
poem Murari calls himself “Murari the vaidya,” that is, the physician, and “a lion among physicians” (“Kadacha” 2.2.11,
2.4.22, 2.7.18, 2.9.1, 4.17.11); Vrindavana Dasa calls him “the best of physicians” (CB, Madhya 10.10–11). The Lord gave
him one more name — “Ramadasa,” of which we shall speak below — and later generations call him Murari Gupta Thakura.

## Origin
Murari came from Shrihatta (Sylhet), the land from which many of the Lord's associates came. Vrindavana Dasa, having
named Shrivasa Pandita, Shrirama Pandita and Chandrashekhara, continues:

“All these Vaishnavas appeared in Shrihatta. A native of Shrihatta, too, was Sri Murari Gupta, who could heal the soul
of the disease of worldliness.” (CB, Adi 2.35)

Krishnadasa Kaviraja, however, names him among those born in Radha-desha, on the western side of the Ganga:

“In the land of Radha were born Thakura Nityananda, Gangadasa Pandita, Murari Gupta and Mukunda.” (CC, Adi 13.61)[^1]
<!-- F8 Q3 Q20 -->

Murari came of a family of vaidyas — hereditary physicians of Ayurveda; in his poem he calls himself just that: “Murari,
the son of a physician” (“Kadacha” 1.1.10). By birth he was not a brahmana, and the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” mentions
him among the non-brahmanas (Antya 10.140). The names of his parents no one has preserved. He was married; his wife,
whose name the sources also do not give, was a chaste and devoted woman, and we shall meet her again in the narratives
of Vrindavana Dasa. <!-- F9 F10 F14 -->

Murari was born before the Lord: he was a little older than Nimai[^2]. Vrindavana Dasa says that by His will
Mahaprabhu's eternal associates were born in Nadia before Him:

“By the order of Lord Chaitanya many of His eternal associates — Sri Chandrashekhara, Jagadisha, Gopinatha Acharya,
Shriman Pandita, Murari Gupta, Sri Garuda Pandita and Gangadasa Pandita — took birth secretly in Nadia before His
coming.” (CB, Adi 2.98–99)

Of this same descent of associates who came into the world before the Lord, Kavi Karnapura (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya”
1.21–25) and Murari himself (“Kadacha” 1.4) also tell. <!-- F11 F12 Q4 -->

The biographies add that Murari's family moved from Shrihatta to Navadvipa and settled next to the house of
Jagannatha Mishra, so that Murari was a companion in many of Nimai's childhood games[^3]. Kavi Karnapura testifies that
Murari knew the Lord's pastimes from His very childhood (“Chaitanya-charitamrita-mahakavya” 20.42–49), and Jayananda in
his “Chaitanya-mangala” names a certain Murari among the boys who went with Gaurachandra to the teacher's house.

## Nimai's Fellow Student
As youths Murari and Nimai studied grammar in the school of Gangadasa Pandita. Vrindavana Dasa names Vishvambhara's
schoolfellows:

“Vishvambhara's school friends were Sri Murari Gupta, Sri Kamalakanta and Sri Krishnananda.” (CB, Adi 8.38)

Murari was the senior among Gangadasa's students. Nimai loved to put tricky questions to His fellows: to Murari,
Krishnananda, Kamalakanta and the others He would explain one and the same sutra now one way, now another, and no one
could get the better of Him. <!-- F19 F11 -->

Murari took no part in these learned skirmishes, and for that very reason Nimai decided to provoke him (CB, Adi 10.11).
He was sixteen then; in learning He surpassed Brihaspati, the teacher of the gods, and gave no peace to those who relied
on themselves alone (10.14–18). Murari silently went about his own business, and the Lord, pleased with His servant,
still went on teasing him (10.19–20):

“He said: ‘O doctor, why study here? Better go and gather leaves and herbs to treat your patients. Grammar books are
hard to understand, and there is nothing in them about phlegm, bile or indigestion. What will you gain by studying on
your own? Better go home and treat the sick.’” (CB, Adi 10.21–23) <!-- F20 -->

Vrindavana Dasa remarks here that Murari is a portion of Rudra and by nature extremely fiery, yet Vishvambhara's words
did not anger him (Adi 10.24). The words “a portion of Rudra” do not reproach him with a worldly flaw but point to his
nature: Hanuman, the eternal servant of Rama, is himself a portion of Rudra. And so his meekness is all the more
precious: fiery by nature, he bears Nimai's mockery without anger and answers Him with dignity, as is fitting<!-- F4 -->:

“Murari replied: ‘Dear sir, why are You so proud? Why do You argue with everyone? Did You ask me a question about the
sutra, the vritti, the panji or the tika, and get no answer? You have asked me nothing, yet You declare that I know
nothing. You are a respectable brahmana — what can I say in reply?’” (CB, Adi 10.25–27)

Then Nimai told him to explain that day's lesson. Murari drew a conclusion from the earlier rules, and the Lord brought
forward another, opposite one and refuted his explanation; then He refuted His own and established it again. “So the
servant and the master could not defeat each other in debate” (Adi 10.28–29). <!-- F21 -->

“By the Lord's mercy Murari Gupta was exceptionally learned. The Lord listened to his explanations with great pleasure.
Pleased with His servant, the Lord touched him with His lotus hand, and Murari's body filled with bliss. Murari Gupta
thought: ‘He cannot be an ordinary man! Could an ordinary man possess such learning? Simply because He touched me, my
whole body has filled with bliss.’” (CB, Adi 10.30–33)

From the touch of that hand a deep attachment to Nimai and love for Him awoke in Murari's heart, and ever new truths
began to open to him. So he understood early that his young companion was no ordinary person. <!-- F22 Q5 -->

“‘Why should I be ashamed to learn from Him? In all Navadvipa there is no one more intelligent.’ Feeling the deepest
satisfaction, that great doctor (Murari Gupta) said: ‘Listen, Vishvambhara: from this day on I will study under You.’”
(CB, Adi 10.34–35) <!-- F23 -->

Lochana Dasa tells another, mischievous story of Nimai's childhood, which is found neither in Vrindavana Dasa nor in
Murari himself. In his youth, Lochana says, Murari was attached to the path of knowledge and to yoga. Once he was walking
along the road with his followers, wagging his head and waving his arms as he expounded a yoga-shastra to them; little
Nimai and the other children ran after him, mimicking his gait and his speech. Murari grew angry and scolded the boy,
and He slyly promised, “You'll find out at mealtime!” When Murari sat down to eat, Nimai came into his house,
approached him and filled his plate with urine. “What is this? Ugh!” — Murari leapt up, while Gaura, clapping His
hands, said, “Abandoning the path of bhakti, you expound yoga! Neglect knowledge and ritual and serve Krishna with all
your heart — He is full of consciousness and bliss, He is the treasure of the gopis' lives” (CM, Adi-khanda, pt. 4,
vv. 383–397). Lochana Dasa explains that the boy's “anger” was mercy: so the Lord broke Murari's attachment to dry
knowledge and turned him to bhakti. A child's prank that made a grown man jump up from his meal was a lesson for life:
food not offered to Krishna and learning not given to His service are equally unfit. The boy vanished, and Murari
understood: “Shachi's son is the Lord Himself.” He hurried to the house of Jagannatha Mishra, fell prostrate before
Nimai in ecstasy, told the parents that their son was the God of gods, bade them keep this secret, and told Advaita of
His pastimes (CM, Adi-khanda, pts. 4–5, vv. 398–403). <!-- F24 -->
