# Srila Krishnadasa Babaji
*Шрила Кришнадас Бабаджи · @krishnadasa-babaji*

## The Beloved Servant of Srila Bhaktivinoda
Among the disciples of Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura there was one whom Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, after his
departure, called “the beloved servant of Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura,” “the best of devotees” and “the wanderer”: Srila
Krishnadasa Babaji. His biographer calls him Srila Krishnadasa Babaji Maharaja, Babaji Mahashaya or simply Krishnadasa,
and speaks of him as magnanimous (*mahanubhava*); another biography mentions him only briefly, as one more disciple of
the Thakura. His father named him Indrachandra; the name Krishnadasa Babaji he received when the Thakura gave him the
initiation of a paramahamsa. <!-- F1 F2 -->

His life was short, and almost all of it was spent in the service of his guru. We know of it from one biography,
written soon after his departure, and above all from the memorial address Srila Bhaktisiddhanta gave at a gathering
in his honor. The acharya's word is our principal primary source: where the biography differs from it, we follow the
acharya; passages from it are given in our own translation from the Bengali. <!-- F38 Q3 -->

## Family and Youth
Indrachandra was born into a vaishya family in the village of Chhatra, in the Narail subdivision of the Jessore
(Yashohar) district, on the 11th of Shravana, 1290 of the Bengali era, at eight in the morning — by our reckoning about
26 July 1883.[^1] The days of his appearance and departure are not in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya
Saraswat Math. <!-- F3 F4 -->

While his son was still a child, his father left household life to worship the Lord and accepted sannyasa in
Hrishikesha. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls him Srila Narottama Dasa Yativara, “the best of renunciants”; the sources do
not preserve his mother's name. Besides Indrachandra the family had two more brothers and two sisters; the sisters,
the biography says, were still living when it was written. <!-- F5 F6 -->

The boy received his early schooling from his maternal uncle in the village of Binodpur. But when his father left home,
Indrachandra, as the eldest, had to give up his studies and return to the family house. He supported the household on
the income of his father's estate and of a small trade of his own, and paid for the schooling of the middle brother. By
Krishna's will that brother studied up to the final school class — and then passed away; soon the other brother too
was gone. <!-- F7 F8 F9 -->

## The Search for a Guru
His father had left home, both brothers had left this life — and Indrachandra saw how barren and fleeting worldly life
is. He was a thoughtful young man, and he began to seek a pure path to the highest goal. His sincere search brought him
to the essential truth: Sriman Mahaprabhu alone grants causeless mercy to living beings, and under the shelter of that
Magnanimous Lord one attains love for Krishna. Hearing the rudiments of Vaishnava practice current in his region, his
mind rose higher, and he longed to take shelter at the feet of a *mahanta-guru*, a great teacher full of love for Hari
and blessed by Mahaprabhu. <!-- F10 F11 -->

There were three such seekers: together with two friends Indrachandra twice came on foot to Navadvipa-dhama. In 1314
and early 1315 of the Bengali era (about 1907–1908) they were blessed with the darshan of the feet of a certain
paramahamsa babaji, ever absorbed in extraordinary service to Sri Vishnu.[^2] By his mercy and on his advice the three
“inquirers into the truth of Krishna” came to Srimad Bhaktivinoda Thakura. <!-- F12 F13 -->

The Thakura foresaw the good that awaited Indrachandra and gave him pancharatrika diksha. Then in Phalguna 1315 (about
February–March 1909), seeing his worth, he also gave him the initiation of a paramahamsa (*paramahamsya-samskara*), so
that nothing should hinder his worship of Krishna. From then on the Vaishnava world knew him as Srila Krishnadasa Babaji.
Having accepted the dor-kaupina, the renunciant's cloth, he drove out of his heart both worldly people and worldly
things entirely. <!-- F14 F15 F16 -->

## The Counted Name
The Thakura told him to sing the name of Krishna loudly day and night — and yet to keep count of the names
(*sankhya-nama*). His guru's instruction took hold of his heart: one who chants in this way does not let inattention
disturb his bhajana. Whoever heard the counted Name sung in his sweet voice understood that “Hare Krishna” from the lips
of a sadhu differs from a worldly utterance of the name as the genuine article differs from a counterfeit.
<!-- F17 F18 I2 -->

## In Sri Kshetra
At the beginning of 1316 (about the spring of 1909), at the wish of the Thakura, his “Acharya Prabhu,” Krishnadasa
Babaji went to Sri Kshetra — Puri — and lived there for a year and a half.[^3] No contact with worldly people clouded
his heart: the heartfelt mercy of his guru, the “Bhagavatacharya Prabhu,” kept him. Many who wore the renunciant's dress
accepted invitations and gifts from worldly people; he accepted neither. He knew that even in alms gathered by the
handful (*madhukari*) from a worldly man's hand lies the seed of worldliness, and he took nothing as alms from anyone
who was not a Vaishnava. Nor did the usual errors of such “renunciants” touch him: taking *dakshina* at invitations —
for which, his biographer says, they are liable to Yama's punishment — calling people together for crowded collective
performances, and praising the begging of alms from householders who are strangers to the Vaishnavas.
<!-- F19 F20 F21 -->

There, in Purushottama, Sri Gaurahari once put him to the test. The Lord sent him a hypocrite in the guise of a
renunciant, one of those who merely wear a devotee's dress. The man invited Krishnadasa into a society of pretended
devotees, tempted him with a pilgrimage on foot to Vrindavana along the forest paths, and assured him that serving the
sampradaya and guarding his guru's ashrama — these transcendental duties of bhakti — were actually to his own harm. But
by the pure mercy of the same Gaurahari, Krishnadasa saw through the deceit. He understood that false renunciation
(*phalgu-vairagya*) ruins the pure wealth of the Vaishnava sampradaya and is worthless; that pilgrimages on foot for one's
own gain and other ritual deeds serve enjoyment and are opposed to “service of the guru with trust” (*vishrambhena guroh
seva*), a limb of bhakti that Srila Rupa Gosvami names among the first (“Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” 1.2.74); and that
service to Sri Gurudeva cannot be compared with any service of worldly things. So the foolish pretender's wiles did
him no harm. <!-- F22 F23 Q1 I3 -->

Far from his guru, he remained with him even in Kshetra: every day he served him by worshipping his image in a
photograph (*alokachitra-murti*). For a disciple the guru's image is not a picture in the worldly sense: serving it, he
served the Thakura himself, and this was one of his fixed duties in Puri. <!-- F24 -->

## Serving the Thakura
On his return from Kshetra, Krishnadasa Babaji served Thakura Bhaktivinoda with undivided devotion day and night — all
sixty *dandas* of the day — until the Thakura's departure. According to another biography, he was already living with
the Thakura at Svananda-sukhada-kunja in Godruma in the spring of 1910, when Jagadisha Bhakti Pradipa received
initiation there. <!-- F25 -->

One who accepts the tridanda gives body, mind and speech — his whole pure nature — to the service of Krishna and His
devotees. Krishnadasa embraced that ideal wholly. He honored his “Acharya Prabhu” as the guru of all devotees
(*sarva-bhaktimatam guru*) and showed the world that service to such a guru is the source of devotion to Krishna, for the
guru is the manifestation of the Lord's own mercy. That service, says his biographer, was the seed from which the
creeper of his bhakti grew, and it has now brought him to eternal service in a kunja on the bank of Radha-kunda — to
service at the feet of the Lord of Svananda-sukhada-kunja and of his own Mistress (*nijadhishvari*).[^4]
<!-- F26 F27 Q2 I1 -->

The same service to his guru showed itself in his heart as unfeigned love for all who had taken shelter of the Thakura,
and as mercy that sought their true welfare. When the Thakura was leaving this world, everything that falls to the
first disciple and the most beloved of those he had blessed was performed by none other than Srila Krishnadasa.
<!-- F28 F29 -->

As the fruit of his service to the Thakura while the Thakura was in this world, Krishnadasa's modest learning swelled
into a river: he became an expert in the “Srimad-Bhagavata,” the ocean of the rasa of bhakti. In him one saw the very life
to which the “Bhagavata” leads; serving Hari according to the “Bhagavata,” he gained his place among the followers of
Sri Rupa, the *rupanugas*. <!-- F30 F31 -->

## Subtleties of Siddhanta, and Humility
By his guru's mercy he understood the subtle difference between separation (*vipralambha*) and union (*sambhoga*), and
he grew anxious whenever he saw in a devotee the vice of the “doctrine of sambhoga” (*sambhoga-vada*). He knew clearly
what is opposed to bhakti: the thirst for enjoyment of the karmis, who seek rasa for the sake of enjoyment; the thirst
for renunciation of the jnanis; the talk of those seeking liberation that the fruit of the Name is control of mind and
senses (*shama* and *dama*); and the pride of being a guru and the preaching of bhakti for the sake of prestige in a
pretended devotee. He knew too that to attain rasa or prema suddenly, bypassing the gradual path (*krama-pantha*), is
impossible, and that to acquire learning, wealth and honor in this world for one's own enjoyment or for one's bodily
kin, and to spend them on those people, is to diminish bhakti. <!-- F32 F33 -->

He knew the countless subtleties of siddhanta by heart, but did not disclose them unless he saw one fit to receive them.
Never did he speak a word that wounded anyone's heart. He himself gave pancharatrika diksha to no one and did not even
regard himself as anyone's shiksha-guru. And yet, seeing the countless people blessed by Sri Thakura Narottama, Sri
Thakura Bhaktivinoda and other divine sages (*divya-suri*), he never presumed to judge that the company of those great
souls did people no good: he understood that to bestow mercy on many was their calling. So in him strictness towards
himself was joined with reverence for the acharyas. <!-- F34 F35 F36 F37 I4 -->

## Departure
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta begins his memorial address thus:

“Srila Krishnadasa Babaji, the beloved servant of Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura, left the earthly world on the 10th of
Ashvina, 1915 by the English reckoning, on the krishna-chaturthi, at two in the afternoon, setting out on the great
journey (*mahaprasthana*) to the lotus feet of his Prabhupada [Srila Bhaktivinoda]” (our translation).

By our reckoning this was 27 September 1915 (1322 of the Bengali era).[^5] He departed in his birthplace, in the presence
of his father and mother, “leaving with them the body they had given,” having completed, in the acharya's words,
twenty-eight years and two months. “On the day his twenty-seventh year was completed,” says Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, “his
master disappeared from him; exactly a year and two months later the devotee went to his master” (our translation).[^6]
<!-- F39 F40 F41 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta last saw him on the 20th of Bhadra (about 5–6 September 1915). Krishnadasa told him of his bodily
illness. “Unable to bear the account of the suffering of one so close, we went elsewhere,” the acharya recalls (our
translation). These are words not of indifference but of love: the pain of one so dear to him in spirit was more than
he could bear. <!-- F42 -->

Before leaving his body, Krishnadasa Babaji asked that his remains be sent to Sri Dhama, and that money from worldly
people never, under any circumstances, be accepted for anything connected with him. So to the very end he kept what he
had been faithful to all his life: service accepts no gifts from those who live for worldly ends. The samadhi rite was
performed according to the rules for paramahamsas at Svananda-sukhada-kunja in Godruma, on the day of Ekadashi — by our
reckoning about 4 October 1915 — beside the samadhi of Sri Thakura. <!-- F43 F44 -->

## The Acharya's Word
The grief of those faithful to the Thakura, writes Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, cannot be put into words. Whoever saw the
beauty of his supremely pure life of service to the guru found a new joy; the pure devotees, bereft of the Thakura, took
some comfort in seeing his serene and gentle form. What pain the departure of this wanderer caused their hearts, Sri
Gaurahari alone knows. And he has gone to the inner chambers of Goloka, which the followers of Sri Rupa serve.
<!-- F45 F46 -->

The acharya closes by addressing him directly: Krishnadasa is in the eternal abode and has attained the service of
Krishna, while “we” remain in the world; his heartfelt love was a step on “our” path of bhakti while he was here — may
his eternal, causeless mercy abide with us now as well. Blessed are his father and mother, the parents of a pure
Vaishnava: their household life is better than the renunciation of those who have turned away from Hari. For what
decides everything is not one's station in life but one's turning towards Hari; and his father was himself a
renunciant. The place of his birth and the place where he left this world are glorified by him. <!-- F47 I5 -->
