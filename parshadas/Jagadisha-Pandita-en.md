# Jagadisha Pandita
*Джагадиша Пандит · @jagadisha-pandita*

## A Branch of Two Trees
Sri Jagadisha Pandita is one of those companions of Sri Chaitanya whom the Lord blessed with His mercy already in His
childhood pastimes. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, counting the branches of Chaitanya's tree, speaks of him together with
Hiranya:

“Jagadisha Pandita and Hiranya Mahashaya received the compassionate Lord's mercy when He was still a child.” (CC, Adi 10.70)

“In these two houses, on the day of Ekadashi, the Lord begged for the food offered to Vishnu and ate it Himself.” (CC, Adi 10.71)

Yet the same Kaviraja names him also among the branches of Nityananda's tree:

“Sri Jagadisha Pandita purifies the whole world; like a heavy monsoon rain, he pours down the nectar of love for Krishna.” (CC, Adi 11.30)

He is therefore counted a branch of both Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda. Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, listing the companions
of Nityananda, says of him:

“Jagadisha Pandita is the supreme abode of radiant holiness; Nityananda with His companions was his wealth and his life.” (CB, Antya 5.736)

The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” calls him Jagadisha and Sri Jagadisha Pandita, the “Charitamrita” Jagadisha Pandita; Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” calls him both Jagadisha Bhatta and Jagadisha Pandita Goswami, and the
biographies call him Sri Jagadisha Pandita Prabhu.[^1]

## Chandrahasa
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes is revealed by Kavi Karnapura:

“Chandrahasa, the dancer of Vraja, a knower of *rasa*, is the lover of dance Sri Jagadisha Pandita.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 143)

In Vraja he is Chandrahasa, a dancer who knows the taste of *rasa*; and now too, in Gaura's pastimes, Jagadisha loves to
dance in the Lord's kirtana. As “the dancer” the vandanas — songs in glorification of the Vaishnavas — also remember
him, and so does the “Pata-nirnaya” of Ramagopala Dasa (about 1675), a list of the holy places, the shripats:

“In Jirat are Madhavacharya and Ganga-devi; in Jashra is Jagadisha, who bears the title ‘Dancer’.” (“Pata-nirnaya” 50)

In the “Pata-paryatana” of Abhirama Dasa he is “Jagadisha, ever rejoicing” (51).

Elsewhere in the same list Karnapura speaks of him and of Hiranya:

“Other wives of the sacrificial priests are Sri Jagadisha and Hiranyaka, whose food the Lord asked for and ate on
Ekadashi.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 192)

The wives of the brahmana priests engaged in sacrifice (*yajna-patni*) fed Krishna in His pastimes; now they appeared as
Jagadisha and Hiranya to nourish the pastimes of Gaura — and again the Lord asked them for food. The biographies cite the
two verses in one order — first 192, then 143 — as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta does in the “Anubhashya.”[^2]

## From Gauhati to the Ganga
Jagadisha appeared in the east of India, in Gauhati — ancient Prag-jyotishapura, now in Assam. He was the son of
Kamalaksha Bhatta; according to the “Anubhashya,” he came of the line of Bhatta-narayana.[^3] His father and mother
were householders and great devotees of Vishnu.

When his parents left this world, Jagadisha, with his wife Dukhini and his brother, left his native land and came to
the Ganga, to Sri Mayapur. There he built a house next to the house of Jagannatha Mishra and settled in it. Which
brother came with him the sources tell differently: according to some, Hiranya Pandita; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the
“Anubhashya,” retelling the tradition of the shripat at Yashada, names the brother Mahesha — Jagadisha settled by the
Ganga to live in the company of Vaishnavas. But whether this Mahesha is Mahesha Pandita, one of the twelve gopalas, the
authoritative books, he remarks elsewhere in the same “Anubhashya,” do not say, “and therefore it is doubtful.”[^4]

A close, heartfelt friendship bound Jagadisha to Jagannatha Mishra. Vrindavana Dasa writes of him and Hiranya:

“The Panditas Jagadisha and Hiranya were great Vaishnavas, one in life with Jagannatha Mishra.” (CB, Adi 6.26)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta adds in his “Gaudiya-bhashya” on these verses that Mishra cherished these two brahmanas dearly:
they were bound to him by the ties of an unbreakable friendship.

Jagadisha's wife was named Dukhini; she is also called Dukhini-mata. He and Dukhini loved Nimai just as Jagannatha
Mishra and Shachi loved Him — with parental love, *vatsalya*. According to one of the biographies, Dukhini was like a
mother to Nimai and at times even nursed Him at her breast. Such fortune falls only to the Lord's eternal associates: to
treat God Himself as one's own child is possible only for those whom He Himself has called into this *rasa*.

Jagadisha showed the pastime of a householder. His son was Ramabhadra Goswami; according to the “Anubhashya,” his only
son.

Jagadisha was born before the Lord, and not by chance. Vrindavana Dasa speaks of the eternal associates who by the
Lord's will were born before Him and until the time came lived hidden in Nadia:

“By the order of Lord Chaitanya many of His eternal associates — Sri Chandrashekhara, Jagadisha, Gopinatha Acharya,
Shriman Pandita, Murari Gupta, Sri Garuda Pandita and Gangadasa Pandita — were born before His coming and lived hidden
in Nadia.” (CB, Adi 2.98–99)

The biographies quote the same verses in their chapters on Chandrashekhara and Gangadasa, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in
his comment on them, lists the places in scripture where Jagadisha's name appears, from the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” to
the “Charitamrita.”[^5]

## Ekadashi
How dear Jagadisha was to Him, the Lord showed while still an infant, in a childhood pastime.

Vrindavana Dasa, listing the pastimes of the Adi-khanda, names this one at once:

“In the Adi-khanda the Lord eats the grain prepared in the house of Jagadisha and Hiranya on the day of Ekadashi.”
(CB, Adi 1.100)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in his “Gaudiya-bhashya”: the day of the Lord's appearance, Ekadashi and certain Dvadashis
are called “Sri Hari-vasara,” the days of Hari; on these days the servants of Hari set aside their worldly affairs and
serve Him by fasting. But Sri Vishvambhara is the Supreme Lord Himself, and so He did not fast as a devotee would, but
accepted the dishes offered to Him. The rule of fasting applies to people, not to the Lord, who Himself lays down the
rules: Sri Hari, the protector of His devotees, accepts the offering of His servants even on Hari-vasara. So Gaura-Narayana
too wished to taste the dishes from the house of Jagadisha and Hiranya. Fasting is the service of the servants; this
time the Lord did not show the pastime of the fast obligatory for them, but showed another pastime — accepting the
offering prepared for Himself.

Mahaprabhu is the father of sankirtana, and already as an infant He made everyone chant the holy name. When He cried,
Shachi and the neighbouring women would clap and chant Hari's names — and He would grow quiet:

“There was, however, one sure remedy: as soon as kirtana began, the Lord stopped crying. Whenever someone began to clap
and sing the names of Hari, the Lord became quiet and docile. To keep the child content, everyone sang the holy names of
God constantly, and the house of Jagannatha Mishra was like Sri Vaikuntha.” (CB, Adi 6.13–15)

But one day, on Ekadashi, Nimai would not be calmed, however much everyone sang. His anxious elders lovingly asked what
He wanted, promising to bring Him anything at all. And He answered:

“The Lord replied: ‘If you want to save My life, go at once to the house of two brahmanas. Jagadisha and Hiranya are
great devotees, and what I desire is with them. Today, on Ekadashi, they are both fasting, but they have prepared an
offering for Lord Vishnu. When I eat it, I will stop crying and go out to play.’” (CB, Adi 6.20–23)

Hearing this, Shachi began to lament:

“‘What you ask for is permitted neither by people nor by the Vedas.’” (CB, Adi 6.24)

But the people around laughed and promised the child to bring Him prasada.

When Jagadisha and Hiranya learned of Nimai's wish, they saw in it neither a child's whim nor a breach of the fast.
Vrindavana Dasa relates:

“Learning of the child's wish, these brahmanas were filled with joy. The brahmanas exclaimed: ‘What a wonderful
request! We have never heard of so wise a child. How did He know that today is Ekadashi? How does He know that we have
prepared many different dishes for the Lord? Now we understand why this child is so beautiful: surely Gopala-Krishna
Himself is acting in His body.’” (CB, Adi 6.27–30)

“Then these brahmanas, with great joy, brought all the dishes. They said: ‘Child, taste these delicacies. Today our
desire to render service to Krishna has been fulfilled.’” (CB, Adi 6.32–33)

Jagadisha, the Lord's eternal associate, recognized in Mishra's son Gopala, the son of Nanda, who wished to taste his
offering in the form of Nimai, and together with Hiranya gave Him everything without hesitation. This is no breach of Ekadashi: the brahmanas gave the Lord His own
*bhoga*, and in this the purpose of all their service to Krishna was fulfilled.[^6]

Having received the offering from His devotees, Nimai's tears ceased and He ate with great joy, tasting a little
of each dish, while all around chanted Hari's names, and He ate and danced (CB, Adi 6.37–39).

One of the biographies retells this pastime in its own way: Shachi struck her forehead in amazement, the women promised
to fulfil the child's wish if He would stop crying, and Jagadisha and Hiranya, who had known before that Hari Himself
had been born as Mishra's son, fed Him to His fill and said in jest that He had eaten Krishna's share. While
Nimai ate, this biography says, there appeared to them the form of the infant Krishna — the colour of a fresh rain cloud,
radiant, with a peacock feather — and in ecstasy they began to sing “Hari! Hari!” It also says that the little Lord
came to their house Himself, and adds to His words: the devotees fast, but Vishnu is not bound by the fast and eats what
He likes.[^7]

Krishnadasa Kaviraja recalls this pastime in his account of the Lord's childhood:

“On the day of Ekadashi, pretending to be ill, He ate the food offered to Vishnu in the house of Jagadisha and
Hiranya.” (CC, Adi 14.39)

And Vrindavana Dasa, much later, when Jagadisha and Hiranya set out for Nilachala, recalls again that in childhood the
Lord had eaten the grain they offered to Krishna in their home on Ekadashi (CB, Antya 8.29). The Ekadashi story is told
in the sixth chapter of the Adi-khanda of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata.”[^8]

The biographies draw a lesson from this pastime: the Lord Himself snatches and eats what belongs to His devotee, but
will not even glance at the gifts of one without devotion. Vrindavana Dasa says the same elsewhere:

“Thus the Lord enjoys the gifts of His devotees and pays no attention to millions of offerings from those devoid of
devotion.” (CB, Madhya 9.185)

Nimai's request on Ekadashi is no breach of the fast but a pastime of God Himself: the servants keep the fast, and the
Lord accepts what they have prepared for Him, and so shows how dear they are to Him.

The house of Jagadisha and Hiranya in Navadvipa where this took place is shown to pilgrims on the circuit of the dhama
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.1111–1115). The guide Ishana, going round Navadvipa with Shrinivasa, says:

“This is the dwelling of the brahmanas Jagadisha and Hiranya, whose offering [the Lord] ate on Ekadashi. Sitting here, the brahmanas, in the sweetest words, joyfully tell of Nimai's restlessness.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.2145–2146)

 According to Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura's “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya,” it stood
about a *krosha* to the south-east of Mishra's house — no child could have known what was being cooked there; according
to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's “Gaudiya-bhashya,” the two brahmanas lived in Godrumadvipa, not far from Jagannatha Mishra's
house.

## In the Kirtanas of Navadvipa
When the Lord began sankirtana, Jagadisha was among the *parshadas* with whom He sang and danced every night in the house
of Shrivasa, and sometimes at Chandrashekhara's, only with His associates. Vrindavana Dasa names him in the list of those
present at these kirtanas (CB, Madhya 8.115).

The Lord was conquered by Jagadisha's pure devotion, and in the same way Nityananda, non-different from the Lord, was
conquered by his love: He regarded Jagadisha as His own and loved him dearly.

After the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai, Jagadisha was among the devotees who sported with the Lord in the waters of
the Ganga (CB, Madhya 13.336–339); on the day the Kazi was subdued, among those who went with the Lord in the night
procession of kirtana through the town (CB, Madhya 23.150).[^9] And when in Shridhara's house the Lord drank water from
his iron pot, Jagadisha wept with joy together with the others:

“Jagadisha, Gopinatha, Nandana Acharya, Shuklambara, Garuda and many other devotees shed tears.” (CB, Madhya 23.452)

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” and the vandanas glorify him among the Lord's associates. Narahari Chakravarti names him among
those who sang with the Lord in Shrivasa's courtyard (BR 12.1853), and in the songs quoted in the “Bhakti-ratnakara”
Jagadisha, “skilled in singing,” sings the song of the abhisheka when the Lord is bathed in Shrivasa's house, and with
Damodara sings in the kirtana on the bank of the Ganga (BR 12.2664, 12.3080). The same author remembers him in the opening glorifications: “Glory to Jagadisha,
Kashishvara and Bhagavan!” (BR 1.25; so also 3.7, 12.4, 15.4, and the “Gaura-charita-chintamani” 1.13), and in the
“Gaura-charita-chintamani” names him among the associates surrounding Gaura in the morning in His courtyard (4.1). His
name stands also in the lists of the “Narottama-vilasa.”

## Jagannatha from Purushottama
By the Lord's order Jagadisha went to Nilachala to spread krishna-bhakti and the religion of the age — the sankirtana of
the names of Hari. When this was is not known for certain.[^10] Seeing in Purushottama Lord Jagannatha — the same
Supreme Truth as Krishna, the son of Nanda, and Gaurasundara, the son of Jagannatha Mishra — Jagadisha was filled with
prema, and on the way back he was overcome by longing in separation from Jagannatha, and wept.

Many see Jagannatha in Puri, but few, on leaving, long for Him in separation. One of the biographies observes: such
longing is a sign of Jagannatha's mercy, and without it His mercy has not truly been received.

Seeing Jagadisha's tears, Jagannatha, full of mercy, came to him in a dream and bade him take His Deity and serve
Him. Then too He ordered the king of Orissa of that day to give Jagadisha that form of His which, at the
Nava-kalevara, the renewal of Jagannatha's body, had been “buried” (*samadhistha-vigraha*); according to one of the
biographies, He appeared to the king, too, in a dream. This is not a form whose service had ended: it is Jagannatha
Himself, who wished to accept Jagadisha's service. And the king counted it his good fortune to have met such a devotee,
and held it a great honour to give him the Deity.

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” that Jagadisha prayed to Jagannathadeva and in answer was allowed to
take the Deity away; one of the biographies, citing him, writes that Jagadisha, having asked Jagannatha's permission,
took His Sri-murti to Yashada and installed Him there.

Jagadisha asked how so heavy a Deity could be carried. Jagannatha replied that He would grow as light as shola pith
(according to another biography — as cork); Jagadisha should wrap Him in fresh cloth and bear Him on a pole over his
shoulder, and lower Him to the ground at the place where he chose to install Him. One of the biographies adds: the place
where He touched the earth would be Jagadisha's home for ever.

With the help of the brahmanas who accompanied him (according to one of the biographies, two, who carried the Deity in
turns), Jagadisha brought Jagannatha to Yashada on the Ganga, within Chakdaha, in present-day West Bengal. There he handed
Him to a brahmana servant to hold on his shoulder while he himself bathed in the Ganga and offered *tarpana*. Then Jagannatha suddenly
became very heavy; the servant could not hold Him and had to lower Him to the earth. Returning, Jagadisha understood:
Jagannatha wished to remain here. The Lord Himself becomes light when He wishes to be carried, and heavy when He chooses
His place.

There is a tradition that Jagadisha brought the Deity from Puri on a pole; this pole — “the staff of Jagadisha
Pandita” — is kept to this day in the temple of Jagannatha and shown to pilgrims.

Chakdaha is an ancient holy place. In the age of the Puranas its name was Rathavarma; at the end of the Dvapara-yuga Pradyumna,
Krishna's son, slew the demon Shambara here, and the place came to be called Pradyumna-nagara; and when Bhagiratha led
the Ganga to deliver the descendants of Sagara, the wheel (*chakra*) of his chariot sank here — hence the name
“Chakradaha,” now Chakdah.[^11]

The news that Jagannatha had come from Purushottama to Yashada spread far, and countless people began to flock to Him.
Since Jagannatha had remained in Yashada, Jagadisha decided to live there and never returned to Mayapur. According to
one of the biographies, Jagadisha and Hiranya moved from Mayapur “probably” after the Lord's sannyasa.

## The Shripat at Yashada
At first Jagannatha stood under a banyan on the bank of the Ganga. Then Raja Krishnachandra of Goari-Krishnanagara
helped to build a temple, and when it fell into disrepair it was restored by Mokshada Dasi, the wife of Umeshachandra
Majumdar; according to the “Anubhashya,” this was in the year 1324 of the Bengali era, as an inscription on a stone
slab records.

The temple has no dome (*chura*) and looks like an ordinary house. In it are Jagannathadeva, Radha-vallabha and
Gaura-Gopala. Jagannatha's Snana-yatra is celebrated here every year with special splendour: a platform is set up on a
wide field, and on the day of the Snana-yatra Jagannatha proceeds to it from the temple; the fair on this field is famous
to this day. There is no Ratha-yatra here, according to one of the biographies. An old, crumbling *dola-mancha*, five centuries
old, also survives: on Dola-purnima Radha-vallabha is brought out onto it.

The shripat stands in the village of Yashada (Yashora, Jashra) in the Nadia district, within a mile of Chakdaha station.
In Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's time the Ganga had receded almost a *krosha* from it; the servitors were Vijayachandra
Goswami and then Lalitamohana Goswami, of the Bandyopadhyaya line, and in Yashada they sang the *suchaka-gana*, a song
of Jagadisha's life composed by the poet Gadadhara. For some time the siddha Bhagavan Dasa Babaji of Kalna lived and
performed bhajana in Yashada; after settling in Kalna he would still come here from there.

The devotees donated much land to Jagannatha, but, according to one of the biographies, in time Jagadisha's descendants
sold it to maintain the service. Later, when they could no longer carry it on, the temple passed to Srila Bhakti Dayita
Madhava Maharaja, and since 1962 the shripat at Jashra has been one of the centres of the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math.
The biographers see a familiar pattern in this: just as the Gopala of Madhavendra Puri, so the Jagannatha and
Gaura-Gopala of Jagadisha seemed Themselves to “offer Themselves” to Bhakti Dayita Madhava Maharaja — the Lord finds
various ways to accept the service of the one He Himself has chosen.

## Gaura-Gopala
Attracted by the parental love of Jagadisha and his wife, the Lord and Nityananda came to Yashada twice, held sankirtana
there and celebrated a festival.[^12]

When the Lord was ready to set out from Yashada for Nilachala, Dukhini wept in separation from Him. To console her, the Lord
agreed to remain in Yashada in the form of the Deity — Gaura-Gopala. According to one of the biographies, this was after
a festival of many days in Jagadisha's house, and Gaurasundara said to her: “I shall live for ever in your house in the
form of the Deity.” Since then He has been served. Gaura-Gopala is golden in colour; according to the “Anubhashya,” He is
a wooden Gopala, and Dukhini-mata herself installed Him. Gaura-Gopala is not a memorial image or a likeness: He is the
Lord Himself, who remained in Yashada out of love for His devotee.

## With Nityananda's Companions
Jagadisha was among Nityananda's companions at the chida-dadhi festival in Panihati:

“Dhananjaya, Jagadisha, Paramesvara dasa, Mahesha, Gauridasa, Krishnadasa Hoda.” (CC, Antya 6.62)

Together with the Gauda devotees he went to the Lord in Nilachala. Vrindavana Dasa writes how he and Hiranya set out
together with Advaita:

“Sri Jagadisha Pandita and Hiranya Bhagavata also came joyfully, both intoxicated with the sweetness of the *rasas* of
divine love for Krishna.” (CB, Antya 8.28)

Murari Gupta says the same (4.17.10); according to one of the biographies, Jagadisha went with the Gaudiyas to the
Ratha-yatra every year.

Probably it was he who was among the devotees when, after His sannyasa, the Lord met Shachi in Shantipura: Vrindavana
Dasa names in the list Haridasa, Murari, Shrigarbha, Narayana, Jagadisha and Gopinatha (CB, Antya 4.273). And when at
the festival in Kheturi the Lord appeared with His associates, the “Bhakti-ratnakara” names Jagadisha too among the
dancers (10.595; so also the “Narottama-vilasa” 7.110).

His dearest disciple was Raghunatha, or Raghunathacharya, the son of the lame Bhagavan; the “Narottama-vilasa” tells
how he met Jahnava-devi on her journey (6.214–215), and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls him a disciple of Jagadisha Pandita
Goswami.

## Appearance and Disappearance
In our calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math the appearance day of Srila Jagadisha Pandita is Gaura Ekadashi of
the month of Narayana (in 2027, 18 January),[^13] and his disappearance day is Gaura Tritiya of the same month (in 2027,
10 January). The biographies and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta also name the shukla-tritiya of the month of Pausha as the day of
his departure; on this day a festival is held every year in Yashada. The sources do not give the year of his appearance
or departure, nor do they say plainly where he left this world.[^14] A late life, the “Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya,” names
the same shukla-tritiya of Pausha and says that, disappearing, he “went to Vrindavana” (12.43).

## According to the “Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” (a Late Source)
The only full life of Jagadisha Pandita is the Bengali poem “Sri Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” by Ananda Dasa (eighteenth
century; manuscript of 1815). Ananda Dasa is fifth in the line of disciples from Jagadisha: Jagadisha — Raghunatha
Acharya — Bhagavatananda — Premananda — Radhacharana — Ananda Dasa. He undertook the book, he says, at the command of
Bhagavatananda given in a dream, and writes what he heard “from the lips of the saints.” It is the tradition of the
shripat, written down much later; in many points it differs from the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” and the “Charitamrita,” and
it is retold briefly below, with reservations in the notes.

Ananda Dasa begins with praise:

“I worship Prabhu Jagadisha, the friend of Duhkhini, maddened by prema for Chaitanya, grieved only by the sufferings [of others].” (“Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” 1.29)

Day and night, he writes, Jagadisha says: “Gaura is the treasure of my life!”, dances crying “Chaitanya!”, and himself
gives the poor and the wretched the jewel of Chaitanya's name.

**Childhood.** His father, Kamalaksha, a brahmana of the “eastern country,” was “a Bandya of Gayaghara, a descendant of
Bhatta Narayana”; his mother was named Bhagyavati. Both served Vishnu day and night, and Narayana, pleased with their
bhakti, promised them a son: “Your son will be the crown of Vaishnavas.” Jagadisha was born in the evening of the bright
Ekadashi of the month of Magha, Bhima-ekadashi, when the names of Hari were being sung in every house; his parents named
him “Jagadisha,” “Lord of the world,” because the son's form “enchants the world” and he “will deliver the world.”
 As soon as he could speak he repeated the names of Radha and Krishna, played sankirtana with the
children of the neighbourhood, and made a form of Krishna out of clay and worshipped it. By the age of eight he had
mastered the shastras, and he honoured the “Bhagavata” as the very form of Krishna; in his ninth year he received the
sacred thread. As a young man he taught the brahmanas' sons the shastras, and having studied the
“Bhagavata,” they all became Vaishnavas; in debate he defeated the pandita Vidyanidhi Bhattacharya, who taught that
Brahman is formless: the impersonal effulgent Brahman is only the radiance of the Lord's body. The defeated pandita
surrendered to him and attained prema.

**Family and Settling by the Ganga.** Jagadisha himself taught the shastras to his younger brother Mahesha. His wife was
Duhkhini (Dukhini), the daughter of a poor brahmana, Tapana. He instructed his aged parents to hear and chant about
Krishna and read them the “Bhagavata,” and “having attained the goal of life, [they] went to the abode of Vishnu”; he
performed the shraddha in a tulasi grove before the shalagrama, offering the ancestors maha-prasada, and completed it
with sankirtana. Seeing that the people around had turned away from Krishna, he resolved to live by
the Ganga. “Wherever you go, there I shall surely go,” said Dukhini, and Mahesha: “I am your servant.” On the seventh day
of the journey they reached the Ganga, and, reflecting on where the golden Lord would descend, Jagadisha settled in
Navadvipa. With him, as in the “Anubhashya,” came his brother Mahesha, not Hiranya.

**Navadvipa.** He lived next to Jagannatha Mishra; the love of Dukhini and Shachi was “like that of two sisters born of
one mother,” and after the Lord's appearance Shachi entrusted Nimai to Dukhini: “This is my son, beautiful Nimai; always
keep Him close to you.” Jagadisha and Hiranya Bhagavata “have one mind and one and the same bhajana”; the
book names no kinship. Both kept Ekadashi, praying that Krishna would descend and deliver the people. The
book builds its account of Ekadashi on both the “Charitamrita” and the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”: Nimai says that “a fit has
come upon” Him and sends His father to the house of the two brahmanas, and they bring the offering themselves.[^15]
 But it adds a second Ekadashi as well: Nimai came while Jagadisha was performing his puja and ate a little
of the *naivedya*; by the marks on His feet Jagadisha recognised Krishna in Him, and Dukhini saw Him four-armed and begged
forgiveness for having raised Him as an ordinary child. The Lord said to them:

“You two were formerly My associates, and now you have taken birth as devotees.” (“Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” 7.106)

So the book relates; this account is not in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata.”[^16]

**Jagannatha.** After the night kirtanas at Shrivasa's and the subduing of the Kazi, Jagadisha learned that the Lord would
take sannyasa and live near Jagannatha, and the Lord Himself told him to go to Nilachala; he left Dukhini in his brother's
house. On the way he bowed down to Gopinatha in Remuna and to Sakshi-gopala in Kataka, and from the bridge of
Athara-nala to the temple he went prostrating at every step. According to the book, Jagannatha spoke to him
in waking and, at his request for His former body that rests in the northern part of the temple enclosure, answered:

“The king's envoys will not hinder [you]; I will go on your back; take [Me] and go without fear.” (“Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” 8.33)

In the morning an officer brought him the king's permission. Jagadisha carried the Deity alone, and at the village of
Yashoda he prayed: “Jagannatha, stay here,” and He commanded: “You serve Me.”[^17] The people
rejoiced that darshan of Jagannatha had come within their reach without a long journey; the local king, doubting, could
not move the Deity, begged forgiveness and gave land free of taxes for the service, and Jagadisha instructed him to care
for his subjects like sons and to establish devotion to Krishna in his kingdom. He married Mahesha, who then
settled in his father-in-law's house; three sons were born to Jagadisha himself.

**The Lord in Yashoda.** The book also tells at length of the Lord's two visits to Yashoda, of which Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
speaks. After His sannyasa, in Shantipura, the Lord felt that Jagadisha was remembering Him, and came to him by night with
Nityananda:

“There [lives] the foremost of mahantas, by whose prema Jagannatha Bhagavan is conquered.” (“Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” 9.25)

Both Lords danced before Jagannatha. The Lord called Dukhini mother and asked for hot *paramanna* — and the
cow gave her milk of herself; for Nityananda Jagadisha found mangoes in the winter month of Pausha, and a wave of the river
cast a fish on the bank for him. When Dukhini, lost in prema, stirred the boiling *payasa* with her hand, it was the
Lord's hand that burned: “This pain Mahaprabhu has taken upon Himself,” Jagadisha told her. Mahesha came
too, and Nityananda accepted him. And when Jagadisha's little son in childish play defiled what had been
prepared for the Lords, Jagadisha, for whom a son without devotion to Krishna is no support of the family, asked the Lord
to take his sons; the three boys left their bodies and went to the Lord, and Dukhini, serving Gauranga, set aside her
grief.[^18]

In the morning, when the Lord was about to leave, Dukhini fainted, and He consoled her:

“Listen, mother Duhkhini, to My words: install My image. In that image I shall always dwell, and I shall call you mother.” (“Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” 10.13–14)

That same night a craftsman sent by the Lord brought forth the Deity of Nimai crawling on His knees, as He had been in
childhood in Dukhini's house, and left it at the door; in the morning she found Nimai crying at the door and gave Him the
breast. The Lord and Nityananda came again, and the Lord said to Jagadisha and Dukhini:

“I pervade everything; I am present everywhere. In wood, in stone or in clay — in whatever a devotee worships Me with devotion, in that, by the power of the devotee's devotion, I abide; I fulfil the desires of devotees — in this there is nothing else. Whoever says that there is a difference between Me, My devotee and My Deity surely goes to hell.” (“Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” 10.87–91)

Nityananda, seeing the Lord and Gaura-Gopala at one meal, exclaimed: “Two Gauras have appeared in your house!” — and:
“The two are one.” As He left, the Lord let Dukhini choose which of the two forms to keep, and she took Gopala on her
lap.[^19]

**Nritya-vinodi.** According to the book, the Lord set out from Shantipura for Nilachala with the devotees, but without
Jagadisha found no joy in the kirtana; Nityananda brought him, and the Lord told him to go in front, dancing:

“Seeing the beauty of that dance, which captivated everyone's mind, [they] gave [him] the name Sri Nritya-vinodi.” (“Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” 11.18)

So the “lover of dance” of Karnapura's verse receives his name in the book — “the one who delights in
dance.”[^20] Later, according to the book, the Lord sent Nityananda to preach in Gauda: “Take with You Jagadisha,
who is as dear to Me as life.” The Lord told Bhagavan Acharya to live as a householder and to entrust his
future son to Jagadisha; so Raghunatha Acharya grew up with Jagadisha and received from him the mantra of Radha-Krishna
with the instruction: “Always say: 'Gauranga! Sri Hari!'” To Raghunatha he also passed on the teaching he had heard from
Mahaprabhu Himself: the complete Brahman is Krishna, the son of Nanda; in the age of Kali He descends as the golden
Krishna-Chaitanya; bhakti is born of the company of devotees; the *sadhaka* should serve under the guidance of the guru
in the outer body, and inwardly in the perfected, spiritual one. Another of his disciples was Kamalakanta Dasa of
Durgapur.[^21]

**The Last Years.** The disciples went out to preach: Raghunatha Acharya in Malipara, Kamalakanta in Durgapur. A daughter,
Rasamanjari, and a son, Ramabhadra, were born to Jagadisha; he gave his daughter in marriage to Gopivallabha of Jirat, the
son of Madhava Acharya and of Ganga, Nityananda's daughter, and passed on to Ramabhadra the power to preach.
Of his departure the book says:

“Thus Sri Jagadisha, having delivered the souls, disappeared, meditating on the feet of Gaura. In the month of Pausha, in the bright fortnight, on the day of the third tithi, he disappeared and went to Vrindavana.” (“Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” 12.42–43)

In Vraja, according to Ananda Dasa, he is Kalavati, who “descended in a male form in the house of a
brahmana” to assist in the pastimes of Gauranga.[^22]

## Significance
Jagadisha Pandita is Chandrahasa, the dancer of Vraja, and one of the priests' wives who fed Krishna; in Gaura's
pastimes he is a branch of both Chaitanya and Nityananda, the one who, in Krishnadasa Kaviraja's words, purifies the
whole world and pours down the nectar of love for Krishna like a monsoon rain.

In his life the Lord revealed two sides of His love for His devotees. As an infant He Himself demanded his offering on
Ekadashi, showing that fasting is the service of the servants, while the Lord accepts what is prepared for Him with love
and joyfully eats what belongs to His devotee. And Jagannatha, seeing his longing in separation, Himself wished to go with
him from Purushottama, Himself became light and Himself chose His place; and Gaura, moved by Dukhini's tears, Himself
remained in her house as Gaura-Gopala. Parental love such as Jagadisha and Dukhini had for the Lord is accessible only to His eternal associates, and
the Lord finds ways to accept the service of those whom He Himself has chosen.
