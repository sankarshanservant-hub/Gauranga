# Sri Advaita Acharya
*Шри Адвайта Ачарья · @advaita*

## Maha-Vishnu in the Form of a Devotee
The lives of Mahaprabhu's companions almost always tell of parshadas — eternal servants of the Lord who descend into the
world with Him, or at another time, to take part in His lilas. Sri Advaita Acharya has to be read differently. He
conducts Himself as one of the companions, but in truth He is Maha-Vishnu Himself, the purusha-avatara by whose will the
universes arise, come in the form of a devotee. So the Gosvamis of Vrindavana teach, and so says Svarupa Damodara in the
verses with which Kaviraja Gosvami opens the “Chaitanya-charitamrita”:

“Maha-Vishnu is the maker of the world, who creates it through maya; and this Lord Advaita Acharya is His avatara” (CC, Adi 1.12)

The next verse explains His name as well: He is called Advaita, “non-different,” because He is non-different from Hari,
the Supreme Lord, and Acharya because He teaches the sacred path of bhakti; He is the Supreme Himself, come as His own
devotee (CC, Adi 1.13). Therefore all that is told below — His “not knowing,” His “tests,” His discipleship, His disputes
and punishments — is not the story of an ascetic rising towards God, but the lila of the Lord Himself, who has taken on
the role of a servant.

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains how creation takes place. Maya acts in two ways, and Maha-Vishnu as the creator
appears in two forms: as the efficient cause, nimitta-karana, in the form of Vishnu; and in the pradhana, the primeval
ground of matter, in the form of Rudra — and this second identity of His is Advaita[^1]. And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati, commenting on the verse of the “Charitamrita” that Advaita bestows good upon the whole world (Adi 6.12),
writes: Advaita is Maha-Vishnu, the supreme teacher of the world and the root of all good; whoever cannot understand
that everything that comes from Him — even what seems tangled and hard in this world — is pure, eternal and complete
good, deprives himself of devotion to Krishna.

In the Pancha-tattva Sri Chaitanya is the bhakta-rupa, the Lord in the form of a devotee; Nityananda is the
bhakta-svarupa, His manifestation as a devotee; and Advaita is the bhakta-avatara, the descent of God as a devotee. All
three are Prabhus, vishnu-tattva, and therefore tulasi is offered at Their feet, as at the feet of Vishnu. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta explains: the purusha-avatara Maha-Vishnu, accepting the mood of a devotee, appears in the world as
Advaita. Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks of the Three Prabhus thus:

“One is Mahaprabhu, and the other two are Prabhus; the two Prabhus serve the feet of Mahaprabhu” (CC,
Adi 7.14)[^2]

Sri Chaitanya is the body of the movement of bhakti, and Nityananda and Advaita are its limbs; Nityananda, non-different
from Baladeva, Sankarshana-Vishnu, and Advaita, “Acharya Gosvami,” are the two “angas” of Sri Chaitanya, two parts of
His body. In the image of the tree of devotion with which Kaviraja Gosvami describes the Lord's companions, Mahaprabhu
Himself is the main trunk, and Advaita and Nityananda are two secondary trunks:

“Higher up, the tree branched into two trunks: one is named Advaita, and the other Nityananda” (CC,
Adi 9.21)

“The glorious Advaitachandra is the second trunk of the immortal tree of Sri Chaitanya, and His followers are the branches of that trunk. To them we bow down” (CC, Adi 12.3)

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, opening his account of Navadvipa on the eve of the Lord's appearance, places Advaita before all
its Vaishnavas:

“In Navadvipa at that time lived Advaita Acharya, the greatest Vaishnava, renowned throughout the world.” (CB, Adi 2.78)

“He was a venerable spiritual teacher, equal to Shiva himself in explaining the science of devotion to Sri Krishna, and
the essence of true knowledge and renunciation.” (CB, Adi 2.79)

And Mahaprabhu Himself, telling Vallabha Bhatta in His humility that He knows nothing of devotion, names those whose
company purified His heart — and Advaita first:

“Advaitacharya Gosani is Ishvara Himself, and in His company My mind became pure.”
(CC, Antya 7.17)

“In all the scriptures and in devotion to Krishna He has no equal; therefore His name is Advaita Acharya, the peerless teacher.” (CC, Antya 7.18)

“By His mercy even mlecchas gain devotion to Krishna. Who can describe the power of His Vaishnava nature?” (CC, Antya 7.19)

Narottama Dasa Thakura, in a song addressed to Sitapati, “the husband of Sita,” begs Advaita for mercy: without it one
cannot attain service either to Chaitanya or to Nityananda. But the same truth has another side. The
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” says:

“One who glorifies Advaita Acharya as the foremost of Vaishnavas is a true Vaishnava. In every birth he will be bound
to Krishna.” (CB, Madhya 10.162)

And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: whoever sees in Advaita the best of Vaishnavas is a
Vaishnava; but whoever considers Him the vishaya, the object of love — that is, Krishna Himself — and Sri Chaitanya His
ashraya, His loving servant, will not reach the feet of Krishna. Advaita is non-different from Hari, but in His lila He
is the servant of Gaura, and wishes to be known precisely so.

## Sadashiva
Speaking of who Advaita is in the lilas of Krishna, the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (76–80) calls Him Sadashiva — a
manifestation of the Lord in Vraja, non-different from Chaitanya. Kavi Karnapura also cites a tradition from the
“Shiva-tantra,” told by Bhairava. Once in the month of Karttika, at the festival of lamps, the Dipa-yatra,
Shankara-deva, Bhairava's guru, saw Krishna and Balarama dancing among the cowherd boys, and himself longed to become a
cowherd boy — and whirled into their dance. So by Krishna's mercy Sadashiva in Vraja is twofold: he is both Shiva
himself and a cowherd boy dancing with Krishna. Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala” calls Advaita Mahesha: “Mahesha
Thakura went forth before all: He was born in a brahmana family, named Kamalaksha” (CM, Sutra-khanda, part 8, v. 600);
and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in his summary of that chapter, says briefly: Shiva came as Advaita Prabhu[^3].

Kavi Karnapura says the same in the “Sri Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya”: Advaita is “He who is known on earth as Rudra,
and here, in a family of brahmanas, bears the name Kamalaksha” (7.26); he calls Him “the master Girisha” (7.30), and
Sarvabhauma in the same poem greets Him with a verse: “Obeisance to You, Advaita, Mahesha, the great soul, by whose
grace love for the feet of Gauranga is born!” (14.45). Rudra, Girisha and Mahesha here are the same Sadashiva,
non-different from Maha-Vishnu, not a demigod.

## Names
His earlier name was Sri Kamalaksha Veda-panchanana; according to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” Kamalaksha, or
Kamalakanta, Veda-panchanana.

The “Charitamrita” explains each of His names. He is Mangala, “auspiciousness,” for He bestows good upon the whole world
(Adi 6.12). He is Advaita:

“Advaita, the abode of all good qualities, is a part of Maha-Vishnu. He is non-different from the Lord, and so the name Advaita, “Non-dual”, fits Him in full” (CC, Adi 6.25)

He is Acharya, for He is wholly engaged in preaching bhakti and has become the teacher of all devotees; the two names
together — Advaita Acharya — make up His full name (Adi 6.28–29). And He is called Kamalaksha, “Lotus-eyed,” because He
is a part of the body of the lotus-eyed Lord:

“Because He is the limb and the part of the Lotus-eyed Lord, He bears the beautiful name Kamalaksha, “Lotus-eyed””
(CC, Adi 6.30)[^4]

Mahaprabhu affectionately called Him “Nara”[^5]. He is also called “Acharya Gosvami,” and the Lord Himself in the
“Charitamrita” calls Him “Acharya Gosai” (Antya 12.70); Narottama in his song calls Him Sitapati.

## Appearance in Shrihatta
Advaita's father was Kuvera Pandita, a Varendra brahmana — according to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (81), Kubera, the
friend of Mahadeva and leader of the guhyakas. His mother was named Nabha Devi[^6]. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.2041–2043)
tells of them: Kuvera, a worshipper of Nrisimha, lived in Navagrama near Sylhet; fortune attended him on the path of
bhakti, and he knew nothing but the feet of Krishna; and his faithful wife Nabha Devi is worthy of the worship of the
whole world — for she became the mother of Advaita.

Advaita's parents lived for a time in Shantipura on the Ganga, and there they saw a prophetic dream. A wondrous person
was asking another to take birth in the world to relieve the sufferings of the people of the age of Kali, and said that
He Himself would come later. Having received this sign, the couple returned to their home in Navagrama (Navegrama) in
Shrihatta (Sylhet), and there a son was born to them[^7].

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells this dream in more detail. Kuvera and Nabha, then living in Shantipura, heard the Vaishnavas
blasphemed and in their grief wished to die; and in a dream Kuvera saw a golden, radiant Purusha who, holding another
Purusha by both hands, said to Him:

“‘To dispel this suffering of beings stricken by Kali, quickly descend You upon the earth. When You draw [Me], I shall
not be able to stay [away]; with [My] elder brother I shall soon appear [in the world].’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 5.2050–2051)

And He entered Nabha's womb. The radiant Purusha is Gaura Himself; the “elder brother” is Nityananda. The couple returned
to Navagrama, and when Advaita was born there, a voice was heard: “Through Him Sri Krishna Chaitanya will appear on
earth. He will soon bring Nityananda-Rama… in every home prema-bhakti will be preached” (5.2058–2060). Having appeared
together with Madhavendra Puri, Shachi and Jagannatha, Advaita, the same book says, “always keeps Himself hidden; at
times, by Sri Chaitanya's will, He becomes manifest” (5.2039, 5.2064).

He appeared on the seventh lunar day of the bright fortnight of the month of Magha — on Maghi shukla-saptami, which has
ever since been called Advaita-saptami. In our calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math) this day — Gaura-saptami of the
month of Madhava — is marked as “The Appearance of the Avatara of Maha-Vishnu, Sri Advaita Acharya.” A song of
Ghanashyama in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.1759–1762) celebrates the day: the “moon” of Advaita rose from the womb of
Nabha Devi; Kuvera gave gifts to brahmanas and the poor, stole into the birth chamber and rejoiced to see his son's
face; the villagers wondered by what merit such a son was granted to an old man, and the gods, unseen by men, showered
the house with flowers.

In the “Bhakti-ratnakara” Ishana also relates that the couple had long wished for a son and received him only in their
old age; Kuvera was honoured as “Mishra” and “Panditacharya”; a song of Narahari Dasa in raga Bhupali echoes Ghanashyama:
“Appearing, the Lord, beautiful Advaita, drove away the intoxication of [the age of] Kali” — and at the moment of His
birth everyone around loudly uttered the names “Sri Krishna” and “Govinda” (12.1753–1767).

According to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” He appeared in 1434. He was fifty-two years older than Mahaprabhu — when
the Lord appeared, Advaita was fifty-two — and seventeen years older than Haridasa Thakura (who, according to the
“Advaita-vilasa,” was born in 1451). The same “Abhidhana” says that He remained on earth until 1559 and departed at the
age of one hundred and twenty-five, and the “Advaita-vilasa,” that He stayed in the world “a century and a
quarter”[^8]. From Shrihatta He moved to Shantipura on the Bhagirathi, not far from Navadvipa.

## Study and Pilgrimage
According to the “Prema-vilasa,” Advaita studied the Vedas and other scriptures under Shantacharya in Phullabati — one
of the quarters of Shantipura — and received from him the title “acharya”[^9]. When Kuvera and Nabha Devi left this
world, He went to Gaya on the pretext of performing
the funeral rites, and from there set out to wander through the holy
places of India.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” adds that Kuvera moved with his fellow villagers from Navagrama to Shantipura so as to live by the
Ganga, and had his son study the shastras; the pandits of Shantipura, seeing His deeds, said, “Advaita is not a man at
all.” And His parents, though they knew who their son was, in their love for Him did not keep it in mind (5.2070–2077).

From the same book comes a story that Raghava Pandita heard “from the mouths of the elders.” In Mathura there lived a
learned brahmana from elsewhere who constantly reviled the Vaishnavas; one day he set about it in Advaita's presence.
Advaita, angered, cried, “Look here: I am here, with the chakra in My hand!” — and became four-armed. The brahmana in
terror begged forgiveness, and Advaita, concealing the form, said:

“‘Always regarding yourself as an offender, give up everything and perform the sankirtana of the name. Even at the cost
of your life, satisfy the Vaishnavas… and the form you have seen, keep it secret.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 5.188–190)

The brahmana began going from house to house among the Vaishnavas of Mathura, weeping, and they guessed: “A brahmana
pilgrim came to Mathura and stayed here in secret… He is the Lord in human form; by His grace the brahmana has become
like this” (5.195–196). Advaita's anger is the same play of mercy, and the form He revealed is His own form as
Maha-Vishnu.

In Mithila, the “Advaita-vilasa” relates, He met Vidyapati. The great Maithili poet was by then very old; in a sweet
voice he recited his verses about Krishna, and the young pilgrim and the old poet glorified each other. The songs of
Vidyapati, a mahajana, were later sung before Mahaprabhu in Puri.

In Vraja Advaita stayed for a long time beneath a banyan tree in Vrindavana. The place is called Advaita-vata: whoever
sees it is purified and attains the love of the gopis. There He became absorbed in the worship of Krishna and in
meditation understood that Krishna was about to appear in Navadvipa. From Vrindavana He returned to Shantipura, staying a
few days in Navadvipa on the way, and the people of Shantipura, who had long pined without Him, were overjoyed.

According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” in Vrindavana the Vrajavasis gave Him shelter; He lived on fruits, roots and milk,
uttered thunderous hunkaras and wept, “Shall I see Krishna?” — and worshipped Krishna beneath a banyan near the Yamuna,
at Praskandana-ghata. Learning that the time of Krishna Chaitanya's appearance had come, He left for Gauda and came by
way of Navadvipa to Shantipura, where the people had prepared a dwelling for Him in advance (5.2084–2089; 12.1773–1776).
Showing Srinivasa this place, Raghava Pandita says:

“The banyan under which Advaita stayed became famed everywhere as Advaita-vata. By the sight of this Advaita-vata all
sins are destroyed, and most rare prema-bhakti becomes attainable.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 5.2091–2092)

## Disciple of Madhavendra Puri
Advaita's guru was Sri Madhavendra Puri — he whose disciple Ishvara Puri gave initiation to the Lord Himself, and whom
Mahaprabhu called the original source of ecstatic love for Krishna. How and when they met is told in different
ways[^10]. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” says that long before the Lord's appearance Madhavendra, in his wanderings, came to
Advaita's dwelling in Shantipura: Advaita saw in him all the signs of a true Vaishnava and bowed down before him, and
Madhavendra embraced Him and bathed Him in tears of love. They became so absorbed in talk of Krishna that they forgot
their bodies, and Advaita accepted instruction from him (CB, Antya 4.433–440). The “Charitamrita” names the occasion as
well: Madhavendra, at the command of Gopala, was going to Puri for sandalwood, and on his way through Bengal stopped at
Advaita's house:

“He came to Shantipura, to the house of Advaitacharya. Seeing Puri's love, the Acharya felt bliss within.”
(CC, Madhya 4.110)

“The Acharya eagerly received the mantra from him, and Puri, having initiated Him, went on to the south.”
(CC, Madhya 4.111)

From Shantipura Madhavendra went south, to Remuna.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” holds to the first version: after His parents' departure Advaita “on the pretext of Gaya went
round all the tirthas; from Madhavendra Puri He received the mantra of initiation” — and it quotes an old verse:

“‘I worship Sri Advaita Prabhu, the giver of prema-bhakti, dear to Sriman Madhavendra Puri, belonging to the Sri
Madhva-sampradaya.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 5.2082)

Advaita is Maha-Vishnu Himself; why should He need a guru? He accepted initiation to show conditioned souls that one
cannot do without a guru — just as Mahaprabhu Himself accepts initiation from Ishvara Puri. Vrindavana Dasa says that
Advaita only played the role of Madhavendra's disciple[^11].

Murari Gupta (“Kadacha” 1.4.5–6) binds them still more closely: one portion of the Lord appeared in two ways — as
Madhava Puri and as Advaita Acharya — and Chandrashekhara, renowned on earth as Acharyaratna, became the disciple of
them both. This does not diminish Advaita: He is Maha-Vishnu Himself, non-different from the Lord, and Madhavendra is
His guru and the root of the tree of devotion.

Madhavendra's disciples in the line of the Madhva-sampradaya are Ishvara Puri, Advaita, Paramananda Puri, Brahmananda
Puri, Ranga Puri, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Raghupati Upadhyaya and others; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta names Ishvara Puri,
Nityananda and Advaita as the chief among them. According to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (23–24), Ishvara Puri received from
Madhavendra the fruit of madhurya, love in the conjugal mood, and Advaita the fruits of dasya and sakhya, service and
friendship. It is said that Pundarika Vidyanidhi received initiation from Madhavendra at the same time as Advaita. The
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” sees in this discipleship the highest praise of Madhavendra himself:

“What more can I say of the devotion of one who became the spiritual teacher of Sri Advaita Acharya?” (CB, Adi 9.157)

And Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains why Mahaprabhu always honoured Advaita as an elder and a teacher: Advaita is the
godbrother of Ishvara Puri, the Lord's guru.

## Sita and Shri; Two Houses
Advaita married two daughters of Nrisimha Baduri (Bhaduri), a devoted brahmana — Shri and Sita. Vishnu has three
shaktis — Shri, Bhu and Nila; without His shaktis Advaita too would not have revealed Himself in fullness. According to
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” Sita is Yogamaya, and Shri is her prakasha, her manifestation[^12]. The wedding with Sita
took place in Phulia-nagara, and after it Advaita settled in Shantipura, in Nadia[^13].

Advaita had two houses: in Shantipura and in Navadvipa-Mayapura, not far from the house of Srivasa. Navadvipa in those
days was called “the Oxford of India,” and in the Navadvipa house Advaita kept a tol — a Sanskrit school — and studied
the scriptures deeply. He lived there from time to time and would talk of Krishna until dawn with the devotees headed by
Srivasa. Seeing around Him people bereft of devotion to Vishnu, He taught the “Bhagavad-gita” and the
“Srimad-Bhagavatam” and revealed their essence as devotional service.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” relates that the wedding was arranged by the worthy people of Shantipura, while Advaita, “smiling
very softly, gave everyone His consent,” and Nrisimha Bhaduri gave Him both his daughters; of the two homes it says,
“Sometimes He lives in Shantipura, sometimes in Nadia,” and in Nadia mostly in Srivasa's house, where, tasting the rasa
of Krishna, He takes no notice of day or night (12.1778–1790).

## The Call
Having returned to Bengal, Advaita became the head of its Vaishnavas. Around Him gathered Srivasa, Chandrashekhara
Acharya, Gangadasa and others; Srivasa's brothers came to His house to hear the “Bhagavatam” and sing sankirtana. And
Navadvipa all around was far from God. The learned were hostile to bhakti; they took for religion karma-yoga, rites
performed for wealth; they taught the “Gita” and the “Bhagavatam” without a word about devotion; they were proud of
birth and learning — and nowhere was the name of Krishna to be heard. The Vaishnavas headed by Advaita grieved.

Kavi Karnapura in the “Mahakavya,” praising Navadvipa, its brahmanas, physicians and merchants, concludes: “This [town]
the great-hearted Advaita Himself adorned, He who is honoured by the saintly for His supreme greatness; and it was
through His love that people beheld the Lord on earth” (2.11). And Nishikanta Sanyal, writing in the 1930s in the line
of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, sees in Advaita the acknowledged head of the small Vaishnava community of Navadvipa: in His tol
all His teaching upheld the supremacy of unalloyed devotion to Krishna, and His simple worship — with tulasi and Ganga
water — He wished to keep distinct from the lifeless rites of those who seek results and those who seek
liberation[^14].

The people of the age of Kali were indifferent to their only good — to the sankirtana of Krishna's name, and Advaita's
heart overflowed with compassion. He wished to awaken the whole people to harinama, and He reasoned: a partial avatara
would not be enough here — the world would be saved only if Hari Himself descended. The “Charitamrita” conveys His
thoughts:

“Only when I have brought Krishna here and spread His kirtana will My name ‘Advaita’ bear fruit.”” (CC, Adi
3.101)

And He began to call the Lord. He worshipped Krishna with Ganga water and tulasi leaves — tulasi-manjaris — often
fasted and gathered the Vaishnavas for common prayer; and He called loudly, with a “roar of love,” prema-hunkara:

“Meditating on Krishna’s lotus feet, He constantly offered Him tulasi buds (*manjari*) with the water of the Ganga” (CC, Adi 3.107)

“With a mighty roar He called out to Krishna, and in this way He brought about Krishna’s descent” (CC,
Adi 3.108)

“This is the chief cause of Chaitanya’s descent: the Lord, the protector of dharma, descends by the desire of His devotee” (CC, Adi 3.109)

Vrindavana Dasa says that this call was heard in Vaikuntha:

“He called out loudly to Sri Krishna. The sound of Advaita Acharya's voice pierced the coverings of the universe and was
heard in Vaikuntha.” (CB, Adi 2.82)

“Hearing the loving call of Sri Advaita, Lord Krishna appeared in person, for He is always captivated by the love of
His devotees.” (CB, Adi 2.83)

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.3753–3764) sings of Him: Advaita's body is a treasury of the nectar of bhakti, non-different
from the body of Chaitanya Himself; He brought Chaitanya down by His roar and then glorified Him everywhere; Advaita is
non-different from Gauranga, and His consort Sita is the mother of the universe; whoever takes shelter of His feet will
attain love for Gaura. So the cause of Gaura's descent was Advaita's compassion for the people of Kali, His “audacious”
call and the prayer of Haridasa, of which more below — and the Lord Himself confirmed this more than once.

The songs gathered in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.3753–3818) glorify Him in various ragas: “Glory, glory to merciful
Advaita Acharya, by whose thunderous roaring Gaura's descent took place!” (12.3761); He weeps, repeating “Gora, Gora!”
(12.3782), roars so that “the pashandis weep with fear” (12.3808), and “made the earth rich, lavishly distributing the
treasure of bhakti” (12.3812). And on the evening when Nityananda was born in distant Ekachakra, Advaita, according to a
song of Narahari, “in Shantipura roars in a surge of bliss… and, slapping [His] armpits, says: ‘I have brought, I have
brought the hero Bala!’” (12.1713).

The loud sankirtana angered the godless. They objected and threatened; they mocked: “The Lord sleeps through the four
months of the rains — wake Him with your shouting, and there will be a great offence, and famine will come!”; they said
that some brahmanas sing only to fill their bellies. When the devotees lost heart, Advaita encouraged them: the Lord
will hear and will come.

## Advaita and Haridasa
Long before the Lord's appearance Haridasa — by tradition, an avatara of Brahma — settled in Phulia, not far from
Advaita's house in Shantipura. He lived in a cave and from time to time came to Advaita in Shantipura; so it was also
after he had preached in Chandpur, in the house of Govardhana and Hiranya Majumdar. Haridasa was drawn by Advaita's
talks, and Advaita recognised in him a bhajanandi who chants the holy name without cease. He built him a cave-like hut,
explained the “Gita” to him every day in the spirit of steadfast devotion, nishtha-bhakti, and sent him prasada from
His house. Haridasa spent most of his time with Advaita — in Navadvipa-Mayapura and in Shantipura; in His circle they
grieved over the godlessness of the people, expounded the “Gita” and the “Bhagavatam” and sang kirtan.

Haridasa was born in a family of yavanas, yet Advaita honoured him as no one else would have dared. At the yearly
shraddha for His father He offered a meal to the Lord, and the first dish, the shraddha-patra, which is given to the
most honoured brahmana, He gave to Haridasa. The mahaprasada of the shraddha is presented to the honoured guest, and the
“Charitamrita” says that Advaita so revered Haridasa that He let him be the first to taste the remnants of the memorial
offerings in honour of His father (Adi 10.44). Haridasa was afraid, and Advaita answered:

“‘Do not be afraid. I shall act only as the scriptures direct.’” (CC,
Antya 3.219)

“‘When you eat, it is as good as feeding ten million brahmanas.’ Saying this, He fed him the *shraddha-patra*, the food of the day of *shraddha*.” (CC, Antya 3.220)[^15]

From then on Haridasa dined at Advaita's house every day. For Advaita faithfulness to scripture stands above caution
about caste: a Vaishnava is worthy of worship whatever family he is born in.

So the two of them together begged the Lord to come. Advaita worshipped Him with Ganga water and tulasi — in Shantipura
and in Navadvipa-Mayapura — while Haridasa in his hut chanted the holy name and called on the Lord to appear; it is said
that their prayer brought the Lord into the world in the form of Gauranga. The “Charitamrita” says:

“He was always thinking of how to deliver the world: ‘How will this world, devoid of Vaishnavas, be delivered?’” (CC, Antya 3.221)

“Advaita vowed to bring Krishna down to this world, and He began to worship Him with water and tulasi.” (CC, Antya 3.222)

“In his cave Haridasa chanted the name, with this one thought in his mind: ‘Krishna will descend.’” (CC, Antya 3.223)

“By the devotion of these two Chaitanya descended, and by preaching the name and love He delivered the world.” (CC, Antya 3.224)

Later Haridasa moved to Navadvipa, wishing to live among pure devotees like Advaita.

## The Lord's Appearance
Before coming Himself, Krishna sends into the world those whom He will honour as elders:

“First He sent down His elders and kin. I shall name them in brief, for they cannot all be told in full” (CC, Adi 13.53)

“Sri Shachi and Jagannatha, Sri Madhava Puri, Keshava Bharati and Sri Ishvara Puri, Advaita Acharya and Srivasa Pandita, Acharyaratna, Vidyanidhi and Thakura Haridasa” (CC, Adi 13.54)

The Three Prabhus descend together with Their eternal companions — with Srivasa, Haridasa and others.

When Shachi was in the sixth month of carrying the Lord, Advaita came to Jagannatha Mishra, left him, and, reciting
hymns and bowing, walked around Shachi — He was worshipping the Child in her womb. The astonished couple asked what this
meant, but Advaita only answered that in due time they would know; and they did not understand.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells the same: in the sixth month Advaita suddenly came, honoured Shachi's womb with sandal and
incense, walked around her and joyfully went home, leaving Shachi and Jagannatha in amazement (12.875–876).

On the evening of the Lord's birth, in Shantipura, Advaita together with Haridasa suddenly broke into a dance of joy,
and no one knew why. It was the day of a lunar eclipse, and on that occasion many gifts were given away; about what had
happened both kept silent[^16].

Advaita sent Sita in a palanquin to the house of Jagannatha Mishra with a casket of precious cloth and ornaments, and
with her husband's permission she came from Shantipura with gifts; seeing the superhuman form of the Infant, she was
greatly amazed:

“Advaita Acharya’s wife, the noble lady revered by all the world, was named Sita Thakurani. With the Acharya’s leave she set out with gifts to see that jewel among children” (CC, Adi 13.111)

“She placed durva grass and paddy on His head and blessed Him again and again: “May the two brothers live long!” Fearing the witches and evil spirits, she gave Him the name Nimai” (CC, Adi 13.117)[^17]

Sita often came from Shantipura to teach Shachi how to care for the Infant, and at the festival of His birthday she was
the first allowed to offer the Child clothing. All the devotees together with Advaita and Sita prayed for the long life
of the Child. Sita is worthy of worship equally with Shachi: her motherly love, vatsalya, for Gaura is of the same kind.

## Nimai and Vishvarupa
The Lord was growing up in Navadvipa, and even the devotees were not sure that Nimai was Bhagavan Himself. Advaita, for
His part, behaved as though He did not know Who He was — and wished more than anyone that the learned Nimai would
become a devotee[^18]. Nimai's elder brother, Vishvarupa, studied in Advaita's tol: every day, after bathing in the
Ganga, He listened to Advaita's lectures and then Himself explained to those gathered that the essence of all scripture is
devotion to Krishna, and Advaita, rising from His puja, would embrace Him. Whole days Vishvarupa spent in Advaita's
house in talks on the scriptures of bhakti. Little Nimai used to run over to call His brother to lunch, and Advaita
could not take His eyes off His face. Many years later He recalled:

“His elder brother, Vishvarupa, always used to come to Me before and discuss the ‘Gita’ with Me.” (CB, Madhya 2.21)

“As a child Vishvambhara was exceptionally beautiful. He always came to Me to call His brother, Vishvarupa.” (CB,
Madhya 2.22)

“Enchanted by His beauty, I always blessed Him with the words: ‘May You attain bhakti.’” (CB, Madhya 2.23)

When Vishvarupa was twelve years old and His wedding was already being prepared at home, He left and took sannyasa
with the name Shankararanya. Advaita grieved, but comforted everyone: soon Krishna Himself would appear; together with
Sita He consoled Jagannatha and Shachi and helped them bring up Nimai. In Advaita's Navadvipa house the “Bhagavatam” was
still read and kirtan sung, and everyone delighted in the voice of Mukunda Datta.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” shows this assembly through the eyes of Ishana, the servant in Shachi's house. When Vishvarupa was
explaining that devotion to Krishna is the essence of everything, “Advaita-deva gives a thunderous roar. Taking
Vishvarupa in His arms, Advaita dances” (12.2164–2165). After Vishvarupa left, the devotees, worn out by the words of the
godless, wanted to go away to the forest, but Advaita encouraged them “with great, proud certainty”; at the cry of Hari's
name little Vishvambhara ran into the assembly: “For what business did you call Me?” — and ran off to play
(12.2172–2177). And when the godless mocked again, Advaita roared: “In a few days, here within Nadia, you will see
Krishna” (12.2204). Sanyal tells it the same way.

## Ishvara Puri in Advaita's House
Once Ishvara Puri, a disciple of Madhavendra, came to Navadvipa unrecognised by anyone and entered Advaita's house:

“When Ishvara Puri, by the will of providence, arrived at Advaita's house, no one could recognise him in such dress.”
(CB, Adi 11.72)

“He humbly sat down beside Advaita, who was performing puja.” (CB, Adi 11.73)

“The radiance of a Vaishnava is never hidden from another Vaishnava. Advaita looked at the guest and could not take His
eyes off him.” (CB, Adi 11.74)

“Advaita asked: ‘Who are you, dear sir? I think you are a Vaishnava sannyasi.’” (CB, Adi 11.75)

The guest answered that he was lower than a shudra. Then Mukunda sang of Krishna, and Ishvara Puri fell unconscious in
the ecstasy of love; Advaita embraced him and was Himself drenched with his tears, and on Him too the signs of ecstasy
appeared. So Ishvara Puri and Advaita met, both disciples of Madhavendra, though Advaita only played that
role[^19].

## The Dream about the “Gita”
When Nimai returned from Gaya transformed, with the signs of the highest devotion, Advaita, Srivasa and the other
devotees rejoiced, and Advaita began little by little to reveal to them Who He was. He told the Vaishnavas His dream.
Not having understood a verse of the “Gita,” He had fasted, and at night someone said to Him: get up, Acharya, and
eat; the meaning of the verse is — worship Me alone; do not cause everyone suffering. And further — about the vow Advaita
had made with arms raised:

“Why do You grieve? You have attained all You strove for. Your desire is fulfilled. That for which You kept fasts,
performed worship and, weeping, chanted the names of Krishna has come to pass. You made an unbreakable vow to call down
the Supreme Lord, and that Lord has now appeared.” (CB, Madhya 2.12–14)

Throughout the land, the voice said, Krishna-kirtan would resound, and by His mercy people would see a bhakti
inaccessible even to Brahma (CB, Madhya 2.15–16). Opening His eyes, Advaita saw
Vishvambhara — and He vanished. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” speaks of this in another place too:

“Having revealed to Advaita the true meaning of the verse of the ‘Bhagavad-gita,’ Vishvambhara closed that door leading
to the path of bhakti.” (CB, Madhya 10.166)

The secrets of Krishna, Advaita added, He could not fathom — but let everyone wish that Nimai would surrender to
Krishna. The devotees bowed to Him and began to sing the name of Hari.

According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” the devotees themselves came to tell Advaita of Vishvambhara's prema, and
all-knowing Advaita revealed the truth to them by many hints and told them His dream — “now He reveals [the truth], now
He conceals it” (12.2325–2328).

## Gadadhara Sees the Worship
Soon Gauranga came with Gadadhara to Advaita. Advaita cried out loudly, “Hari! Hari!” — and Gauranga fell unconscious.
Then Advaita worshipped His feet with all the articles of puja and recited hymns to Govinda. Gadadhara objected: it is
not fitting for an old man to worship a boy! “In a few days you will know,” Advaita answered. Coming to Himself, the
Lord begged Advaita for mercy, and Advaita begged Him to lead them all in kirtan; and the Lord agreed.

Then Advaita withdrew to Shantipura: if Gauranga is Krishna, He Himself will draw Him to Himself. Advaita's mind is hard
to understand — now He reveals Nimai, now He pretends not to know Him, now He “tests” Him and hides; but all this is so
that the Lord should reveal Himself to the world[^20].

## “Nara!”
Soon the Lord revealed Himself to Srivasa. He kicked the door of his temple: “Whom are you worshipping? Here I am!” —
and showed His four-armed form. “Because of your loud sankirtana, because of the persecution of the devotees and because of
Advaita's audacious call,” He said, “I have left Vaikuntha.”

And on the eve of Nityananda's Vyasa-puja, at the kirtan in the house of Srivasa, the Lord began to call loudly: “Nara!
Nara!” So He called Advaita, whose cry had brought Him down from Vaikuntha — and now Advaita lived without a care in
Shantipura with Haridasa:

“Only because of Nara, whom you all know as Advaita Acharya, have I now descended into this world.” (CB, Madhya 5.51)

“Nara called Me from Vaikuntha, and now lives without a care together with Haridasa.” (CB, Madhya 5.52)

Another time the Lord would say that He lay in the Ocean of Milk and His sleep was broken by the roar of Nada (CB, Madhya
22.16)[^21].

## In Nandana Acharya's House
Soon after the Vyasa-puja the Lord, filled with the mood of the Supreme, sent Ramai Pandita, Srivasa's younger brother,
to Shantipura: “Tell Advaita: He for whose sake He worshipped with Ganga water and tulasi, wept and fasted, has come.
Nityananda too has come. Let Him come with His wife and with all that is needed for puja.” Ramai could not utter a word
for joy, but to Advaita everything was already revealed: “It seems Mahaprabhu has sent for Me,” He said. And when Ramai
delivered the message, Advaita raised His arms, wept and fell unconscious, and coming to Himself exclaimed:

“I have brought Him! I have brought My Lord! Because of Me the Supreme Lord has come from Vaikuntha!” (CB, Madhya
6.38–39)

Sita, little Achyutananda and all the servants wept with happiness too — and soon they set out[^22].

But Advaita wanted the Lord to reveal Himself. To Ramai He said:

“If He shows Me His divine majesty and blesses My head with His lotus feet, I shall acknowledge Him as My eternal
Master. I swear it shall be so.” (CB, Madhya 6.47–48)

He resolved to hide — let the Lord find Him Himself. Coming to Navadvipa, He concealed Himself in the house of Nandana
Acharya, and told Ramai to say that Advaita had not come:

“I shall stay secretly in the house of Nandana Acharya, but you tell Vishvambhara that I have not come.” (CB, Madhya 6.57)

And this “test” too is a lila: in this way Advaita let the Lord reveal His omniscience to all.

The Lord, meanwhile, ascended the vishnu-khatta, the throne of Vishnu, in Srivasa-angana and proclaimed:

“Nara is coming! Nara is coming! He wants to behold My majesty!” (CB, Madhya 6.63)

Nityananda held an umbrella over Him, Gadadhara and others served. As soon as Ramai came in, the Lord said to him:

“Nara is coming here. He knows perfectly well Who I am, and yet He is always testing.” (CB, Madhya 6.68)

“I know perfectly well that Nara is now in the house of Nandana Acharya. He has sent you here to test Me.” (CB, Madhya
6.69)

And the Lord sent Ramai again — to Nandana's; so Advaita learned that there is no hiding from the Lord.

Advaita and His wife prostrated themselves before the Lord from a distance, and Advaita recited hymns. A vision opened to
Him: the Lord in radiance, in the ornaments of which the dhyana-mantras speak; four-, five- and six-faced gods pray to
Him; Ananta shades Him with His hoods, and the whole sky is full of gods and sages. The Lord said that He had come at His
call from His rest in the Ocean of Milk (CB, Madhya 6.73–95). Advaita was struck dumb; then He washed His feet and worshipped Him with the
five offerings, panchopachara, with the mantra “namo brahmanya-devaya…”.

He worshipped the Lord with various gifts — and the Lord, fulfilling His desire, placed His feet on His head, while all
around cried, “Jaya!” At the Lord's bidding Advaita danced, forgetting Himself, and the devotees marvelled at such a
dance at so advanced an age. One of the biographies adds that Advaita had brought Sita with Him and was the first of all
to worship the Lord's feet.

The Lord placed His own garland on Advaita and told Him to ask for a blessing. “For Myself I have nothing to ask,”
Advaita answered. “But let bhakti go to people of low station: those proud of learning, wealth, birth and renunciation
only hinder the preaching” (cf. CB, Madhya 6.158–170). Advaita's desire is not for Himself: devotion is for the low, the unlearned and the fallen,
for pride closes the way to it.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” renders these words of the Lord thus: “For the sake of your vow is My descent”; and before the
puja He ordered, “Worship Me together with your wife”; and asked to choose a boon, Advaita said, “I have attained every
perfection,” and chose only one thing: “Be merciful to the living beings” (12.2481–2496).

Kavi Karnapura in the “Mahakavya” tells of this meeting differently — without a messenger and without Nandana's house.
When Shripati, Srivasa's younger brother, whispered that Advaita had come, the Lord shut Himself in a room and there
revealed His innate radiance; and Advaita, “who had come from his own home, made a firm vow in His mind: ‘He must be seen
[by me] with His innate [form] fully manifest’” (5.28). The Lord Himself unbolted the door —

“Beholding the Lord in His splendour exactly as He had resolved in His mind, the deeply loving Advaita took a bunch of
straw between His teeth and fell on the ground like a rod.” (“Mahakavya” 5.30)

— and the Lord raised Him, embraced Him and bathed Him in tears. Another time, according to the same poem, the Lord sent
devotees to Kamalaksha together with Nityananda; hearing of the Lord's six-armed and four-armed form, Advaita rejoiced,
and the next morning He came and, “seeing Gaura, the moon, became splendid with a roar like that of an intoxicated
lion”; the Lord revealed His radiance to Him in Srivasa's shrine, and they danced until dawn (7.25–37).

There too Karnapura puts into the Lord's mouth words that explain everything: “Surely it is for your sake that I have
come here… It is you indeed who are a devotee; surely it is for your sake that I have come here to the earth” (6.76,
6.79). The love for which the devotees pray “will be given them for your sake” (6.78). And when Srivasa timidly remarked
that this was only the Lord's mercy, He answered in anger:

“‘In this land of Bharata, is there manifest anyone apart from Advaita who is worthy of the homage of all the hosts of
the immortals? Therefore He must be regarded as equal to Me; nowhere in the world is there ever anyone like Him.’”
(“Mahakavya” 6.82)

And is there here, apart from Advaita, any devotee of His like Uddhava or Shuka (6.81)?

## The Mahaprakasha
From then until the Lord's sannyasa Advaita lived together with Haridasa in His Navadvipa house, in Mayapura, every day
attended the kirtan in Srivasa's house, and the two of them almost always engaged the Lord in kirtan. Another time, seated on the vishnu-khatta, the Lord ate all the offerings and the betel and called: “Nara!”;
Nityananda held an umbrella over Him, and Advaita, His palms joined, recited hymns. The Lord told Him to ask for a
blessing, for it was for His sake that He had come, and then embraced each one and wept for Krishna.

In the Mahaprakasha — the great revelation that lasted seven praharas, twenty-one hours — the Lord called each of the
devotees, told him of his past and bade him ask for what he wished. Advaita said that He had nothing to ask. Then the
Lord reminded Him of that dream about the verse of the “Gita” — and Advaita wept, for only He knew of it. “Be merciful
to the unlearned and the fallen,” He asked; and, growing calm, He rejoiced that the Lord would regard Him as His
servant.

The “Mahakavya” has this detail of the great night as well: when the Lord, having said “I am leaving,” fell unconscious,
Advaita “with a hundred roars sprinkled His face with water flung from His hand,” but the Lord came to only through sweet
kirtan (5.104).

## Shantipura and the Bank of the Ganga
Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala” tells of other days as well. The Lord sang kirtan in Advaita's house, and They
embraced each other. Advaita came to Navadvipa, bowed to the Lord and danced, and at Srivasa's question the Lord
expounded the truth about Advaita, advaita-tattva: “The shastras describe Him as Maha-Vishnu; know that this Advaita
is the avatara in the form of a devotee” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 4, v. 98). Nityananda stayed two days as Advaita's guest, and Advaita worshipped
the Lord in Srivasa's house. In Advaita's house the Lord told Him to preach love for Krishna without discriminating who
is worthy of it and who is not; the devotees gathered there, and to all of them, headed by Advaita, the Lord entrusted
the preaching.

Once, on the bank of the Ganga, Gauranga seated Gadadhara beside Himself, and Advaita performed arati to Them both.
Before His sannyasa Mahaprabhu came more than once with Nityananda to Advaita's house in Shantipura and sang kirtan day
and night. Parameshvari Dasa tells in a pada: the Lord sat in Advaita's temple, Nityananda and Advaita sat beside Him and
playfully discussed the festival; Sita came in, smiling, and the Lord told them to call the Vaishnavas and prepare
everything for kirtan.

Karnapura also describes this kind of visit of the Lord: singing and dancing He came to Advaita's house and Himself fell
on the ground before Him, and when they embraced, “the crowd wondered: ‘Is this Advaita? Or is it He, Gaurachandra?’”
(“Mahakavya” 6.54–55). Advaita exclaimed: let those fools look who say there is no bhakti in Kali! — and the Lord, angry
with them, confirmed: “In Kali bhakti reigns always!” (6.57–58). There too the Lord expounded to His people a teaching on
the Atma “very hard to understand” — and at once, in tears, said that the three worlds are protected and purified only by
His devotee who sings and dances in love (6.63–69)[^23].

Once, according to the “Mahakavya,” the Lord Himself honoured Advaita by all the rules: He washed His feet, offered durva
with unbroken rice, flowers and sandal and fed Him, and Advaita, receiving the mahaprasada with awe, danced with the Lord
in kirtan (7.72–75).

## Honour That Wounds
The Lord in His humility conducted Himself as one of the devotees, and honoured Advaita as a guru, as an elder: He fell
at His feet and would not let Him touch His own. Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains this by Advaita's being the
godbrother of Ishvara Puri, the Lord's guru. But such honour gave Advaita pain.

Once, when the Lord lost consciousness in sankirtana, Advaita smeared Himself with the dust of His feet and worshipped
them according to all the rules. Coming to Himself, the Lord in confusion threw Himself into the Ganga; Nityananda and
Haridasa pulled Him out, and afterwards He lived for some time in another place[^24].

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells this in its own way: coming to Himself, the Lord said, “I do not know where an offense has
occurred,” looked at Advaita, understood everything, seized Him and began to rub His feet on His own head
(12.2750–2753).

The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Madhya 17) tells of the same leap differently. Once the Lord felt no bliss in kirtan, and
Advaita, without stopping His dance, said to Him:

“How will You feel ecstatic love, if Nara has dried it up?” (CB, Madhya 17.21)

“Neither I nor Srivasa have attained love for God. You enjoy Your pastimes of divine love with oil-sellers and
gardeners. The Avadhuta has become the servant of Your love, while I and Srivasa are left out. We have no access to
prema, whereas this Avadhuta has come and become its keeper. O Gosani! If You do not reward Me with Your prema, I shall
dry up all prema. And there will be no fault of Mine in it.” (CB, Madhya 17.22–25)

Vrindavana Dasa at once explains that Advaita said this in the frenzy of prema, not remembering Himself — for Krishna
increases the glory of His devotees (Madhya 17.26–27). The Lord decided there was no point in living without prema and
threw Himself into the Ganga; Nityananda and Haridasa pulled Him out, and He secretly took shelter with Nandana
Acharya.

The devotees searched everywhere for the Lord, while Advaita, considering Himself a great offender, went home and
fasted from grief. In the morning the Lord told Nandana to bring Srivasa and at his request came to Advaita. Advaita lay
without strength, almost unconscious[^25]; but as soon as the Lord's voice sounded, He rose and broke His fast. He begged
forgiveness for His audacious words and prayed:

“O Lord, grant Me the mood of loving service to You and keep Me at Your feet as the son of Your maidservant.” (CB,
Madhya 17.83–87)

The Lord answered that Krishna punishes only one who is His servant birth after birth (Madhya 17.97), and Advaita,
understanding that He had been punished as a servant, rejoiced: “From now on I can say that You are My Lord!” (CB,
Madhya 17.100). In Nandana's house the Lord, Nityananda and Advaita all hid at one time or another.

## The “Yoga-vasishtha”
The Lord's honour was a torment to Advaita, and He resolved to provoke His anger. He began to expound the
“Yoga-vasishtha,” placing knowledge, jnana, and liberation above bhakti, and on some pretext went away to Shantipura.
Advaita Himself says in the “Charitamrita”:

“In the past Mahaprabhu used to honour Me. This pained Me, and I thought of a way to change it” (CC, Adi 12.39)

“I expounded the *Yoga-vasishtha*, extolling liberation as the highest goal, and the Lord grew angry and treated Me with dishonour” (CC, Adi 12.40)

According to Srila Bhaktivinoda, He explained the path of knowledge to “a few unfortunate people.” Vrindavana Dasa
conveys how it looked:

“Intoxicated with bliss, He constantly swayed from side to side, commenting on the book ‘Yoga-vasishtha’ and glorifying
jnana.” (CB, Madhya 19.20)[^26]

The Lord understood His design and went to Shantipura with Nityananda; according to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” They
swam along the Ganga to Advaita's house, and the Lord threatened to show how He would punish Nara, who had brought Him
down and was now hiding the glory of bhakti (Madhya 19.118–126). Seeing Them, Advaita began to extol jnana still more
zealously. “Nara! Tell Me, which is higher — jnana or bhakti?” the Lord asked. “Jnana is always higher,” Advaita
answered (CB, Madhya 19.131–132). Then the Lord dragged Him out into the courtyard and began to beat Him with His own
hand (according to one of the biographies, with His fists), reproaching Him:

“If You wished to hide the path of loving service to God, then why did You become the cause of My descent?” (CB, Madhya
19.142)

Advaita's wife, the mother of the universe, understood what was happening, but still tried to stop the Lord (Madhya
19.135):

“He is an old brahmana! Spare His life!” (CB, Madhya 19.136)

And Advaita, having received the punishment, danced with joy: He understood that He had won, and rubbed His head
against the Lord's feet. “My desire is fulfilled,” He said. “Before, the Lord was sparing with Me, honouring Me as an
elder, but now He has dealt with Me as with a servant and saved Me from the heresy of mayavada.” The Lord, seeing
through His ruse, was abashed, but was pleased and merciful to Him:

“The Lord honoured Acharya Gosani as His guru, and this made the Acharya very unhappy” (CC, Adi 17.66)

“So the Acharya, by design, began to expound the path of knowledge, and the Lord in anger treated Him with disrespect” (CC, Adi 17.67)

“Then Acharya Gosani was filled with joy, and the Lord, abashed, showed Him His grace” (CC, Adi 17.68)

The Lord embraced Him: “I will be pleased with anyone, even one who has offended Me, if he takes Your shelter.” Advaita
answered: “Whoever rejects You is nothing to You.” Little Achyutananda too saw his father's punishment.

Then the Lord called Advaita's wife mother and told her to prepare an offering for Krishna, and He Himself went with
Nityananda, Advaita and Haridasa to bathe in the Ganga. Returning, He bowed down to Krishna, and then

“Advaita fell at the feet of Vishvambhara, and Haridasa fell at the feet of Advaita.” (CB, Madhya 19.232)

Rising, the Lord saw Advaita at His feet, quickly sprang up and exclaimed: “Vishnu! Vishnu!” (Madhya 19.234). Then the
Three Prabhus sat down to their meal, and Haridasa by the door.

So Advaita's “path of knowledge” was only a ruse for the sake of punishment, not a conviction. He arranged this lila to
show what a blessing punishment from the Lord, from the guru or from a Vaishnava is. The highest thing for Advaita is to
be a servant; honour from the Lord is a torment to Him, and for the sake of punishment He is ready to “preach” jnana and
rejoice under the blows. And the Lord promises His mercy to anyone who takes Advaita's shelter — even an offender.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” adds vivid touches to this story. Advaita planned it outright: “I will do such a thing that the
Lord will punish me, seizing me by the hair” (12.1956). Sita said to Shri, “I do not understand what play He is revealing
now. The Lord will surely come here” — and began to prepare everything needed (12.1961–1962). To the Lord's question,
“Tell Me: jnana and bhakti — which of them is higher?” Advaita answered, “Jnana is highest of all”; the Lord seized Him by
the hair and threw Him into the courtyard; Sita begged for mercy, Haridasa stood aside in fear, Nityananda laughed softly,
and Advaita, clapping His hands, danced and placed the dust of the Lord's feet on His head; afterwards everyone wept — Sita
and little Achyutananda too (12.1970–1980).

And there too is a bitter lesson. One of Advaita's followers, Shankara, took this play in earnest and became fixed in
jnana. Advaita said to him again and again:

“‘I acted in this way to fulfil My desire. Give it up, give it up, you madman! You are ruined.’ He did not give it up,
and Advaita rejected him.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.1986–1987)

He, the book says, “sowed the seed of great averseness,” and the wise knew it would grow[^27].

## Shachi and Advaita
Shachi blamed Advaita for Vishvarupa's taking sannyasa. Before, she had kept silent, fearing to offend a Vaishnava — she
knew Advaita to be a Vaishnava. But now Nimai too spent all His time in Advaita's house, leaving Lakshmipriya at home,
and Shachi feared that He too would leave. In her sorrow she said to herself:

“Why is he called ‘Advaita’? In truth this Gosani is ‘Dvaita.’ He has already induced one son of mine to leave home, and
now He wants to deprive me of my other son.” (CB, Madhya 22.113–117)

Only her Son knew of this.

Once, when the Lord, having revealed Himself, was seated on the throne and giving blessings to the devotees, Srivasa
asked:

“Lord, we wish that You would grant ecstatic love for Krishna to mother Shachi.” (CB, Madhya 22.24)

The Lord refused: she had offended a Vaishnava, and only that Vaishnava himself can free one from such an offence — so
Ambarisha freed Durvasa from it (Madhya 22.25–34):

“She has offended Nara, and therefore she will attain ecstatic love for Krishna only if Nara forgives her.” (CB, Madhya
22.35)

“Let her place the dust of Advaita's feet on her head, and then I shall grant her divine love for Krishna.” (CB, Madhya
22.36)

Hearing of this, Advaita said that Shachi was His mother, for from her womb His Lord had appeared, and began to glorify
her — until He lost consciousness; then Shachi came, took the dust of His feet and herself fell unconscious with bliss.
The Lord declared to her:

“Now you have attained devotional service to Vishnu, freed from the offence you committed against Advaita.” (CB, Madhya
22.52)[^28]

So the Lord showed how grave vaishnava-aparadha is: it bars the way even to Shachi, and only the forgiveness of the
offended Vaishnava saves one from it. And He showed that Vaishnavas are not proud: Shachi went without delay to beg
forgiveness. Shachi herself is the eternal mother of the Lord, Yashoda; Vrindavana Dasa says plainly that in truth it
was not even an offence, but the Lord called it so (CB, Madhya 22.58)[^29].

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells this story in the same way: Shachi only once thought, “Acharya Gosani has taken away my two
sons!” — and “this was the only offence Ai committed”; Advaita, glorifying the greatness of mother Shachi, fainted in
prema, and she, seizing the moment, placed the dust of His feet on her head (12.2002–2005). But Kavi Karnapura in the
“Mahakavya” describes differently how Shachi received prema: that day Advaita, at the Lord's command, was dancing with
the Vaishnavas, and Shachi received mercy from the foot of the Lord Himself — at Srivasa's request (5.81–90)[^30].

## The Universal Form
Once Advaita, sorrowful, was dancing in Srivasa's courtyard and lamenting that His hope had still not been fulfilled;
only after two praharas, some six hours, could He be calmed. The Lord, seeing how He rolled on the ground and wept, led
Him into the room of Vishnu, and Advaita asked: “Let Me see what Arjuna saw.” And before Him appeared the universal
form, the vishvarupa: a dark-blue four-armed Krishna, in whose body were the luminaries, the seas and the mountains,
millions of faces, arms and eyes; Arjuna was reciting hymns to Him, and Advaita, taking a blade of grass between His
teeth as a sign of humility, begged for service (cf. CB, Madhya 24.33, 24.47–55).

According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” which here refers to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” that day Advaita was dancing in the
mood of a gopi — a mood that He later, in Nilachala, begged of the Lord as a boon; the Lord, knowing His prayer, shut the
door and showed Him the universal form, and at that moment Nityananda came in:

“Nityananda and Advaita — both were beside Themselves. Only he has the power to understand the deeds of these two who has
no notion of difference between Nityananda and Advaita.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.3452–3453)

## The Drama in Chandrashekhara's House
When in the house of Chandrashekhara Acharyaratna the Lord staged a drama about Krishna, Advaita was given the role of
the vidushaka — the jester: forgetting Himself, He “danced in various moods like an experienced jester” (CB, Madhya
18.35–36). The Lord danced in the mood of Rukmini and then appeared as the Adya-shakti, the original energy, Lakshmi;
Gadadhara too played Rukmini, Nityananda the Lord's grandmother, Srivasa Narada, Haridasa a watchman[^31]. The Lord
announced that only those who had controlled their senses could watch the drama, and Advaita and Srivasa sadly said
that this meant they could not. But the Lord smiled and answered: “If you do not come, then who will be present during
the dance?” (Madhya 18.24) — and reassured them:

“Today you will all become the greatest mystic yogis. Beholding My dance, none of you will be bewildered.” (CB, Madhya
18.26)

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says the same briefly: Advaita and the others were afraid to watch the dance, but by the Lord's
will all became masters of yoga, and for seven days a great radiance remained in the Acharya's house (12.2910, 12.2929).
Kavi Karnapura in the “Mahakavya” gives Advaita a different role — that of Krishna Himself: “Then Advaita entered there,
imitating the dress and deeds of Bhagavan, joyfully holding a flute in both hands,” and the Lord gave Him His own dress,
flute, crest of peacock plume and yellow garment (11.19–20)[^32].

## Night Kirtans and the Procession to the Kazi
Advaita was beside the Lord in sankirtana — in Srivasa's courtyard, in the streets of Navadvipa and on the bank of the
Ganga — and among those who sang all night long:

“All of them sang in jubilation: ‘Krishna, Rama, Mukunda, Murari, Vanamali!’” (CB, Madhya 23.29)

“Nityananda and Gadadhara whirled, holding hands. Advaita, like a lion, ran about the room in ecstatic bliss.” (CB,
Madhya 23.30)[^33]

When Chand Kazi forbade the sankirtana, the Lord led Navadvipa out into the streets, and Advaita led one part of the
procession, dancing and loudly singing the names:

“The Lord danced as He moved along the bank of the Ganga, and all who walked beside Him sang the names of Lord Hari.”
(CB, Madhya 23.202)

“Advaita Acharya with His party went in front and danced in indescribable bliss.” (CB, Madhya 23.203)

And the Kazi became a friend and vowed never again to hinder the sankirtana[^34].

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” describes the procession to the Kazi with the same picture: “At the Lord's command the joyful Sri
Advaita Raya goes ahead from here with one sampradaya” (12.2067); “In front dances Advaita, for whose sake [came] the
descent” (12.3117); and in the courtyard of Shridhara, where the Lord drank from his leaky pot, Nityananda, Advaita and
all the others wept (12.3140). Songs of the same book recall how Advaita performed arati to the Lord adorned by Gadadhara
(12.2019).

Murari Gupta, whom the “Bhakti-ratnakara” quotes, adds a lesson of humility: once the Lord, with a hoe on His shoulder and
a broom in His hand, went to clean a temple, and “the great souls headed by the Acharya, with hoes and brooms in hand,
became Krishna's sweepers” and together with Him cleaned the wall (“Kadacha” 2.13.3–4; “Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.2092–2096).

And the songs quoted in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” paint festivals of Navadvipa about which the biographies are silent. At
the Nanda-utsava in Srivasa's house “Srivasa and Advaita, in cowherd attire, in rapture, scatter yogurt and turmeric over
the courtyard” (12.3158), Advaita pours a pot of yogurt over Nitai's head and smears His face with butter; at the feast
by the Ganga He seats everyone in a circle and gives Nitai a drink of chhena, and Nitai puts sweets into His mouth; at
the swing festival of jhulana Advaita gives thunderous roars, and in spring He plays with Nitai, Gadadhara and Srivasa
with the coloured powder of phagu (12.3170–3327)[^35].

## The Lord's Sannyasa
At the beginning of 1510 Mahaprabhu accepted sannyasa in Katwa. Intoxicated with love for Krishna, He wandered for three
days, thinking He was going to Vrindavana, while Nityananda sent Chandrashekhara Acharya ahead:

“Nityananda Gosani said to Acharyaratna, ‘Go quickly to Advaita Acharya.’” (CC, Madhya 3.20)

“I shall bring the Lord to His house. Let Him wait ready on the bank with a boat.”
(CC, Madhya 3.21)

From there Chandrashekhara was to go on to Navadvipa for Mother Shachi and all the devotees (Madhya 3.22)[^36]. The news
of the Lord's sannyasa broke hearts, and, according to one of the biographies, Advaita wept until He fell down as though
lifeless; then a voice from the sky told Him not to grieve: in a few days the Lord would be with them. Elsewhere the
same biography says that, on receiving the news, Advaita went with Haridasa from Navadvipa to Shantipura.

## “Wherever You Are, There Is Vrindavana”
Nityananda taught the cowherd boys to point out the road to the Lord so that it led Him not to the Yamuna but to the
Ganga, opposite Shantipura. Taking the Ganga for the Yamuna, the Lord offered her a prayer and entered the water — and
just then a boat came to the bank:

“Just then Acharya Gosani arrived by boat, bringing a new *kaupina* and outer cloth.” (CC, Madhya 3.30)

“‘You are Acharya Gosani, aren't You? Why have You come here? How did You know that I was in Vrindavana?’” (CC, Madhya 3.32)

“The Acharya said, ‘Wherever You are, that is Vrindavana. It is My good fortune that You have come to the bank of the Ganga.’” (CC, Madhya 3.33)

The Lord exclaimed that Nityananda had deceived Him by calling the Ganga the Yamuna; but Advaita objected that there was
no deception: here the Ganga and the Yamuna flow in one bed, and along the western bank flows the Yamuna, so the Lord had
truly bathed in the Yamuna (Madhya 3.35–37). He dressed the Lord in dry cloth, brought Him by boat to His house and washed
His feet[^37].

Advaita fell prostrate before the Lord and embraced His feet, and the Lord raised Him up. To Advaita and Sita, says one
of the biographies, it was as though life had been given back.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” recalls Advaita's lament on the morning after the Lord's departure in the words of the
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya”: “O Vishvambhara-deva, O treasure-house of virtues, O ocean of the waters of prema… for what fault
of ours do You abandon [us]?” (12.3550–3551). And Karnapura in the “Mahakavya” relates that the Lord, turning towards
Shantipura, sent Nityananda to Navadvipa: “Go quickly to Advaita's house; I too shall soon come there” (11.62–63);
arriving, He embraced Advaita, bathed in tears, sang the names with the devotees and ate the food offered by Advaita
(11.68–70).

Advaita's little son Achyutananda came running too. Vrindavana Dasa speaks of him thus:

“Advaita had a little son named Achyutananda, whose naked body seemed to radiate effulgence.” (CB, Antya 1.213)

“Understanding that the Lord had come, this child, covered with dust from head to foot, came laughing to see Him.”
(CB, Antya 1.215)

“Coming, he fell at the lotus feet of Gaurachandra, and the Lord took this dust-covered child onto His lap.” (CB, Antya
1.216)

“The Lord said: ‘O Achyuta, Advaita Acharya is My father. Therefore we are brothers.’” (CB, Antya 1.217)

“Achyuta replied: ‘By Your own sweet will You have become the friend of all living beings, whereas the Vedas extol You as
the father of all that exists.’” (CB, Antya 1.218)[^38]

## Ten Days in Shantipura
The Lord had eaten nothing for three days, and in Advaita's house a great feast was prepared for Him. Sita cooked, and
Advaita offered everything to Vishnu; the rice lay in mountains on whole banana leaves. The Lord, says one of the
biographies, after His fast accepted prasada from Sita's hands. At the meal Advaita joked with Him and with Nityananda,
and Nityananda and Advaita began a playful quarrel: Advaita called Nityananda a “fallen avadhuta,” and Nityananda, as if
in anger, threw a handful of rice — and Advaita danced with joy that the remnants of Nityananda's meal had touched Him.
Such “disputes” of Advaita with Nityananda are lila: the one who seems to censure Nityananda in truth glorifies Him. When Advaita wished to massage the Lord's feet, the Lord stopped Him and sent Him to eat together with Mukunda and
Haridasa (Madhya 3.105–107). On the following days the Lord ate Shachi's cooking as well.

The next morning Chandrashekhara brought Shachi from Navadvipa:

“In the morning Acharyaratna came with the devotees, bringing Shachimata in a palanquin.” (CC, Madhya 3.137)

The people of Navadvipa came as well; in Shantipura the Lord was visited by Gangadasa, Shridhara, Damodara,
Nandana Acharya and others. For four days Sita did not leave Shachi, who lay as though at the point of death.

For ten days sankirtana never ceased in Advaita's house:

“Everyone danced in bliss, calling ‘Hari! Hari!’, and the Acharya's house became the city of Sri Vaikuntha.” (CC, Madhya 3.156)

According to one of the biographies, at the evening kirtan Advaita danced holding on to Nityananda, with Haridasa
behind Them. For many days Advaita fed and lodged the guests from Navadvipa and the surrounding villages:

“The Acharya's storehouse was inexhaustible and never grew less: however much He spent, as much again was there.” (CC, Madhya 3.159)

Then, at His mother's request, the Lord resolved to live in Puri, and in that same year 1510 He went there, having
encouraged the devotees, whom Advaita now led. For Advaita and all Navadvipa a time of separation began.

## Every Year to Nilachala
From Puri the Lord set out to travel through South India. When Kala Krishnadasa brought to Bengal the news that He had
returned, Advaita in jubilation cried out at the top of His voice, sang and danced much (CC, Madhya 10.78–80); one of the
biographies says that this news as it were brought Shachi, Srivasa, Advaita and Haridasa back to life. The devotees
gathered in Advaita's house and then, having received Shachi's permission in Navadvipa, set out for Puri (Madhya
10.85–88). Devotees from Shrikhanda
came to Advaita's house as well, to travel together:

“Mukunda, Narahari and Raghunandana came from Khanda to the Acharya, to go with Him to Nilachala.”
(CC, Madhya 10.90)

Kaviraja Gosvami speaks of their first coming thus:

“In the first year after His sannyasa the devotees headed by Advaita went to Niladri to see the Lord.” (CC, Madhya 1.46)

“Having seen the Ratha-yatra, they stayed there for four months, dancing and singing with the Lord in supreme joy.” (CC, Madhya 1.47)

“When they took leave, the Lord told them all, ‘Come every year, all of you, to see the Gundicha festival.’” (CC, Madhya 1.48)

According to one of the biographies, Advaita set out with Shachi's permission even before the Ratha-yatra, and the Lord
sent mahaprasada ahead to meet the travellers at Cuttack[^39].

Of the twenty-four years after His sannyasa the Lord spent six in travel and eighteen in Puri (Madhya 1.249), and all
that time the Bengalis came to Him every year:

“Every year the devotees of Gauda came there and stayed four months in the Lord's company.” (CC, Madhya 1.250)

“Advaita, Nityananda, Mukunda, Srivasa, Vidyanidhi, Vasudeva, Murari and all His other servants came together every year and stayed four months.” (CC, Madhya 1.255)

And it was always Advaita who led them:

“The devotees set out for Nilachala under the leadership of Advaita Acharya.” (CB, Antya 8.6)[^40]

Sita and Achyutananda went with Him.

According to the “Mahakavya,” the Lord Himself invited them, telling “the Acharya and the Pandita” — Advaita and Srivasa —
of Jagannatha's procession to the garden of Gundicha, which “especially awakens memories of Vrindavana”: “those like you
should come here every year” (14.2–6). The poet describes one such journey: in the month of Madhava Advaita and Srivasa
gathered people, stayed two days with Shachi, and Advaita said, “Whatever day of departure is auspicious for you is
auspicious for me too” (14.11–19). Beyond Jajpur something happened that no one else mentions: Prataparudra, having seen
a dream, sent a palanquin for Advaita, and Advaita, though He is Ishvara, grew anxious — “What will the Master say?” —
and took Vasudeva Datta with Him as a companion, while the rest went another way with Srivasa (14.58–62)[^41]. On the way
Sarvabhauma met them and greeted Advaita, calling Him Mahesha (14.45).

## The Meeting at Narendra-sarovara
One of the biographies tells that in Puri the Lord declared before all that He had descended into the world at
Advaita's call — and the people of Puri were filled with devotion to Advaita. Learning that the Bengalis headed by
Advaita had already reached Athara-nala, the bridge at the entrance to Puri, the Lord Himself went out to meet them;
with Him were Kashishvara, Bhagavan Acharya, Pradyumna Mishra and others. At Narendra-sarovara both sides fell prostrate
before one another. “It is I who brought Him!” Advaita cried, and all bowed to Him. Nityananda and Advaita embraced, and
the Lord, first of all, placed Jagannatha's garland on Advaita.

Kavi Karnapura tells of the meeting differently. Already at Lake Narendra Govinda
brought Advaita a garland from the Lord and His instruction — to keep a fast and meet in Mishra's house — and Advaita
“surmised, ‘This is a stratagem,’ yet He gave His consent just so, since He Himself is under [the Lord's] control”
(“Mahakavya” 14.78). And after the darshan of Jagannatha the Lord came out:

“…Mahaprabhu, reciting Brahma's hymn of praise that begins ‘I praise You, O worthy of praise, whose body is like a
cloud,’ Himself bowed down to Lord Advaita. And Advaita, overcome with happiness, the hairs of His body standing on end,
bowed down to Him. And the two could not [stop] even for a moment in their praises and obeisances to each other.”
(“Mahakavya” 14.92–93)[^42]

## The Ratha-yatra and Gundicha
At the Ratha-yatra the Lord Himself divided the singers into groups and appointed a dancer for each — Nityananda,
Advaita, Haridasa and Vakreshvara. In the first group the lead singer was Svarupa Damodara and the dancer Advaita. There
was also the Shantipura group, which Advaita Himself had gathered; His son danced in it, and Gopala Mishra was in it too:

“The Acharya of Shantipura had His own group as well: Achyutananda danced there, and all the others sang.” (CC, Madhya 13.45)

Before the kirtan the Lord with His own hand adorned His senior companions with sandal paste and garlands:

“Advaita Acharya and Nityananda Prabhu also rejoiced at the touch of His holy hand.” (CC, Madhya 13.31)

In other years too, going around the temple of Jagannatha in the bera-kirtan, the Lord formed seven groups:

“In the seven parties seven persons danced: Advaita Acharya and Prabhu Nityananda…”
(CC, Antya 10.59)

“Vakreshvara, Achyutananda, Srivasa Pandita, Satyaraja Khan and Narahari Dasa.” (CC, Antya 10.60)

In the days when Jagannatha stayed at Gundicha, the Lord sang with the devotees in the temple courtyard from morning
till evening:

“Sometimes He made Advaita dance, sometimes Nityananda; sometimes Haridasa, sometimes Achyutananda.” (CC, Madhya 14.71)

And when the Gundicha temple was washed before the festival, everyone carried water except a few of the elders:

“Except for Nityananda, Advaita, Svarupa, Bharati and Puri, everyone filled pots and brought water.” (CC, Madhya
12.109)[^43]

Advaita also played with the Lord and the devotees in the water pastimes at Narendra-sarovara.

Among the Lord's companions in Puri Svarupa Damodara was especially dear to Advaita:

“He was most dear to Advaita and Nityananda, and to Srivasa and the other devotees he was as dear as life itself.” (CC, Madhya 10.117)

Together with the Lord, Advaita also went to the garden of Tota-Gopinatha to hear Gadadhara Pandita read the
“Bhagavatam.”

Kavi Karnapura has preserved the details of these days as well. Before the procession the Lord first anointed with
sandal the chest of Advaita — “Him whose nature is non-duality” (“Mahakavya” 15.102); when He fell in ecstasy before the
chariot, Advaita put His hand beneath His chest (16.27); in the garden the Lord and Advaita laughingly poured water over
each other (17.46–49); in the waters of Narendra Advaita lay on the water, “and the Treasure-house of Mercy… lay down upon
Him and fell into a doze” (18.14). And every morning, when the Lord returned from Jagannatha, Advaita came first with
gifts, washed His feet and anointed His forehead with sandal, and the Lord “[in His turn] especially honoured the master
Advaita with charming flowers, and then with an abundance of pure durva grass and unbroken rice” (18.30–32). The place
where Advaita sat at Tota, hearing the “Bhagavatam” from Gadadhara, was shown to Narottama half a century later
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.279).

## A Meal in the Storm
Once Advaita began to entreat the Lord to come to Him for a meal. He wanted to feed the Lord alone: when the Lord came with
the sannyasis, out of respect for them He ate very little. Advaita cooked Himself, while His wife helped Him — cutting
vegetables and handing Him the provisions brought from Bengal (CB, Antya 9.20–24)[^44]. When the cooking was done,
Advaita placed a tulasi-manjari on every dish and sat down to meditate: let the Lord come alone.

Suddenly a storm arose — wind, downpour and hail — and the sannyasis, losing their way, did not reach the place, while at
Advaita's it only drizzled a little. The Lord came alone and ate everything Advaita served Him. When He had eaten,
Advaita began to glorify Indra:

“‘O Indra! Today I have understood your greatness. Today I have understood that you are a Vaishnava.’” (CB, Antya 9.61)

But the Lord saw through Him: He asked why the Acharya was hiding the truth — was not this wind and rain the work of His
hands? (CB, Antya 9.65) — and explained:

“You thought that if I came together with the sannyasis, I would not eat much.” (CB, Antya 9.69)

“In truth Indra carried out Your order, so that Your real greatness would not be revealed to all. The chance to serve
You is the greatest fortune for him.” (CB, Antya 9.72)

Indra, the servant of Krishna, fulfils the desires of His devotees; if Krishna fulfils Advaita's desires, what wonder is
it that Indra obeys Him? (cf. CB, Antya 9.73–74). One of the biographies places this meal in the third year, when the Bengalis came with their
wives and brought foodstuffs with them.

## A Kirtan in Glory of Gaura
The Lord would not allow Himself to be glorified: He called Himself only a servant of Krishna, and the devotees feared
His anger. But one day Advaita told them:

“Today we will glorify no avatara other than Lord Chaitanya, Who is the source of all other avataras.” (CB, Antya 9.159)

“I will dance, and all of you sing the glory of Lord Chaitanya. For if I sing as thunderously as a lion, I will frighten
you all.” (CB, Antya 9.162)

No one could disobey Advaita, and the devotees
began to sing, while Advaita danced and sang a song He Himself had composed in glory of the Lord:

“‘O Sri Chaitanya! You are Narayana Himself! You are an ocean of mercy and the friend of the distressed. Please be
merciful to Me.’” (CB, Antya 9.168)

Hearing Himself glorified, the Lord was embarrassed and went home. When the devotees came to Him singing, He lay turned
away and reproached them; and then He was answered that the sun cannot be hidden with one's palm — just so the truth
about Him cannot be concealed (CB, Antya 9.204–208)[^45].

Thus Advaita was the first openly to introduce kirtan in glory of Sri Krishna Chaitanya — honouring Him equally with
Krishna, for He is Krishna. In this one sees Advaita's chief trait — spiritual fearlessness: it sounded in His loud call,
in the shraddha-patra given to Haridasa, and in this kirtan.

## Again in Shantipura: the Journey to Gauda
Five years after His sannyasa the Lord resolved to go to Vrindavana by way of Bengal. According to the
“Charitamrita,” He visited Srivasa in Kumarahatta, then Kulia, where He stayed a week in the house of Madhava Dasa and
granted deliverance to crowds of people — even to sinners and offenders (the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” 9.33, also speaks
of those seven days in Kulia), and from there He came to Shantipura, to Advaita's house, and consoled His mother there.
From Shantipura He went to Ramakeli, where He met Rupa and Sanatana, reached Kanai-natashala and returned again to
Shantipura, staying there ten days this time (CC, Madhya 16.205–212)[^46]. It is said that in those days Vamshivadana was
born, and the Lord and Advaita were present at his birth.

## Achyuta and the Question about Keshava Bharati
Even before the Lord came back from Ramakeli, a certain sannyasi came to Advaita's house and asked what relation Keshava
Bharati bore to Sri Chaitanya. From the spiritual point of view the Lord has not and cannot have a guru; but Advaita
reasoned that one may begin from the worldly side — so the Lord, Who has neither father nor mother, is glorified as the
son of Devaki (one of the biographies adds: and Sandipani is called His guru) — and answered: “Keshava Bharati is
Chaitanya's guru” (CB, Antya 4.146–151). Just then five-year-old Achyuta ran in, naked and
all dusty from play. The “Charitamrita” tells:

“When he heard his father say that Keshava Bharati was the guru of Chaitanya Gosani, he was deeply pained” (CC, Adi
12.14)

“‘How can You teach such a thing about the guru of the world?’ he said. ‘This teaching of Yours will bring ruin on the whole land.’” (CC, Adi 12.15)

“Chaitanya Gosani is the guru of the fourteen worlds; no scripture anywhere says that anyone else is His guru.”” (CC, Adi 12.16)

“Hearing a boy of five speak the very essence of the scriptures’ conclusions, the Acharya was boundlessly pleased” (CC, Adi
12.17)

According to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” Advaita in bliss embraced His son, bathed him in tears of love and said:

“‘You are My father, and I am your son. You have appeared as My son in order to instruct Me.’” (CB, Antya 4.174)

He begged His son's forgiveness, and the boy, hearing himself praised, lowered his head in embarrassment. The sannyasi
fell prostrate and exclaimed: “As the father, so the son!” (CB, Antya 4.177–178)[^47]. Advaita danced together with His
son: in His house an associate of the Lord had been born (4.184–187). Achyuta is an example of what a true son of Advaita is
like: he cannot bear even worldly speech that belittles the Lord.

At that moment the Lord Himself arrived. Advaita fell prostrate before Him, crying “Hari! Hari!”; the Lord embraced Him
and bathed Him in tears, and Advaita lost consciousness, and on coming to Himself seated the Lord. Advaita and Nityananda
embraced. The Lord took Achyuta on His lap and washed the dust off him with His tears; He blessed the boy.

The “Mahakavya” says that the Lord came to Advaita's village by boat on the Ganga, singing the names; they embraced at the
door, Shachi came, and for six days the Lord ate His mother's food, while “every day that Acharya likewise joyfully
regaled abundantly” the hundreds of thousands of people who gathered (20.20–25).

## The Festival at Advaita's; Raghunatha
Advaita sent for Shachi. She came in a palanquin, and the Lord fell prostrate at her feet; she herself cooked for her Son.
The festival in Advaita's house lasted several days, and no one knew where the provisions came from.

At that time Raghunatha Dasa came to Shantipura. His father Govardhana Majumdar respectfully served Advaita with gifts,
and therefore Advaita presented the young man to the Lord:

“His father always served the Acharya, and so the Acharya was gracious to him.”
(CC, Madhya 16.225)

“By the Acharya's grace he received the Lord's plate of remnants, and for five or seven days he saw the Lord's feet.” (CC, Madhya 16.226)[^48]

Raghunatha had seen the Lord already on His first coming to Shantipura, after the sannyasa (Madhya 16.223), and later, in
Puri, he also received Advaita's blessings. Now the Lord told him to go home and wait until He came back from Vrindavana.

Taking leave, the Lord told the devotees to come every year to the Ratha-yatra, and consoled Advaita: He hoped to see Him
in Nilachala within that very year.

## Nityananda's Wedding
Later, when Nityananda, then living in Bengal, agreed to marry, Advaita rejoiced: “Let it happen soon!” He was
among the devotees at the wedding in Boroganchhi, and afterwards Nityananda with His wives, Vasudha and Jahnava, at
Shachi's word stayed with Advaita in Shantipura, and Sita embraced them both.

According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” Srivasa informed everyone of the match, and Advaita first of all (12.3930). The same
book, following the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” recalls how Nityananda, preaching in Gauda, came from Saptagrama to Advaita's
house: “seeing each other, both overwhelmed with prema, could not stay steady”; after three or four days He went with
Advaita's leave to Navadvipa, and Advaita came there to sing with Him in Srivasa's courtyard (12.3819–3870; 8.187).

## Puri: the Following Years
On one of their visits — according to the “Charitamrita,” in the third year — the Bengalis took their wives to Puri
as well:

“They all gathered and went to Advaita Acharya, and the Acharya joyfully set out to see the Lord.” (CC,
Madhya 16.13)

“That year all the ladies went to see the Lord. Achyuta's mother went with the Acharya.” (CC, Madhya 16.21)

All gathered at Advaita's house; He first took leave of Shachi and received her permission, and the travellers moved on
to Nilachala, carrying the foods the Lord loved:

“Throughout the whole journey the devotees performed sankirtana, purifying all the places through which they passed.”
(CB, Antya 8.41)

“Taking their wives, children and servants with them, the devotees went in great ecstasy to see Lord Chaitanya.” (CB,
Antya 8.43)

Once the Lord, sending Shrikanta Sena, Shivananda's nephew, back from Puri, told him to tell the Bengalis not to come
that year:

“‘This year I shall go there Myself, and there I shall meet everyone — Advaita and all the others.’” (CC, Antya 2.41)

“Advaita Acharya, who had been setting out, stayed where He was.” (CC, Antya 2.45)

The month of Pausha passed, but the Lord did not come (Antya 2.46–47).

One of the wonders of Puri concerned Advaita's son Gopala. When, on one of the Bengalis' visits, the Gundicha temple was
being washed, the Lord told him to dance, and the boy fell unconscious in prema and stopped breathing. Advaita laid him on
His lap; together with the devotees He loudly chanted the mantra to Nrisimha and sprinkled His son with water, but the
boy did not come to himself, and all wept. Then:

“Mahaprabhu placed His hand on the boy's chest and called out loudly, ‘Rise up, Gopala!’” (CC, Madhya 12.148)

“As soon as he heard this, Gopala regained consciousness, and all the devotees danced, calling out ‘Hari!’” (CC, Madhya
12.149)

The “Charitamrita” tells of this in the Adi-lila as well (12.19–26).

## Kamalakanta Vishvasa
Kamalakanta Vishvasa, Advaita's servant and disciple, without His knowledge wrote to King Prataparudra that Advaita was
the Supreme Lord Himself, but by the will of fate had run into a debt of three hundred rupees, and asked the king to pay
this debt. The letter reached the Lord. He said that to call Advaita the Supreme Lord was no mistake, but to present the
Supreme Lord as a poor beggar was a disgrace, and He told Govinda no longer to let Kamalakanta in to see Him (CC, Adi
12.29–36). Kamalakanta grieved, but Advaita rejoiced and told him that he did not understand his immense good
fortune: for it was the Supreme Lord Himself — Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — who had punished him (Adi 12.38). And Advaita
went to the Lord to “complain” that He had shown Kamalakanta more mercy than He had shown Him (Adi 12.43–45). The Lord
laughed, called Kamalakanta and instructed him:

“A Vaishnava Acharya must never accept a king’s wealth as a gift. If he eats the food of worldly men, his mind is defiled” (CC, Adi 12.50)

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains: although Advaita is God Himself, in this world He plays the role of a man in order
to teach people. It is shameful for an acharya to beg from a king, and a teacher of the holy name takes no payment for
it. And Kaviraja Gosvami says that these confidential exchanges were understood only by Advaita and the Lord: only the
Lord grasped the essence of Advaita's intention, and only Advaita appreciated the full depth of the Lord's instruction
(Adi 12.48, 12.54).

## Mercy to Rupa; the Debt of Love
When Rupa Gosvami came to Puri, the Lord Himself brought him to Advaita and Nityananda and asked Them to bless him:

“The Lord said, ‘Advaita and Nityananda, both of You, give Rupa Your mercy with all Your heart.’”
(CC, Antya 1.56)

And Advaita with Nityananda and all the devotees bestowed mercy on Rupa and embraced him (Antya 1.207)[^49].

In the year when Nityananda too came with the Bengalis — against the Lord's order to remain in Bengal — the Lord, at the
end of the four months of the rains, taking leave of the devotees, said of Advaita, “Acharya Gosai”:

“Acharya Gosani has come here, showing Me mercy. I am bound by a debt of love that I can never repay.” (CC, Antya 12.70)

And then He addressed everyone: for My sake you have left your homes, wives and children and walked a hard road; I am a
penniless sannyasi, and I have nothing to give you but My body — this I give to you (Antya 12.71–74)[^50].

## The Riddle
Every year the Lord sent Jagadananda Pandita to Nadia to console Shachi. Once, having stayed there a month,
Jagadananda came to take leave of Advaita, and Advaita sent with him a message to the Lord — a riddle, a tarja,
understandable to Him alone:

“Tell the madman (*baul*) that the people have gone mad. Tell the madman that rice no longer shells in the market.” (CC,
Antya 19.20)

“‘Tell the madman that the *aul* — the one mad with love — is no longer engaged in worldly affairs. Tell the madman that a madman has said this.’” (CC, Antya 19.21)[^51]

Hearing the riddle, the Lord smiled slightly, said, “Such is His order,” and fell silent. To the question of Svarupa Damodara, who
himself knew its meaning, He answered only that the Acharya is a great knower of worship according to the agamas: for the puja He invokes the
Deity, and when the worship is finished He sends Him away — and the meaning of the riddle, said the Lord, even He could
not understand (Antya 19.23–28). Svarupa Damodara grew sad: from that day the Lord's separation from Krishna became twice as
strong, and His divine madness grew (19.29–31). One of the biographies sees in this a sign that the Lord's pastimes in
this world were soon to be concealed.

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura reveals the meaning of the riddle: all are already intoxicated with prema, and the purpose for
which Advaita called the Lord has been achieved; now the Lord is free to act as He pleases[^52].

In the “Bhakti-ratnakara” Narahari Sarakara, telling Srinivasa of the riddle, says: “The Lord is about to conceal these
pastimes… He is the independent Lord; who can fathom His design?” (3.39–44); and elsewhere the same book says plainly:
“By Advaita's wish the Lord withdrew His pastimes; the whole world sank into an ocean of grief” (6.334) — in the sense
explained above.

## Sons
One of the biographies counts six sons of Advaita. The eldest is Achyutananda, after him Krishna Mishra and Gopala
Mishra: these three grasped the essence of their father's teaching and were devoted to Gaura; according to the
“Advaita-charita,” they were born of Sita, and they are called the “three jewels” of Gaura. The fourth son is Balarama,
the fifth Svarupa, the sixth Jagadisha; they did not accept Sri Chaitanya[^53]. The “Charitamrita,” listing the branches
of Advaita's tree, speaks of this thus:

“It is like a heap of paddy: one first measures it along with its chaff, and afterwards winnows the chaff away to clean the grain” (CC, Adi 12.12)

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya”: in the course of time some of Advaita's followers
adopted other teachings, but the essence is the teaching of Advaita Himself, and whoever transgresses it will attain
nothing:

“The Acharya’s teaching is the essence; whoever goes against His order is empty, mere chaff” (CC, Adi 12.10)

“Those followers of the Acharya who accepted the view of Sri Achyutananda are *maha-bhagavatas*, great devotees”
(CC, Adi 12.73)

“It is they who received the Acharya’s mercy, and without effort they attained the feet of Chaitanya” (CC,
Adi 12.74)

Vrindavana Dasa says the same still more sternly:

“If a person worships Advaita but neglects Gaurachandra, he dooms himself to ruin, even if he is Advaita's son.” (CB,
Antya 4.183)

Achyutananda, according to the “Charitamrita,” is a great branch of Advaita's tree: from his very birth he served the
feet of Sri Chaitanya (Adi 12.13). The “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (87–88) calls him the darling of Krishna-Chaitanya and the beloved disciple of
Gadadhara Pandita and sees in him Karttikeya and the gopi Achyuta — both are true, for the two are united in one (others
consider Krishna Mishra to be Karttikeya). One of the biographies gives
the year of his birth as 1428 Shaka (1506), and the place as Shantipura[^54]. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya”
calls Achyutananda the first of Advaita's disciples, while the “Shakha-nirnayamrita” calls him a disciple of Gadadhara. In
the Lord's last years, according to the “Charitamrita,” he remained living in Jagannatha Puri, having taken shelter at
the feet of Sri Chaitanya (Adi 10.150); according to one of the biographies, Advaita's other sons also lived there at
that time.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” sees Krishna Mishra and Gopala at the memorial festival in Katwa: “And what of the two sons of
Prabhu Advaitachandra — Krishna Mishra and Gopala, full of supreme bliss! Beautiful in every limb, expert in all the
shastras; Sri Krishna Chaitanya is the treasure of the life of both. They distribute prema-bhakti to the fallen and the
wretched” (9.410–412).

Yet another biography mentions “Sri Nrisimha, the son of Advaita Acharya,” who lived in Ramakeli[^55].

According to one of the biographies, Achyutananda and his brothers Krishna Mishra and Gopala lived in celibacy, serving
the Lord's mission, while Advaita's line continued from His other sons, who did not follow the Lord. The same biography
says that Balarama and his descendants rose against the teaching and that Srila Vishvanatha Chakravarti contended with
them.

## Disciples
Advaita's mercy extended also to those who were bound to Him as disciples. Yadunandana Acharya of Chandpur, the guru of
Raghunatha Dasa, was a close disciple of Advaita; Srinatha Chakravarti, the guru of Kavi Karnapura, a sub-branch of His
tree; Padmanabha Chakravarti, the father of Lokanatha Gosvami, a dear follower of His. Kamalakanta Vishvasa, of whom we
have told above, was also Advaita's disciple.

Karnapura relates that on the eve of His sannyasa the Lord came one evening to Murari Gupta, told him of Advaita's life
and bade him take shelter of Him (“Mahakavya” 11.47).

Another biography also names as Advaita's disciples Balarama Acharya of Chandpur — the priest of Hiranya and
Govardhana, in whose house Haridasa lived (CC, Antya 3.164–167) — and Padmanabha together with his wife. The late
“Prema-vilasa” tells that the “conqueror of the quarters” Bada Shyamadasa, defeated by Advaita in debate at Shantipura,
received from Him the Krishna mantra and became known as Bhagavata Acharya (24.186–191); it was he who arranged
Advaita's marriage (24.331–334). (According to the “Charitamrita,” Bhagavata Acharya is a branch of Gadadhara Pandita,
Adi 12.80.)

## Concealment
When and where Advaita concluded His pastimes in this world, most of the biographies do not say. According to the
“Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” it was in 1559, in the hundred and twenty-fifth year of His life (see above, “Appearance in
Shrihatta”); the biographies do not name the lunar day of His departure, and in our calendar there is no day of Sri
Advaita's departure[^56].

It is known that Advaita remained in the world even after the Lord's concealment, and concealed Himself before Sita.
Srinivasa, returning from Puri, where he no longer found Gadadhara Pandita, learned on the high road from people of Gauda
that Advaita and Nityananda too had concealed Themselves, and in grief resolved to give up his body; but both Prabhus
appeared to him in a dream and consoled him (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 3.325–358). Entering Shantipura, he again saw Advaita:
He appeared to him and consoled him. And Sita, barely alive in separation from her husband, placed her feet on his head,
blessed him and entrusted him to Madana-Gopala — Advaita's Deity (4.63–79)[^57]. Advaita came to Srinivasa in
dreams later too: He told him to marry — even before Narahari told him the same.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells of this dream thus: “In the guise of a dream, Prabhu Sri Advaita, full of prema, appeared in
person,” pressed Srinivasa to His breast and said: “Through you many souls will be delivered; you will preach the
Prabhu's teaching everywhere. The wise will tell you to marry; you shall marry — do not grieve over it in your heart”
(7.541–547).

He appeared to Narottama as well. “Prabhu Sri Advaita, the lord of Shantipura, sports in Shantipura, unseen by the
world,” and the day before He informed Achyutananda that Narottama would come; as Narottama walked to the Acharya's house,
“Lord Sitanatha, showing great mercy, unseen by others, revealed Himself [to him] together with His associates,” and
Narottama fainted; Achyutananda, wasted by separation, recognized him without a word, and Advaita's associates kept him in
Shantipura for three or four days (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.115–132)[^58].

In Shantipura the service was continued by Advaita's sons. Achyutananda, Gopala and Krishna Mishra received Jahnava
there; in Advaita's house Narottama spent two days with Achyutananda and bowed to him, and Krishna Mishra received
Virachandra with honours and sankirtana. At the great festival in Kheturi were Achyutananda, Krishna Mishra and Gopala
from Shantipura; and when kirtan was sung there, the Lord Himself appeared with His associates — both manifest and already concealed — and the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.573–604) exclaims: how Advaita and Nityananda shone in ecstasy!

The same picture — Gaura among His associates, manifest and unmanifest, with Advaita and Srivasa in front — Jahnava saw in
a dream in deserted Navadvipa (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 11.751–768). And Ishana, concluding his account of Navadvipa for
Srinivasa, says: “Sri Advaita together with [His] associates is ceaselessly absorbed in singing of Gaura's deeds. Now in
Shantipura, now in Nadia He dwells” (12.4022–4023) — and recalls a song of Narahari:

“…Becoming the husband of Sri Sita, and performing many austerities every day, He brought down Krishna and Haladhara.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.4025)

## Places of Remembrance and Books
In Ramakeli, in the temple of Madana-mohana, the Deity of Advaita is worshipped together with Nitai-Gauranga. In
Mayapura, on the site of Advaita's Navadvipa house, in 1932, on the day of Gaura-purnima, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati
laid the foundation stone of a new temple; the Advaita-bhavana in Mayapura became a centre of the Gaudiya Math.

About Advaita were written the “Advaita-mangala,” the “Advaita-vilasa” and the “Sita-charitra”; Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya
composed in His glory the “Advaita-dvadasha-nama,” the “Advaitashtaka” and the “Advaita-ashtottara-shata-nama.” Most
fully He is spoken of in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita,” the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” the “Bhakti-ratnakara” and the
“Advaita-vilasa”; there is also the late “Advaita-prakasha”[^59].

## Advaita in the Memory of the Devotees
Advaita's name sounds wherever Sri Chaitanya is sung of. He is glorified by the benedictions at the beginning of the
chapters of the “Charitamrita” and by the verse “jaya advaitadi…”; the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” glorifies Him too at the
beginning of the ninth chapter of the Adi-khanda; Vrindavana Dasa says that the Lord descended into the world amid
Nityananda, Advaita and Gadadhara. His name is in the Pancha-tattva mantra: “sri-krishna-chaitanya prabhu-nityananda
sri-advaita gadadhara srivasadi-gaura-bhakta-vrinda.” The “Bhakti-ratnakara” recalls Him at the kirtan at Shridhara's
(12) and tells how Narottama prayed to Advaita together with other associates of the Lord (1). He is remembered in small
things too: one evening it was to Advaita that Haridasa confessed he would no longer go preaching with Nityananda — so
unpredictable were the avadhuta's pastimes. And in every list of the companions at the Ratha-yatra Advaita is among the
first.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” glorifies Advaita at the beginning of almost every one of its waves — “to Advaita, the son of
Nabha Devi” (9.1–4) — and says that bhakti, so rare, became accessible “by the mercy of Chaitanya, Nityananda, Advaita
and Their shaktis” (1.492–530); Narahari, ending the book, prays to be absorbed in the pastimes of Advaita with Sita
(16.35–52). Of Padmanabha, Lokanatha's father, it says: “Padmanabha was very dear to Prabhu Advaita” (1.298). Advaita is
seen also by Pundarika Vidyanidhi, who bows to Him at his first meeting with the Lord (12.1813), and by Gopala Bhatta in
his dream of Navadvipa (1.99–124).

Sanyal sums up the meaning of His hope: “It is the privilege of the pure devotee to have the Sight of Krishna being served
by His devotees. Sree Advaita was right in maintaining this view” (vol. II, ch. 7).

So the life of Advaita Acharya closes in a circle. His call, full of compassion for the people of Kali, brought down the
Lord; His fearlessness revealed to the world Who Sri Chaitanya is; His humility showed how a servant seeks not honours but
punishment from the Lord; and His riddle announced that the purpose was fulfilled — the world was intoxicated with prema.
Whoever takes shelter of His feet will, in the words of the “Charitamrita,” easily find shelter at the feet of Sri
Chaitanya.

Narahari Chakravarti's “Namamrita-samudra” — prayers in which he calls upon the Lord's associates one after another —
opens with a verse that names Advaita too:

“Sri Guru, Sri Radha-Krishna, Chaitanya, Nitai, Advaita Gosai — give me shelter at Your feet!” (“Namamrita-samudra” 1)

A little further on he entreats Advaita's parents, Kuvera and Nabha Devi, as well:

“Be gracious, Sri Kuvera and Nabha Devi, that I may serve the feet of your son Advaita.” (“Namamrita-samudra” 5)
