# Prakrama 1, Sarga 7. The Childhood Plays

*The story of Vishvarupa: his sannyasa and the parents' blessing; Vishvambhara's promise to serve his parents; His pranks and the dream of Jagannatha Mishra; Vishvambhara asks His mother not to eat on Ekadashi; the question of Damodara*

**1.** Having heard this, and blissful in meditation on the lotus feet of Hari, Damodara inquired about the holy story of Hari's elder (brother):

**2.** "Tell truly the great, renowned (story) of Vishvarupa." Hearing this, (Murari) said: "O brahmana, listen, I shall tell you."

**3.** Having spoken thus, the physician[^1] began to tell the heart-charming, auspicious, purifying story of Vishvarupa, the portion of Baladeva:

**4.** "Sriman Sri Vishvarupa, a treasury of all virtues, utterly pure, at sixteen attained the rank of acharya by hearing and reflecting on the Self. Of powerful intellect, a devotee full of love, all-knowing, always joyful, his mind attached to the feet of Narahari, peaceful and contented, he was not attached to the world — a knower of the Vedas, a knower of rasa.

**5.** The father considered in private (who) would be a bride fit for his son's marriage; and the brahmana's son himself knew everything that he pondered in his mind.

**6.** Knowing thus all the inner intentions of his father, the forbearing Vishvarupa left home, crossed the heavenly river and took sannyasa, impossible for others.

**7.** Hearing of this, the father was overwhelmed, and the virtuous mother lamented in grief. (But then) both, wishing their son well, said: "May our son be devoted to the dharma of sannyasa!"

**8.** Having given their son such blessings, both, like sages, maintained their fortitude; casting off grief, they placed their son — the Lord of the world — on their lap and at once attained joy.

**9.** Then Hari said: "Father, My brother has gone far away, leaving you. It is I who will serve you and mother — and may you always be happy."

**10.** Hearing such words of their son — weighty, deep, charming and full of meaning — the mother and father embraced Him and, with tears of joy in their eyes, were filled with delight.

**11.** Their limbs were sated with the sweetness of touching His body; bathed in tears, they knew nothing else — as accomplished yogis absorbed in their own yoga see neither this world nor the next.

**12.** Studying, His mind engaged in serving His father, He was absorbed in play amid a band of boys; playing with His companions, all grey with dust, even when hungry He did not think of food.

**13.** Once, seeing his son self-willed, the father, wishing Him well, scolded Him: "Abandoning study and everything else, you play among the boys, tormented by hunger!"

**14.** Then at night, at the end of sleep, the foremost of the best brahmanas spoke to him in a dream: "Do you not esteem your son highly? Or, like a beast, do you not recognize the touchstone?

**15.** His slender body (deserves) ornaments of jewels and silks — or does (a beast) not chew even silks?"[^2] Mishra fearlessly replied to him: "Even if my son here is Narayana Himself,

**16.** still it is my duty to punish Him." Hearing this, the brahmana said to him: "Well (said)." Having spoken thus, the foremost of brahmanas departed, and the Vatsya, awakening, afterwards related (the dream).

**17.** Hearing of the dream, the people were at once delighted: they considered Vishvambhara the best of the best of men, and him (the father) one whose heart's desire was fulfilled; the father joyfully considered himself so, and the mother was satisfied.

**18.** Then once, staying in His house, red as the rays of the rising sun, He shone with His body filled with His own splendour and said: "Mother, do as I say."

**19.** Seeing her son blazing thus with His own splendour, frightened and amazed, she said to Him: "Child, whatever you say I shall do; tell me yourself what is in your mind."

**20.** Hearing such nectarean words, He again said to her: "Mother, on the lunar day of Hari you should not eat."[^3] Hearing her son's word, she said, "So be it," and joyfully accepted (the vow).

**21.** Then, having eaten the betel nut and other things that a brahmana had offered (to the Lord), He again said to her: "I am going. Guard your son's body, which has become motionless for half a moment."

**22.** Having said this, He suddenly rose and fell to the ground like a stick. Seeing that Vishvambhara had gone, the mother, full of grief,

**23.** bathed Him with the nectar-like waters of the Ganga. Then He awoke, became well and remained happy,

**24.** with His natural splendour. Hearing of this, Jagannatha was amazed and said: "We do not understand this divine maya."

**25.** Hearing this wondrous story, the brahmana Damodara said: "What is this that has been told, good sir? Krishna Himself, the teacher of the world,

**26.** was born (here) — how then could He say to His mother, 'I am going; guard your son, O good lady'? About this I have a great doubt.

**27.** Is this the maya of the Lord of the world? You should explain it here, for the deeds of Hari are for the good of the world."

*Thus ends the seventh sarga, "The Childhood Plays" (Balya-krida), of the first prakrama of the great poem "Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita."*

[^1]: Murari Gupta was a physician (vaidya) by caste and occupation.
[^2]: The verse is syntactically obscure in all witnesses; the sense of the brahmana's speech appears to be: you do not value your son, as a beast values neither the touchstone nor the silk it chews.
[^3]: The lunar day of Hari is Ekadashi, the eleventh day of the lunar fortnight, a fast day.
