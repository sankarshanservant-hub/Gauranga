# Prakrama 3, Sarga 14. Sri Jiyada-Nrisimha

*The Lord travels south: at Alalanatha He awakens love for Krishna in the people He embraces on the way; at Kurma-kshetra He heals the leper brahmana Vasudeva; at Jiyada-Nrisimha He tells the ancient story of that Deity*

**1.** Meanwhile Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya[^1][^2] was so distressed that he was as if senseless; and so too all the devotees were troubled at heart at that time.

**2.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya, for His part, set out southward and, coming to Alalanatha[^3], lost all control of His body for love:

**3.** loudly, again and again, He cried, "Krishna, Krishna, Krishna!" — now rolling on the ground, now swooning, now babbling,

**4.** now singing the names "Govinda, Krishna, Rama!" — and at the sight of Alalanatha His whole body was flooded with great love.

**5.** Meeting a man on the road, He embraced him and passed His power into him; and the man, overwhelmed by love, at once began to dance and sing for joy.

**6.** Bathed in a hundred streams of love, that man went home, and whenever he met the people of other villages he embraced them with love.

**7.** And they in turn, at rest in love, sang and rejoiced. Thus, from one to another, He made them all alike.

**8.** He spent one night in the land of Alalanatha, and on the next day He rose, completed His morning duties,

**9.** and, setting out for the southern country, sang thus and danced:

**10.** "Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, O! Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, O!

**11.** Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, save me! Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, Krishna, protect me!"[^4]

**12.** Reciting this mantra, He was flooded with tears of love; He rolled on the ground and ran, shaken with trembling; saying, "Here is Hari!" He wept, His voice choked with tears, and cast loving glances at the trees and creepers.

**13.** When He came to Kurma-kshetra, where Janardana dwells in the form of the Tortoise, the best of brahmanas, named Kurma, set about honouring Him

**14.** and with faith fed the Lord on fine food — prasada[^5].

**15.** Then Bhagavan, wishing to show grace to the people, set out and at Kurma-kshetra[^6] beheld Jagannatha in the form of the Tortoise.

**16.** A certain brahmana named Kurma, for whom the sight of the Lord was a great festival, joyfully offered Him hospitality and counted that day well spent.

**17.** And Vasudeva[^7], the best of brahmanas, seeing Sri Purushottama, recognised Him as Krishna in the rapture of that sight and began to dance.

**18.** Bhagavan embraced that brahmana, a leper and the best of great devotees, and he shone like gold.

**19.** Seeing both His devotees filled with love, the Husband of Shri said to them, "By My command lead the people to devotion to Krishna — easily and joyfully."

**20.** So saying, Hari Gaurachandra at once vanished, chanting "Krishna, Krishna!" and leaving His people in amazement.

**21.** Going on a little further, He beheld with great delight the Nrisimha called Jiyada[^8]; His eyes filled with tears of love and the hair of His body stood on end.

**22.** And the Lord of the worlds Himself, dear to His devotees, told the ancient story of how this Nrisimha submitted to His devotee:

**23.** "Once there lived here a farmer named Pundaya, known for growing mayambu fruit[^9].

**24.** Murari, taking the form of a boar, tore his field to pieces, and the strong and virtuous cowherd fought with Him.

**25.** But the Boar, pierced by his arrow, began to cry out, 'Rama, Rama!' — and from this the cowherd knew that He was the Lord, and he began to fast and keep other vows.

**26.** And the merciful Bhagavan said, 'Sprinkle this place with milk, and you will surely see Me together with the king.' Such was His word.

**27.** Hearing the word of Bhagavan, the cowherd, overflowing with love, made the command known to the king, and the king carried it out exactly.

**28.** Merely by the pouring of milk Bhagavan revealed Himself — His holy form — to the virtuous, and, as He did, kept the others away[^10].

**29.** Some time later a man who lived by trade came, accompanied by his wife, wishing to behold the Lord.

**30.** Intoxicated with the bliss of that sight, he entered the holy temple, and on reaching and beholding the holy lotus feet he was filled with joy.

**31.** Bhagavan said to that good man, 'Choose the boon you desire.' — 'My name is Jiyada: take that name, O Lord of the world!'

**32.** The Source of the world said, 'So be it,' and from then on He became renowned as Sri Jiyada-Nrisimha. For Hari is ever submissive to His devotees."

**33.** Having told this, Hari Himself, Sri Gauranga Mahaprabhu, vanished from there — and who saw Him go?

*Thus ends the fourteenth sarga, "The Story of Sri Jiyada-Nrisimha" (Shri-jiyada-nrisimha-prasanga), of the third prakrama of the poem "Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita."*

[^1]: These and the following events take place after the Lord's departure from Puri (end of sarga 13).
[^2]: The verses of this sarga are given in the order of the Bengali edition (Calcutta, 1945): in this order the narrative follows the Lord's route (Alalanatha — Kurma-kshetra — Jiyada-Nrisimha) without a break. In the principal edition (Haridasa Shastri) verses 1–14 of this sarga stand as 14–27 and verses 15–27 as 1–13, so that the story of Jiyada is broken there; verses 28–33 are the same.
[^3]: Alalanatha: a temple of Vishnu at Brahmagiri, south of Puri.
[^4]: This prayer is also quoted in Krishnadasa Kaviraja's "Chaitanya Charitamrita" (Madhya 7.96).
[^5]: Verses 13–14 and 15–16 tell of the same event; v. 14, like v. 9, survives in all the editions only as a half-verse.
[^6]: Kurma-kshetra (Srikurmam): a shrine of Vishnu in the form of the Tortoise on the coast south of Orissa.
[^7]: Vasudeva: a brahmana suffering from leprosy, whom the Lord healed (v. 18).
[^8]: Jiyada-Nrisimha: the Deity of Nrisimha at Simhachala (near Vishakhapatnam); the origin of the name is told in vv. 29–32.
[^9]: The farmer's name and the name of the crop are obscure in the editions and are given as they read.
[^10]: The second half of the verse is obscure; the rendering is conjectural.
