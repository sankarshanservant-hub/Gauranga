# Prakrama 1, Sarga 4. On the Descents

*The Lord's companions as portions of the Lord, of gods and of sages; the story of Haridasa; the two kinds of avatara: the avataras of the ages and the avataras for a particular purpose*

**1.** Having heard all this, Sri Damodara Pandita, greatly pleased, said: "Tell, then, the story of Nrihari[^1].

**2.** Who, in these descents, came down to the earth? And of how many kinds are the descents? Tell me of them in order."

**3.** Hearing these words of the foremost of the twice-born, Sri Murari said respectfully and with great affection: "Listen!

**4.** Now I shall tell you of something else — of the descent of Hari's own portions: of the devotees renowned as pure devotees, who are forms of the Lord.

**5.** First was born the best of the twice-born, the master Sri Madhava Puri. A portion of the Lord, dividing in two, (appeared as him) and as Advaita Acharya, endowed with good qualities[^2].

**6.** The disciple of them both was the divine Chandrashekhara, a portion of the Moon; of great fame, he is renowned on earth as Acharyaratna.

**7.** Sriman Srivasa Pandita was born as a portion of Sri Narada; Sri Mukunda of the Vaidya (physician) caste, the fine singer, was a portion of a Gandharva.

**8.** Sriman Sri Haridasa was a portion of a sage. Hear how this was once told by a brahmana bitten by a snake[^3].

**9.** Formerly there lived in Dravida, in a Vaishnava holy place, an illustrious, excellent sage named Rama, a great ascetic, who loved his son dearly.

**10.** His son washed tulasi leaves and placed them in a clean vessel, but they fell on the ground, and without washing them again

**11.** he gave them to his father; and he, the great sage named Sri Rama, offered them to Bhagavan. For that reason he was born in a Yavana (Muslim) family.

**12.** He is righteous, wise, peaceful and versed in all knowledge; he is also a portion of Brahma[^4] — and therefore the illustrious Haridasa is, beyond doubt, a true devotee.

**13.** The Avadhuta Nityananda, of great power, the greatest, was born as a portion of Baladeva; he is the great yogi, the Lord Himself.

**14.** I do not venture to describe his family, character and deeds: even Brihaspati himself, in a hundred years,

**15.** could not tell of them — what then of others, of us petty creatures! He is the second form of Sri Krishna, dear to Gauranga as life itself.

**16.** And many others — hundreds of gods and eminent sages — were born on earth as portions; I do not venture to enumerate them.

**17.** Next: it is declared that the descents of the Purusha are of two kinds. The first is the avatara of the age (yuga-avatara); the second appears for a particular task.

**18.** Those are called avataras of the ages who appear in age after age and establish dharma. Hear of them in order.

**19.** In the Satya age meditation alone brings a man to the goal. For this purpose the White One descended, four-armed, with matted locks;

**20.** like a thousand moons, a sage always absorbed in meditation, He became the teacher of meditation for all living beings.

**21.** In the Treta age the one dharma that achieves every aim is sacrifice. Then Yajna Himself was born, with the sacrificial ladles and the other implements.

**22.** Together with brahmanas who performed sacrifice, He, Janardana, the enjoyer of sacrifice, the Victorious One, Himself performed sacrifice and taught it to all the people.

**23.** In the Dvapara age worship is ordained for attaining the goal of life. Knowing this, Vishnu Himself appeared in the form of Prithu[^5].

**24.** Righteous, He performed worship and instructed the people in it, and the minds of all turned to worship.

**25.** But in Kali the highest good is kirtana — the dharma that benefits all, full of all powers, directly bestowing the highest bliss.

**26.** Having resolved thus in His mind, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu Himself was born on earth, bringing happiness to the saintly.

**27.** Full of joy, He Himself performed kirtana and caused others to perform it. These are the avataras of the ages. Hear now of the others — those who descend for a particular task.

**28.** As the Fish — the rescue of the Vedas; as the Tortoise — the bearing of Mount Mandara; as the Boar — the lifting up of the earth; as Narasimha — the tearing apart (of Hiranyakashipu);

**29.** as Vamana He took for Himself the sovereignty of the worlds from the king of the Danavas; and Bhargava[^6] conquered the earth, defeating the arrogant kings,

**30.** and Vishnu, the sole deliverer of the world, gave that earth to a brahmana. As Sri Rama He slew Ravana, and the world was filled with His glory.

**31.** And in the descent of Sriman Krishna, Hari Himself, endowed with all powers, removed the burden of the earth.

**32.** As the Buddha the Supreme Bhagavan bewildered (people) regarding the Vedas, and in the form of Kalki He destroyed the mlechchhas.

**33.** Such are the many deeds of the many-formed Nrihari; the great rishis have described these avataras who descend for a particular task."

*Thus ends the fourth sarga, "On the Descents" (Avataranukarana), of the first prakrama of the great poem "Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita."*

[^1]: Nrihari ("Man-Hari") is usually a name of Nrisimha; here, as in v. 33, it names the Lord in general.
[^2]: The sense is that a single portion of the Lord appeared in two ways — as Madhavendra Puri and as Advaita Acharya.
[^3]: The brahmana's account itself is not given in the poem; Murari relates only its substance (vv. 9–11).
[^4]: According to tradition, Haridasa is a portion of Brahma, who appeared as a devotee to atone for an offence.
[^5]: The avatara of the Dvapara age is usually named otherwise; here Murari Gupta names Prithu, the king who first established the order of worship.
[^6]: Bhargava is Parashurama, the descendant of Bhrigu.
