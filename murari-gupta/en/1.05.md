# Prakrama 1, Sarga 5. The Appearance of Sri Chaitanya

*Shachi bears in her womb a portion of Hari; the hymn of the gods to Shachi; the Lord's birth on the full-moon night of Phalguna during a lunar eclipse; the prediction of Nilambara Chakravarti; the rites after the birth*

**1.** "Hear attentively, O brahmana, the new account of the descent of Chaitanya — the Lord of the world, the all-powerful ocean of compassion.

**2.** When the best of the divine sages[^1] had gone to his own hermitage, the Supreme Bhagavan Achyuta entered the mind of Jagannatha, the brahmana sage.

**3.** The great splendour placed by him the chaste (wife) received in due time. Thus the virtuous Shachi, devoted to her husband,

**4.** auspicious, conceived an embryo — a portion of Hari — as the Ganga once received the seed of Shambhu[^2]. And her splendour grew like the moon in the bright fortnight.

**5.** Seeing her endowed with beauty and shining like molten gold, Jagannatha, graced with good fortune, rejoiced with all his heart.

**6.** Then, seeing her so, the gods headed by Brahma and others — the Gandharvas, the immortals and the dwellers of the sky together with Indra —

**7.** joining their palms out of joy, with tears in their voices and eyes, their necks bent in obeisance, all of them joyfully praised her:

**8.** "We bow to you who ever bear Him in your womb — Aditi, the mother of Hari, bearing in your womb the radiance of moon, sun and fire, bearing pure being in your womb — steadfastness and forbearance;

**9.** bearing freedom from malice in your womb, perfection itself, bearing in your womb the Veda — Hari Himself; you are Devaki, Rohini and Yashoda, the source of being in every way[^3].

**10.** You bear in your womb Him who will spread the supremely holy sacrifice called kirtana — that which is not attained by (ordinary) sacrifices.

**11.** The joy that arises in such as us in half a moment from hearing the kirtana of Nrihari would not arise even from millions of sacrifices.

**12.** Oh! Great was the nectar of immortality that Hari Himself once gave after churning the ocean[^4] — but a sweetness millions of times greater

**13.** we taste here, hearing the glory of Sri Hari: because of the kirtana of Hari the mind counts even liberation as worthless."

**14.** Having spoken thus, the gods with Indra, placing Brahma at their head, bowed to her and departed, singing the glory of Sri Hari,

**15.** to their own city, overjoyed that a portion of the Lord of Shri had been born on earth; praising the good fortune of (the age of) Kali, they danced, overwhelmed with love.

**16.** And then, when the moon, the lord of the night, was full, at midnight in the auspicious month of Phalguna, at a time excellent in every quality and filled with a pure breeze,

**17.** when the minds of the gods and the saints were serene and the water of the heavenly river had become cool and pure — Hari Himself was born.

**18.** Having obtained a son with eyes like blossoming lotuses, with a face like the shining full moon, golden, who by His own splendour freed the quarters of darkness —

**19.** he, Sri Jagannatha Mishra Purandara, could not reach the further shore of the ocean of joy, like a poor man who has gained the padma treasure[^5], and his voice constantly faltered with love.

**20.** At the time of His birth Rahu swallowed the moon entirely: defeated by the lotus face of Krishna, the moon, as if out of shame, entered the mouth of the enemy of the gods[^6].

**21.** At that holy time the people performed kirtana of Narahari, worship, and at once bathing in the waters of the Jahnavi, the giving of gifts and cleansing from sin, in purity.

**22.** The hosts of gods with Mahendra, led by the Lotus-born and Mahesha, rejoiced, and the leaders (of the Gandharvas), together with the apsaras absorbed in dancing, rained down flowers.

**23.** Delighted by His birth, Nilambara Chakravarti, the knower of all scriptures, hurried to the home of his son-in-law.

**24.** Calling Jagannatha and addressing Shachi, the wise man, who knew (from the stars) the time of his grandson's birth, spoke these words:

**25.** "Oh! This lion among men is born when Brihaspati (Jupiter) is in exaltation[^7]. He will always be the protector of the whole world;

**26.** of good character, the refuge of all dharmas, the best of sannyasis, He will give joy to all beings like the full moon pouring nectar.

**27.** He will forever deliver both lineages — his father's and his mother's." When the brahmana had spoken thus, all the people rejoiced.

**28.** The mother, hearing these words of her father, was filled with great joy, and the Vatsya[^8] held the great festival of the jatakarma, the birth rite of his son.

**29.** He gave the brahmanas betel, sandal paste, garlands and fragrances, and in due order performed the auspicious rites beginning with the rite of rising (of the mother after childbirth)[^9]."

*Thus ends the fifth sarga, "The Appearance of Sri Chaitanya" (Sri Chaitanya-avirbhava), of the great poem "Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita."*

[^1]: Narada, at whose entreaty the Lord agreed to descend (Sarga 1.03).
[^2]: An allusion to the birth of Skanda: the seed of Shiva (Shambhu) was received by the Ganga.
[^3]: The reading of the last word of the verse is uncertain; the translation is tentative.
[^4]: The churning of the Milk Ocean by the gods and asuras, from which the nectar of immortality (amrita) was obtained.
[^5]: Padma is one of the nine treasures (nidhi) of Kubera.
[^6]: The Lord's birth coincided with a lunar eclipse; Rahu is the demon, "the enemy of the gods," who swallows the moon.
[^7]: The "exalted position" (uccha) of a planet in astrology is the sign of its greatest strength.
[^8]: The Vatsya is Jagannatha Mishra, of the Vatsya clan.
[^9]: Another understanding is possible: the "rite of rising" as the infant's first going out of the lying-in chamber.
