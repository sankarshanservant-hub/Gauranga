# Prakrama 1, Sarga 6. The Description of the Pastimes from Birth among the Childhood Plays

*Vishvambhara's infancy: the naming, His form, childhood plays; the wandering brahmana; the broken pots; the teaching on pure and impure; the brick thrown at His mother; the two coconuts; the sound of ankle bells*

**1.** After some time, the husband and wife, seeing the sweetly babbling infant crawl on His knees, were filled with great joy.

**2.** On His smiling face, like a red lotus, the rays of "the king of the twice-born"[^1] shone, dispelling the darkness in the minds of the saints.

**3.** "He has sustained the universe of old" — so (thinking), the father himself gave Him the most beautiful name Sriman Vishvambhara[^2].

**4.** His body was golden like molten gold, His eyes wide like shining lotuses; clad only in the wind, wearing a silver necklace, with a garland in His curls — (such was) Hari.

**5.** His face was like the full moon, His babble sweet as nectar; charming in form, He was adorned with bracelets and armlets put upon Him.

**6.** With palms and soles red as a lotus or ground vermilion, pure, He grew constantly, digit by digit, like the moon in the bright fortnight.

**7.** In time He, of boundless radiance, began to walk, and with His red feet He removed the burning of the earth born of separation (from Him)[^3].

**8.** Janardana ate the food of a brahmana who wandered among the holy places, and so reminded him of the wonder in the house of Nanda[^4].

**9.** He played with His boy companions with shoots of trees, and all the children, struck by them, romped joyfully before Him.

**10.** Standing on the ground on one foot and touching another's knee with His knee, Hari, a child by His own maya, played like a monkey.

**11.** Once, seeing His mother in anger ready to seize Him, He, Himself full of anger, broke the pots.

**12.** When once a pot was broken, Yashoda bound Him with cattle ropes; (remembering this,) Shachi looked at His face and trembled with fear[^5].

**13.** Sitting down in an impure place on a heap of discarded clay pots piled one upon another, He laughed before His mother.

**14.** Seeing this, Shachi said: "Child, leave this loathsome place! Bathe again and be clean, and climb onto my lap."

**15.** When this was said, Bhagavan, the knower of all truths, wholly filled with the mood of Dattatreya[^6], who completes (the knowledge) of the all-knowing, said to her:

**16.** "Listen: pure or impure is a mere notion. For the world is (only) earth, water, air, fire, ether and mind; here the one Hari alone shines, the ocean of compassion, whose lotus feet are non-dual and prior to all the spread-out manifestation. Know that there is nothing else.

**17.** Therefore I am pure and in no way impure. Know this, mother, and do not entertain any other doubt here."

**18.** When her son had spoken thus, she hastily took Him by the hand, brought Him and bathed Him with the clear waters of the heavenly river.

**19.** After some time Shachi again saw her son seated on a heap of thrown-away clay pots and scolded Him:

**20.** "Foolish one! Why do you sit in an impure, forbidden place?" Hearing these words of His mother, filled with anger,

**21.** Sriman Vishvambhara said: "Foolish woman! There is nothing impure anywhere. I told you this before — so why do you reproach Me?"

**22.** Having said this, He angrily threw a brick at her face. Struck by the blow, she was pained, fainted and fell.

**23.** Then all the women gathered and sprinkled her with cool water. And then Hari, who acts in human ways, came there

**24.** and at once Himself burst into tears: "Mother, mother!" — placing on her face His beautiful hand that removes every sorrow.

**25.** Then Shachi at once came to her senses and, taking her son onto her lap, loving, she rejoiced, overwhelmed with love for her son.

**26.** Then a certain merry woman, fond of jest, said to the Teacher of the world: "Two coconuts

**27.** bring and give her — then she will be well. If not, she will die — and what will you do then?"

**28.** Hearing her words, He, filled with (…) for His mother[^7], went out, brought and gave two coconuts.

**29.** Hari (brought) a pair (of fruits) whose stalks were moist with sap as if just fallen. Seeing this, the amazed (women) said: "Where did you get this fruit?"

**30.** Then the great-hearted one, the banner of the Vatsa clan[^8], silencing all (the women) with cries of "hum!", gave His mother His smiling lotus face.

**31.** Now hear further of the wonderful mighty deeds of the Great Soul — excellent deeds beyond the worldly, of the Supreme Self, the Lord of maya.

**32.** Once at night Shachi, having fallen asleep, noticed that the house seemed full of people, and, alarmed, (took) her son who lay on her lap;

**33.** frightened, she hastily sent to her husband's room Sriman Vishvambhara — Hari, who was worshipped on the way by the gods[^9].

**34.** As the son walked along, the jingling of ankle bells was heard again and again from His quite bare feet. Hearing it, the alarmed Vatsya asked Shachi, and Shachi the Vatsya: "What is this? Whence this sound?"

**35.** When the son came near, the brahmana, greatly amazed, saw that his son's lotus feet were quite bare: "Whence, then, was the sweet jingling of ankle bells heard?" — and, embracing his son, he was filled with joy.

*Thus ends the sixth sarga, "The Description of the Pastimes from Birth among the Childhood Plays" (Balya-krida: janmadi-lila-varnana), of the first prakrama of the great poem "Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita."*

[^1]: "The king of the twice-born" (dvijaraja) is the moon; but "the twice-born" (dvija) are also the teeth: a play on words — the shining of His teeth in His smile.
[^2]: Vishvambhara means "He who sustains the universe."
[^3]: The earth, according to tradition, suffered in separation from Krishna's feet after His departure.
[^4]: The story of the pilgrim brahmana whose food, offered to the Lord, was eaten by the infant Nimai; so too the infant Krishna once ate the food of a wandering brahmana in the house of Nanda.
[^5]: Shachi remembered how Yashoda bound Krishna when He broke the pot of butter — and feared that her son too would be angered.
[^6]: Dattatreya is an incarnation of the Lord, an avadhuta who made no distinction between pure and impure.
[^7]: The end of the line is corrupt in the sources and cannot be recovered.
[^8]: The Vatsa clan (gotra) is the clan of Jagannatha Mishra (cf. "the Vatsya").
[^9]: The people Shachi saw in the house were evidently the gods, come to worship the Lord.
