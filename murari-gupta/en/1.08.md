# Prakrama 1, Sarga 8. The Passing of Jagannatha Mishra

*Murari's answer: the Lord enters the devotee's heart, and the devotee takes on His form (the examples of Prahlada, the gopis and Narada); Vishvambhara's studies; the illness and passing of Jagannatha Mishra on the bank of the Ganga; the funeral rites*

**1.** Having heard his words, having thought and reflected, (Murari) bowed to Hari and spoke again: "Listen with full attention.

**2.** Through meditation on Bhagavan, through kirtana and through hearing (of Him), Hari enters the heart of a person — a great soul.

**3.** Such a person imitates Him: forgetful of his own body and the rest, he constantly bears His splendour and His might.

**4.** So it happens; and then, in time, he again comes to outer consciousness and does his ordinary acts — as once happened with Prahlada:

**5.** in the ocean he became identified (with the Lord), and on the shore the remembrance of his body returned[^1]. So indeed the virtuous gopis too at times experience such identification[^2].

**6.** The Lord did this, showing the world a teaching: for a devotee of Krishna there can be such identity of form (with Him).

**7.** So that people here should not be bewildered, He taught: the devotee's body is truly the very self of Bhagavan; there is no doubt of this.

**8.** Krishna, having slain Keshi, showed Narada His glory and His power; and then the best of sages

**9.** fell to the ground like a stick. And a Vaishnava who goes to the city of Mathura obtains at that place a hundredfold greater reward[^3].

**10.** So too Rama, the source of the world, showed His universal form, and then again for the welfare (of the world) performed human deeds[^4].

**11.** Hear further, O brahmana, the auspicious story of Chaitanya, hearing which with faith a mortal is freed from the bondage of worldly existence.

**12.** Living in the house of His teacher, the Victorious One studied all the Vedas, and then He Himself, the husband of Sarasvati[^5], taught students.

[^1]: Prahlada, thrown into the ocean at Hiranyakashipu's command, became absorbed in meditation on the Lord and felt himself one with Him; on the shore he again became aware of his body.
[^2]: The gopis, in separation from Krishna, imitated His pastimes, identifying themselves with Him.
[^3]: The place where the demon Keshi was slain in Vrindavana is revered as holy (Keshi-ghat).
[^4]: Perhaps the tradition is meant according to which Rama showed His universal form to His mother Kausalya.
[^5]: The husband of Sarasvati (the goddess of learning) — the Lord as master of all knowledge.
