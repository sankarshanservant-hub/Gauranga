# Varna 2. The Description of the Birth and the Annaprashana

*In a dream Bhagavatananda bids the author describe Jagadisha's life; Kamalaksha and Bhagyavati receive from Narayana the boon of a Vaishnava son; Jagadisha's birth on Bhima Ekadashi; the festival; the annaprashana and the naming*

*The second varna*

**1.** *Having received in a dream the supreme command of Srimad Bhagavatananda,
I describe the life of Sri Jagadisha.*[^1]

**2.** Glory to Prabhu Bhagavatananda, full of mercy!
Be kind and show mercy to me, a wretch.

**3.** My birth has become fortunate and fruitful:
therefore I have seen those rosy feet.

**4.** On the twenty-ninth [of the month] of Bhadra I was overcome by sleep;
at that time I saw a wondrous form.

**5.** The beauty of those feet surpassed gold;
I saw the radiance of the holy body, surpassing millions of suns.

**6.** I saw the beautiful face — [beside it] the moon seemed stained.
That great personage appeared before me.

**7.** Smiling, he speaks sweet words to me:
"Describe the life of Jagadisha."

**8.** "I am a fool — what shall I describe?" I thought in my heart.
My heart grew timid with fear; words would not come.

**9.** Seeing my fear, that jewel of a man says to me:
"Ananda, never be afraid in your heart.[^2]

**10.** Know for certain: I am Bhagavatananda.
You will surely carry out my command.

**11.** Through your mouth I myself will describe [it];
the devotees will surely accept [it]."

**12.** Mercifully the Prabhu gave me this command —
and at that very moment my sleep was broken.

**13.** Awake, I no longer saw that form;
my mind grew eager to carry out the command.

**14.** Like one mad, I write in the book an account of myself;[^3]
I do not know good from bad — the Prabhu's command is my strength.

**15.** One who becomes a devotee of Sri Jagadisha
will surely accept this book.

**16.** What will others understand of this book's narration?
He will understand for whom Jagadisha is the treasure of life.

*Tatha hi:*

**17.** *Hear, then, of the form, birth and deeds
of the great-souled Sri Jagadisha Pandita, described by me.*

**18.** Glory, glory to the devotees of Sri Mahaprabhu!
Hear the account of Jagadisha's life.

**19.** Be kind and show mercy to me, a fool,
so that the book's description may flow with ease.

**20.** In the eastern country there lived a twice-born named Kamalaksha —
a Bandya of Gayaghar, a descendant of Bhatta Narayana.[^4]

**21.** His wife was chaste and wholly devoted to her husband;
her renowned name was Srimati Bhagyavati.

**22.** One equal to Bhagyavati is not seen in the world:
day and night she performs the worship and service of Vishnu.

**23.** Kamalaksha Bandya was wholly devoted to Vishnu;
with body, mind and speech he served Vishnu.

**24.** Apart from the truth of bhakti to Vishnu, no words were on his lips;
in taking the name of Vishnu he abode in great happiness.

**25.** With the mood of bhakti the two worshipped Vishnu;
their bhakti pleased Narayana.

**26.** Graciously the Lord said to them both:
"Whatever you desire — take it as a boon."

**27.** The Bandya says: "If You will give a boon to one who has nothing,
let firm bhakti abide at Your feet.

**28.** Wealth, sons, worldly things do not attract my mind.
Do not bind me, poor and wretched, in the net of maya."

**29.** The Lord says: "Keep no doubt at all in your mind:
know for certain that you both are dear to Me."

**30.** The Bandya says: "Lord, if it is Your mercy,
then give me this boon, O merciful one:

**31.** devoted to Vishnu, learned in all the shastras —
let a son be born to me from Bhagyavati's womb."

**32.** Hearing this, the Lord speaks gracious words to him:
"Your son will be the crown of Vaishnavas."

**33.** Hearing this, Bhagyavati was filled with joy:
the Lord had declared that the son would be a Vaishnava.

**34.** The Devi thrilled all over; [tears] streamed from both her eyes.
She says: "A Vaishnava will come into my womb!

**35.** What fortune: my birth has become fruitful.
Surely in a former life I did some good deed.

**36.** By the fruit of that merit this good fortune will be mine:
the Lord has declared that the son will be a Vaishnava."

**37.** With a happy heart the chaste lady serves Vishnu:
by hearing, chanting, remembering, worship and prayer.

**38.** Thus in various ways she served Sri Vishnu;
after many days the Devi conceived.

**39.** One, two — in due order ten months were completed;
all that the injunctions and custom require was done.

**40.** Looking at Bhagyavati, the Bandya rejoices in his heart:
her body is like a mass of great effulgence.

**41.** Seeing [this] wonder, the Bandya reflects:
"Surely some great [soul] has come within her."

**42.** With a happy heart Kamalaksha worshipped Vishnu;
at an auspicious moment Bhagyavati gave birth to a son.

**43.** In the month of Magha, in the bright fortnight, on the lunar day of Ekadashi,
known among people as Bhima Ekadashi,[^5]

**44.** at the end of the day, when the sun had set,
the dusk (godhuli) passed and the time of evening came.

**45.** At that hour people perform the evening worship
and play karatalas, mridangas and other instruments.

**46.** Lighting incense and lamps, people worship Vishnu;
in every house is the sound of the conches and bells of arati.[^6]

**47.** On the night of Ekadashi, the day of Sri Hari, people
loudly sing the names "Hare Krishna."

**48.** Under an auspicious lagna, with auspicious planets, in an auspicious house and sign,
Jagadisha appeared — the treasure-store of all virtues.

**49.** The people keeping the Ekadashi vow are full of bliss;
at that hour Bhagyavati gave birth to a son.

**50.** Wondrously beautiful is the form of that child;
Bhagyavati gazes [at him] with unwavering eyes.

**51.** The beautiful face is like a dancing moon;
the brows reach to the ears, the eyes are large.

**52.** The nose surpasses the sesame flower, the lips are a lovely bimba;
a pair of long ears, a charming forehead.

**53.** The two arms are like lotus stalks without thorns;
the chest is broad, the navel like a lake.

**54.** The waist is very slender, the hips are broad;[^7]
seeing his thighs, the elephant's trunk was put to shame.

**55.** The two lotus feet surpass the land-lotus;
on them are five and five holy toes, perfectly pure.

**56.** The nails of those toes are [like] the rising of the moon:
they filled with light the lying-in room, where there was no lamp.[^8]

**57.** His lovely yellow hue surpasses turmeric and champaka;
on the child's body all auspicious marks are manifest.

**58.** Seeing [this], Bhagyavati Devi called the Bandya;
coming, Kamalaksha Bandya looks at his son's face.

**59.** Seeing the son's radiance, he was amazed in his mind:
"A great personage has appeared from Bhagyavati's womb."

**60.** Seeing on the child's body the signs of a portion of Ishvara,
the brahmana thrilled all over and became very happy.

**61.** Wealth, grain and whatever else was in his house
he gave away to the brahmanas with love in his heart.

**62.** The singers, dancers and all others who came
the Bandya satisfied with gifts of money.

**63.** In Kamalaksha's house there was a great festival;
hearing of it, the neighbours came to see.

**64.** Seeing the child's form, [all] were filled with delight;
in bliss all the people give [him] their blessings.

**65.** All together they joyfully brought presents;
Kamalaksha distributed all of it to the brahmanas.

**66.** Bhagyavati, taking her son on her lap,
gazes at him constantly without blinking.

**67.** According to custom, twenty-one days passed;
having bathed, mother and son came into the house.

**68.** Having brought wife and son into the house, the worthy Bandya
gave many gifts with a joyful heart.

**69.** Having gained a son, they grow happier day by day;
both worship Vishnu with the greatest care.

**70.** Thus gradually the fifth month passed,
and the sixth month [of the child's life] began.

**71.** Resolving to perform the annaprashana for their son,
they fixed a day at an auspicious hour in the month of Ashadha.

**72.** Holding their son in their arms, the two, lying down,
consider what name to give the son:

**73.** "The form of our son enchants the world;
it seems he will deliver the world.

**74.** The signs of greatness are imprinted on his body;
therefore it is fitting to give him the name Jagadisha."[^9]

**75.** Having decided this, they were filled with great happiness;
rising in the morning, they invited many brahmanas.

**76.** According to the rules of the shastras they performed the holy annaprashana;
having given the name Jagadisha, they rejoiced at heart.

**77.** With the brahmanas they held a great festival;
for the son's welfare they gave gifts of many kinds.

**78.** I have no power to describe this in detail;
by the Prabhu's mercy I describe it only briefly, like a sutra.

**79.** As the moon of the second day gradually becomes full,
so little by little Jagadisha grew.

**80.** I am a fool, of bad conduct, utterly wretched;
[my] description is wayward, the book is not beautiful.

**81.** At the feet of the Lord's devotees I beg pardon:
do not hold against me any offence [I have committed] in this.

**82.** In a dream Prabhu Bhagavatananda commanded:
"Ananda, describe the life of Jagadisha."

**83.** By the strength of that command, placing hope in those feet,
the servant of his servants describes Jagadisha's life.

*Thus in the Charitra-vijaya of Sri Jagadisha Pandita ends the second varna, called "The Description of the Birth and the Annaprashana".*

[^1]: The edition reads «স্বপতঃ» (*svapataḥ*); taken as «স্বপ্নতঃ» (*svapnataḥ*), "in a dream" (cf. 82). Bhagavatananda, a disciple of Raghunatha Acharya, is third in the line of disciples from Jagadisha; the author is third in the line from him (through Premananda and Radhacharana).
[^2]: Ananda — an address to the author, Ananda Dasa.
[^3]: «আত্মবার্ত্তা» (*ātma-vārttā*), literally "news of oneself, an account of oneself"; apparently the dream just described. The sense is not quite clear.
[^4]: The eastern country (পূর্ব দেশ, *pūrva-deśa*) is apparently East Bengal. Bandya is a short form of the family name Bandyopadhyaya; the Bandyopadhyayas trace their descent from Bhatta Narayana, one of the five brahmanas said to have been invited to Bengal by King Adishura. «গয়ঘড়» (*gayaghaṛa*; so in the scan) is apparently the name of Kamalaksha's gami (ancestral village); not identified.
[^5]: The Ekadashi of the bright fortnight of Magha, also called Bhaimi Ekadashi.
[^6]: The edition reads «বর» (*bara*); taken as «রব» (*raba*), "sound".
[^7]: The edition reads «নব্য দেশ» (*nabya deśa*); taken as «মধ্যদেশ» (*madhya-deśa*), "the middle [of the body]", "the waist".
[^8]: The lying-in room — «অরিষ্ট» (*ariṣṭa*, *ariṣṭa-gṛha*), the room where mother and newborn stay after the birth.
[^9]: Jagadisha (*jagad-īśa*) means "lord of the world"; the parents draw the name from their son's form "enchanting the world" (*jagat-mohana*) and from his destiny to "deliver the world" (*jagat tāribā*).
