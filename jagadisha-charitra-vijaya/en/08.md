# Varna 8. The Account of Sri Jagadisha's Residence in the Village of Yashoda and the Manifestation of Service to Sri Jagannatha

*At Mahaprabhu's command Jagadisha, leaving Duhkhini with Mahesha, sets out for Nilachala; the journey in prema; Gopinatha at Remuna, Sakshi-gopala at Cuttack; the Nilachakra, Athara-nala and the approach to the temple by prostrations; the darshana of Jagannatha and Jagadisha's prayer; Jagadisha asks for Jagannatha's body kept at the temple's "Vaikuntha", and Jagannatha promises to ride on his back; the king's permission; Jagadisha brings Jagannatha to Yashoda (Jatra) and manifests His service; Mahesha and Duhkhini move there; the pilgrims; the local king cannot lift the Deity, offers land and receives instruction; the author's signature*

*The eighth varna*

**1.** Glory, glory to Jagadisha! Being kind to me,
bestow mercy, [casting on me] a merciful glance.
Happy at heart, I write your virtues in a book —
mercifully bring [it] to completion.

**2.** All the pastimes that Jagadisha thus performed —
who can count them?
What I hear from the mouths of saints, that alone I describe a little,
in order to purify my own heart.

**3.** Mahaprabhu gave the command, and Sri Jagadisha set out
to see the kshetra of Jagannatha.
Choked with the rasa of prema, bowing, he takes each step;
streams of prema flow from both his eyes.

**4.** Loudly raising the cry of "Hari!", the crown-jewel of Vaishnavas
set out in great happiness.
Having consoled Duhkhini and left [her] in Mahesha's house,
he went towards the south.

**5.** On and on he walks along the road, ever singing Gaura's virtues,
not resting even for a moment.
In his mind — always meditation on Krishna; his ears hear talks of Krishna;
his tongue always takes Krishna's name.

**6.** In this way Sri Pandita, greatly delighted,[^1]
walks along the road, absorbed in prema.
All the toll-keepers at the river crossings, seeing the Prabhu, join their palms;
no one raises any obstacle [for him].

**7.** The Prabhu walks on happily; the temples in place after place
he visits, absorbed in prema.
He knows no sorrow at all, ever happy in the name of Krishna;
the weariness of the road never comes over him.

**8.** Sri Jagadisha Pandita, joyful at heart,
in due course came to Remuna.
There, seeing Sri Gopinatha, he fell prostrate
and with a happy heart offered himself to Him.[^2]

**9.** Humbly he prays: "O merciful One, be merciful,
be gracious to me,
so that by Your grace, going to Nilachala,
I may obtain the darshana of Jagannatha."

**10.** Having prayed thus, he spent that night
in the abode of Lord Gopinatha.
Rising in the morning, Sri Pandita, having bowed to Sri Gopinatha,
went on with happiness in his heart.

**11.** Intoxicated with prema he walks on, like a crazy baul;
people take him for a madman and laugh.
But whoever is most fortunate recognizes the Prabhu,
offers praises and floats in the bliss of prema.

**12.** Such is the Prabhu's way: equal kindness to all;
he takes the virtues and has no eye for faults.
Bearing Gaura's command on his head, he goes to the city of Nilachala,
singing the names and virtues of Gauranga.

**13.** Thus Sri Pandita arrived at Cuttack,
and there he saw Sakshi-gopala.[^3]
Seeing the form of Sri Gopala, he became supremely happy
and offered [Him] many obeisances and praises.

**14.** Staying there two days and seeing the service of the eight periods of the day,
the Prabhu became greatly delighted.
Coming to Tulasi-chura, Jagadisha, the abode of virtues,
beheld the Nilachakra.[^4]

**15.** The bliss in the Prabhu's mind on seeing the Nilachakra —
who has the power to tell it?
His body trembling with prema, he dances, making so many playful gestures;
streams of prema flow from both his eyes.

**16.** At every step [falling] flat like a rod, he bows hundreds and hundreds of times,
offers praises in prose and verse.[^5]
Crying "Gaura! Jagannatha!" he dances with both arms raised
and forgets himself in the bliss of prema.

**17.** Thus, absorbed in prema, coming to Athara-nala,
he danced much and sang kirtana.[^6]
From there, in great happiness, in the delight of prema for Krishna,
he began to prostrate himself with eight limbs.

**18.** Jagadisha, the vessel of prema — the Prabhu walks one step
and again prostrates himself with eight limbs.
Thus step by step, as his heart desired,
he goes on, bowing down again and again.

**19.** Thus, coming to Nilachala in great eagerness,
he beheld Jagannatha.
Trembling with prema, rolling on the ground,
he fell prostrate in the mood of bhakti.

**20.** Bowing to Sri Jagannatha, he offers praises with joined palms,
gazing [at Him] with a joyful heart.
His body is heavy with the rasa of prema, his eyes are full of tears,
no words come forth from his holy mouth.

**21.** Thus for some time he gazed [at Him];
after that, external awareness returned.
Then, happy at heart, standing facing east,
he offered many praises thus:

**22.** "O Krishna, full of mercy, have mercy on me;
You are the wealth and life of the destitute.
Out of fear of Time my heart is always trembling;
mercifully deliver me.

**23.** I, a tiny soul, born upon the earth,
am bound in the net of maya.
You are the maker of the world, the one sustainer of the universe;
forgiving my offences, You have shown Yourself.

**24.** Govinda, son of Nanda, lifting Sri Govardhana,
You mercifully protected Gokula.
Lord of the life of the gopis, together with the cowherd boys
You performed hidden pastimes in Vrajapura.

**25.** Friend of the lowly, be kind — You bear the name "the Merciful";
You, Lord, are the life of those who have nothing.
I am bound by evil sense objects; granting [me] Your lotus feet,
Lord, deliver me.

**26.** I am a person without bhakti, I have no bhajana or sadhana —
how shall I cross this samsara?
Being merciful, granting [me] bhakti-yoga to Yourself,
make me Your servant and keep me at Your holy feet."

**27.** Thus Sri Pandita offered many hymns,
and Jagannatha became pleased.
He says to the Pandita: "Take a boon, whatever you wish:
I have appeared before you in person."

**28.** Seeing the Lord's form, radiant as a sapphire,
Jagadisha joined both his palms.
His eyes filling with tears, his heart overwhelmed with prema,
he prostrated himself with eight limbs.

**29.** "That body of Yours which lies at the place [called] Vaikuntha,
in the northern part of the Sri Mandira —
if I receive Your mercy, I shall take that form
and manifest [Your] service in the land of Gauda."[^7]

**30.** Then the Lord gave His command — He gave the command to Jagadisha:
"I accept [your request].
Go alone, taking My body,
to whatever place you wish."

**31.** Hearing this, Sri Jagadisha Pandita rejoiced
and began to speak humbly:
"A form as heavy as a load for three carrying-poles — that I should have the strength to carry it —
how can this be possible?[^8]

**32.** And the king's men — why would they let [me] go?
These two hesitations are in my mind.
Though You, Lord, have given the command, still I feel fear in my mind;
make this lowly person fearless."

**33.** Hearing this, Jagannatha, the Lord of the life of the destitute,
says to him kindly:
"The king's envoys will not hinder [you]; I will go on your back;
take [Me] and go without fear."

**34.** Rising in the morning, Sri Pandita, greatly delighted,
took his morning bath in the waters of the ocean.
At that moment a king's servant, coming near him,
made this submission:

**35.** With many obeisances and praises [he said]: "The king has given permission
to take away the body [of Jagannatha]."
Hearing this, he was happy: all sorrow went far away,
and his bliss became boundless.

**36.** Tying it as a load upon his back, the body of Jagannatha
Thakura Pandita took up.
That account is full of nectar; by the command of Bhagavata[nanda]
this Ananda has described [it] a little.[^9]

**37.** Taking the body of Jagannatha, the Pandita
walks the road in prema, filled with joy.

**38.** Day and night singing the names of Gaura and Krishna,
in due course he arrived at the village of Sri Yashoda (Jatra).

**39.** Having come there, the Pandita regained external awareness
and made a plea: "Jagannatha, stay here."

**40.** Jagannatha accepted his words
and gave this command: "You serve Me."

**41.** Receiving Sri Jagannatha's command, the Pandita
became very joyful in his mind.

**42.** Mahesha Pandita and Duhkhini Thakurani —
he himself brought both of them to that place.

**43.** Jagadisha manifested the service of Jagannatha;
hearing of it, the people of that land came to see.

**44.** All the people say: "This land has become blessed:
a brahmana has brought Jagannatha and serves [Him].

**45.** To see Jagannatha in Nilachala
one must spend money and toil on the road.

**46.** Without effort we shall have darshana in this place;
this good brahmana has delivered us all."

**47.** In this way all the people come eagerly,
take darshana and go away happy in the bliss of prema.

**48.** Jagannatha's arrival became widely famous;
hearing of it, the king of that land came to see.

**49.** Coming and seeing [Him], the maharaja was astonished
and then reasoned within his mind:

**50.** "I see that this body is a load for three carrying-poles,[^10]
[yet] the brahmana brought it on his back alone.

**51.** Whether it is true or false I cannot understand; my mind is confused.
Or did he make Jagannatha out of shola?"[^11]

**52.** Thinking thus, that king took hold of Jagannatha;
he made great effort, yet could not lift [Him].

**53.** Astonished, the king bowed down
and, coming from there, clasped the Prabhu's feet.

**54.** The king says: "Forgive my offence, O merciful one:
I have taken shelter of you, Prabhu."

**55.** Jagadisha says: "You are the ruler of the kingdom;
it is not proper for you to praise me."

**56.** The king says: "Prabhu, hide behind maya no more[^m56];
mercifully grant [me] the shade of your feet.

**57.** I have come to know your greatness well:
you have descended to deliver this land.

**58.** Casting a merciful glance upon me, Prabhu,
accept a few of my possessions.

**59.** Then all my offences will be forgiven;
if the offence is not forgiven, I shall surely perish."

**60.** Seeing him distressed, Pandita Thakura
showed him abundant mercy.

**61.** Jagadisha says: "King, whatever you wish —
for your satisfaction I will accept [it]."

**62.** The king says: "Prabhu, this is my request:
accept some land in my kingdom."

**63.** The Prabhu says: "I live in your kingdom;
I live on an allowance, and you are the master of the kingdom."

**64.** The king says: "For the sake of Jagannatha's service
accept fertile, tax-free land.

**65.** Staying in this place and serving Jagannatha,
deliver all the fallen worldly people."

**66.** The Prabhu was pleased by the king's humility,
and the Prabhu accepted the land given by the king.

**67.** Again that king, clasping the Prabhu's feet,
makes a plea for his own welfare.

**68.** The king says: "I have fallen into this well of samsara;
in what way shall I be delivered from it?

**69.** Mercifully tell me the means for that;
I have taken refuge at your reddish feet."

**70.** Seeing the king's earnest longing, Prabhu Jagadisha
joyfully gave instruction.

**71.** The Prabhu says: "King, listen to my words:
care for your subjects with love, as for sons.

**72.** In your own kingdom establish bhakti for Krishna;
without effort you will attain the feet of Govinda."

**73.** Receiving instruction from the Prabhu, the best of kings
offered the Prabhu many obeisances and praises.

**74.** Then that maharaja, taking the Prabhu's leave,
went to his own abode, joyful.

**75.** Going home, he carried out the Prabhu's instruction:
he established bhakti for Krishna in his own kingdom.

**76.** Here, meanwhile, Prabhu Jagadisha in his own home
serves Sri Jagannatha with a joyful heart.

**77.** The way he performs the service of Jagannatha —
who has the power to describe it?

**78.** Sri Jagadisha's virtues cannot be described:
Jagannatha was won over by his service.

**79.** How Jagannatha came to the village of Sri Yashoda
I have described in brief.

**80.** Sri Jagadisha's pastimes are like an ocean of nectar:
because of his good character Jagannatha showed [him] mercy.

**81.** Pandita Thakura is the Prabhu of my Prabhu;
without his feet I have no refuge.

**82.** A tiny soul, I describe his pastimes;
all of you, mercifully forgive [my] offences.

**83.** Lowly, sinful am I, of very wicked intent;
I do not know what I write, I do not understand my own heart.

**84.** As Prabhu Bhagavatananda commanded,
so, in accordance with that command, Ananda composed [this].

*Thus in the Charitra-vijaya of Sri Jagadisha Pandita ends the eighth varna, called "The Account of Sri Jagadisha's Residence in the Village of Yashoda and the Manifestation of Service to Sri Jagannatha".*

[^1]: The edition reads «মহামুরষিত» (*mahāmuraṣita*); taken as «মহাহরষিত» (*mahā-haraṣita*), "greatly delighted" (cf. 34: «হই মহা হরষিত»).
[^2]: Remuna is a village in Orissa near Baleshvar. Sri Gopinatha is the Deity of Remuna known as Kshirachora-Gopinatha (CC Madhya 4).
[^3]: Sakshi-gopala ("the witness Gopala") is the Deity who came from Vrindavana as a witness for a brahmana; King Purushottama-deva brought Him to Cuttack (CC Madhya 5).
[^4]: "The eight periods of the day" (অষ্টকাল, *aṣṭa-kāla*): the eight services offered to the Deity in the course of the day. Tulasi-chura (তুলসী চূড়া, *tulasī cūṛā*): a place on the way to Puri, not identified. The Nilachakra ("blue wheel") is the disc on the top of Jagannatha's temple; pilgrims bow to it on seeing it from afar.
[^5]: The edition reads «গদ্য গদ্য ছন্দে» (*gadya gadya chande*); taken as «গদ্য পদ্য ছন্দে» (*gadya padya chande*), "in prose and verse".
[^6]: Athara-nala ("Eighteen Arches") is the bridge at the entrance to Puri (CC Madhya 5).
[^7]: Vaikuntha is apparently Koili-vaikuntha, a place in the northern part of the temple enclosure at Puri where, at the renewal of the Deities' bodies (nava-kalevara), Their former bodies are interred. "Body" (কলেবর, *kalevara*) here and below means the body of the Deity Jagannatha.
[^8]: The edition reads «তিন সঙ্গের ভারি» (*tina saṅgera bhāri*), and in 50 «তিন সাঙ্গের ভারী» (*tina sāṅgera bhārī*); the reading is unclear. Taken as "a load for three carrying-poles": সাঙ (*sāṅ*) is a pole on which several porters carry a heavy load. "Heavy [even] for three together" is also possible.
[^9]: «ভাগবতের আজ্ঞা» (*bhāgavatera ājñā*) is taken as the command of Bhagavatananda (the name shortened); a variant of the author's signature, cf. 84.
[^m56]: Maya here is the veil by which a devotee hides his greatness, i.e. humility arranged by yogamaya; it is not the external maya that deludes living beings.
[^10]: See 31 and note 8: «তিন সাঙ্গের ভারী» (*tina sāṅgera bhārī*), taken as "a load for three [porters'] carrying-poles".
[^11]: Shola (সোলা, *solā*) is the light, spongy pith of a marsh plant, from which ornaments and figures are made in Bengal.
